# When a page is done: front matter, then review

Every page gets reviewed when it is done — a new page, or a rework of an existing one. Before the review, its front matter is brought up to date.

## Order

1. **Listed from the start.** A new page goes into its index (the subjects or persons list, the parent page's links) as soon as it is created, so it can be reached and reviewed on the dev site. The gate is the commit, not the listing.
2. **Front matter first.** Update it to match the finished text: `description` (says what the page now holds; 155 characters at most — see `tone-of-voice/standards.md` § Meta descriptions), `lastmod` (only if the update is substantial — see `posting-guide.md` § Sitemap lastmod), and `title` if the page's name changed. Check the breadcrumb and any link labels elsewhere that name the page.
3. **Then review.** A cold review by the reviewer agent against the tone-of-voice guidance for the page's register, with the page's deliberate choices stated so they are not mistaken for errors. The review reads the front matter too, and looks outward: which existing pages the new material now refines (step 4a).
4. **Then decide and apply.** Jules decides on the findings; the agreed changes are applied — and if they change what the page holds, the front matter is checked again. Simple fixes that touch neither meaning nor a wording choice (a missing comma, a stray capital, a comma splice) are not put to Jules: they are applied and listed as done. Anything that changes the wording or the meaning goes to him, one point at a time, printed in full (Jules, 2026-11-11, on the Maxwell's Demon review). Keeping the site consistent is Claude's job: on reference-library pages and in the ripple, Claude makes the calls the guidance settles and applies them, saying what was done. Where an aspect is unclear, Claude opens a discussion to get a clear picture together, rather than handing Jules a list of decisions to take. This is not a details site: when in doubt, say less, and stay out of conflicting quotes and scholarly detail (Jules, 2026-11-21).
4a. **Then the ripple.** The site evolves by refinement: each new page or iteration should refine the positions that existing pages already hold. The review's outward look names the existing pages (subjects, affinity rings, reality pages, other persons) that now say less than, or something at odds with, what the new material carries; those refinements go through step 4 like any other finding and are made in the same round, not left as leftovers. A refined page then gets its own cold review before the commit (Jules, 2026-11-21, on the Henry, Stein and Zahavi pages).
5. **Then commit.** Before every commit, check the front matter again against the text as it now stands, including Jules's own last edits: `description` (its wording, not only its content: a change in the body such as "we" to "one" carries over), `words`, `lastmod`. Front matter is Claude's responsibility. Bring it up to date before the commit without asking and say what changed; never commit with a mismatch flagged as an open question (Jules, 2026-11-17, on the Thermodynamics post).

## Why

The front matter is part of the page: it is what search results and link previews show before anyone reads the body. Reviewing a page whose description still describes the old version reviews the wrong thing — the reviews of Start Here and About (2026-10-12/13) both found descriptions that no longer matched the rewritten pages.
