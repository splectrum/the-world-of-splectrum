# The cognition revolution — three post candidates

Working note, opened 2026-09-17. Candidates, not drafts: storylines settled far enough to be written, no slots claimed. The pipeline decision stays with Jules.

These come out of *A Revolution in Cognition?* (2026-10-01), which was cut back to an opener. That post ends on a door rather than a conclusion — a revolution in cognition would be a revolution in what we are prepared to count as cognition at all — and these three walk through it.

## 1. The Decentralised Cognition Revolution

Keeps the original title.

**The argument, and it is structural rather than historical.** The claim is not that AI caused a realisation. It is that cognition is decentralised wherever it is looked at, and that building it in our own image is what puts this in front of us.

Three levels, one pattern:

- **The brain.** Many processing centres, each taking input and producing output that is then used elsewhere. The routing is not fixed. There is no central reasoner receiving reports and issuing instructions — the competence is distributed across the centres and their traffic.
- **Life around us.** Living creatures cooperating, competence held across the participants rather than in one place. The same shape at a different scale.
- **Our own implementation.** Building it, we grant autonomy to components and let their outputs be used by other components. That is not a design quirk; it is the shape the other two already have. We arrive at decentralisation because it is what the thing is.

**The turn:** so the waking-up is not to what our machines might become. It is to the cognition already driving agents all around us — which was there before the machines and is visible once the assumption that cognition is centralised stops being invisible.

**What to catch in drafting.** The original text asserted a connection between machine autonomy and noticing cognition elsewhere, and the pivot line was circular ("cognition will decentralise because we become aware it is decentralised, already"). Both go. The structural argument replaces them.

**Material carried over from the cut:** the cocoon (all our extending done inside a myopic, strongly anthropocentric shell, extensions as inert instruments, the Cartesian subject at the centre with capability radiating outward, the assumption sealed in that cognition is what *we* do); the boundary between tool and agent dissolving; the giant on whose shoulders we stand.

**Backing, all built:** [Maturana](/positioning/persons/m/maturana/) on cognition as coextensive with life, [Descartes](/positioning/persons/d/descartes/), the basal cognition subject and its close-affinity page, Levin, Lyon, the cognition meta subject.

## 2. The upward direction

How decentralised cognition extends *our* mental capabilities — the direction the opener defers explicitly ("a subject with enough in it to deserve its own post, another time").

**Jules has material for this one (2026-09-17) — it needs work, but the direction is live and the world is already moving that way: how we go about solving problems is changing rapidly as the AI capabilities land.** That is the post's ground — not a promise about what machines will do, but a change already under way in how work gets approached.

Open before it can be written: which mental work is handed over and which is not; what our brains are actually suited for, stated without flattery; whether "freed for" is the right frame, or whether the handover changes what the remaining work *is*.

## 3. Alien cognition

Cognition not in our lineage, in assembly-theory terms.

**The finding that makes this a post rather than a speculation.** On [Walker](/positioning/persons/w/walker/)'s account (*Life As No One Knows It*, 2024) high-assembly objects exist only where selection has operated, which makes technological artefacts products of the *same construction lineage* as organisms — a phone or a city is the living process continuing, not something outside it. So **AI is not alien cognition.** It feels alien and is not: it is four billion years of accumulated selection, still running.

Alien cognition would be cognition arising from a construction history that is not ours at all. That is the post: the thing we call alien is family, and what would actually be alien is something we have not met.

**Why the lineage criterion matters.** It keeps the question specific. Without it the post drifts toward "is a thermostat cognitive?", which the site's threshold-free definition makes a live question and which is not the position. Lineage gives a criterion that is checkable rather than intuitive.

**Backing:** the assembly theory bundle (the extensions page carries the boundary-of-life claim), Walker, Cronin.

**Ordering:** this one needs the first two under it.
