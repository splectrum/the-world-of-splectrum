# Sara Imari Walker: research note for a portrait post

Working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

**Author points**

- [A] The post makes the case, in Walker's terms, that technology is inclusive to who we are: part of life, made by the same process that made us.
- [A] We seem to suffer from a bad immune response, identifying technology as a foreign enemy. (The immune image is developed in a separate post; this note gives only Walker's side.)
- [A] (2026-11-08, shared by the Walker post and The Decentralised Cognition Revolution) The main point is that technology forms part of "the body", and like everything else that makes up the body it needs to be managed properly. The immune image is the frame, not the subject: what the body does with it should not overshadow that it is part of it.
- [A] The hype about superintelligence: AI is (now) super intelligent on the past, not on the present or the future. It is mining knowledge we already have. To become super apart from us it would need the evolution side, and that is nowhere realised.
- [A] We think our electrical brain is it, but we have very little understanding of the chemical "brains" of our cells.
- [C] The superintelligence point in Walker's terms: AI is life, not alive. In the author's own terms, life is the record (the artifacts created so far) and alive is the process running; AI is trained on the record. Counter-case marking the limit: in closed worlds with a score, self-play has gone past us without our record (AlphaZero, chess and Go) [U]; open-ended evolution in the world is what is missing.
- [C] Part of the body: Gilbert, Sapp and Tauber's "we have never been individuals" (the holobiont) carries this more directly than the danger or discontinuity models, which can drop to a clause.

## 1. "AI Is Life" (Noema, 27 April 2023)

**Definitions** (section "Technology Evolved"):

- "By 'life,' I mean all objects that can only be produced in our universe through a process of evolution and selection. Being 'alive,' by contrast, is the active implementation of the dynamics of evolution and selection. Some objects — like a dead cat — are representative of 'life' (because they only emerge in the universe through evolution) but not themselves 'alive.'" [C, S1]
- The sentence just before it: "Technology, like biology, does not exist in the absence of evolution. Technology is not artificially replacing life — it is life." [C, S1] The second sentence is also the standfirst.
- The diagnosis: "Attempts to define life have so far failed because they focus on containing the concept of life in terms of individuals rather than evolutionary lineages." [C, S1]

**The life/alive split is older than the 2023 essay.** Michael Lachmann and Walker, "Life ≠ alive" (Aeon, 24 June 2019): "the cat and sofa are both life, but only the cat is alive" [C, S2]. In 2019 the wording differs: "Life … refers to the process that generates the required information", and "Alive refers to the continual use of negative entropy… Things that are alive are constructors" [C, S2]. So in 2019 *life* is a process; in 2023 *life* is the set of objects only that process can make. This changes how a page should phrase her (see §6). The 2019 essay also has the strongest one-line inclusion claim: "anything we create is part of our own lineage, and therefore represents the same life" [C, S2].

**Her conclusion on AI and technology** (S1, all [C]):

- One lineage: "Human technology would not exist without humans, so it is therefore part of the same ancient lineage of information that emerged with the origin of life."
- "The technologies we are and that we produce are part of the same ancient strand of information propagating through and structuring matter on our planet… We are lineages, not individuals."
- No difference in kind: human technologies "are therefore not much different from other innovations produced in our planet's 3.8-billion-year living history — with the exception that they are in our evolutionary future, not our past." "Life seeing life" (microscopes and telescopes).
- Biosphere to technosphere (section "AI Is A Major Transition In Planetary Evolution"): AI and planetary-scale computation "can be seen as an evolutionary progression — a biosphere becoming a technosphere."
- **On fear:** "A world in which machines acquire sufficient intelligence to replace biological life is the stuff of nightmares. But this fear of the artificiality of technology misses the potentially far-reaching role technologies may play in the evolutionary trajectories of living worlds." And: so-called artificial intelligences "are often discussed as disembodied and disengaged from any evolutionary context."
- **Is AI alive?** The essay does not say so. AI is *life* in her defined sense. On *alive* it says only: "we are already a few rungs down in the hierarchy of informational systems that might be considered 'alive' on this planet right now." Close: "life is what we are evolving into", with life "we might generate to co-evolve with us."

Word test: "technosphere" is Walker's own term here, not one a reader has put on her. The word itself is older (Peter Haff and others) [U].

## 2. Assembly theory

**The paper.** Sharma, Czégel, Lachmann, Kempes, Walker, Cronin, "Assembly theory explains and quantifies selection and evolution", *Nature* 622, 321–328, published 4 October 2023 [C, S3].

- Plain words, from the paper: "an assembly index is the number of steps on a minimal path producing the object", reusing parts already built; copy number is how many identical copies are observed [C, S3]. "The assembly index on its own cannot detect selection, but copy number combined with the assembly index can." [C, S3] The headline is that many identical copies of a hard-to-make object point to selection, not chance.
- The paper lists Pierrick Bourrat and George Ellis among the reviewers [C, S3, peer-review statement].

**The threshold of about 15.** It comes from Marshall et al., "Identifying molecules as biosignatures with assembly theory and mass spectrometry", *Nature Communications* 12, 3033 (24 May 2021); Walker is a co-author [C, S4]. Two passages:
- A model estimate: molecules with an assembly index (then "MA") "of between 15 and 20 would have a chance formation of one molecule in 10^23" [C, S4].
- The measured result: "it appears that only living samples produced MA measurement above ~15", with false negatives admitted, and: "not all molecules produced by living processes have high MA… but all high MA molecules are produced by living (or technological) processes." [C, S4] The parenthesis matters for the post: technology sits on the life side of the biosignature from the first measurement paper on.

**Main criticisms, in brief:**
- *Minerals.* Hazen, Burns, Cleaves, Downs, Krivovichev, Wong, *J. R. Soc. Interface* 21, 20230632 (2024): mineral heteropolyanions reach indices "as large as 21"; "values of molecular assembly indices ≥15 do not represent unambiguous biosignatures" [C, S5 abstract]. Response: Walker, Mathis, Marshall, Cronin, 20240367: their indices are "experimentally measured for organic, covalently bonded molecules", Hazen's are "theoretical" [C, S6 abstract]. Reply: Hazen et al., doi 10.1098/rsif.2024.0622: which threshold applies across organic and inorganic chemistry is "deserving of additional study" [C, S7 abstract].
- *Algorithmic information.* Abrahão, Hernández-Orozco, Kiani, Tegnér, Zenil, *PLOS Complex Systems* (2024): the assembly index "is equivalent to the size of a minimal context-free grammar" and is "subsumed into algorithmic information theory" [C, S8 abstract]. Uthamacumaran, Abrahão, Kiani, Zenil, *npj Syst. Biol. Appl.* 10 (2024): AT "performs similarly to other simple coding schemes" [C, S9 abstract]. Defence: Kempes, Lachmann, Iannaccone, Fricke, Chowdhury, Walker, Cronin, *npj Complexity* 2 (2025): the index "belongs to a different computational complexity class" [C, S10 abstract].
- *Hype and scope.* Johannes Jaeger, *J. Mol. Evol.* 92 (2024): "assembly theory has merit but is not nearly as novel or revolutionary as claimed"; it gives no "new grounding of biology in physics"; the paper "uses the term 'selection' in a very broad sense that no biologist would recognize" [C, S11].
- Kauffman: I found no critique by him. He is a collaborator on the related TAP framework, not a critic [U]. Benner and Tour were not re-checked [U].

Headline for the post: the inclusive claim does not depend on the threshold or the AIT dispute. It rests on the lineage argument (Noema, Aeon), which assembly theory formalises. The critiques bear on the measure, not on the lineage view.

## 4. Interviews: her own words on technology and fear

Lex Fridman Podcast #433, 13 June 2024, official transcript [C, S16; check the wording against the audio before quoting]:

- 00:26:36 — technosphere: "I mean the global integration of life and technology on this planet."
- 00:32:39 — why technology is life: "Because it's creative. But with us, obviously. Not independently of us. And also because of this lineage view of life."
- 00:42:36–00:43:33 — "a culture that's going through an existential trauma of inventing technologies that we don't understand right now"; "humans have always been afraid of our technologies though… every time we invent something we don't understand, it takes us a little while to catch up with it."
- 02:15:24 — on LLMs: "obviously signatures of intelligence and a part of a ecosystem of intelligence… But I don't know that individually I would assign all the properties to them that people have."
- **02:27:20 — the passage closest to the immune point:** "I don't like thinking our technologies as a separate species… this is why I talk about the technosphere. It's a system that is both human and technological. It's not human or technological… We're really good at identifying things as other. We're not really good at understanding when we're the same or when we're part of an integrated system that's actually functioning together…"
- 02:29:19 — "I really get upset when people talk about the species of artificial intelligence… it's not like the biosphere eliminated all of its past history when it invented a new species. All of these things get scaffolded, and we're also augmenting ourselves at the same time that we're building technologies."
