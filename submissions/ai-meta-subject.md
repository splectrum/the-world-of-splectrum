# AI as a meta subject — working document

*Status: working document, 2026-10-07. A record of a discussion and the proposal that came out of it — not a build plan. Creation of the doorway will take research, discussion and evolution; insight is what matters, not speed.*

---

## 1. Aim

To work out how SPLectrum should approach AI as a meta subject (keyword doorway), and to capture guidance for that approach.

Two aims sit together:

- **The doorway's aim.** AI is a doorway because it is what people respond to. The meta subject is the material speaking for itself, organised in a way that suits SPLectrum's approach. Its structure should make inroads into disentangling the confusion around AI — technology, product, use, human relationship and more — so that a number of contentious issues are addressed through proper categorisation and unpicking alone, *before one SPLectrum word is spoken*.
- **The wider aim.** To understand the confusion around AI well enough that SPLectrum's own treatment (reality side, rings, fence) can later speak to it clearly.

---

## 2. How the discussion ran

**Starting point.** Olivia Guest (Associate Professor of Computational Cognitive Science, Radboud; [olivia.science](https://olivia.science/)) was taken as an example of the confusion around AI and as a guide to how to approach the subject. The issues raised at the outset: a strongly anthropocentric stance; a human-versus-machine stand-off; limited grasp of technical internals; AI treated like consciousness (poorly defined); no separation between technology, products, sales hype, (mis)use of setup/training for products, and use cases; poor categorisation of use cases; underappreciation that the knowledge base is human and will import human issues (racism, sexism).

**First correction of framing.** Guest is an example of *the challenge we face*, not a target. Reading her work closely turned several of the starting issues around:

- She is explicit about conceptual clarity (her critical AI literacy names "terms should refer" as a dimension) and untangles the terminology in a published term table (AI, ANN, chatbot, ChatGPT, generative model, LLM).
- She has written extensively on bias and gender (Pygmalion displacement; decolonising computational sciences; image tagging and stereotyping).
- Her technical formation is real: computer science degree, PhD on computational models of semantic memory, connectionist modelling, a *Psychological Review* metatheory of classical and modern connectionism, a 2026 preprint arguing that mysterianism about the known mechanism of ANNs is mysticism.

What remained, sharpened:

- **Regime-bound intuition.** Her formation is in small connectionist networks used as cognitive models, where knowing the units and the training regime largely *was* knowing the system. That intuition does not carry over to models at current scale, where system-level behaviour is not readable off the parts. Not ignorance — a competent lens applied outside its range.
- **Separation that collapses.** Her analysis separates the referents of "AI"; her conclusions re-bundle them into one object of refusal.
- **Anthropocentrism as a value commitment.** Centring human cognition, and refusing to put human–chatbot relations in the same category as human–human ones, is deliberate.
- **Bias placed differently.** She reads bias as an indictment (industry, provenance, power) and a reason for refusal. The discussion read it as inheritance: the corpus is us. If the machine's issues are human issues, the human/machine stand-off is partly a stand-off with ourselves.

**The diagnosis that grew from there (Xiaoling's points, developed in discussion).**

- Overfocus on human issues blurs overall insight: the classical human-versus-machine preconceptions; the mirror — with current models we look into a mirror and see ourselves, which obfuscates the technology; too much focus on hype and poor product use.
- Cognitive science is general in name and principle but **human in calibration, by choice** ("it should be called human cognitive science" — the human is the only reference point it chooses to have, in the main, and this is slowly changing). The human focus brings in mind and being (embodiment), comparisons of the "like us" type, the obsession with comparing a part to a whole, and double standards for machines (self-driving cars as the example).
- **The biggest downside:** bringing in unnecessary human complexity makes it hard to do basic science.
- An AI model is **a component, not an organism**. Mechanistic interpretability runs the risk of being blind to the complex-adaptive-system side.
- **Technology versus use.** The data loaded into a system is use. The knowledge base is not the being: *load it with dog knowledge and it will be dog-like.*
- **Soft emergence of associative ability.** Logical steps are used to create a context and dynamic that goes beyond them. What emerges is the *ability* to find solutions by associative thinking rather than by logical steps — a technology-side capacity.
- **Cognition** (SPLectrum's simplified definition as a starting point): acquiring knowledge and understanding, starting at the most basic — the binary.

**Then to the doorway.** The wish for a guidance document on AI as a meta subject opened the second half: review of the evolution meta subject as the first real go at a doorway, then the shape of the AI doorway.

## 3. What the discussion established

### 3.1 Insights on AI (SPLectrum-side — for later, not for the doorway)

These are SPLectrum's reading. They belong on the reality side, in the rings or on the fence — not in the doorway. Listed here so the doorway's structure can be checked against them, not so they can be stated there.

- **Technology versus use.** Technology is the learning substrate; everything loaded into it and done with it is use, data included.
- **The knowledge base is not the being.** Current language models are like *our text*, not like *us*: a model of a written trace compared with whole, embodied beings is the part-for-whole error again. Anthropomorphism comes from the load, not the technology.
- **The mirror.** Both hype and critique ask the mirror the same question — *is it like us?* — one answering yes, one emphatically no. The question itself is the anthropocentric frame (the Turing test's question). A resonance with Rorty's *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature* was noted.
- **Component, not organism.** The model is a component; the comparison with an individual human is part against whole. Human cognition is itself distributed (language, institutions, artefacts), and the model is trained on that distributed system's output.
- **Soft emergence.** The associative ability emerges from logical implementation: derivable in principle, readable only by running, not by deduction. "Known mechanism" treats the run as if it were the deduction.
- **A layer pattern on both sides.** Hype places a mind at the system level with no pathway; deflation denies anything at the system level because the parts are known. Mechanistic interpretability and Guest's deflation both stay at component level, in different ways.
- **Human calibration and basic science.** Biology had model organisms (peas, flies, *Aplysia*, *C. elegans*); cognitive science started from the most complex case and kept it as the benchmark. Basic science on cognition needs a substrate-neutral account of what cognition is; SPLectrum's simplified definition is a starting point.

*Points raised in discussion but not confirmed — held open:*

- Whether, on SPLectrum's definition of cognition, the cognitive process sits mainly in training, with the deployed (frozen-weights) model mostly acquired knowledge plus thin in-context acquisition — and whether much public argument is therefore about the snapshot.
- Double standards: a distinction between anthropocentric asymmetry and asymmetry with structural grounds (correlated failure across a fleet, accountability).
- The seam where use leaks into technology (tokenisers built from the corpus, training objectives chosen for a use).

### 3.2 Guest's place on the site

- She is an example of the challenge, not a target.

### 3.3 Lessons from the evolution meta subject

Reviewed: the landing, *The word*, *The criterion*.

**What worked**

- The main confusion (Darwinian descent versus unfolding) was untangled into named senses in the field's own words.
- The default reader was redirected, not dismissed ("readers who arrive expecting biology…").
- *The criterion* presented the dominant case's instrument at full strength, then said what it does not measure — backed by fields' own words, with placements reported as the fields' own. This decentres the dominant measure without a verdict.
- Disputes appeared as headlines (Bowler against Richards), attributed and left open.

**What went less well**

- The disentangling lived in the prose, not in the structure: two senses set up in the landing, then a flat list of nineteen pages with an implicit but unlabelled order.
- The landing carried a lot of dense synthesis.

**Why (Xiaoling):** with evolution, what binds the diverse list sits in the background; with AI it is more in the foreground. And it was the first real go.

**The inversion this gives:**

- Evolution: the reader arrives with *diversity* and the binding has to be shown — prose does that.
- AI: the reader arrives assuming *unity* ("AI" as one thing, the chatbot standing for all of it) — the doorway has to show the diversity within it, and grouped structure does that.
- General principle: where the binding sits in the background, the prose shows the unity; where it sits in the foreground, the structure shows the separations.

## 4. Proposal: the AI doorway

### 4.1 Status

AI is a doorway in its own right, cross-linked to cognition — not a facet of cognition. It is what people respond to. (Filing AI under cognition would bring back the "is there a mind in there" framing.)

### 4.2 Voice and organisation

- The material speaks for itself, in the field's words.
- The organisation suits SPLectrum's approach: glasses on, no voice.
- Category names are ordinary words; the arrangement carries the approach.
- Contentious issues are addressed through categorisation and unpicking, before any SPLectrum word is spoken.

### 4.3 Shape

- **The word up front.** A history of "AI" as a word comes first, and is expected to help fine-tune the structure. The founding document (the 1955 Dartmouth proposal) already binds a technical programme to the question of mind — the word was born tangled. Dating each later sense (the field naming itself, reportedly choosing the term over the cybernetics label; the rebrandings — expert systems, machine learning, deep learning; "generative AI"; AI as a consumer-product label) shows which entanglements are original and which were added.
- **An entry that meets the reader.** A short redirect for readers who arrive with a chatbot or product in mind, in the manner of evolution's "readers who arrive expecting biology".
- **A structure that unravels naturally.** Readers entering should meet a structure that starts to unravel in a natural way; each part then addresses its different aspects. One candidate order peels outward from what people meet: product → what's inside (technology) → what was loaded (data and training) → what it's used for → how people stand towards it → how it is studied and talked about.
- **A deep hierarchy.** The depth is the categorisation structure. The leaves will often live elsewhere — existing person and subject pages (Turing, Searle, Fodor, Dennett, Hofstadter, Shannon, von Neumann, Wiener, Friston, Levin, Lyon, Walker; evolutionary computation; machine translation; complex adaptive systems; basal cognition; emergence and its relation with mechanism).
- **Leaves attached to several branches.** One can expect leaves to be attached to different branches of the categorisation. Each entry says what that branch brings to the leaf (cross-bleeding by aspect, as in the bottom row). Turing, for instance, appears under the technology's foundations and again under the comparison debates.
- **External leaves allowed.** Where no internal page exists, an external reference serves as backing and as a visible marker of a judgement not yet made; leaves are brought inside over time.
- **Depth without thin pages** (proposal). Levels can be headed sections within a branch hub; a node earns its own page when there is enough beneath it. Each hub shows where it sits in the unravelling.

### 4.4 Neutral review by annotation

Where the doorway reviews contested ground — the mind questions above all — it reports without being opinionated. SPLectrum's own reading comes later, when it can speak.

The method: **apply the categorisation and annotate what is reviewed with the categorisation aspects.** A position is not judged and does not need one correct placement; the annotation records which aspects it actually touches, and the disentangling becomes visible by itself.

Illustrations:

- **The Chinese room** — technology (symbol manipulation); people (what counts as understanding).
- **The Turing test** — people (the judge's discrimination); technology (the machine's performance).
- **Stochastic parrots** — data (training on linguistic form); technology; discourse (the hype it answers).
- **Guest's critical AI literacy** — industry; products; use (academia); discourse. Laid out this way, a reader can see the critique sits mostly on the use side, without anyone saying so.

The tree gives each item a primary home; the annotation shows everything else it touches. Two views of one structure — no single placement has to carry the full weight of fairness.

### 4.5 History threaded per branch

The standard history of AI — summers and winters — is itself an entanglement: the winters were funding and discourse events while the technology kept moving underneath (backpropagation popularised between the two winters; convolutional networks developed during the second). So:

- each branch carries its own history (technology, products, the comparison debates from ELIZA onwards, discourse);
- the word page carries the term's own history;
- no single merged timeline.

### 4.6 Industry: the industrial-revolution angle

Industry (companies, compute, chips, energy, investment, concentration) is approached from an industrial-revolution angle. In discussion this looked right because:

- it places AI as one component in a larger system — energy, labour, capital, infrastructure, institutions — the glasses doing their work without a word said;
- the field backs it, with leaves already on the site (Mokyr, Arthur, Schumpeter, Mumford; the evolution subject's technology branch), and external candidates (Carlota Perez's model of technological revolutions running from bubble to deployment, which places hype in a historical pattern; Bresnahan and Trajtenberg's general-purpose technologies; Solow's productivity paradox; Gordon's scepticism; the "Fourth Industrial Revolution" framing);
- both camps fit inside it: the Luddite history (Brian Merchant's *Blood in the Machine*) and the revolution narrative alike — "we've been here before" is a claim both sides make.

Whether AI *is* a revolution is reported as part of the material; the present-day industry structure is the current chapter of that history.

### 4.7 Draft categorisation (an exercise, to be tuned after the word history)

Top level in candidate unravelling order, with a second level:

0. **The word** — "AI" from the 1955 proposal onwards; the senses dated.
1. **Products** — what people meet
   - chatbots and assistants (ELIZA onwards); generative media; predictive and decision systems (scoring, hiring, risk); embedded and autonomous systems (recommenders, search, vehicles, robotics); scientific systems (protein structure, materials); product history (the expert-systems boom onwards)
2. **Technology** — what's inside
   - approaches (symbolic, connectionist, statistical, evolutionary, hybrid); architectures and learning methods (perceptron to transformers and scale); foundations (computation, information, cybernetics); capacities and their study (emergence claims, interpretability, theoretical limits)
3. **Data and training** — what's loaded, and how
   - corpora and provenance (sources, consent, labour); training regimes (pretraining, fine-tuning, preference tuning); inheritance (what the data carries in); evaluation (benchmarks as technical measures)
4. **Use** — what it is applied to, by domain
   - science; industry and engineering; medicine; education and academia; creative work; public sector; military; everyday consumer use
5. **People and AI** — how people stand towards it
   - the yardstick (human-level benchmarks, AGI definitions, the imitation game — and what they measure, in the manner of evolution's criterion page); anthropomorphism (the ELIZA effect, Pygmalion displacement); trust and double standards (algorithm aversion, self-driving); work (automation, displacement, deskilling); companionship and care
6. **Study of AI** — how it is studied
   - philosophy of AI (the mind questions — thinking, understanding, consciousness, moral status — hosted under the field's own name, all camps reported); cognitive science's uses of AI; critical AI studies; AI ethics
7. **Discourse** — how it is talked about
   - hype and cycles; futures and fears (AGI timelines, risk, safety); policy and regulation; myth and fiction (Pygmalion, the golem, *R.U.R.*)
8. **Industry** — the industrial-revolution angle
   - AI in the history of industrial revolutions; general-purpose technology; bubbles and deployment; labour and the Luddite history; the current chapter (companies, chips, compute, energy, export controls)

Separating *Study of AI* from *Discourse* keeps scholarship (including Guest's) out of the same box as the marketing it criticises.

### 4.8 The test: contentious issues unpicked by categorisation alone

The structure is doing its job if each familiar confusion comes apart into branches. *This table is a working check — it belongs to the guidance, not to the doorway, where a list of "confusions" would be the site talking.*

| Contentious issue | How categorisation unpicks it |
|---|---|
| "AI is biased" | appears by aspect: where it comes from (data), what was done about it (products), who it lands on (people) |
| "ChatGPT is AI" | one product under products; the technology is a different branch |
| "AGI is coming" | futures under discourse; capacities and limits under technology — side by side, apart |
| "AI is useless" / "AI is a revolution" | broken down by use domain, where claims can be weighed case by case; the revolution claim sits in industry history |
| "Can it think?" | hosted in philosophy of AI, annotated by the aspects each position touches |
| The AI-winters history | splits into a technology history and a discourse/funding history that don't line up |
| Self-driving double standards | people (trust, double standards) annotated against use (transport) |

## 5. Guidance principles (consolidated)

1. **AI is a doorway** because it is what people respond to.
2. **The material speaks; the organisation suits SPLectrum's approach.** Glasses on, no voice. Ordinary words for categories.
3. **Address contentious issues through categorisation and unpicking** before any SPLectrum word is spoken.
4. **Where the binding sits in the foreground, the structure shows the separations.** Grouped, visible branches — not a flat list with the disentangling hidden in prose.
5. **Put the word up front** and let its dated senses tune the structure.
6. **Meet the reader where they arrive** and let the structure unravel naturally from there.
7. **The depth is the categorisation; the leaves often live elsewhere.** Leaves attach to several branches, each entry saying what that branch brings.
8. **Neutral review by annotation.** Report without being opinionated; annotate what is reviewed with the categorisation aspects.
9. **Thread history per branch;** never one merged timeline.
10. **Industry through the industrial-revolution angle.**
11. **Test the structure against the known confusions** — each should come apart into branches.
12. **External leaves are allowed** and brought inside over time.
13. **SPLectrum's own reading lives elsewhere** — reality side, rings, fence — and links down into the doorway; the doorway does not link up.
