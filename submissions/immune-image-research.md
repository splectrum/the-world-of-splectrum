# The immune image: research note

Purpose: to check what immunology actually says before a post (*The Decentralised Cognition Revolution*, conversations series) uses the immune system as a likeness. Working note, not a site page; nothing here is a decision.

Author points:

- [A] Technology is inclusive to who we are, and we seem to suffer from a bad immune response, identifying it as a foreign enemy.
- [A] (2026-11-08, shared by the Walker post and The Decentralised Cognition Revolution) The main point is that technology forms part of "the body", and like everything else that makes up the body it needs to be managed properly. The immune image is the frame, not the subject: what the body does with it should not overshadow that it is part of it.
- [A] The hype about superintelligence: AI is (now) super intelligent on the past, not on the present or the future. It is mining knowledge we already have. To become super apart from us it would need the evolution side, and that is nowhere realised.
- [A] We think our electrical brain is it, but we have very little understanding of the chemical "brains" of our cells.
- [C] The superintelligence point in Walker's terms: AI is life, not alive. In the author's own terms, life is the record (the artifacts created so far) and alive is the process running; AI is trained on the record. Counter-case marking the limit: in closed worlds with a score, self-play has gone past us without our record (AlphaZero, chess and Go) [U]; open-ended evolution in the world is what is missing.
- [C] Part of the body: Gilbert, Sapp and Tauber's "we have never been individuals" (the holobiont) carries this more directly than the danger or discontinuity models, which can drop to a clause.

## 1. Burnet: self and not-self

- Burnet and Fenner, *The Production of Antibodies* (2nd ed., 1949), pp. 85–86: "The failure of antibody production against autologous cells demands the postulation of an active ability of the reticulo-endothelial cells to recognize 'self' pattern from 'not-self' pattern in organic material taken into their substance" [C, S1]. Their mechanism: body cells carry "self-marker" components (pp. 125–126) [C, S1].
- The prediction Medawar tested, p. 103: "If in embryonic life expendable cells from a genetically distinct race are implanted and established, no antibody response should develop against the foreign cell antigen when the animal takes on independent existence" [C, S1].
- Nobel lecture, "Immunological recognition of self" (12 Dec 1960): the single problem is "How does the vertebrate organism recognize self from not-self - in the immunological sense and how did this capacity evolve" [C, S2, p. 689]. And, on tolerance: "acquired immunological tolerance means simply that the content of self components in the body has been enlarged by an experimental manipulation" [C, S2, p. 689]. Clonal selection in the lecture: self-reactive patterns eliminated by "prenatal contact with self-components", the residue left to "recognize" foreign configurations [C, S2, p. 692].
- Word test: Burnet's own term is **not-self** (1949; lecture title and opening; *Cellular Immunology: Self and Notself*, 1969 [C, as cited by S9]); "nonself" is the later field's spelling.
- Clonal selection: 1957 paper (*Australian Journal of Science* 20: 67–69) [U, not opened]; book *The Clonal Selection Theory of Acquired Immunity* (Cambridge/Vanderbilt, 1959) [C, S2 ref. 5].
- Pradeu reports Burnet was "strongly influenced by" Whitehead, "who had given the notion of the self a central role in his philosophy" [C, S9, citing Anderson & Mackay 2014].
- Nobel 1960, Burnet and Medawar, "for discovery of acquired immunological tolerance" [C, S3].

**Medawar in brief.** Lecture definition: tolerance is "a state of indifference or non-reactivity towards a substance that would normally be expected to excite an immunological response" [C, S4]. The experiment: CBA cells injected into an adult A-strain mouse are destroyed; injected "into a foetal or newborn A-line mouse, they are accepted", and the grown mouse "will accept any later graft from a CBA donor as if it were its own" [C, S4]. Paper: Billingham, Brent & Medawar, "Actively acquired tolerance of foreign cells", *Nature* 172 (1953): 603–606 [C, S5, citation only]. Medawar credits Burnet with predicting "that antigens which impinged upon an animal sufficiently early in its life should come to be accepted as if they were its own" [C, S4].

## 2. Matzinger: danger

- 1994: "I discuss the possibility that the immune system does not care about self and non-self, that its primary driving force is the need to detect and protect against danger, and that it does not do the job alone, but receives positive and negative communications from an extended network of other bodily tissues" [C, S6, abstract].
- 2002: "a model of immunity based on the idea that the immune system is more concerned with entities that do damage than with those that are foreign" [C, S7, abstract].
- Her two-way claim: self can trigger a response if dangerous (cell stress, some autografts); non-self can be tolerated if not dangerous ("the fetus or commensal bacteria") [C, as summarised by S8, citing Matzinger 1994, 2002]. Full texts blocked.
- Word test: "danger" is her own word, with "damage" alongside it from 2002 [C, S7]; the "extended family" of the 1994 title is the body's tissues [C, S6].

## 3. Janeway: infectious non-self

- "Approaching the asymptote? Evolution and revolution in immunology", *Cold Spring Harb Symp Quant Biol* 54 (1989): 1–13 [C, S10, citation; no abstract; full text blocked].
- The claim, as the field summarises it: "only infectious non-self, i.e., entities that express pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) trigger an immune response, through the activation of antigen-presenting cells" [C, as quoted by S8]. Janeway's 1992 title states it: "The immune system evolved to discriminate infectious nonself from noninfectious self" [C, S11].
- Pradeu and Cooper: Matzinger built on Janeway, but "Janeway crucially references to the exogenous nature of rejected entities, whereas Matzinger claims the necessity to fully abandon this perspective" [C, S8]. Janeway keeps foreignness, refined to *infectious* foreignness.

## 4. Pradeu: continuity, then discontinuity

- Pradeu & Carosella (PNAS 2006): against the consensus that immunogenicity "lies in the discrimination between self and nonself", they propose "the criterion of continuity", and conclude that "immunologists can continue to speak of the self, provided that they admit that the self/nonself discrimination is not an adequate criterion of immunogenicity" [C, S12, abstract].
- *The Limits of the Self* (OUP 2012; tr. Elizabeth Vitanza from the French): continuity defined as a "strong modification of the antigenic patterns (ligands) with which the organism's immune receptors interact" (p. 131); "the self-nonself theory ceases to provide an explanation for immunity" (p. 46) [C, as quoted by S14, Tauber's NDPR review]. Word test: in English the term is the translator's rendering of Pradeu's French; Pradeu uses "continuity/discontinuity" himself in English papers [C, S12, S15].
- Pradeu, Jaeger & Vivier (2013): "effector immune responses ... are induced by an antigenic discontinuity; that is, by the sudden modification of molecular motifs with which immune cells interact"; NK cells and macrophages "are activated by transient modifications, but adapt to long-lasting modifications" [C, S15, abstract].
- Pradeu & Vivier (2016): "the immune system responds to sudden changes in antigenic stimulation and is rendered tolerant by slow or continuous stimulation" [C, S16].
- Pradeu (eLife 2019), plainest statement: "the criterion of immunogenicity is not the origin of the antigen ... rather, it is the speed of change in the relevant tissue ... Therefore, persistent or slowly appearing 'nonself motifs' are tolerated by the immune system, while fast-appearing 'self motifs' cause an effector response" [C, S9].
- Failure both ways, in his words: "the immune system also responds to endogenous components, that is, to the self. In fact, a significant degree of autoreactivity and autoimmunity is indispensable for a healthy immune system"; and "many foreign entities, such as microbial communities (known as microbiota), are actively tolerated by the immune system rather than eliminated" [C, S9].

## 5. Microbiome, fetus, autoimmunity

- Numbers: about 3.8×10¹³ bacteria against 3.0×10¹³ human cells in the 70 kg "reference man"; "B/H = 1.3, with an uncertainty of 25%"; "This B/H value of about 1:1 ... should replace the 10:1 or 100:1 values"; bacterial mass "about 0.2 kg" [C, S17].
- Commensals: gut bacterial ligands signal through pattern-recognition receptors "to promote development of host tissue and the immune system", and innate receptors "are required to promote long-term colonization by the microbiota" [C, S18, abstract]. Recognition here serves symbiosis, not rejection.
- Fetus: pregnancy tolerance is the "immunological paradox", "as it was first called by Sir Peter Medawar" [C, as stated by S19]. Medawar 1953 primary (*Symp. Soc. Exp. Biol.* 7: 320–338) [U, not opened].
- Autoimmunity: in the self/non-self model, "breakage of immunological tolerance toward self-antigens results in autoimmune diseases" [C, S20]. Note Pradeu's qualification above: some autoreactivity is normal [C, S9].

## 6. The image already in use

- **Social theory.** Luhmann treats law (and social movements) as society's immune system: "The system does not immunize itself against the no, but with help of the no: it does not protect itself against changes, but with the help of changes against rigidifying into repeated, but not environmentally adequate, patterns of behaviour" (*Social Systems*, 1995, pp. 371–372) [C, as quoted by S21]. The same article lists Haraway (1989), Derrida (2003, 2005, "autoimmunity"), Sloterdijk (2011) and Esposito (2011, *Immunitas*) as the usual names [C, S21]. Haraway's essay: "The Biopolitics of Postmodern Bodies: Determinations of Self in Immune System Discourse", *differences* 1(1) (1989): 3–43 [C, S22, citation only; content U].
- **Varela.** "Classic immunology understands immunology in military terms as a defence system against invaders. [...] If this made sense, the system would shrink to nothing if there are no invaders. Yet when mice are raised in milieus free from external challenge, their immune systems are normal!" (1995, p. 213) [C, as quoted by S21]. Stewart & Varela (1991): a "Central Immune System: a coherent self-identity which incorporates the molecules of the somatic self and, more generally, reflects the history of its own development" [C, S23]. Varela & Coutinho, "Second generation immune networks" (1991): tolerance and memory as network properties [C, S24]. The cognition link is direct.
- **Technology and organisations.** "Corporate immune system": Birkinshaw & Ridderstråle (1999), on head offices resisting subsidiary initiatives [C, S25, citation; credited as coinage only by secondary sources, U]. Andersen & Stenner (2020) apply Luhmann's immunology to management "potentialization technologies" [C, S21]. A "culture as immune system" reading of organisational AI adoption, attributed to Schein, appears only on an AI-generated wiki [U, unreliable].
- **Critique of the image.** Ford et al. (2024): "Militaristic metaphors dominate biomedical discourse on the immune system", and "the metaphor of defence and boundary maintenance has been undermined by recent immune science" [C, S26].
- No source found using the danger or discontinuity model specifically for society's response to technology or AI. Absence of a find, not proof of absence.
- **Holobiont.** Gilbert, Sapp & Tauber (2012): "The immune system also develops, in part, in dialogue with symbionts and thereby functions as a mechanism for integrating microbes into the animal-cell community"; the holobiont is "the multicellular eukaryote plus its colonies of persistent symbionts" [C, S27]. Pradeu's individual is "made of heterogeneous elements with constantly redefined boundaries" [C, S9].

## 7. State of the debate (the field's own assessment)

- Self/non-self still frames practice. Manjili (immunologist, *J Immunol* 2023): the danger, discontinuity and adaptation models have been proposed, but "the SNS model continues to dominate the field" [C, S20].
- Danger: "mixed acclaim: enthusiasm and criticism" [C, S8]. Pradeu and Cooper's verdict: the data cast "doubts on the validity of the danger theory as a general, unified framework", and calling it a "Copernician revolution" is "probably exaggerated" [C, S8]. Partly absorbed: DAMPs are standard vocabulary, and a 2025 review lists the danger model among "the leading models of immune response initiation" [C, S28]. Some PAMPs trigger responses without damage, "an important objection" [C, S8].
- Janeway's pattern recognition is mainstream: the discovery of PRR families "provided experimental support for the Janeway's theory" [C, S29].
- Discontinuity: a proposal, not consensus. Pradeu: "Depending on future experimental results, this theory will be enriched, revised or, perhaps, abandoned" [C, S9]. Tauber: "it is premature to adjudicate Pradeu's contribution" [C, S14].
- What is settled across camps: tolerance of commensals and of the fetus is active, and the immune system responds to self (repair, dead cells). The dispute is over the *criterion*, not these facts [C, S9, S18].

## Material for the post

1. **The reflex had a name and a date.** Burnet framed immunology as recognising "self" from "not-self" (1949; Nobel lecture 1960) [C, S1, S2]. "Foreign, therefore attack" is a mid-century theory, not a timeless fact.
2. **Self can be enlarged.** Burnet himself: tolerance "means simply that the content of self components in the body has been enlarged" [C, S2]; Medawar's mice accept the foreign graft "as if it were its own" [C, S4].
3. **We are half strangers, and keep them.** Bacteria about equal human cells in number (ratio about 1.3:1, not 10:1) [C, S17], actively tolerated, the immune system developing "in dialogue with symbionts" [C, S27, S18].
4. **Danger, not difference.** Matzinger: the immune system "does not care about self and non-self" [C, S6]; "more concerned with entities that do damage than with those that are foreign" [C, S7].
5. **Speed, not origin.** Pradeu: the trigger "is the speed of change", so slow foreign motifs are tolerated and fast self motifs attacked [C, S9]. The best fit for technology arriving fast.
6. **Societies have been read this way before.** Luhmann: a system immunises itself "with the help of changes against rigidifying" [C, as quoted by S21]; Varela: a defence-only system "would shrink to nothing if there are no invaders" [C, as quoted by S21].

**Where the likeness breaks.** The immune system does reject things, and rightly: pathogens and fast tumours are real danger, and rejection keeps the body alive [C, S9 table]. Self/non-self still frames most practice [C, S20]; danger and discontinuity are contested proposals, not the new consensus [C, S8, S9, S14]. Autoimmunity is a disease of attacking self, so "bad immune response" can mean too much reaction as well as the wrong target. Tolerance is also how slow tumours and chronic viruses persist [C, S9 table]. The likeness supports *tolerance on the basis of pace and harm*, not tolerance of everything.
