# Common understanding as a shared language

Discussion findings, 2026-09-18. A thread that will straddle the seed section and the reality side. Not a plan and not a position yet — the reasoning as it stands, and the questions it leaves.

Arose from the Putnam post, where the question was why internal realism could not stop short of relativism.

## The problem it answers

Putnam's difficulty, stated accurately: internal realism replaced correspondence-truth with what would be ideally rationally acceptable within a conceptual scheme. But the criteria of acceptability — coherence, simplicity, empirical adequacy — are themselves stated within a scheme. So the standard goes relative along with everything else, and the position slides toward the relativism it was built to avoid. He needed a standard holding *across* schemes and could not have one from inside any.

Note the shape of the trap: the standard has to stand above the schemes, and there is no above.

## The move

Convergence is not a standard. It is an outcome, checkable from where subjects actually stand. Objective is not "true independently of any game" but **converged within what is commonly understood**. No God's-eye view is needed because none is claimed, so the regress does not start.

Already on the site, in `reality/epistemology/`: convergence as the dynamic of learning, the same by which we come to agree on the meaning of a word; explicitly *not* the pragmatist's convergence of inquiry, sitting a layer deeper — in the tools any inquiry already runs on. And the honest concession that a whole community can converge on what proves false, which is the ordinary condition of knowing rather than a breakdown. The discipline is the coupling itself: one cannot privately decide a word means whatever one likes and still be understood.

## What crosses the boundary — understanding, not agreement

The load-bearing distinction. What converges across language boundaries is *understanding* — what the terms mean, what is being talked about — not *agreement* about what is true. Two languages can reach common understanding and disagree completely, which is the sign that no ranking has been smuggled in: understanding another language costs nothing in equal standing.

The epistemology page already carries the ordering — we converge on the meaning of a word before we can converge on anything said with it.

## Common understanding IS a language (Jules, 2026-09-18)

The formulation the thread turned on, twice.

First: the decohered common understanding can be seen as **a shared language, used to convey what each subject tries to express in its own specific language game**. So convergence produces a *carrier*, not a shared conclusion, and disagreement rides on it intact.

Then the closing step: common understanding requires the tools to express it, and tools are what a language is. So implicitly or explicitly it has to map to a language. There is no intermediate layer where understanding is shared but unexpressed — it either has the tools or it is not yet common. P0 gets there directly: a common understanding is held between subjects, so it has a language.

Two consequences, both worth holding:

- **The non-neutrality worry dissolves.** A shared language sediments from particular couplings and carries their marks; some things are easier to say in it than others. But there is no neutral alternative to fall short of — an unexpressed common understanding is not a purer version, it is nothing. The partiality is the ordinary condition, the same move `reality/emergence/` makes about the partial cut.
- **The shared language is a game in its own right.** Not merely a medium: it has its own moves, its own competence, its own boundaries, and each subject stands to it as to any game it does not wholly own. More consequential than a carrier metaphor suggests.

## Where it touches

- **Seed section.** A metalanguage of the common, and a shared language particular subjects built to talk across theirs, are not obviously the same thing. Same layer described twice, or two layers? The coin page's perimeter material is adjacent — the seed's domain being the common, its own edge coinciding with the structure's.
- **Reality side.** Epistemology holds the convergence account. Aesthetics is where expression lives — the working of shared tools into what one has to say — so the mapping-to-language step is aesthetics' territory as much as epistemology's.
- **Language register.** If the shared language is a game, the register question is which game a given surface is speaking in, and that is already the site's own discipline applied to its subject matter.
- **RQM and physics.** The decoherence rhyme is the reason the framing arrived: what crosses is what survives coupling with independent others, and redundant uptake across genuinely different languages is stronger than agreement within one. **Held as candidate, not settled** — decoherence is recorded in `scheduled-tasks.md` as a candidate *mechanism* under belonging, to be researched and discussed when that subject is taken up, and the two guards stand (relational/RQM reading only, the many-worlds gloss smuggling back a universal wavefunction; and is-like at full force). The understanding-not-agreement point does not depend on the physics at all, and should not be made to.
