# Objectivity and widening circles of subjective convergence

*Captured from discussion, 2026-11-19/20. It grew out of a reading of Thomas Nagel's account of objectivity, prompted by his November 2026 Wired interview. Planned outcome: a subject page on objectivity (the field) and a reality page (SPLectrum's position), in that order.*

- **[A]**: the author's position, verbatim or near-verbatim.
- **[C]**: observation or suggestion from Claude, to be confirmed.
- **[U]**: unverified. Everything marked [U] goes through a research round before it backs anything.

## The position

- **[A]** The focus: widening circles of subjective convergence. Objectivity in the limit, but always bound to a shared language context.
- **[A]** A "soft reasoned" approach that counteracts the absolutism that is still very much around.
- **[A]** The position is well supported by other thinkers, more in aspects of it than in the totality of the SPLectrum approach.
- **[A]** If the path to objectivity starts with sharing, SPLectrum goes as far as converged subjectivity as "reachable objectivity". Others (Nagel among them) argue that sharing takes on a universal character, and that objectivity is, for instance, devoid of cultural context.
- **[A]** The way out of the subject: for Nagel by detachment, for SPLectrum by ever widening circles of subjective convergence.
- **[C]** Already on the site, in SPLectrum's voice: objectivity is converged subjectivity, with nothing above the convergence to certify it (the epistemology pillar and the shared-voice page). New ground for these pages: objectivity approached from the side of the language of expression, the widening circles, objectivity as a limit, and what a claim to universality does.

## The language of expression

- **[A]** Not yet on the site: objectivity approached from the side of the language of expression. Nagel's universal objectivity does not factor in the language needed to get there.
- **[A]** Objectivity is always expressed in a context language (local). One only gets into view-from-nowhere land by proclaiming what one cannot really justify: a language expression universal enough to lift what are considered objective remarks into universality.
- **[A]** The earth around the sun can as well be described as around the centre of the Milky Way, or the sun around the earth.
- **[C]** Physics allows the motion to be described from any of these frames; the geocentric description is not false, only more complicated. Which counts as "the fact" depends on the frame chosen to express it: even the hardest objective case is stated in a frame of reference, a language.
- **[C]** Where the exclusion happens in Nagel, *The Last Word* (1997), p. 38: the language one speaks "is essentially a tool of thought… Understanding it is a form of thought, but it is not the material of which thoughts are made." Language as a vehicle that can be set aside; objectivity placed in what it carries.
- **[A]** Positioning in reasoning that excludes the language it is expressed in from the picture: any form of "universal" objectivity leads to what the author calls colonialism, a context-specific language expression assigning itself a dominant position and demoting others, on what are, in the author's view, subjective thoughts.
- **[C]** The mechanism: once the language of a claim is out of the picture, its context-specific expression reads as "how things are", and other expressions become "beliefs", "culture", "superstition". For public text the word "colonialism" needs its definition alongside it.

## The widening circles

- **[A]** Point-of-view expansion (Nagel's method of objectivity) is fair play and works well, for physics for instance. But it remains expressed in language, within a cultural context. And then universal character is claimed for reason.
- **[A]** The stepping back is a form of abstract conceptualisation, a good insight. But it runs on shared tools, language and concepts, which converge first, at least in order to be broadcastable.
- **[A]** Someone could do it all privately, but then we wouldn't know. Once sharing starts, convergence has to happen in understanding, in the language, before the objectivity of a knowledge position can be transferred. There are assembly theory patterns in this: a position has to make its way into the complexity of expression.
- **[C]** The assembly reading: a knowledge position is built in the shared language from parts already shared; the receiver must hold the sub-assemblies; the complexity of expression is its assembly index; copy number is convergence, the trace of a building history rather than of something given in advance. Is-like, not is.
- **[C]** Convergence comes in widths: a conversation, a community, a culture, a science, the sciences across cultures. What gets called universal is the widest convergence there is, claimed as final.

## What is not convergence

- **[A]** A thought that is correct and never shared is not objective: it cannot be judged outside the subject. Calling it correct requires "from nowhere", non-participating powers of observation and evaluation, and leaves those who talk about it out of the reality assigned to the subject.
- **[A]** Content that does not depend on who has it has been shared and has converged. It is hindsight, within a cultural context, that allows one to make that claim.
- **[A]** "Physics and mathematics converge across cultures that never met" is a contradiction. Inevitably there is a shared base. And importing something as settled does not make one part of convergence.
- **[C]** So: parallel arrival is not convergence (that results are "the same" is seen only once cultures meet and translate); a shared base (the same kind of embodied being, the same disclosed reality, the same kind of language) explains agreement without a seat outside; adoption of settled results is not taking part. [U] Historical cases if used: the Pythagorean relation in Babylonian, Indian and Chinese mathematics; zero in India and among the Maya.
- **[C]** Not a free-for-all: reality discloses itself through language, so convergence is constrained. What is declined is a seat outside the sharing from which to name the constraint.

## Fact and value: one mechanism

- **[A]** "Consciousness decides who belongs to the moral universe" is a god decision. The same mechanism is at play in the moral universe: human moral principles are pushed onto animals we judge similar enough to us (feel pain, are conscious). The moral universe is expressed in languages of the lower circles of subjective convergence.
- **[A]** The author's approach to life in general is non-discriminatory. The lifecycle of individuals is essential to the way life is expressed. We can't eat stones; we eat what is still alive or was not long ago. Deciding what has a higher status in it is very often an act of cruelty out of ignorance.
- **[C]** Two cases, one mechanism: a language of a narrow circle proclaimed universal, membership or truth decided from a seat no one occupies. Ranking by similarity to us puts lowest what we understand least; ignorance becomes status, and status becomes conduct.
- **[C]** Register: SPLectrum's ethics is descriptive. Concern for animals is read as a value of our game, held in our language; what is questioned is its proclamation as universal. The non-discriminatory stance itself probably belongs on the ethics pillar; the objectivity pages name the mechanism.

## The field, as a map

*[C] All [U], from memory, for the research round. "Page" means a person page exists.*

- **The universal pole:** objectivity as detachment toward a world and a reason independent of all of us. Thomas Nagel (page): objectivity as a method of stepping back, in degrees, the view from nowhere as an ideal never reached; convergence as effect and evidence, not ground; reason's universal authority (*The Last Word*). Frege and the rationalist line behind him.
- **The convergence pole:**
  - Peirce (page): truth as what inquiry would converge on, in the limit.
  - Popper (page): objectivity as intersubjective testability.
  - Habermas (page): communicative rationality, agreement under ideal conditions.
  - Helen Longino (no page): objectivity as social, criticism within a community (*Science as Social Knowledge*, 1990).
  - Davidson (no page): triangulation between two speakers and a shared world.
  - Wittgenstein (page): agreement in judgements, forms of life.
- **The situated critique:**
  - Donna Haraway (no page): situated knowledges; the view from nowhere as the "god trick" (1988).
  - Putnam (page): conceptual relativity, no view from nowhere.
  - Santiago Castro-Gómez (no page): the "hubris of the zero point" (2005); with Quijano, Mignolo, de Sousa Santos on the coloniality of knowledge.
- **The historical view:**
  - Lorraine Daston and Peter Galison (no page): *Objectivity* (2007), objectivity as an epistemic virtue with a history.
  - Ludwik Fleck (no page): thought collectives and thought styles (1935).
- **The moral circle:** Peter Singer (page to check), *The Expanding Circle* (1981): the circle widens by similarity to us.
