## The starting point

- **[A]** "The limits of my language mean the limits of my world" is very SPLectrum. Read with the remarks around it, including those on solipsism, the resonance is the personal nature of reality: *my* language. It has nothing to do with vocabulary.
- **[A]** The remarks show the struggle with reality and the subject: "the subject does not belong to the world."
- **[A]** Philosophy has a habit of excluding the subject from the world. It is even more in the habit of excluding the language used to talk about the world from that world.
- **[A]** The passage puts a finger on where the struggle is: recognising a third way between solipsism and a subject that does not exist in the world.
- **[C]** The sequence in the *Tractatus* [U, Pears–McGuinness wording from memory]:
  - 5.6: "The limits of *my* language mean the limits of *my* world." The original reads *Die Grenzen meiner Sprache bedeuten die Grenzen meiner Welt*, with "my" emphasised. The widely shared quote card has "means", which is wrong.
  - 5.61: logic pervades the world; "what we cannot think we cannot say either".
  - 5.62: "what the solipsist *means* is quite correct; only it cannot be *said*, but makes itself manifest. The world is my world: this is manifest in the fact that the limits of language (of that language which alone I understand) mean the limits of my world."
  - 5.621: "The world and life are one."
  - 5.63: "I am my world. (The microcosm.)"
  - 5.631: "There is no such thing as the subject that thinks or entertains ideas."
  - 5.632: "The subject does not belong to the world: rather, it is a limit of the world." 5.633 adds the eye that does not appear in its own field of vision.
  - 5.64: "solipsism, when its implications are followed out strictly, coincides with pure realism. The self of solipsism shrinks to a point without extension, and there remains the reality co-ordinated with it."
  - Preface: to draw a limit to thought, "we should have to find both sides of the limit thinkable".
- **[C]** The book excludes both things the author names:
  - The subject is placed at the limit (5.632) and shrinks to a point (5.64).
  - The book's own language goes too. In 6.54 its propositions are nonsense, a ladder to be thrown away, and logic shows itself but cannot be said. Here the language that talks about the world stands outside the world it talks about, and the book says so openly.
  - This makes the *Tractatus* a clear case of a habit the author has named before: philosophy assuming that the language it describes the world in stands outside the system it describes. The other cases are the metalanguage placed one level up (Tarski's object-language/metalanguage hierarchy, Carnap's external questions). Neurath, Quine and the later Wittgenstein work from inside.

## The second strand: private, shared, fed back

- **[A]** Another strand: "the language which only I understand" and the private language argument. The missing link is sharing and convergence. A language has to start in private (which only I understand), hook onto something public (shared), and feed in.
- **[A]** The generative side of reality (my world / the world) is implicitly present.
- **[C]** The two Wittgensteins each close one route:
  - 5.62 gives the language "which alone I understand". That is the personal world, with no connection to anyone else.
  - The *Investigations* private language argument gives the shared world, with the private side gone. [U] §243 defines the target; §258 is the diary sign "S"; §293 is the beetle in the box.
- **[C]** §243 defines a private language as one another person *cannot* understand, that is, in principle [U]. The author's starting point is private in fact: not yet shared, but shareable. On that reading the argument rules out the first and leaves the route open.
  - This is to check in the secondary literature. Some readings take §243 more broadly (Kripke's community view, for one).
- **[C]** The generative side: each "my world" feeds "the world". In the *Tractatus* the limits are fixed by logic. Here they move each time something private is fed into the shared language.
- **[C]** Links on the site:
  - The Epistemology close-affinity piece ("disclosed and private in its happening, stabilised by convergence in its holding").
  - Privacy as a core value.
  - This strand would add the language side of that loop.

## Stance

- **[A]** The author does not work in terms of opponents. This is "language brewing" in action: an exchange of ideas that results in evolving insight, or clearer expression of it. The opponent game is for people who need it as motivation to brew.

## Field material for the piece

**[C]** Candidates, all [U] until the research round. "Page" means a person page exists.

The subject in the world:
- **Schopenhauer** (page): "the world is my representation". He is Wittgenstein's source for the "my world" theme.
- **Heidegger** (page): being-in-the-world.
- **Merleau-Ponty** (page): the body-subject is "of the world".
- **von Uexküll** (page): the *Umwelt*, a personal world that is still part of nature.
- **von Foerster** and **Maturana** (pages): second-order cybernetics, the observer inside the observed system. Maturana: "everything said is said by an observer".
- **Rovelli** (page): the observer as one physical system among others. "We are undoubtably limited parts of nature, and we are so even as understanders of this same nature" (checked at source: the closing section of "Meaning = Information + Evolution", arXiv:1611.02420, where Rovelli raises a "small red flag" over how far the structure physics finds is put there by ourselves).
- **Neurath** (no page): the boat rebuilt at sea.
- **Quine** (page): no first philosophy.

The language in the world (private, shared, fed back):
- **Merleau-Ponty**: *parole parlante* / *parole parlée*. Speech that speaks, made new by one speaker, becomes the spoken speech the next speakers inherit. Closest to the author's route.
- **Saussure** (page): *parole* changing *langue*.
- **Humboldt** (no page): language as *energeia*.
- **Polanyi** (page): tacit knowing, held before it can be said.
- **Peirce** (page): truth as what the community of inquiry converges on.
- **Davidson** (no page): triangulation.
- **Vygotsky** (page): the reverse direction, the social taken in as inner speech. With him the loop is visible from both sides.
- **Later Wittgenstein** (page): language shared in practice.
