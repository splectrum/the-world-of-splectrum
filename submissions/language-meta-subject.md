## 1. Aim

To work out how a neutral meta subject (keyword doorway) for **language** should be shaped: the field's own uses of the word, gathered and organised so the diversity under it becomes visible, in the field's voice. Language is SPLectrum's central keyword; the doorway is where a reader who does not hold the position can still find the field.

It is also the home found for a line of work that came up in discussion — meaning arising from physical systems, from correlation through signal to language — which needed a place in the field's terms.

## 2. How the discussion ran

The discussion started from Carlo Rovelli's essay "Meaning = Information + Evolution" (arXiv:1611.02420), read beside Kolchinsky and Wolpert's semantic-information paper (2018), and moved in four steps.

### 2.1 From correlation to concept

- **[A]** Rovelli's essay is in a sense more interesting than Kolchinsky and Wolpert's construction.
- **[A]** A receptor on the outside of a bacterium's membrane that picks up "food smell" molecules is a language correlation — knowledge. If that correlation is coupled to a specific swimming state, we have a coupled action.
- **[A]** At least in principle, a mapping can be constructed from correlation to concept, and so to language.
- **[C, checked at source]** Rovelli takes the first step himself. Relative information (Shannon's mutual information) is ubiquitous — "The world teams with correlated quantities" — and so "not sufficient to account for meaning". Relevance enters through survival: "surviving mechanisms survive by using correlations." "Intentionality is built into the definition": the variable "'knows' something external". The bacterium's variable takes values L and R, and "R means 'food is on the right' and L means 'food is on the left'"; if x is on L while the food is on the right, the information "is 'not true'". Meaningful information grows recursively — "This opens the door to recursive growth of meaningful information and arbitrary increase of semantic complexity" — up "to contexts where there is a brain, language, society, norms".
- **[A]** The non-uniqueness of the mapping is the fuzziness of concepts.
- **[A]** Choosing the variables in which the mapping is stated is system description — a normal task for physical systems.
- **[A]** The mechanistic detail does not matter; what it expresses matters.
- **[corrected]** Claude framed the non-uniqueness as the content-indeterminacy problem (Dretske; Millikan; the frog's "fly" or "small dark moving thing"); the author: it is the fuzziness of concepts, normal rather than a defect. Claude framed rendering the bacterium's concept in human words as translation; the author: system description. Claude offered the chemotaxis mechanism (*E. coli* compares now with a few seconds ago, via receptor methylation) as a refinement; the author: what it expresses matters, not the detail.
- **[C, checked at source]** Further from Rovelli: the definition "does not directly refer to anything mental"; it gives the physical base of the information brains work with. His truth notion is "based on the effectiveness of the consequence of holding something for true", which he equates with the natural sciences' sense of a theory's truth as the success of its predictions. He picks macroscopic variables xn for the organism and yn for the environment, with an entropy over the microstates — system description in the author's sense.
- **[C, unchecked]** Supporting material for the three redirections. Fuzziness as the normal case for concepts: Wittgenstein's family resemblance, Rosch's prototype theory, Zadeh's fuzzy sets; the demand for one determinate content mirrors the demand for a frame-free, intrinsic standard in Kolchinsky and Wolpert — a constraint asking for the wrong thing. System description: saying "R means food on the right" is the same task as choosing temperature and pressure for a gas. What it expresses: the physics sense of emergence, where the details wash out and what is expressed carries over.
- **[C]** A working formulation offered (not confirmed): a concept as a fuzzy distinction coupled to action, picked out by system description, independent of the mechanism that carries it.
- **Open — basal cognition. [C, unchecked]** Asked whether basal cognition uses any of this. It shares the ground — bacterial chemotaxis as its floor; Lyon's biogenic definition, organisms becoming familiar with, valuing and exploiting their environment — but, as far as recalled, does not build on the information-theoretic route from correlation to meaning. The field's standing objection (Adams and Aizawa's demand for intrinsic, non-derived content) is where Rovelli's "intentionality is built into the definition" would bear. The citation record (§2.2) shows the essay reaching cognition and life–mind continuity work at the edges; whether Lyon or Levin take it up is to check in research.

### 2.2 Purpose as a consequence of selection

- **[A]** Rovelli makes the simple observation from Darwin's theory that the causal relation can be reversed: a function that works persists, and so does its effect. Purpose is a consequence of evolution, naturally.
- **[C, checked at source]** Rovelli: "The key of Darwin's discovery is that we can legitimately reverse the causal relation between the existence of the mechanism and its function." He traces the idea to Empedocles, quoting Aristotle, *Physics* II.8, 198b29.
- **[C, checked via OpenAlex citation records, 2026-10-07]** Uptake of the essay (14 citations of the published version, "Meaning and Intentionality = Information + Evolution", in *Wandering Towards a Goal*, 2018): Kolchinsky and Wolpert (2018), the main route by which it travelled; Jeffery, Pollack and Rovelli, "On the Statistical Mechanics of Life: Schrödinger Revisited" (2019); synthetic cells extracting semantic information (Ruzzante, Del Moro and Magarini, 2023; Del Moro, Magarini and Stano, 2024); cognition and life–mind continuity (Dodig-Crnković, 2025; Kriete, 2025; Friedrich and Fischer, 2025, 2026); a critique, "Does purely physical information have meaning? A comment on Carlo Rovelli's paper" (2020).
- **[C, unchecked]** The reversal's deeper lineage: the selected-effects theory of function (Larry Wright, "Functions", 1973; Millikan's proper functions, 1984; Neander, 1991); teleosemantics building meaning on it (Millikan, Papineau, Neander; Shea, *Representation in Cognitive Science*, 2018); Dennett's "free-floating rationales"; Deacon, *Incomplete Nature* (2011).

### 2.3 Others working on the link from physical systems to signal and language

- **[A]** With Shannon we are getting close to signal — information stream — language.
- **[C, unchecked]** The work found along that chain:
  - *Signals from correlation and payoff.* Brian Skyrms, *Signals: Evolution, Learning, and Information* (2010), building on David Lewis's signalling games (*Convention*, 1969): meaning emerging without prior semantics, from bacteria and quorum sensing to human language, with Shannon-style information content. Luc Steels' language games with robots (the "Talking Heads" experiments, late 1990s): a shared vocabulary from repeated interaction between embodied agents.
  - *Shannon's own side.* Warren Weaver's three levels (1949) — technical, semantic, effectiveness; Bar-Hillel and Carnap's semantic information (1952).
  - *Biology.* Howard Pattee's symbol–matter problem, semantic closure and the "epistemic cut"; Bernd-Olaf Küppers, *The Language of Living Matter*; Marcello Barbieri's code biology; biosemiotics (Jesper Hoffmeyer, *Signs of Meaning in the Universe*, 1996; Kalevi Kull); John Maynard Smith, "The concept of information in biology" (2000), and the debate it started (Godfrey-Smith, Sterelny, Jablonka).
  - *Physics and origin of life.* Sara Walker and Paul Davies (information as causal in the origin of life); Wheeler's "it from bit"; Ernst and Christine von Weizsäcker's "pragmatic information" (1972), measured by effect on the receiver; Deacon's three levels (Shannon → Boltzmann → Darwin); Friston's free-energy principle.
  - *Philosophy.* Stevan Harnad, "The Symbol Grounding Problem" (1990); Dretske and Millikan's teleosemantics.
- **[C]** Mapped onto the chain: correlation → meaningful (Rovelli, Kolchinsky–Wolpert, Pattee); meaningful → signal (Skyrms, Lewis, the Weizsäckers); signal → language (Steels, Skyrms, biosemiotics). Weaver's three levels name the whole span as a programme; Deacon attempts it in one account. Skyrms and Steels looked the most promising: Skyrms runs the chain with no semantics assumed, Steels runs it in physical agents as language games.

### 2.4 How to categorise it — and the move to language

- **[A]** Asked whether the work is "meaningful information" or just "meaning".
- **[C]** "Meaning" alone is too wide (philosophy of language, hermeneutics, the meaning of life). "Meaningful information" is Rovelli's phrase; the field's established word is *semantic information* (Bar-Hillel and Carnap through Floridi to Kolchinsky and Wolpert). But semantic information does not cover the span: signalling, biosemiotics and code biology, teleosemantics and symbol grounding each work under their own names.
- **[A]** What if we create a language meta subject which would, amongst others, encompass this?
- **[C]** Agreed as the better home. The reasoning is in §3.

## 3. Why language, and what needs care

### 3.1 Why it fits

- **The central keyword.** "Being implies language" is P0; meaning lives in language on the position, so meaning can be served inside a language doorway rather than needing one of its own.
- **Three surfaces, two already standing.** The site's top-level Language section is the position's own voice ("If being implies language, language is wherever there is interaction…"), with natural, formal, software and DSL types. A language meta subject in Positioning would be the field's side — how the fields themselves use the word. The Language section links down into the doorway; the doorway does not link up.
- **The physical-route work gets a home the field itself backs.** Skyrms traces "from signals to language"; Steels runs language games in robots; biosemiotics speaks of signs and codes. A branch on signals and codes can sit in the doorway on the field's own words, with semantic information as one of its leaves.
- **A ready-made criterion page.** Hockett's design features of language (1960) and the recursion debate (Hauser, Chomsky and Fitch, 2002) are the dominant measure of what counts as language — presentable at full strength, then shown for what they do not measure, as the evolution doorway's criterion page does.

### 3.2 What needs care

- **Structure shows the separations.** The reader arrives assuming unity — language as human speech. Where the binding sits in the foreground, the structure has to show the diversity: grouped, visible branches, and a short redirect for readers who arrive expecting linguistics.
- **The word check.** The field uses "language" itself for linguistics, formal and programming languages, animal "language" research, "the language of the genes", and language games — and not for signalling, semantic information or semiotics, which need the treatment of territory carried under other names.
- **The live dispute is whether the extensions are literal.** Is the genetic code a language or a metaphor? Do animals have language or only communication? The field is divided, and the position's broad sense takes a side. The doorway reports the dispute without settling it; the position already speaks in the Language section.
- **Backing before the doorway.** The territory is large. The backing check below is the start.
