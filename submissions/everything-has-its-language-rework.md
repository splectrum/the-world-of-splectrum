## The sequence it completes

- **[A]** The End of Philosophy, Really? → the intent of the natural philosophy project survives, not the name → it can be named again: SPLectrum's way of looking at it.
- **[C]** What each step hands on. The first asks what actually ends. The second separates name, project and meeting place, and closes on: "Does anything still hold them together?" This post is the answer to that question.

## What the post has to carry

- **[A]** Philosophy still lacks the unifying centre, and feels lost not being able to be absolutist.
- **[A]** The scientific method (a language family) has to be pegged down to the level of the other languages.
- **[A]** There is a need for a centre that brings all languages together with equal standing.
- **[A]** The move to facets and the many languages that came with it led to the death of a philosophy that is judge and jury over every other kind of knowledge. The scientific method that was supposed to unify all catalysed the inability to do so.
- **[A]** That may have ended the named field of natural philosophy, at the top of a hierarchy. It has not removed the project of natural philosophy.

## No absolute rock

- **[A]** Together with the proliferation of specialisms, the pursuit of the scientific method itself led to the insight that there is no absolute rock to stand on (Russell, Wittgenstein, and others).
- **[A]** They had already arrived at language, but had not relinquished the hierarchy: the judgement seat as the rock.
- **[A]** What unifies is the experience of reality, which gives rise to the diversification of language as knowledge grows.
- **[A]** The centre can't be a specific language, because languages evolve with growing knowledge.
- **[A]** Absolutism is the pursuit of "time standing still", the end of process.
- **[C]** This reads back onto the first post: Hegel's arrival, Marx's realisation, Heidegger's completion are all ends of process. Those announcements of the end may be absolutism's own wish, stated as a date.
- **[A]** The end of alive, with life remaining frozen.
- **[C]** A possible figure: a language that stops changing is what we call a dead language.
- **[C]** So two routes end the seat: specialisms from outside, the search for foundations from within. The second is new to the sequence.
- **[C]** Sources still to check, from memory: Popper on science resting on piles and not on "solid bedrock" (*Logic of Scientific Discovery*); Neurath's ship rebuilt at sea; Gödel for the formal side. Carnap's "only a method" and Wittgenstein's agreement in judgement are already on the site.
- **[C]** The later Wittgenstein may not fit "not relinquished": language-games give up the one ideal language. The early *Tractatus* and Carnap's logic of science fit better.

## What the second post now sets up

- **[C]** The name stayed with physics; the intent went to the sciences: astronomy, cosmology, biology, psychology.
- **[C]** The figure of the room: "the place where they met", "without the room they used to share". Whewell, 1834: "physical science itself is endlessly subdivided, and the subdivisions insulated". The British Association members had "no general term", hence "scientist".
- **[C]** Hawking and Mlodinow: "Traditionally these are questions for philosophy, but philosophy is dead", asked from the science that kept the name.
- **[C]** Rorty's capital P: philosophy as judge and jury. So the new centre must not read as that seat returning. A room, not a bench.
