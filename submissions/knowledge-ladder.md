# The knowledge ladder

*Captured 2026-11-23, from the site review's discussion of what knowledge is. Outcome open: a post, a subject page, or both; judge where it takes us.*

## Why it came up

Jules's take: knowledge is information turned into concepts; understanding is the insight gained from it. The site defines cognition as "the process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience". The take lands close to a known ladder.

## The ladder (from memory, unchecked)

- **[C, unchecked]** Russell L. Ackoff, "From Data to Wisdom" (1989): data → information → knowledge → understanding → wisdom. His version keeps understanding as a rung of its own; most later versions drop it.
- **[C, unchecked]** Usually traced back to T. S. Eliot, *The Rock* (1934): "Where is the wisdom we have lost in knowledge? / Where is the knowledge we have lost in information?" Earlier or parallel versions: Harlan Cleveland (1982), Milan Zeleny (1987).
- **[C, unchecked]** In information science it became the four-step "knowledge pyramid" (DIKW).
- **[C, unchecked]** Critique: Martin Frické, "The knowledge pyramid: a critique of the DIKW hierarchy" (2009): knowledge is not refined information; the hierarchy rests on an outdated picture of inference. David Weinberger and others on the pyramid as a management construct.
