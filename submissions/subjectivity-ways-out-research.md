# Subjectivity and its ways out: research round (2026-11-20)

*Working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.*

**Context, in place.** A close-affinity page on subjectivity is planned. SPLectrum's position, for orientation only: experience is had by a subject before it is anything else, and no description from outside stands in for it; the way out of the subject is by sharing, in ever widening circles of subjective convergence. The page would read the field with one pattern: every strong defence of the subject needs a way out of it, and each thinker builds a different one. Read, not graded (resonance only; the field is the grammatical subject; no scoring of thinkers). This round checks, per thinker: (a) how the subject is defended; (b) the way out to others and a shared world, or why none is needed; (c) whether the irreducibility is individual (one person against another) or of a kind (Nagel: "Rather it is a type"). Nagel was checked in an earlier round; his results are restated here so the note stands alone.

**Marks.** [C, S#] = checked against source S#, opened, quoted as found (OCR slips silently corrected, nothing else). [C, as quoted in S#] = verbatim, but second-hand: the primary was not opened. [U] = unverified. "Reading [C]" = Claude's interpretation. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / UNVERIFIED. Translations marked "my rendering" are Claude's, from an original opened at source.

**Site check.** Person pages exist for Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Levinas, Kierkegaard, Sartre, Nagel, Schopenhauer, Heidegger, Habermas, Wittgenstein, Buber. No page for Michel Henry, Dan Zahavi, Edith Stein or Max Scheler (Scheler is named on the social-construction subject and the Berger page). No same-surname collision found for these names. ## The headline first: the field names the pattern, and also disputes it

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with a sharpening that matters for the page's shape.** The "way out of the subject" is a named classic problem, and several of the cast state it about their own work. But a large part of the field holds that the problem is badly posed: there was never a closed inside to get out of. The pattern the page plans to read is therefore itself one side of a live dispute.

- Husserl opens the Fifth Meditation with the objection against himself: "When I, the meditating I, reduce myself by phenomenological epoché to my absolute transcendental ego, do I not become solus ipse; and do I not remain that, as long as I carry on a consistent self-explication under the name phenomenology?" (*Cartesian Meditations*, §42, Cairns trans. p. 89; Hua I 121) [C, S1; OCR of this page is garbled, word order confirmed against S2's quotation].
- Merleau-Ponty states the pattern almost in so many words: "There is here a solipsism rooted in living experience and quite insurmountable" (*Phenomenology of Perception*, Smith trans. p. 358), and then: "Solitude and communication cannot be the two horns of a dilemma, but two 'moments' of one phenomenon, since in fact other people do exist for me" (p. 359) [C, S5].
- Michel Henry names it as the standing objection to his own philosophy: "La possibilité même de construire une philosophie de l'affectivité pure d'un côté, celle pour une ipséité pathétique et acosmique d'entrer jamais en relation avec une autre du même type, de s'inscrire dans une intersubjectivité effective et concrète de l'autre, c'étaient là les deux objections soulevées par les thèses de *L'essence de la manifestation* dès sa parution" (*Phénoménologie matérielle*, 1990) [C, as quoted in S16; page U]. My rendering: the possibility for a pathetic, acosmic ipseity ever to enter into relation with another of the same type was one of the two objections raised against his first book from the start.
- Zahavi, survey statement: "On some readings, the problem of intersubjectivity is really another name for the problem of other minds. Why should there be a problem? Because the only mind I allegedly have direct access to is my own." And the charge against the tradition: "due to its preoccupation with subjectivity, phenomenology has not only failed to realize the true significance of intersubjectivity, but has also been fundamentally incapable of addressing the issue in a satisfactory manner (cf. Habermas 1994)", which he calls "astonishingly misguided" (Zahavi, "Intersubjectivity", Routledge Companion to Phenomenology, 2011, preprint) [C, S20].
- The dispute, from the analytic side: the SEP entry "Other Minds" (Avramides, rev. 2023) says the phenomenological approach "rejects the isolationism of the self that characterizes Cartesian philosophy", and that these philosophers "refuse to allow themselves to be pre-occupied by the epistemological issues that they see as arising from a mistaken starting point" [C, S23]. It also notes "there is little agreement either about the problem or the solution to it. Indeed, there is little agreement about whether there is a problem here at all" [C, S23].
- Heidegger is the plainest refusal: "a bare subject without a world never 'is' proximally, nor is it ever given. And so in the end an isolated 'I' without Others is just as far from being proximally given" (*Being and Time*, Macquarrie & Robinson, p. 152; H. 116); "the world is always the one that I share with Others. The world of Dasein is a with-world [*Mitwelt*]" (p. 155; H. 118) [C, S12].
- Reception headline on the classic attempt: "Interpreters generally agree that the *Fifth Cartesian Meditation* fails to achieve its task, but they do not agree on what that task is" (Staehler 2008, abstract) [C, S4].

Reading [C]: three postures, not one. (1) A defended inside with a built way out (Husserl, Stein, Kierkegaard, Nagel; Levinas with the way coming from the other side). (2) No closed inside to begin with: the subject is with others from the start (Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty in his answer, Scheler, Zahavi's direct-perception view). (3) No way out needed, because what is shared lies beneath the subject, not between subjects (Henry). The planned pattern reads (1) cleanly; (2) and (3) can be read as further doors only if the page says "a way out, or a reason none is needed", which the brief already allows ("or why they need none").

Zahavi also notes one feature common to the phenomenologists that bears on a sharing-through-language reading: "Without ever denying the eminently intersubjective character of language the phenomenologists have often endeavoured to unearth pre- or extra-linguistic forms of intersubjectivity", which he names as "one of the decisive differences" from Habermas, "who famously argues that language is the foundation of intersubjectivity (Habermas, 1973, p. 198)" ("Beyond Empathy", 2001) [C, S19; the Habermas claim itself U].

## 1. Husserl

**Verdict: CONFIRMED in substance; the "accessible in its inaccessibility" formula CORRECTED (it is a later gloss; Husserl's own wording differs).**

**(a) Defence of the subject.** The transcendental ego as the field in which everything has its sense. Even at the end of the Fifth Meditation he keeps it: "the proposition that everything existing for me must derive its existential sense exclusively from me myself, from my sphere of consciousness retains its validity and fundamental importance" (§62, Cairns p. 150; Hua I 176) [C, S1]. The other's own life is never given originally: "Neither the other Ego himself, nor his subjective processes or his appearances themselves, nor anything else belonging to his own essence, becomes given in our experience originally. If it were, if what belongs to the other's own essence were directly accessible, it would be merely a moment of my own essence, and ultimately he himself and I myself would be the same" (§50, Cairns p. 109; Hua I 139) [C, S1, S2].

**(b) Way out.** Experience of the other (*Fremderfahrung*), which Husserl first introduces as "so-called 'empathy'" (Cairns p. 92) [C, S1]: pairing of my body with a body over there, and appresentation of what cannot be presented. The formula the brief asks about, in Husserl's own words: "The character of the existent 'other' has its basis in this kind of verifiable accessibility of what is not originally accessible" (§52, Cairns p. 114; Hua I 144) [C, S1]. German: "In dieser Art bewährbarer Zugänglichkeit des original Unzugänglichen gründet der Charakter des seienden Fremden" [C, as quoted in S3, which cites the Ströker edition]. "Accessible in the mode of inaccessibility" is Staehler's phrasing (S4, abstract) [C]; Zahavi glosses it as "it is exactly this inaccessibility, this limit, which I can experience (cf. Husserl, 1973a, p. 144)" [C, S19]. So: the idea is Husserl's; the paradox-formula is the commentators'.
The way out ends in a community, not a pair: "my ego… can be a world-experiencing ego only by being in communion with others like himself: a member of a community of monads" (§60, Cairns p. 139); "there can exist only a single community of monads… Hence there can exist only one Objective world" (p. 140) [C, S1]. And: "The illusion of a solipsism is dissolved" (§62, p. 150) [C, S1]. "The intrinsically first being, the being that precedes and bears every worldly objectivity, is transcendental intersubjectivity" (Hua I 182) [C, as quoted in S2]. He also extends the same structure to cultures: "To me and to those who share in my culture, an alien culture is accessible only by a kind of 'experience of someone else', a kind of 'empathy'" (§58, Cairns pp. 134–135) [C, S1].

**(c) Individual or kind.** Individual: the line runs between my ownness and any other ego ("he himself and I myself would be the same"). Husserl gives culture a parallel level (my culture as "primordial, over against every alien culture", p. 134–135) [C, S1]. Reading [C]: the irreducibility is individual; the "we" of culture is a second, derived ownness.

**Word test.** *Einfühlung* is Husserl's word but introduced as "so-called"; *Fremderfahrung* (experience of the alien/other), *Appräsentation*, *Paarung* and *Intersubjektivität* are his. Zahavi: Husserl was "the first philosopher ever to engage in a systematic and extensive use of the very term intersubjectivity" [C, S20].

## 2. Merleau-Ponty

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with one correction to the site's Merleau-Ponty page (see placement).**

**(a) Defence of the subject.** He grants the inside its full weight: "the generality of the body will never make it clear how the indeclinable I can estrange itself in favour of another, since this generality is exactly compensated by the other generality of my inalienable subjectivity" (*PhP*, Smith p. 358); "The truth of solipsism is there" (p. 360) [C, S5].

**(b) Way out.** Three steps, all his. Bodily: "it is precisely my body which perceives the body of another person, and discovers in that other body a miraculous prolongation of my own intentions… my body and the other person's are one whole, two sides of one and the same phenomenon" (p. 354) [C, S5]. Through language: "There is one particular cultural object which is destined to play a crucial rôle in the perception of other people: language. In the experience of dialogue, there is constituted between the other person and myself a common ground; my thought and his are inter-woven into a single fabric… We have here a dual being… we are collaborators for each other in consummate reciprocity. Our perspectives merge into each other, and we co-exist through a common world" (p. 354) [C, S5]. And against the solipsist: "I can evolve a solipsist philosophy but, in doing so, I assume the existence of a community of men endowed with speech, and I address myself to it" (p. 360); "The refusal to communicate, however, is still a form of communication" (p. 361) [C, S5]. Later, the term itself: "he and I are like organs of one single intercorporeality" ("The Philosopher and His Shadow", *Signs*, McCleary trans. 1964, p. 168) [C, S6]. The flesh: "has no name in any philosophy" (*The Visible and the Invisible*, p. 147); "thickness of flesh between the seer and the thing" (p. 135) [C, as quoted in S7].
Against Sartre's look, explicitly: "the other's gaze transforms me into an object, and mine him, only if both of us withdraw into the core of our thinking nature… the objectification of each by the other's gaze is felt as unbearable only because it takes the place of possible communication" (pp. 360–361) [C, S5].

**(c) Individual or kind.** Both, held together: the "indeclinable I" and "inalienable subjectivity" are individual; the "anonymous existence of which my body is the ever-renewed trace henceforth inhabits both bodies simultaneously" (p. 354) is a shared generality [C, S5]. Reading [C]: the closest of the cast to a both-and.

**Word test.** "Intercorporeality" (*intercorporéité*) is not found in the *PhP* text (Smith 1962, full-text search); it appears in *Signs* (1960) and the late work [C, S5, S6]. For *PhP* the page should use his own phrases there ("one whole", "common ground", "dual being").

## 3. Levinas

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** One finding: in *Totality and Infinity* the way out is language, explicitly.

**(a) Defence of the subject.** Separation and interiority: "One can call atheism this separation so complete that the separated being maintains itself in existence all by itself… One lives outside of God, at home with oneself; one is an I, an egoism" (*TI*, Lingis trans. 1969, p. 58); "The inner life is the unique way for the real to exist as a plurality" (p. 58); "The plurality required for conversation results from the interiority with which each term is 'endowed'" (p. 59) [C, S8].

**(b) Way out, from the other side.** "The way in which the other presents himself, exceeding the idea of the other in me, we here name face" (p. 50) [C, S8]. The relation is language: "We shall try to show that the relation between the same and the other… is language… The relation between the same and the other, metaphysics, is primordially enacted as conversation, where the same, gathered up in its ipseity as an 'I'… leaves itself" (p. 39) [C, S8]. "Language, far from presupposing universality and generality, first makes them possible. Language presupposes interlocutors, a plurality" (p. 73); "language is spoken where community between the terms of the relationship is wanting, where the common plane is wanting or is yet to be constituted" (p. 73) [C, S8]. And the shared world: "language accomplishes the primordial putting in common… The generality of the word institutes a common world. The ethical event at the basis of generalization is the underlying intention of language" (pp. 173–174) [C, S8]. Reading [C]: Levinas's way out is close to "sharing institutes the common", with the reversal that the move is called from the other's side and stays asymmetrical.

**(c) Individual or kind.** Individual, and more: no common kind at all. "He and I do not form a number… I, you—these are not individuals of a common concept" (p. 39) [C, S8].

**Word test.** *Visage* (face), *Autrui* (the personal Other, capitalised by Lingis), *séparation*, *intériorité*, *discours*/*conversation*.

## 4. Michel Henry

**Verdict: CONFIRMED in outline from secondary sources; primary wording mostly as quoted. "Co-pathos" UNVERIFIED as Henry's term.**

**(a) Defence of the subject.** The most radical in the cast: life as auto-affection, the self-revelation that grounds all appearing. SEP (Seyler, rev. 2024): the origin of manifestation is "a self-revelation or… an auto-affection that refers to nothing but itself"; life "is not the opening to an other, but enclosed in itself, undergoing itself in self-experience", which is "the condition of possibility of any experience of an other"; joy and suffering express "that life cannot escape from itself, that it is irremediably tied to itself" [C, S9]. Henry: "It is not outside of itself but in itself, not transcendence but radical immanence" (*Material Phenomenology*, p. 81) [C, as quoted in S9].

**(b) Way out, or why none.** He refuses the outward route (Husserl's perception of the other's body, Heidegger's world): community is not reached by going out but lies in the life each living shares. SEP: "absolute life is shared by all living and provides the ground for an invisible and immanent community" (summarising Part III of *Material Phenomenology*) [C, S9]. Henry: "Devons-nous dire alors que, en dépit de l'apparence, toute communauté est invisible. Prenons ce risque" (*Phénoménologie matérielle*, "Pathos-avec", p. 166) [C, as quoted in S17]; "La communauté est un a priori" (pp. 175–176) [C, as quoted in S16]. In English: "Let us place ourselves outside this presupposition and ask ourselves what experience of the other is such as it is really experienced by each one of us within ourselves" (*Material Phenomenology*, Davidson trans. 2008, p. 102); community is "nothing but a group of living individuals"; "the attempt to oppose the community and the individual… is pure nonsense" (p. 121) [C, as quoted in S18]. The book section is titled "Pathos-avec" (pathos-with), pp. 136–179 of the French edition [C, as listed in S16].

**(c) Individual or kind.** Individual ipseity, generated by one life common to all: "Life as capable of engendering the form of ipseity is, for Henry, absolute life, which transcends each individual living being"; "absolute life gives itself to itself through the individuality of the living" [C, S9]. His own statement of the objection speaks of relation with "une autre du même type" [C, as quoted in S16].

**Word test.** Henry's: *auto-affection*, *pathos*, *pathos-avec*, *Vie absolue*, *ipséité*, *communauté invisible*. "Co-pathos" was not found in any source opened [U]; do not use it as his term until seen in the text.

## 5. Sartre

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** A register question for the page (see placement).

**(a) Defence of the subject.** The pre-reflective cogito: "every positional consciousness of an object is at the same time a non-positional consciousness of itself" (*Being and Nothingness*, Barnes trans., Introduction §III) [C, S10; page U].

**(b) Way out.** The other is met, not constructed: "We encounter the Other; we do not constitute him"; his existence is a "contingent necessity" (p. 337) [C, S10]. The look gives me an outside I cannot give myself: "the Other is the indispensable mediator between myself and me" (p. 302); "If there is an Other… then I have an outside, I have a nature. My original fall is the existence of the Other" (p. 352) [C, S10]. But the relation does not converge: "Conflict is the original meaning of being-for-others" (p. 475); the We-subject "is a psychological experience", "a purely subjective impression which engages only me" (p. 551); "the subjectivities remain out of reach and radically separated" (p. 550); "The essence of the relations between consciousnesses is not the *Mitsein*; it is conflict" (p. 555) [C, S10; Washington Square Press pagination].

**(c) Individual or kind.** Individual.

**Word test.** *Le regard* (the look), *être-pour-autrui*, *nous-sujet*/*nous-objet* are his. "Hell is other people" is a line spoken by a character (Garcin) in the play *Huis clos* (1944), not a thesis of *Being and Nothingness* [U for the speaker attribution; the play is not opened here].

## 6. Kierkegaard (Johannes Climacus)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED in the Danish critical text; one attribution CORRECTED: the thesis is the pseudonym's.**

**(a) Defence of the subject.** *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (1846) is signed by Johannes Climacus, Kierkegaard as editor. The chapter title: "Den subjektive Sandhed, Inderligheden; Sandheden er Subjektiviteten" (Part Two, Section Two, ch. 2); Climacus calls it "min Thesis, at Subjektiviteten er Sandheden"; elsewhere "Subjektiviteten er Sandheden; Subjektiviteten er Virkeligheden" [C, S11]. The definition: "den objektive Uvished, fastholdt i den meest lidenskabelige Inderligheds Tilegnelse, er Sandheden, den høieste Sandhed der er for en Existerende" [C, S11]. Hong's English, "An objective uncertainty, held fast through appropriation with the most passionate inwardness, is the truth, the highest truth there is for an existing person" (CUP 1:203) [U, from memory]. The thesis is scoped: Evans, quoting CUP 1:199, shows it "far from denying the objectivity of propositional truth, includes an affirmation of such truth", and applies to "the truth that is 'essential' to human existence" [C, S13]. The same book also says "Subjektiviteten er Usandheden" as the starting point of the religious [C, S11].

**(b) Way out.** Indirect communication. The subjective thinker thinks the universal, "men som existerende i denne Tænkning, som erhvervende denne i sin Inderlighed bliver han mere og mere subjektiv isoleret" (my rendering: but existing in this thinking, acquiring it in his inwardness, he becomes more and more subjectively isolated) [C, S11]. Hence double reflection in the form of communication: "saaledes beroer Meddelelsens Hemmelighed netop i at frigjøre den Anden" (my rendering: so the secret of communication rests precisely in setting the other free) [C, S11]; Hong renders it around CUP 1:74 [U]. A sharp line on agreement: when one person states something and the other repeats it word for word, "saa antages de at være enige og at have forstaaet hinanden… Han aner derfor ikke, at den Art af Enighed kan være den høieste Misforstaaelse" (my rendering: they are assumed to agree and to have understood each other… he has no idea that this kind of agreement can be the highest misunderstanding) [C, S11]. Objective thinking, by contrast, communicates directly and promises "at alle Mennesker engang ville antage denne Sandhed", leaning on "Stemmefleerhedens Sikkerhed" (the security of the majority) [C, S11]; Climacus adds that where objective thinking is in its right, as outside the religious, "er dens ligefremme Meddelelse ogsaa i sin Orden" [C, S11].

**(c) Individual or kind.** Individual: the existing single individual (*den Enkelte*), "subjectively isolated".

**Word test.** "Subjectivity is truth" and "truth is subjectivity" are both Climacus's wording; *indirekte Meddelelse* and *Dobbelt-Reflexion* are his. A Danish search summary suggested the slogan is "not a direct quote"; that is wrong for the wording, which is in SKS 7 verbatim (S11). Whether the pseudonym's thesis is Kierkegaard's own is a standing scholarly question; for the page it is enough to attribute to Climacus.

## 7. Dan Zahavi

**Verdict: CONFIRMED from his own open papers; *Self and Other* (2014) itself not opened.**

**(a) Defence of the subject.** The experiential (minimal) self as for-me-ness: experiences "are all characterized by what might be called a dimension of for-me-ness or mineness"; it "doesn't refer to a specific experiential content… it refers to the first-personal presence of experience. It refers to the fact that the experiences I am living through are given differently (but not necessarily better) to me than to anybody else"; denying it "would be tantamount to a denial of the first-person perspective" ("The Experiential Self: Objections and Clarifications", in *Self, No Self?*, OUP 2011, proof pp. 58–59) [C, S21].

**(b) Way out.** Empathy as direct, perceptual experience of the other, keeping the asymmetry: "the fact that my experiential access to the minds of others differs from my experiential access to my own mind is not an imperfection or shortcoming. On the contrary, it is a difference that is constitutional. It is precisely because of this difference… that we can claim that the minds we experience are other minds" ("Empathy and direct social perception", 2011 preprint) [C, S22]. "The otherness of the other is exactly manifest in his elusiveness and inaccessibility. To demand more… would lead to an abolition of the difference between self and other" ("Beyond Empathy", 2001) [C, S19]. Reviews of *Self and Other* describe empathy there as "a distinct other-directed form of intentionality" [U, search summary only]. The later step to a "we" (Part III of *Self and Other*) is not checked [U].

**(c) Individual or kind.** Both, explicitly. The objection that first-personal givenness is "too formal to act as individuating principle" because "all experiences, not only mine but everybody's, are characterized by first-personal givenness" is answered with two clones: from a third-person view nothing distinguishes them, but "only my experiences are given in a first-personal mode of presentation to me, whereas the qualitatively identical experiences of my clone are not given first-personally to me at all" [C, S21]. Reading [C]: a structure every subject has (a type) that individuates from inside. This sits directly between Nagel's "type" and the individual line of Husserl and Levinas.

**Word test.** "For-me-ness", "mineness", "experiential self", "minimal self" are Zahavi's (mineness after Husserl's *Meinheit*, Hua XV 351, per S21).

## 8. Nagel (restated from the earlier round)

**Verdict: CONFIRMED (earlier round, primary sources opened there).**

- **(a)** The subjective character of experience is irreducible, and objectivity leads away from it: "any shift to greater objectivity—that is, less attachment to a specific viewpoint—does not take us nearer to the real nature of the phenomenon: it takes us farther away from it" ("What Is It Like to Be a Bat?", *Philosophical Review* 83, 1974, p. 445) [C].
- **(b)** Detachment, in degrees, toward a centreless view, by an "objective self" each person contains ("The Objective Self", 1983, pp. 226–228) [C]. Convergence follows, it does not ground: "Because a centerless view of the world is one on which different persons can converge, there is a close connection between objectivity and intersubjectivity" (1983, p. 228) [C]; consensus "arises from the convergence among different individuals, all reasoning to get at the truth" (*The Last Word*, 1997, p. 31) [C].
- **(c)** Kind, explicitly: "I am not adverting here to the alleged privacy of experience to its possessor. The point of view in question is not one accessible only to a single individual. Rather it is a type" (1974, p. 441) [C].

## Optional candidates

**Edith Stein: CONFIRMED; fits well.** (a) Empathy is "a kind of act of perceiving [*eine Art erfahrender Akte*] sui generis", "the experience of foreign consciousness in general" (*On the Problem of Empathy*, W. Stein trans., ICS 1989, p. 11); "Empathy in our strictly defined sense as the experience of foreign consciousness can only be the non-primordial experience which announces a primordial one" (p. 14) [C, S14]; German: "nur das nicht-originäre Erlebnis, das ein originäres bekundet" (1917 dissertation) [C, S15]. (b) A "we" that keeps the I: "Have thus the barriers separating one 'I' from another broken down here? Has the 'I' been freed from its monadic character? Not entirely… from the 'I' and 'you' arises the 'we' as a subject of a higher level" (p. 17); "we empathically enrich our feeling so that 'we' now feel a different joy from 'I,' 'you,' and 'he' in isolation. But 'I,' 'you,' and 'he' are retained in 'we'" (p. 18) [C, S14]. And the world: "Were I imprisoned within the boundaries of my individuality, I could not go beyond 'the world as it appears to me'… Thus empathy as the basis of intersubjective experience becomes the condition of possible knowledge of the existing outer world" (pp. 63–64) [C, S14]. (c) Individual ("monadic character… not entirely" freed). Reading [C]: Stein's enrichment-by-sharing, with the I retained, is the closest single passage in the field to widening circles of sharing.

**Schopenhauer: CONFIRMED; fits as a type-level case.** (a) "'THE world is my idea:'—this is a truth which holds good for everything that lives and knows" (*WWR* I, §1, Haldane & Kemp, 6th ed. 1907, p. 3) [C, S24]. (b) Theoretical egoism "can never be demonstrably refuted", but "as a serious conviction… it could only be found in a madhouse"; it is "a small frontier fortress. The fortress cannot indeed be taken, but the garrison can never sally forth from it, and therefore we pass it by" (§19, pp. 135–136) [C, S24]; the way past is analogy from the double knowledge of one's own body to the will in all things, and in ethics compassion ("Tat twam asi… 'this living thing art thou'", §44, p. 284) [C, S24]. (c) Kind: the representation-thesis is stated for "everything that lives and knows" [C, S24]. The site's Schopenhauer page already carries representation and compassion.

**Heidegger: CONFIRMED; fits only as the "no inside" posture** (quotes above, S12). The site's Heidegger page does not mention *Mitsein*, although the epistemology and core-values close-affinity pages already credit him with it (backing gap, pre-existing).

**Habermas: UNVERIFIED here.** Language as "the foundation of intersubjectivity" is Zahavi's report (S19), Habermas 1973 not opened. If the page wants a way out through language as such, Habermas is the field's name for it; he has a person page.

**Wittgenstein, Buber, Scheler:** not researched. The private language argument (Wittgenstein's page already carries it) and Buber's relation-first I–Thou both belong to the "no closed inside" posture; Scheler (*The Nature of Sympathy*) is the first phenomenological critic of the argument from analogy (S19, S20, S23) and has no person page.

## Placement proposal (options, not decisions)

**Shape.** Option A: one page, the pattern stated in the opening, three postures as sections (a built way out; no closed inside; what is shared lies beneath). Option B: the brief's single pattern, with "or why none is needed" carrying Heidegger and Henry in one section. A is closer to the field's own map (S20, S23); B is closer to the brief. Either way, the intersubjectivity paragraph on the existing epistemology close-affinity page overlaps; it could stay short and the new page carry the depth.

**Cast that carries the page (headlines):**
- Husserl: the classic statement of the problem and the classic way out, "verifiable accessibility of what is not originally accessible", ending in "a single community of monads" and "only one Objective world".
- Merleau-Ponty: solitude and communication as "two 'moments' of one phenomenon"; dialogue as "a common ground"; "a community of men endowed with speech".
- Levinas: separation first; the way out comes from the other, and is language: "The generality of the word institutes a common world."
- Stein: the "we" that enriches and keeps the "I"; empathy as condition of knowing a shared world.
- Nagel: the point of view as a type; detachment, with convergence downstream.
- Zahavi: for-me-ness, a structure every subject has that still individuates; the other's inaccessibility as what makes the other other.
- Henry: the inside so radical that the shared is found beneath it ("toute communauté est invisible").
- Kierkegaard (Climacus): inwardness as truth, and communication whose secret is "to set the other free".
- Sartre: optional and a register question. His way out is real (the other as "the indispensable mediator between myself and me"), but his account of relation ends in conflict and a "we" that is "a purely subjective impression". Under the resonance-only register he can carry one clause (the outside I receive from others) or be left to his person page.

**Backing to flow down first (person pages):**
- Husserl: add the §42 solipsism objection, the §52 formula in his words, the community of monads and "one Objective world" (§60), "the illusion of a solipsism is dissolved" (§62).
- Merleau-Ponty: add the *PhP* dialogue and "community of men endowed with speech" passages; date the term "intercorporeality" to *Signs*/late work. Check against the page's closing claim that "the social and the linguistic remain underdeveloped… how shared language emerges from bodily encounter": the *PhP* text gives language "a crucial rôle" and dialogue "a common ground". A correction for the author to weigh, not a trim.
- Levinas: add "the relation… is language" (p. 39), "Language… first makes [universality] possible" (p. 73), "institutes a common world" (pp. 173–174), and separation as atheism (p. 58). Check the page's line that "plurality and community enter only through the third party" against pp. 173–174.

## Discard list

- "Accessible in its inaccessibility" as Husserl's wording: it is Staehler's and Zahavi's gloss. Husserl: "verifiable accessibility of what is not originally accessible" (Cairns p. 114).
- "Co-pathos" as Henry's term: not found; his section title is "Pathos-avec".
- "Intercorporeality" as a *Phenomenology of Perception* term: not found in that text; it is *Signs* (1960) and later.
- "Hell is other people" as Sartre's thesis in *Being and Nothingness*: it is a line in the play *Huis clos*.
- "Subjectivity is truth" attributed to Kierkegaard in his own name: the thesis is Johannes Climacus's ("min Thesis"), and it is scoped to existential truth.
- The claim (from a search summary) that "Subjektiviteten er Sandheden" is not a direct quote from the Postscript: false; it is in SKS 7 verbatim.
- "Every phenomenologist defends a closed subject and then seeks a way out": the field disputes this. Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty's answer, Scheler and Zahavi deny the closed inside; SEP calls the approach a rejection of "the isolationism of the self".
- Zahavi's empathy as "inaccessible": he holds the other's experience directly perceivable, with an asymmetry that is "constitutional", not a barrier to knowing.
