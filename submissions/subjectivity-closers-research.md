# Subjectivity closers: research round (2026-11-21)

*Working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.*

**Purpose, in place.** A person-page closer ("Where X stops") either routes a gap to a named thinker who presses it, in their words, or grounds it in the thinker's own tension. It never states an absence on nobody's authority. This round checks who presses what for Stein, Zahavi, Henry and Merleau-Ponty, and checks the "solitary Husserl" charge and the Schutz/Habermas critique for the Phenomenology subject page.

**Marks.** [C, S#] = checked against source S#, opened this round, quoted as found (OCR slips and long-s silently normalised, nothing else). [C, as quoted in S#] = verbatim but second-hand: the primary was not opened. [U] = unverified. "Reading" = Claude's interpretation. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / UNVERIFIED. German renderings marked "my rendering" are Claude's, from the original opened at source.

**Site check.** Person pages exist for Stein, Zahavi, Henry (all three new, uncommitted), Merleau-Ponty, Husserl, Habermas, Sartre, Searle, Luckmann, Berger. No page for Schutz; he is named on the Phenomenology subject, the social-construction subject and the Luckmann page, which link out to the SEP. Margaret Gilbert is named on the social-construction subject. No page for Szanto, Schmid, Metzinger, Albahari, Schechtman or Janicaud. No same-surname collisions found.

## Headline first: four of the five current closers are contradicted by the thinkers' own texts

- **Stein** (current: her account "stays with the experience of shared feeling between present persons"). CORRECTED. Her 1922 and 1925 treatises analyse communities lasting across generations, a people's shared world, and the state.
- **Zahavi** (current: the layers beyond meeting "appear as the setting of his analyses rather than their subject"). CORRECTED in part. He names the large-scale we explicitly and gives it an account: it is grounded in face-to-face sharing. That grounding is exactly what Schmid disputes.
- **Henry** (current: the passage from the invisible to the visible community "stays outside the method"). CONFIRMED in substance, but it now has named pressers: Zahavi, plus the SEP author's "aporia". Two biographical slots are CORRECTED.
- **Merleau-Ponty** (current: "left the scale beyond the encounter on the table"). CORRECTED. Institution, history and politics are major themes of his: a 1954–55 course, two political books, and the essays in *Signs*.
- **Husserl closer** (in the working tree). Mostly CONFIRMED. The Sartre line it quotes comes from his critique of Heidegger, not Husserl; Sartre's direct line against Husserl is a different sentence (§6).

## 1. Edith Stein beyond the face-to-face

**Verdict: the current closer is CORRECTED. Dates CONFIRMED. The editing of Husserl is CONFIRMED. No named critic of her community account was found (UNVERIFIED).**

**Community beyond present persons.** These are in Stein's own words, from the first editions in Husserl's *Jahrbuch*, which are public domain.

- A community's experiences outlast any individual: "die Erlebnisse einer Gemeinschaft [können] sich über eine weit größere Dauer erstrecken … als die eines Individuums. Der Haß der Guelfen gegen die Ghibellinen … [sind] einheitliche Erlebnisse im Erlebnisstrom der betreffenden Partei und erstreckten sich über ganze Generationen von Individuen". My rendering: a community's experiences can extend over a far greater duration than an individual's; the Guelphs' hatred of the Ghibellines was one experience in the party's stream, extending over whole generations of individuals. (*Beiträge*, Jahrbuch V, 1922, p. 127) [C, S2].
- A people's common imaginative world: Sleeping Beauty and Little Red Riding Hood "zur gemeinsamen Umwelt unseres Volkes gehört. Keinem von uns wird es einfallen zu behaupten, daß jeder sein eigenes Dornröschen habe" (p. 135) [C, S2].
- Values become real in works: "Wo eine nationale Kultur erwachsen ist, wo eine ausgebildete Moral, ein ausgebildetes Recht, eine Dichtung als gemeinsames Volksgut usw. vorliegt" (pp. 197–198) [C, S2].
- What community is, at root: "Das Wesen des Gemeinschaftslebens sahen wir ja gerade darin, daß die Subjekte nicht aufeinander gerichtet, sondern gemeinsam einem Gegenständlichen zugewendet sind". My rendering: the essence of communal life lies in subjects not being directed at one another but jointly turned toward something objective (p. 244) [C, S2].
- In the state treatise, community forms outlast their members: "Im Gemeinschaftsleben bilden sich feste Formen aus, deren Ausfüllung von verschiedenen Individuen nacheinander übernommen werden kann". My rendering: communal life develops fixed forms which different individuals can fill in succession. A crowd "besteht immer nur, solange die sie konstituierenden Individuen in aktueller Berührung sind" (*Eine Untersuchung über den Staat*, Jahrbuch VII, 1925, pp. 1–2) [C, S3]. The state needs a body of "Träger" who feel belonging and responsibility, which "die große Masse der Staatsbürger nicht kennt oder doch nicht zu kennen braucht" (p. 20) [C, S3].
- The SEP summary, for the headline: the state "cannot be grounded in either 'society' or in 'community' alone"; its central thesis is the "equivalence of statehood and sovereignty"; the state cannot be an ethical subject, because "the state is not capable of such acknowledgment … and in particular incapable of feeling" [C, as quoted in S1, Szanto & Moran].

**Her own fixed point, usable as the closer's tension.** All constitution stays in individual egos: "Die Erlebnisse der Gemeinschaft haben letzten Endes, wie die individuellen, ihren Ursprung in den individuellen Ichen"; "Ein Gemeinschaftssubjekt als Analogon des reinen Ich besteht nicht" (*Beiträge*, pp. 120–121) [C, S2]. The relation of communal to individual experience "ist Konstitution, nicht Summation" (p. 130) [C, S2]. There is no single all-embracing communal stream; the streams are plural (S1, citing ESGA pp. 139–140) [C, S1]. Later, after her turn to Aquinas, Stein "criticized Husserlian transcendental philosophy for seeking to establish objectivity from within subjectivity" (S1, citing *Erkenntnis und Glaube* p. 33) [C, as reported in S1; primary U].

**Critic or limit.** UNVERIFIED. No named critic was found in an open source. The SEP entry, whose authors are themselves Stein scholars, registers one worry and answers it: "despite occasional appearances to the contrary, which may result from misleading notions such as a 'character', or 'personality of the community' … Stein's account of community steers clear of any problematic form of collectivism" [C, S1]. So a collectivist reading exists, but its proponents are not named. Gerda Walther's 1923 treatise on community could not take Stein's essay into account: "Die Arbeit E. Steins über 'Individuum und Gemeinschaft' … konnten wir leider nicht mehr berücksichtigen" (Jahrbuch VI, 1923, footnote at the opening of her treatise) [C, S4]. Walther is therefore a parallel account, not a critic. Candidates for the author to fetch are listed below.

**Dates.**
- *Finite and Eternal Being* was written in the Cologne Carmel from 1935 and published posthumously in 1950 [C, S1 "posthumously published (1950/2006)"; the start date is C as reported in S5, en.wikipedia citing Gricoski 2024].
- *The Science of the Cross* was written at Echt in 1941–42 and left unfinished. Part III, "Kreuzesnachfolge", is printed as a "Fragment". The editors describe reconstructing the manuscript from pages "teils in der geistlichen Heimstätte der Autorin zurückgefunden, teils aus den Trümmern eines benachbarten Klosters aufgelesen". First edition: Louvain, Nauwelaerts, "Copyright 1950", Edith Steins Werke I [C, S6: scan of the 1954 printing on Wikisource, with its contents page and editors' afterword §3].
- Both late works are therefore posthumous, from 1950.

**Husserl's assistant.** Stein was "Husserl's first paid assistant from October 1916 until February 1918". She "transcribed and edited, with major interventions, Husserl's research manuscripts, including *Ideas II* (finally published in 1952), and his *Lectures on the Consciousness on Internal Time* … brought to press by Heidegger in 1928 (with only the slightest acknowledgment of Stein's involvement)". She resigned "as he was unwilling to discuss his own work with her" [C, S1].

**Word test.** *Gemeinschaft*, *Gemeinschaftserlebnis*, *Erlebnisstrom der Gemeinschaft*, *Gesamtpersönlichkeit*, *Masse/Gesellschaft/Gemeinschaft* and *Staat* are Stein's own terms. "Collective intentionality" and "social ontology" are the SEP authors' present-day labels for them [C, S1].

## 2. Dan Zahavi

**(a) Shame. Verdict: the site's line "an emotion that exists only under another's gaze" is CORRECTED. Zahavi rejects that thesis but keeps an irreducible other.**

- "To claim that shame only occurs in situations where a discrediting fact about oneself is exposed to others is not convincing. One can certainly feel shame when alone—that is, shame does not require an actual observer or audience" ("Self, Consciousness, and Shame", *Oxford Handbook of Contemporary Phenomenology*, 2012, p. 310) [C, S7, OUP proofs].
- But: "shame contains a significant and irreducible element of 'alterity'"; and "intrapersonal shame is subsequent to (and conditioned by) interpersonal shame" (p. 320). "Prototypical forms of shame provide vivid examples of other-mediated forms of self-experience" (p. 321) [C, S7].
- The shamed self is more than the minimal self: "The shamed self is not the experiential core self … a self that can be shamed is a more complex (and complicated) self than the minimalist experiential self" (p. 319) [C, S7].
- On whose view he is opposing: he names Sartre as the classic other-centred account, and Deonna, Rodogno and Teroni, and Lewis, as the inward-directed accounts he argues against (pp. 311, 319–321) [C, S7].
- For the book itself: "while arguing that the most fundamental level of selfhood is not socially constructed and not constitutively dependent upon others, Zahavi also argues that there are dimensions of the self and types of self-experience that are other-mediated" (University of Copenhagen announcement of *Self and Other*, 2014) [C, S8]. The book's shame chapters were not opened; that they carry the 2012 wording is [U].

**(b) Critics of the minimal self. Verdict: CONFIRMED, with named pressers on three fronts.**

- **No-self critics.**
  - Thomas Metzinger, *Being No One* (2003): "no such things as selves exist in the world: Nobody ever was or had a self" (p. 1) [C, as quoted in S9, Zahavi's critique "Being Someone", *Psyche* 2005].
  - Miri Albahari, *Analytical Buddhism* (2006), "considers all three [unity, unbrokenness, invariability] to be real features of consciousness, but she nevertheless considers the self to be illusory" [C, as reported in S10, Zahavi, "The Experiential Self: Objections and Clarifications", in *Self, No Self?*, 2011, proofs].
  - Georges Dreyfus, in the same volume, accepts subjectivity but "insists that distortion arises the moment we interpret this subjectivity as a bounded, unified self" [C, as reported in S10].
  - Evan Thompson is a co-editor of *Self, No Self?*. His own position was not checked [U].
- **Social-constructivist and narrative critics.**
  - Zahavi names Wolfgang Prinz (2003): "the social construction of subjectivity and selfhood relies on, and is maintained in, various discourses on subjectivity"; selves are "sociocultural constructs" [C, as quoted in S11, Zahavi, "Is the Self a Social Construct?", *Inquiry* 2009, preprint]. He also names György Gergely (2007), and Maclaren (2008) for the charge of Cartesianism [C, S11].
  - Marya Schechtman, "'The Size of the Self': Minimalist Selves and Narrative Self-Constitution" (2015), sets the narrative view ("a persisting subject exists only by its … narrative appropriation of different experiences over time") against the minimalist one [C, S12, abstract only; her verdict on Zahavi U].
- **"Too thin."** Joe Higgins: "the 'thinnest' construal of minimal experiential selfhood fails to properly account for characteristics that are essential to human selfhood; namely, the intimate, embodied interactions that unfold at the incipient moments of human life". He argues for "the de facto equiprimordiality" of the minimal self with "a 'minimal' form of relational selfhood" ("The 'We' in 'Me'", *Topoi* 39, 2020, abstract) [C, S13].

**(c) The "we" in *Being We*. Verdict: the current closer is CORRECTED in part. Face-to-face is Zahavi's basic form and the ground of the large-scale we; Schmid presses against it by name.**

- Zahavi on the face-to-face: "any plausible account of the we has to factor in the embodied face-to-face relationship. Schmid by contrast has repeatedly denied that the we is founded upon an other-experience and in any other way involves or presupposes some kind of reciprocal relation between self and other" (seminar text, *Filozofija i društvo* 28(4), 2017, p. 1036) [C, S14]. Hans Bernhard Schmid's own wording was not opened [U].
- The large-scale we, named and grounded: "There are, of course, important differences between dyadic kinds of we where the members know each other in person and the kinds of large-scale we, like religious communities or national communities, whose members have never met, but who are nevertheless united via shared rituals, traditions and normative expectations. But the latter would never have become possible were it not for the direct, embodied, experiential sharing that we find in the I-you relationship" (Zahavi, quoted in the University of Copenhagen news release on *Being We*, 21 Feb 2025) [C, S15]. The same wording appears in his own article, "While recognizing that size matters …", where he calls "the dyadic kind that exists between individuals who interact face-to-face in the here and now" "arguably the most basic type of we" ("We in Me or Me in We?", *Journal of Social Ontology* 7, 2021, pp. 16–17) [C, S16].
- *Being We* table of contents. Part III, "Varieties of We", has ch. 11 "Dyads and Triads" and ch. 12 "Communal Bonds" [C, S17, publisher listing]. Contents of ch. 12 [U].
- Pressers of "community first", whom Zahavi argues against: communitarians, quoting MacIntyre ("I belong to this tribe, that clan, this nation … These constitute the given of life, my moral starting-point"); Lynne Rudder Baker; and Zeedyk and Fotopoulou & Tsakiris, who hold that consciousness is "interpersonally constituted" (S16, pp. 5–12) [C, S16]. Thomas Szanto and Felipe León are co-authors and colleagues rather than critics (León, Szanto & Zahavi 2019, cited in S16) [C, S16].
- Zahavi also turns Schutz against himself: Schutz's claim that a we-relation is established by a reciprocal thou-orientation "seems a clear mistake … while second-person engagements might play an important role in the constitution of we-relations, they are not sufficient" (S16, p. 17 n. 4) [C, S16].

**Word test.** "Minimal self", "experiential self", "for-me-ness", "other-mediated self-experience", "community first view" and "we-experience" are Zahavi's own terms.

## 3. Michel Henry

**Verdict: the closer's substance is CONFIRMED, and it now has named pressers. "École Normale Supérieure" is CORRECTED to the Lycée Henri-IV. "Under Jean Wahl" is CORRECTED to Wahl and Hyppolite. The *Marx* headline is CONFIRMED from a secondary source.**

- **Zahavi presses the gap, in his words.** Henry "never presents us with a convincing explanation of how a subjectivity essentially characterized by such a complete self-presence can simultaneously … be directed intentionally toward something different from itself; how it can be capable of recognizing other subjects … how it can be in possession of a bodily exteriority". "Henry's approach is problematic and insufficient because it conceives of self-manifestation in abstracto, rather than accounting for the self-manifestation of the self-transcending temporal, intentional, reflexive, corporeal and intersubjective experiences" ("Subjectivity and Immanence in Michel Henry", in Grøn et al. eds., *Subjectivity and Transcendence*, 2007, pp. 133–147; preprint pp. 15–16) [C, S18]. Zahavi also credits Henry: he is "the phenomenological thinker who has been most attentive to the problem of self-manifestation" (ibid.) [C, S18].
- **The 1999 paper exists.** Zahavi, "Michel Henry and the Phenomenology of the Invisible", *Continental Philosophy Review* 32 (1999), 223–240 [C, metadata, S19]. Its content was not opened [U]. The SEP draws on it for a defence of Henry against Janicaud: "practically all of the major phenomenological thinkers cross such boundaries" (Zahavi 1999: 236) [C, as reported in S20]. Zahavi therefore defends Henry on method and presses him on the other.
- **The SEP author names the tension in politics.** On public life there is "the tension—the aporia even—that must exist between invisible life as ipseity and public affairs as general". And: "the formidable challenge Henry's phenomenology faces lies in its application to what it regards as transcendent, namely … the world and even ourselves as intentional beings" (Seyler, SEP "Michel Henry", rev. 2024) [C, S20]. Henry's own position, as reported there: politics and economics are legitimate only as "a mediation for what is ante-political—namely life, both individual and communal"; democracy "can be nothing but a mediation" (*La vie et la république*, VR 162) [C, as quoted in S20].
- **Janicaud, in one line.** Dominique Janicaud, *Le tournant théologique de la phénoménologie française* (1991), targeted Henry with Levinas, Marion and Chrétien. The critique was "not so much directed against an explicit reference to 'theology' … but against an attempt to radicalize phenomenology beyond the scope of object manifestation and intentionality" [C, as reported in S20]. Janicaud's own wording [U].
- **The *Marx* headline.** Henry read Marx as a philosopher of the living individual and of praxis, "l'individu vivant—la praxis—en lequel il voit le fondement de la réalité économique". He put Marxism in brackets as "l'ensemble des contresens qui ont été faits sur Marx" (fr.wikipedia, citing *Marx I*, 1976, pp. 9–33, 193, 207) [C, as reported in S21; primary U]. English title: *Marx: A Philosophy of Human Reality* (partial translation, 1983) [C, S20]. Headline CONFIRMED (secondary). Note for the closer: the *Marx* volumes and *Du communisme au capitalisme* (1990) are where Henry himself takes life into economics and politics (S20) [C]. The visible social world is in his work; what is pressed is how invisible life can be shown in it.
- **ENS or Henri-IV.** "In Paris, Henry became a student at the prestigious Lycée Henri IV, then graduated with a Master thesis on Spinoza in 1943 with Jean Grenier as director" [C, S20]; "au lycée Henri-IV" [C, S21]. Only the en.wikipedia infobox, unsourced, says ENS [C, S22]. CORRECTED: the page's "studied at the École Normale Supérieure" is not supported. The Resistance code name "Kant" is [C, as reported in S21, citing Brohm & Leclercq 2009]. The SEP gives only the "Pericles" division in the Haut-Jura [C, S20].
- **Supervisor or jury.** He worked "under the supervision of both Jean Wahl and Jean Hyppolite". The 1963 committee was "Jean Wahl, Jean Hyppolite, Ferdinand Alquié, Paul Ricoeur, and Henri Gouhier" [C, S20]. CORRECTED: "written under Jean Wahl" should read "under Jean Wahl and Jean Hyppolite".

## 4. The social-world critique: Schutz and Habermas

**Verdict: both are CONFIRMED as pressers, but the proposed clause is CORRECTED. Schutz presses from inside phenomenology, and his target is transcendental constitution, not a start from consciousness as such. Habermas's "1973, p. 198" is *Erkenntnis und Interesse*, not the 1971 Gauss lectures.**

- **Schutz, in his own words** [C, as quoted in S23, *Bulletin d'Analyse Phénoménologique* 14(7), 2018, citing *Collected Papers* III, p. 82]:
  - intersubjectivity "is not a problem of constitution which can be solved in the transcendental sphere but is rather a datum (Gegebenheit) of the life-world";
  - "the possibility of reflection on the self, discovery of the ego, capacity for performing any epochē, and the possibility of all communication … are founded on the primal experience of the we-relationship".
  - Further: "it must be earnestly asked whether the transcendental Ego in Husserl's concept is not essentially what Latin grammarians call a 'singular tantum,' that is, a term incapable of being put into the plural … or whether intersubjectivity and therefore sociality does not rather belong exclusively to the mundane sphere of our life-world" [C, as quoted in S24, Zahavi, "Merleau-Ponty on Husserl: A Reappraisal", 2002, preprint p. 9].
  - SEP summary: "Intersubjectivity, Schutz concluded, was a matter of everyday life to be simply described and not to be constituted within the transcendental sphere" [C, S25, Barber, rev. 2021].
- **Schutz reaches beyond the face-to-face.** His social world runs from consociates through "Contemporaries with whom one shares only the same time" to "Predecessors and Successors", in degrees of "anonymity" [C, S25].
- **Zahavi answers Schutz.** Schutz "tacitly accepts" the "traditional Kantian understanding" of the transcendental subject. Husserl rejects that understanding, and his late work shows "a decisive expansion of the transcendental sphere … to consider the transcendental significance of such issues as generativity, tradition, historicity, and normality" (S24, preprint pp. 9–10) [C, S24].
- **Habermas.**
  - Language as the foundation: "Habermas, who famously argues that language is the foundation of intersubjectivity (Habermas, 1973, p. 198 …)". The reference list gives "Habermas, J. (1973), *Erkenntnis und Interesse* (Frankfurt am Main: Suhrkamp)" (Zahavi, "Beyond Empathy", *JCS* 8, 2001) [C, S26; the Habermas page itself U].
  - The solipsism charge: Habermas "accuses Husserl's philosophy – and by extension phenomenology as such (Habermas 1992:42) – of being solipsistic" (Overgaard & Zahavi, "Phenomenological Sociology", preprint p. 3, citing *Postmetaphysical Thinking*) [C, as reported in S27; Habermas's wording U].
  - In his own frame, the "philosophy of consciousness" (*Bewusstseinsphilosophie*) grounds claims "on the individual subject's self-knowledge"; postmetaphysical thinking puts reason "embedded in the intersubjective communicative processes of the lifeworld" (SEP "Habermas", 2023) [C, as reported in S28].
  - He also criticises Schutz's life-world as "abridged in a culturalistic fashion" (*TCA* 2: 126–132) [C, as quoted in S25].
- **Reply on record** (prior round, restated so the note stands alone). Zahavi calls the charge that phenomenology is "fundamentally incapable" of addressing intersubjectivity "astonishingly misguided" (Zahavi, "Intersubjectivity", *Routledge Companion to Phenomenology*, 2011) [C in the prior round; not reopened here].

## 5. Merleau-Ponty on institution, history and politics

**Verdict: the current closer is CORRECTED. He worked on the scale beyond the encounter. What stayed incomplete is the late ontology meant to carry it.**

- **Institution.** In the 1952 essay "Indirect Language and the Voices of Silence", he "offers an alternative understanding of 'institution' (from Husserl's *Stiftung*) as the creative establishment of a new field of meaning that opens an historical development" [C, S29, Toadvine, SEP rev. 2023]. The 1954–55 Collège de France course, "Institution in Personal and Public History", is published as *L'institution, La passivité* (2003) and *Institution and Passivity* (2010) [C, S29]. Keller reports that in its introduction "the institution he has in mind is a phenomenological substitute for the primacy of the consciousness" [C, as reported in S30; the course text U]. The definition usually quoted from the course summary ("those events in an experience which endow it with durable dimensions") is [U]; to fetch.
- **History and politics.**
  - *Humanism and Terror* (1947) and *Adventures of the Dialectic* (1955) [C, S29].
  - In *Adventures*: "History forms a third order, beyond subjects and objects, of interhuman relations inscribed in cultural objects and institutions, and with its own logic of sedimentation and spontaneity" [C, S29, summarising AdD]. Against Sartre, a consciousness "unconstrained by any sedimentation" "can recognize no inertia or spontaneity at the level of institutions, and therefore no genuine historical becoming" [C, S29]. His own words, quoted there: "In politics, truth is perhaps only this art of inventing what will later appear to have been required by the time" (AdD 29) [C, as quoted in S29].
- **Society through exchange and symbols.** "If so, the exchange would not be an effect of society but society itself in act" ("From Mauss to Claude Lévi-Strauss", *Signs*, McCleary trans. 1964, pp. 115–116) [C, S31].
- **His own tension.** He died on 3 May 1961. *The Visible and the Invisible* is a manuscript and working notes "on which he was working at the time of his death"; *The Prose of the World* was "drafted in 1950–51 and apparently abandoned" [C, S29]. The institution analyses survive as course notes, not a book [C, S29: course volumes are "based either on student notes or Merleau-Ponty's own lecture notes"].
- **Backing note.** The site's Merleau-Ponty page does not mention institution, history, *Humanism and Terror* or *Adventures*. Under the backing-flows-down rule, the body needs a key concept on these before the closer is changed.

## 6. Husserl: the solitary-method charge and Zahavi's counter-reading

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with one CORRECTION to the Husserl page as it stands in the working tree.**

- **Sartre against Husserl, directly:** "Because Husserl has reduced being to a series of meanings, the only connection which he has been able to establish between my being and that of the Other is a connection of knowledge. Therefore Husserl can not escape solipsism any more than Kant could" (*Being and Nothingness*, Barnes trans., Washington Square Press, p. 318) [C, S32].
- **CORRECTION.** The page quotes "We encounter the Other; we do not constitute him" as the sharpest form of the charge against Husserl. In Sartre it closes his critique of *Heidegger*: "as the result of our critical examination of Heidegger's teaching: Human-reality remains alone because the Other's existence has the nature of a contingent and irreducible fact. We encounter the Other; we do not constitute him" (p. 336) [C, S32]. Either swap in the p. 318 line or attribute the p. 336 line correctly. That choice is Jules's.
- **Others who press the charge.**
  - Schutz: the "singular tantum" passage (§4) [C, as quoted in S24].
  - Habermas, 1992 (§4) [C, as reported in S27].
  - Within Merleau-Ponty scholarship, Zahavi names Madison and Dillon, for whom "Husserl remained an intellectualist, an idealist, and a solipsist to the very end" (S24, preprint p. 3) [C, S24].
- **Zahavi's counter-reading, in his words.**
  - "Although Husserl has often been regarded as a methodological solipsist, he in fact undertook what Karl-Otto Apel and Jürgen Habermas have later become known for: An intersubjective transformation of transcendental philosophy" (*Husserl's Phenomenology*, 2003, p. 109) [C, S33].
  - "I do think there is far more congruence between Husserl's philosophical project and Merleau-Ponty's, than say between Husserl's project and Heidegger's or Sartre's. In that sense Merleau-Ponty certainly was the most Husserlian of the three major post-husserlian phenomenologists" (S24, preprint pp. 11–12) [C, S24].
  - *Husserl's Legacy* (2017) was not opened [U]. Of the readings given in the brief, the 2003 book and the 2002 paper are checked.

## Placement proposal (options, not decisions)

All closers are sketches in the neutral person-page register, with no SPLectrum vocabulary. Jules decides.

**Stein.** Option A, her own tension:

> Stein's account of community reaches far past the face-to-face: communal experience can span generations, a people shares a common world of tales and works, and the state rests on its own sovereignty rather than on any community. What she holds fixed throughout is that the constituting consciousness stays individual. Communal experiences have their origin "in the individual I's" that belong to the community, and "a community subject as analogue of the pure I does not exist". After her conversion she came to criticise Husserl's transcendental philosophy for seeking objectivity from within subjectivity, and her later ontology starts from being instead.

Option B adds a named presser once one is checked (open check). The body first needs a key concept on *Beiträge* and *Staat*, under the backing rule, and dated key works: 1922; 1925; 1950 (written from 1935); 1950 (written 1941–42, unfinished).

**Zahavi.**

> Zahavi grounds every "we", up to communities whose members never meet, in the face-to-face sharing of the I–you relation. Hans Bernhard Schmid denies that the we is founded on an experience of the other at all; communitarians and social constructivists put the community first, which Zahavi rejects. On the self, no-self critics (Metzinger, Albahari) hold that for-me-ness is real but is no self, and relational critics (Higgins) find the minimal self too thin to be the first layer.

Fix in the body: "shame, an emotion that exists only under another's gaze" becomes, for example, "shame, which he holds can be felt alone but carries an irreducible reference to others".

**Henry.**

> Zahavi presses the point from inside phenomenology: Henry never shows how a self-presence so complete can be directed at anything other than itself, recognise other subjects or have a bodily outside. Henry's political writing meets the same edge from the other side: politics can only be a mediation for invisible life, and his commentators name the tension between life as ipseity and public affairs an aporia. Janicaud's "theological turn" charge targets the method itself, for going beyond what appears.

Biography fixes: Lycée Henri-IV, not ENS; thesis under Wahl and Hyppolite, with a five-member jury; the "Kant" code name only from a secondary source.

**Merleau-Ponty.**

> Merleau-Ponty carried the common ground beyond the encounter: institution as the founding of a field of meaning that opens a history, history as a third order of relations inscribed in institutions, and politics in *Humanism and Terror* and *Adventures of the Dialectic*. The ontology meant to hold all this, the flesh, was left as an unfinished manuscript at his death in 1961, and his account of institution survives mainly in course notes.

The body needs the institution and politics backing first.

**Phenomenology subject page.** The proposed clause, "from outside, Schutz and Habermas press that a start from consciousness cannot reach the social", is not accurate as worded. Two options:

- Option A: "From within, Schutz argued that the other cannot be constituted in the transcendental sphere and that intersubjectivity is a given of the everyday life-world; from outside, Habermas charged phenomenology with solipsism and moved the foundation of intersubjectivity to language."
- Option B: add after A, "Zahavi answers that the charge misreads Husserl."

Backing: no Schutz page exists. An SEP external link is the existing pattern (social-construction subject, Luckmann page). The Habermas page does not yet carry the solipsism charge or "language as foundation", so that line would flow down first, or be sourced on the subject page only.
