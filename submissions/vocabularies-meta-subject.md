## 1. Aim

To work out how a neutral meta subject (keyword doorway) for **vocabularies** should be shaped: the field's material on the parts a language uses — concepts, categories, lexicons, terminologies — gathered and organised so the diversity becomes visible, in the field's voice, with SPLectrum-inspired categorisation.

## 2. The decision

- **[A]** Two doorways, not one. **Language is about totality; vocabularies are about parts.** Vocabularies are an independent unit; the two are linked.
- **[A]** Vocabularies are the concept schemes — the registers — that language uses. Language is the relational constructs that bring all registers together.
- **[A]** A conceptual scheme, in the field's sense, is a construct that uses vocabulary. It belongs to the language doorway, not here.
- **[corrected]** Claude proposed a sibling doorway named "concept", with conceptual schemes inside it. The author: the doorway is vocabularies; conceptual schemes go to language.
- **Mirrors the site.** The top-level Vocabulary section ("the conceptual") is the position's side; this doorway is the field's side. The section links down; the doorway does not link up. It cross-links with the language doorway.

## 3. What needs care

- **Rule 5 — the word check.** The field rarely says "vocabulary" in this sense. Its words are *concept*, *category*, *lexicon*, *terminology*, *controlled vocabulary*, *taxonomy*. The scope definition has to do that work, and the bundle probably needs a page for the territory carried under other names, as the emergence bundle has.
- **Avoid "scheme" on this side.** In the field "conceptual scheme" names the construct (language side). Category names here use "concepts", "categories", "vocabularies".
- **"Register."** The working word on the position side. In linguistics a register is a variety by situation (formal, technical); the doorway uses the field's words.
- **The line with language.** Change *of a concept* is here; change *of a scheme* is language. Plain controlled vocabularies and taxonomies are here; axiomatised ontologies (taxonomies plus axioms and relations, e.g. OWL) are constructs using a vocabulary — language side.

## 4. Draft branches (a working sketch, to be tuned)

0. **The word** — "vocabulary", "lexicon", "concept", "category": where the field uses which, and the territory carried under other names.
1. **Concepts** — theories of what a concept is. **[C, unchecked]** classical (definitional) view; prototype and exemplar theories (Rosch); theory-theory; conceptual atomism (Fodor); the Margolis and Laurence overviews.
2. **Fuzziness and boundaries** — **[C, unchecked]** Wittgenstein's family resemblance; Rosch's prototypes; Zadeh's fuzzy sets; vagueness. Links to the language document §2.1, where fuzziness is held as the normal case for concepts, not a defect.
3. **Categories and categorisation** — **[C, unchecked]** cognitive science of categorisation; Lakoff, *Women, Fire, and Dangerous Things* (1987); cross-linguistic colour terms (Berlin and Kay, 1969) and their critics.
4. **Concept formation** — in development and in learning; and the physical route: Rovelli's correlation-to-concept step (language document §2.1) as the field's floor, with signal and language onward on the language side.
5. **Change of a concept** — **[C, unchecked]** Kuhn on "mass" (the term kept, the content changed); conceptual history (Koselleck's *Begriffsgeschichte*); Thagard's *Conceptual Revolutions* (1992) as it bears on single concepts. Field home for the evolution core's "a concept can evolve language-game-wise and remain the same concept in name" (`plan/evolution-follow-on/evolution-follow-on.md`).
6. **Lexicons and terminologies** — lexicology and lexicography; terminology science (Wüster); controlled vocabularies, thesauri and taxonomies in information science.
7. **Language and thought** — the seam with the language doorway; held here or there and cross-linked (open).
