**Current, line 12:**
> Heidegger broke the spectator model of knowledge. Being-in-the-world — always already involved, always already understanding — replaced the subject contemplating objects with a being inseparable from its world. **Proposed, line 12:**
> Heidegger asked the question of being: not what there is, but what it means *to be*. *Being and Time* (1927) approached it through the being that we ourselves are, which he called Dasein. Dasein is being-in-the-world: it is what it is through its dealings with things, with others and with its own projects, and it meets things first in use. Looking at them as detached objects comes later and depends on that involvement. With this analysis he contested the modern picture, set by Descartes, of a subject standing over against an objective world. The later work turned to being itself and its history, a sequence of epochs in which things show up differently, ending in the technological age. In each epoch works of art and language take part in opening up the world, and language, in his phrase, is "the house of Being". Trained in [Husserl](/positioning/persons/h/husserl/)'s [phenomenology](/positioning/subjects/p/phenomenology/), he turned it to the question of being, and his work went on to shape existentialism, [hermeneutics](/positioning/subjects/h/hermeneutics/the-ontological-turn/) and [deconstruction](/positioning/subjects/d/deconstruction/). His support for National Socialism makes the relation between his thought and his politics what the *Stanford Encyclopedia* calls "the most controversial issue in contemporary discussions" of his work.

Backing: the question of being, Dasein as being-in-the-world "constituted by our relationships to the practical and social contexts", the "critique of post-Cartesian accounts of human beings as subjects in an objective world", the "sequence of distinct 'epochs' of being, culminating in the contemporary technological age", and language and art structuring "being-historical worlds" [C, S1 lead]; the influence on Sartre's existentialism, Gadamer's hermeneutics and Derrida's deconstruction [C, S1 lead]; "The most controversial issue in contemporary discussions of Heidegger's work is that of determining the extent to which his thought is compromised…" [C, S1 §3]; use before observation [C, S2 §15, below in check 3]; Descartes and the *subiectum* [C, S4, already on the page]; "Language is the house of Being" [C, S3]. The wording is not a close paraphrase of the SEP lead; the one phrase taken over is quoted and attributed.

**Current, line 14:**
> [Martin Heidegger](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/) (1889–1976). Asked the question of Being — not what exists, but what it means *to be*. His answer displaced the subject-object split that had structured Western philosophy since Descartes. We do not first exist and then encounter a world; we are always already in a world, disclosed through our involvement with it. Heidegger served as rector of Freiburg University in 1933–34 under the Nazi regime, joined the NSDAP, and never publicly recanted. The *Black Notebooks* (published 2014 onward) revealed antisemitic passages. The relationship between his philosophy and his politics remains one of the most debated questions in twentieth-century intellectual history.

**Proposed, line 14:**
> [Martin Heidegger](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/) (1889–1976). Born on 26 September 1889 in Messkirch, a small town in rural south-west Germany, the son of a master cooper who was also sexton of the Catholic church. At Freiburg he studied Catholic theology, the natural sciences and mathematics before settling on philosophy. He took his doctorate in 1913 and his habilitation in 1915, on Duns Scotus, under the neo-Kantian Heinrich Rickert. He became Husserl's assistant in 1919. Their relationship was strained, but he dedicated *Being and Time* to Husserl "in friendship and admiration". At Marburg (1923–28) his lectures drew students including [Gadamer](/positioning/persons/g/gadamer/), Karl Löwith, [Jonas](/positioning/persons/j/jonas/) and [Arendt](/positioning/persons/a/arendt/). *Being and Time* appeared in 1927, and in 1928 he succeeded to Husserl's chair at Freiburg. In 1929 he gave his inaugural lecture there, "What Is Metaphysics?", which [Carnap](/positioning/persons/c/carnap/) made the test case of his attack on metaphysics, and he met [Cassirer](/positioning/persons/c/cassirer/) in the Davos disputation. Elected rector of Freiburg in April 1933, he joined the Nazi Party on 1 May. He resigned the rectorship in April 1934 (see *The Nazi period* below). Through the war years he lectured on Nietzsche and Hölderlin, and on Schelling, Hegel and the pre-Socratics. After hearings before a denazification committee, the French military government barred him from teaching. The ban was lifted in 1949 and he took emeritus status, and his later work appeared mostly as lectures and essays. He died in Freiburg on 26 May 1976 and was buried in Messkirch.

Backing: every fact is in the SEP's "Life and Work" [C, S1 §1]: birth, father, studies, the 1913 and 1915 degrees under Rickert, assistant 1919, the dedication "in friendship and admiration" (SZ vi), the Marburg years and the students Gadamer, Löwith, Jonas and Arendt, the 1927 promotion, the 1928 chair "previously held by Rickert and Husserl", Davos 1929, rector April 1933, party member 1 May, resignation April 1934, the wartime lectures, the teaching ban and its lifting in 1949, death and burial. The inaugural lecture was given on 24 July 1929 [C, S3, Krell's introduction]. Carnap: the site's Carnap page carries the attack (check 6). The wording "barred … from all teaching" and "lifted in 1949" is the SEP's. Other accounts date the lifting to 1950/51 [U]. That is a detail and does not change the headline.

Reliance check for the cut sentences: "displaced the subject-object split" and "always already in a world" are backed again by the new intro and the Dasein concept; the politics move to check 8's section; "Asked the question of Being" survives in the intro. Nothing on the site quotes the cut wording.

### 1b. New key concept: technology

**Add after "The age of the world picture" (exact text):**
> **Technology (*Gestell*).** In "The Question Concerning Technology" (lecture 1953, published 1954) technology is not first of all an instrument: "Technology is therefore no mere means. Technology is a way of revealing." Modern technology reveals everything as "standing-reserve" (*Bestand*), on call for further ordering, and its essence is *Gestell*, Enframing: "that way of revealing which holds sway in the essence of modern technology and which is itself nothing technological" (tr. Lovitt). In the history of being the technological age is the epoch in which that history culminates.

Backing: both quotations and "standing-reserve [*Bestand*]" in Lovitt [C, S4]; lecture 1953, published 1954 [C, S1 bibliography, GA7]; culmination in the technological age [C, S1 lead]. Lovitt's own note gives the Munich lecture date with an OCR misreading ("1955") [C, S4]; go by the SEP.

**Correct — truth (line 28).** Current: `Truth, for Heidegger, is not correspondence but unconcealment (*aletheia*).` The SEP says this overstates it. Heidegger treats propositional truth as one species of unconcealment and rejects correspondence understood as a relation "between a mental representation and a state of affairs", and "it is clear that Heidegger does not reject the commonsensical idea that beliefs or assertions are true in virtue of their agreement with the world" [C, S1 §2.7]. Tugendhat's objection and Heidegger's late concession are the reception headline [C, S1 §2.7]. The concession is in "The End of Philosophy": "to raise the question of *alētheia*, of unconcealment as such, is not the same as raising the question of truth. For this reason, it was … misleading to call *alētheia*, in the sense of clearing, 'truth'" [C, S3 p. 323; one word OCR-garbled in the copy, so it is elided].
Replace with (exact):
> Truth, for Heidegger, is in the first place unconcealment (*aletheia*): an assertion can be correct only because entities have already been uncovered and their being disclosed, so correctness rests on unconcealment rather than on a match between a mental representation and a state of affairs. Ernst Tugendhat objected that this loses what makes truth truth, its opposition to falsehood, and Heidegger later granted that it was "misleading" to call unconcealment "truth" ("The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking").

The rings' "rather than the matching of a proposition to a fact" stays compatible with this, since the rings speak of the ground, not of a rejection of agreement. No change is asked of them.
