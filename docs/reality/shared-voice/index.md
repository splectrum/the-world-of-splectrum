# The Shared Voice

**Common understanding is a language: a shared voice.**

Each of us has a voice of our own. We create, understand and speak in a language that is ours, and no one else holds it identically. Yet our language in common is strong enough for coexistence: we agree, disagree, trade, teach and read one another, and all of that runs on something held between us. That common understanding is a shared voice, and it is what makes a plurality of voices interrelational rather than a scatter of voices with nothing between them.

---

## A language, not a medium

Whatever is understood in common has to be expressible, and the tools of expression are what a language is. There is no intermediate place where understanding is shared but unsaid. So a common understanding maps to a language, implicitly or explicitly, and it is a language game in its own right: with its own moves, its own competence, its own boundaries. Each subject stands to it as to any game it does not wholly own. Nobody speaks the shared voice alone, and nobody speaks only it.

That is why it is a voice and not a medium. A medium is neutral and carries whatever is put through it. The shared voice carries content of its own and runs up against edges of its own.

## Built, not given

The shared voice is made by the interaction the whole picture holds to be primary. Seen from where the subjects stand, what they share moves at two speeds. Slower, and underneath from here, is the common base: what has settled between them into shared tools, sedimented. Convergence is how the base is built. It is the same convergence by which we come to agree on the meaning of a word, an outcome checkable from where the subjects stand, not a standard applied from above them. On top of the base run the waves: agreement and disagreement, growth and recession, the exchanges of the moment. The waves explore; they do not optimise. What lands settles into the base over time. What does not land fades into insignificance, which is the ordinary fate of most of what is said, not a failure of the voice.

What converges is the language, not every sentence spoken in it. Two subjects, or two communities, share a voice when they hold enough in common that a claim made by one has a sense for the other: what it says, what would count as checking it, what would follow if it held. Whether they then agree is a wave on that base. The shared voice gives claims their meaning; it does not settle them. That is what lets it hold claims about what no one can now check, the unrecoverable past among them: the language they are made in is common even where the verdict is out of reach.

The common ground moves in both directions. It thickens where interaction is dense and thins where it stops; communities that stop talking drift, and a voice once shared fragments into dialects that no longer read each other easily. None of this is loss or calamity. It is the ebb and flow of what is held in common, and a shared voice at any moment is the current state of that flow.

Because it is built, it is particular: not one shared voice but many, at every scale from two people who have worked together for years to a civilisation's common sense, overlapping and nested. The nesting carries the point of view with it. What serves as base at one scale is itself a wave on something deeper and slower, and a single exchange has a base of its own, in what the two speakers have already settled between them. There is no final bottom. A base is always a base from here, at this scale, and itself moving.

## What it makes possible

**Reading across games.** A reading of another's game is always made from somewhere, and its reference point is the common understanding between the reader's context and the game being read. A reading is sound where it stays anchored in what is genuinely shared, and unsound where it imports the reader's own non-shared content as though it were common. The shared voice is that anchor. Without it there is no reading, only projection.

**Understanding without agreement.** What crosses a language boundary is understanding, not agreement. Two communities can understand each other completely and disagree entirely, and the disagreement is the surest sign that the shared voice is working: to differ vehemently one must first hold enough in common to differ about. Agreement is optional. Readability is the achievement.

**Convergence without a standard.** Objectivity, in this picture, is converged subjectivity: what has come to be held in common within a shared voice, stable enough to rely on, always open to the next experience that does not fit. Nothing above the games certifies it, and nothing needs to, because the discipline is the coupling itself: one cannot decide alone what a shared word means and still be understood.

**The judgement "this does not make sense".** The shared voice has boundaries of its own, and they are where a particular kind of judgement comes from. Across a family of languages it is possible to say of a claim that it does not make sense, and to say it without ranking anyone's content. It is a judgement about form and coherence made in the common: whether a claim is shaped like the rest of what is held, whether it can be checked in any way the common recognises. A culture's no-go areas live in the same place, sedimented into its base, and they shift as the base does.

## In both realms

The shared voice is where the two realms of the pillars meet. In the realm of coexistence it is the base from which [ethics](/reality/ethics/) reads across games and the ground on which [politics](/reality/politics/) finds its balance among them. In the realm of realisation it is what [epistemology](/reality/epistemology/) converges toward and [aesthetics](/reality/aesthetics/) works from: the shared tooling a subject takes up to say something of its own. One concept in both realms, and the same concept: what a subject knows in common is what it coexists in.
