# P4 Interrelational and of Equal Standing 101

**P4 — Languages are interrelational and have equal standing in potential.**

Language relates concepts, and every concept is itself a bag of relations, held in language. So a new language never starts from scratch. It picks up concepts that already mean something and carries them somewhere new.

Take hot and cold. It starts with a touch: the stove, the snow. Then it is also felt from a distance: the heat of a fire, the cold from an open door. From there it becomes a property of things themselves: hot tea, cold water. Then of the weather, with its numbers in the forecast. And further, to things you can't touch at all: a warm welcome, a cold shoulder. Each step borrows from the ones before and adds its own.

What can be carried along? Anything that has meaning to someone, or comes to. And nothing ranks the steps. The touch of the stove is not truer than the forecast, nor the forecast than the cold shoulder. There is no ladder, and no rule about where a concept may go, other than its use.

The same goes for the languages we tend to call higher: the more abstract ones, like mathematics or physics, or the ones about ways of life, like a religion or a philosophy. They are no different. These artefacts of our culture are made the same way, and nothing puts one above another or gets to dictate what the others can and cannot say. They have equal standing.

Equal standing "in potential" leaves it open to choice. You may rank languages for yourself, or not: nothing forces it one way or the other. SPLectrum calls this interrelational pluralism: related, but different, and with a right to be so.
