# P0 Being Implies Language 101

**P0 — Being implies language.**

That something is, that it exists, is often thought of as static. It isn't: to be a being is always a process. For living beings this is easy to see. To be means always to be in interaction with your surroundings: breathing, eating, sensing, moving. Stop, and you stop being.

The same holds for things that aren't alive. A stone holds its shape against weight, weather and warmth. If it didn't interact with its surroundings at all, it wouldn't be there. That interaction is what the first principle calls a language: wherever something exists, there is a language between it and its surroundings.

The picture is an old one: being as a tension between something and its environment, standing apart from it while staying in touch. Heraclitus put it some 2,500 years ago: "the way up and the way down are one and the same". In the twentieth century, the logician Spencer-Brown built a whole calculus from a single instruction: draw a distinction. SPLectrum's reference library sets nine traditions side by side, from ancient Greece and China to India and modern logic: [Being as Tension](/positioning/seed/being-as-tension/).
