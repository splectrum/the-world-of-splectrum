[Shannon](/positioning/persons/s/shannon/)'s theory of information measures correlation and says nothing about whether a correlation is *about* anything or matters to anyone. A thermostat and a thermometer both carry information about a room, and nothing in the mathematics says whether either of them has anything at stake in it. The gap is deliberate — Shannon was solving a problem about telephone lines — and it has bothered people ever since, because what was left out is what most of us mean by information.

In 2018 [Artemy Kolchinsky](/positioning/persons/k/kolchinsky/) and [David Wolpert](/positioning/persons/w/wolpert/) [tried to close it](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08053), under a condition that makes the attempt worth watching. The definition had to be **intrinsic**: grounded in the system's own dynamics, not in a goal supplied from outside. A utility function fails the condition, because it makes the meaning, in their words, "for the external scientist who imputes goals to the system, rather than for the system itself." They wanted something that would apply to a rock, a hurricane and a cell alike, without anybody deciding in advance what any of them was for.

The construction is elegant. Take a system coupled to an environment and define a **viability function**: how tightly the system is concentrated in states that keep it in being. Scramble the correlations between system and environment selectively and see how much viability is lost. Correlations whose destruction costs nothing were not doing anything; **semantic information** is what remains, the correlations that are load-bearing for the system's continued existence. Run it on the three examples and a gradient appears: a rock's correlations are worth nothing to its persistence, a hurricane's little, a food-caching bird's a great deal. Agents come apart from passive structures without anyone having defined agency first, which is a real result.

Then the problem, which the authors are the ones to state. A viability function needs a standard of doing well, and the standard has to come from somewhere. The paper says where:

> It is important to re-emphasize that, in our framework, the viability function is exogenously determined by the scientist analysing the system, rather than being a purely endogenous characteristic of the system.

Where to cut between system and environment is a choice of the same kind, and both, they write, "generally represent 'subjective' choices of the scientist." They have an argument for not being embarrassed: staying in existence is a precondition for almost anything else a system might be said to be doing, so viability is far less arbitrary than a scenario-specific utility. That is fair. But the definition was to be intrinsic, and the standard still arrives from outside. The paper treats it as a residue, to be scrubbed out by future work.

Except that "subjective" is carrying two things here, and they come apart. The first is that we do not know the standard in advance. Nobody reads a system's closure off the system; the scientist proposes one and the dynamics answer — the system holds those states or it does not, scrambling costs or it does not. That is a fact about our position, and it is the ordinary shape of a model: propose, and be told. The paper's own examples already work this way; nobody asked the bird.

The second is that once the cut is made, the quantity is indexed to a coupling. Which correlations hold between this system and that environment is a fact about the interaction, not about anyone's knowledge. That is [Rovelli](/positioning/persons/r/rovelli/)'s position, carried from [relational quantum mechanics](/positioning/subjects/r/relational-quantum-mechanics/) to [entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01125) and stated for exactly this situation: "A cup of hot tea does not cool down because of what I know or do not know about its molecules." Objective, and relative to an interaction, not to a knower. The framework is already relational at the cut. Fused, the two read as a subjective choice to be removed. Kept apart, the not-knowing is ordinary science and the indexing is structure, and neither is the defect the paper takes it to be.

The paper credits Rovelli's [essay on meaning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02420) for its founding idea, that meaningful information is survival-relevant correlation, and the two positions sit side by side in the field: one reads relativity to a cut as a defect awaiting removal, the other as the ordinary form of an objective quantity. A viability function read from nowhere is absolute space, and special relativity did not recover absolute simultaneity by better bookkeeping; it dropped the demand, and the physics stayed objective without a privileged frame.

There is a general pattern here, and the paper is an instance of it rather than its diagnosis. A constraint is set, the construction violates it, and the violation is filed as an engineering problem for later, when the live option is that the constraint was the wrong ambition. Intrinsic did not have to mean frame-free, and it did not have to mean known without a scientist either. The coupling fills the chair. What we do is say what it holds, and stand to be corrected.
