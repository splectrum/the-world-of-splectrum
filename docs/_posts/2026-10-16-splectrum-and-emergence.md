History keeps a file marked *strong emergence* — properties of wholes that cannot be derived, even in principle, from full knowledge of their parts. And history keeps emptying it. Vitalism filed *life* there; biochemistry restated it. [C. D. Broad](/positioning/persons/b/broad/), the most careful mind ever to organise the file, put *chemical bonding* in it in 1925 — his confident example of a relation no amount of knowledge could close. Quantum mechanics began deriving chemical bonds within the decade. Philosophers of chemistry still argue about how complete that derivation is, and the argument matters — we will come back to it — but the example did what confident examples in this file keep doing: it moved.

Now set a different case beside it. For two and a half centuries, Newtonian mechanics could not account for the drift in Mercury's orbit. The relation between the theory and the observation could not be stated. And nobody called it emergence. Astronomers filed it as a *problem* — an unknown — and kept working, and general relativity closed it. The instinct was right. So here is the question the file poses: why does an unstatable orbit get filed under *unknown*, while an unstatable whole gets filed under *strong emergence*?

SPLectrum's answer is that the second filing says nothing the first doesn't — except a claim about the future, made from the one position that cannot make it. In SPLectrum's picture, what we know lives in meaning contexts: languages, each with its own vocabulary, instruments and standards, each exact within its bounds. Something genuinely new arriving in the world looks, from inside our knowledge, like a new context manifesting on top of an existing one. Chemistry on physics. Biology on chemistry. The market on the trades. The new context is not a decoration — it has its own workable relations, its own daily exactness, and it does not disappear into the context below even when relations across the seam get stated. That is the fact the philosophers of chemistry are guarding, and it survives every derivation: chemistry remains a working language *on top of* physics, not a chapter inside it. This — a new context, real novelty, layered on an existing context, with relations across the seam statable or growing statable — is what the literature calls soft emergence. In SPLectrum's voice, it is the only kind there is.

Not because the strong kind has been refuted. Because of what the strong claim *is*. "This relation can never be stated" is a statement about all future meaning contexts, issued from inside the current one — the single vantage point from which the future of knowledge is not visible. Newtonian astronomy was in no position to declare Mercury's drift closable or unclosable; it was in a position to say *we cannot state this*, and that is what it said. SPLectrum holds itself to the same discipline. Where the current context cannot state the relation, the honest file is *unknown* — and unknown is not a lesser filing. Mercury sat in it for two hundred and fifty years. It was not waiting for a metaphysics.

There is one more clause, and it is the pluralist one. SPLectrum is one voice among many, and other languages are free to define more. A philosophy can make emergence ontological on principle — [David Chalmers](/positioning/persons/c/chalmers/)'s psychophysical laws have exactly this shape, and his apparatus is built, deliberately and skilfully, to be immune to the growth of knowledge. Read from outside, such a definition is an *axiomatic claim*: it sets a primitive of that language rather than reporting a finding the language could have made. That is not a demotion — every language runs on axioms, this one included. It is a description of the claim's status, and it is closer to how the strongest such positions present themselves than to how their critics do. What follows from it is only this: an axiom binds inside its language, and travels as a proposal, not a discovery.

So the position, stated plainly. Novelty is real, and it arrives as a new context on top of an existing one — say what layered on what, and within which language you are speaking. Where your context cannot state the relation, file it under unknown and keep working; the file has a good record. And where a discussion of emergence is running in no well-defined context at all, it is a discussion about the word — the reference library carries [what the word's many communities each mean by it](/positioning/subjects/e/emergence/), and that is the better place to start.
