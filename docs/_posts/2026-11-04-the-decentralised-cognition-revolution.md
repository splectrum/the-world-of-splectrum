In "[A Revolution in Cognition?](/blog/2026/10/a-revolution-in-cognition/)" I said the revolution worth having is to understand cognition from the ground up, in AI and equally in life. The part I keep coming back to is a turn of attention from how powerful to how related, which is to say from the centralised to the decentralised. Power gathers at a centre; relating happens everywhere at once. Decentralisation here is not a way of redistributing power. It is how living things already relate.

Our attention goes to the centralised: an individual with a brain, thinking, and now the scaled-up image of that, a single mind larger than any of ours, placed outside us and imagined ruling the world. That image is the cocoon at its most extreme, the centred reasoner made bigger and put at a distance. It is a natural place for attention to settle, because it is how we experience ourselves from inside. Three faces of the turn away from it: the species has no seat, interaction has no referee, association has no controller.

**From the individual to the species.** The cognitive power that changed the world was unleashed not by any individual member of it but by what gets passed on and built on: language, tools, ways of doing things that outlive the one who found them. One way of seeing this is that the individual is a node the species' thinking passes through.

The brain tells the same story from inside. There is no room in the head where the thinking happens: many processing centres, each taking input from somewhere and producing output used elsewhere, the routing not fixed. Hubs, but no seat. The unity is what the running produces.

**From cooperation to interaction.** The related life is not only the cooperative one. Competition, predation, parasitism and symbiosis are all ways of being related, and none of us functions without them: the microbiome is a population of other organisms we depend on and contend with, the immune system a lifelong negotiation with what arrives. Competence keeps turning up in the interaction between participants rather than in any participant, and the interaction is as often friction as help.

**From the rational to the associative.** The paradigm shift in AI, to my eye, is not that it reasons but that it associates: it weights what goes with what, and whatever looks like reasoning grows from that rather than being installed above it. [Hume](/positioning/persons/h/hume/) gives this a historical frame: the association of ideas as the binding force of thought, and reason as the slave of the passions. Read this way, the rational is not the opposite of the associative but something that shows up out of it, in us as in the machine.

What AI can do is wake us up. Watching an associative process do things we had filed under reasoning is a shock, and a shock is a chance to look again at what was already going on around us: the species thinking through its members, life running on interaction, the brain with no seat. None of that needed a machine to be true; the machine may make it hard to leave alone. Not guaranteed, but a chance.

It matters because cognition steers collective agency. What we can do together depends on how we make sense together, so a turn in how we see cognition is a turn in what we can do; that is what gives the word revolution its weight here, a change in what we can do together rather than in who holds power. And it runs both ways, since what we do reshapes how we think. So the approach to integrating AI matters. Integrate it as power and it reshapes thinking toward power; integrate it as relating and it reshapes thinking toward relating.

So the question changes. It stops being how to build the most powerful mind and becomes how to integrate the cognition already around us, and now the artificial kind, as a participant in that web of relations rather than a new centre. One way of seeing it: in the spirit of [assembly theory](/positioning/subjects/a/assembly-theory/) and [Sara Walker](/positioning/persons/w/walker/), technology can be read as an extension of life, living or not, which puts the machine among the things we are related to.

Decentralised belonging has no centre that grants membership; it is made at every point of relating. That is the word I would use for the relation, in its graded sense: a way of relating, not a membership, which is why it comes in degrees and holds friction as well as collaboration. A partnership that did not would not be one.

Which is a strange way round for a technological revolution to work: the artificial drawing us back to look properly at the living. It is the part I find most worth being hopeful about.
