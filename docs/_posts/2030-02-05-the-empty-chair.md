Shannon's theory of information measures correlation and says nothing about whether a correlation is *about* anything or matters to anyone. A thermostat and a thermometer both carry information about a room; nothing in the mathematics distinguishes the one that would be in trouble if it stopped. That gap is deliberate — Shannon was solving a problem about telephone lines — but it has bothered people ever since, because the thing left out is the thing most of us mean by information.

In 2018 Artemy Kolchinsky and David Wolpert tried to close it, and their attempt is a good place to watch a hard problem misbehave.

## The attempt

They set themselves a condition, and the condition is what makes the story worth telling. The definition had to be **intrinsic** — grounded in the system's own dynamics rather than in a goal supplied from outside. They were explicit about why the alternatives fail it. Utility-function approaches specify what matters exogenously, which makes the meaning "for the external scientist who imputes goals to the system, rather than for the system itself." Teleosemantics grounds meaning in a selection history rather than in what the system is doing now. They wanted something that would apply to a rock, a hurricane and a cell alike, without anybody deciding in advance what any of them was for.

The construction is elegant. Take a system coupled to an environment. Define a **viability function** measuring how tightly the system is concentrated in states that keep it in being. Now scramble the correlations between system and environment, destroying them selectively, and see how much viability is lost. Correlations whose destruction costs nothing were not doing anything. **Semantic information** is what remains: the correlations that are load-bearing for the system's continued existence.

Run it on their three examples and a gradient appears. A rock's correlations with its surroundings are worth nothing to its persistence. A hurricane's are worth little — it is a dissipative structure, not an agent. A food-caching bird's are worth a great deal. Agents come apart from passive structures without anyone having defined agency first, which is a real result.

And then the problem, which to their credit they are the ones to state.

A viability function needs someone *for whom* persistence matters. The slot cannot be empty: a measure of how well a system is doing requires a standard of doing well. The paper says who fills it:

> It is important to re-emphasize that, in our framework, the viability function is exogenously determined by the scientist analysing the system, rather than being a purely endogenous characteristic of the system.

Two further choices of the same kind are acknowledged — where to cut between system and environment, and over what timescale. All three, they write, "generally represent 'subjective' choices of the scientist."

They are not embarrassed, and they have an argument. Viability is less arbitrary than a scenario-specific utility function, because staying in existence is almost always a necessary if usually unstated condition for anything else a system might be said to be doing. That is fair, and it is a genuine difference from what they rejected. But it does not close the gap they opened: the definition was to be intrinsic, and the standard by which a system counts as doing well still arrives from outside. The paper treats this as a residue, to be scrubbed out by future work.

## The answer in the citation

Here is what makes this more than a technical shortfall.

The paper credits Carlo Rovelli for the idea it is built on — meaningful information as survival-relevant correlation — citing his essay on meaning and evolution and thanking him among the participants in a Santa Fe working group. The debt is in the text.

What is not taken up is Rovelli's other move, the one he is known for. In relational quantum mechanics the state of a system is not absolute but relative to whatever it is interacting with, and Rovelli's insistence is that this is **structure, not limitation**. You do not need frame-independence in order to be objective. The value is real; it is real *relative to* an interaction. He has since made the same move for entropy, and is careful to keep it clear of any claim about knowledge: "A cup of hot tea does not cool down because of what I know or do not know about its molecules." Objective, and relative to a physical coupling.

Set the two side by side. Kolchinsky has a quantity well defined relative to a chosen cut, and reads the relativity as a defect awaiting removal. Rovelli, cited two pages earlier, has spent a career arguing that relativity to an interaction is not a defect and never was.

The parallel to the history of physics is close enough to be uncomfortable. What the framework wants is a viability function read from nowhere — the equivalent of absolute space. What it has is one indexed to a cut. Special relativity did not recover absolute simultaneity by better bookkeeping. It dropped the demand, and the physics turned out to be entirely objective without a privileged frame.

## The unexploited citation

I want to be careful about what this observation is. It is not a refutation — the framework does what it says it does, and the residue is disclosed rather than buried, which is more than most frameworks manage. Nor is it a claim that the relational reading would be easy to carry through; nobody has tried, and the mathematics may resist in ways that only show up under the attempt. There is also no pile-on available: the critical literature on this framework is close to nonexistent, which says more about how few people have engaged with it than about its quality.

What it is, is a case of something I suspect is common enough to be worth naming. A thinker sets a constraint, notices they have violated it, and treats the violation as an engineering problem to be solved later — when the live option is that the constraint itself was the wrong ambition. The resources for seeing that were in the room. They were in the citation, two pages earlier.
