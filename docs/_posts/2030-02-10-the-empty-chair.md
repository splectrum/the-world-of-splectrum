In 2018 [Artemy Kolchinsky](/positioning/persons/k/kolchinsky/) and [David Wolpert](/positioning/persons/w/wolpert/) [set out to find the meaningful correlations between a system and its environment](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.08053). They set themselves one condition, and it makes the attempt worth watching: the answer had to be **intrinsic**, grounded in the system's own dynamics rather than in a goal supplied from outside. A utility function fails that test, because it makes the meaning, in their words, "for the external scientist who imputes goals to the system, rather than for the system itself." They wanted a definition that would work for a rock, a hurricane and a cell alike, with nobody deciding in advance what any of them was for. The observer's chair was to stay empty.

The construction is elegant. Take a system in an environment and define a **viability function**: a measure of how well the system stays in the states that keep it in existence. Then scramble the correlations between system and environment, selectively, and see how much viability is lost. If destroying a correlation costs nothing, it was not doing anything. What remains, the correlations the system's continued existence depends on, is **semantic information**. The paper's worked examples show a gradient: a rock's correlations are worth nothing to its persistence, a hurricane's a little, a food-caching bird's a great deal. Agents separate from passive structures without anyone having to define agency first. That is a real result.

Then comes the problem, and the authors state it themselves. A viability function needs a standard of what counts as doing well, and that standard has to come from somewhere. The paper says where:

> It is important to re-emphasize that, in our framework, the viability function is exogenously determined by the scientist analyzing the system, rather than being a purely endogenous characteristic of the system.

The same goes for where to draw the line between system and environment. The cut, the timescale and the starting distribution, they write, "generally represent 'subjective' choices of the scientist." They have a good argument for not being embarrassed by this: staying in existence comes before almost anything else a system might be said to do, so viability is far less arbitrary than a utility tailored to one situation. That is fair. But the definition was meant to be intrinsic, and the standard still comes from outside. Someone is sitting in the chair after all. The paper treats this as a leftover, to be removed by future work.

But the word "subjective" is doing two jobs here, and they are worth separating. The first is that we do not know the standard in advance. Nobody can read a system's standard off the system. The scientist proposes one, and the dynamics answer: the system holds those states or it does not, and scrambling costs viability or it does not. That is a fact about our position, and it is how every model works: you propose, and you are told. The paper's own examples already work this way. Nobody asked the bird.

The second is that once the line between system and environment is drawn, the result depends on that pairing. Which correlations hold between this system and that environment is a fact about how they interact, not about what anyone knows. That is [Rovelli](/positioning/persons/r/rovelli/)'s position, carried from [relational quantum mechanics](/positioning/subjects/r/relational-quantum-mechanics/) to [entropy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.01125), and he states it for exactly this situation: "A cup of hot tea does not cool down because of what I know or do not know about its molecules." The quantity is objective, and relative to an interaction rather than to a knower. So the framework is already relational at the point where the line is drawn. Run the two senses together and they look like a subjective choice that should be removed. Keep them apart and the not-knowing is ordinary science, the relativity is part of the structure, and neither is the defect the paper takes it to be.

The paper sets its founding idea, that meaningful information is correlation that matters for survival, beside Rovelli's [essay on meaning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.02420), and the two now sit side by side in the field. One treats dependence on where the line is drawn as a defect awaiting removal; the other treats it as the normal form of an objective quantity. Physics has been here before. Asking for a viability function that holds from nowhere is like asking for absolute space. Special relativity did not rescue absolute simultaneity with better bookkeeping; it dropped the demand, and the physics stayed objective without a privileged frame.

The paper is an example of a more general pattern. A constraint is set, the construction breaks it, and the breach is filed as an engineering problem for later, when the real possibility is that the constraint asked for the wrong thing. Intrinsic did not have to mean independent of any frame, and it did not have to mean known without a scientist. The chair was never empty, and it is not the scientist who fills it: the coupling does. What the scientist does is say what it holds, and stand to be corrected.
