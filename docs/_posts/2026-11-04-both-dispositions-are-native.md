Of everything in [Mark Moffett](/positioning/persons/m/moffett/)'s account of societies, the claim I keep returning to is the one that sounds least dramatic. He argues that human nature contains *both* the disposition to draw a boundary between us and them — which shows up early in development, before anyone teaches it — *and* the capacity to fold strangers into a shared identity. And that neither is the learned one.

That is a small sentence with a lot of machinery behind it, because almost every account of this pairing makes one of the two primary and the other derived. The pessimistic reading: tribalism is what we are, cosmopolitanism a thin achievement laid over it, maintained by effort and lost under stress. The optimistic reading, which is the same structure inverted: openness is our nature, tribalism a distortion produced by scarcity or manipulation or bad institutions, removable in principle. Both readings tell you which disposition is the real one. Both, therefore, tell you what the work is — suppress the deep thing, or remove what distorts the good thing.

Moffett declines the choice. On his account the boundary-drawing is native, the folding-in is native, and a society is what happens in the tension between them. That is not a compromise position or a both-sides hedge. It is a different *kind* of claim: a description of what is running, rather than an identification of which part is authentic.

I said in [the previous post](/blog/2026/10/the-biologist-who-studies-vocabularies/) that the interesting thing about Moffett is a methodological discipline — comparison held with the difference in view, definitions treated as marks rather than essences. This claim is that discipline applied to ourselves, which is the hardest place to apply it. When we describe our own dispositions, the pull toward saying which one is *really* us is nearly irresistible, because that judgement is where the moral payload sits. He declines to make it, and the description survives the declining.

What does the site do with this? The politics position here runs on coexistence — [not as an achievement but as the standing concern](/positioning/close-affinity/politics/), the whole range from deep common ground to the breakdown of living-together lying inside it, with the arrangement read off what the actors actually hold between them rather than measured against a standard issued from above. There is no seat above the actors from which to issue one.

Read from there, Moffett's refusal is the same refusal. Deciding which disposition is authentic is exactly the move that needs the seat above — a vantage from which one part of what a subject is counts as its nature and another part as interference. Without that seat, the question stops being answerable, and stops being interesting. What is left is what the two dispositions are doing between them: the boundary being drawn somewhere, the folding-in happening somewhere, the arrangement that results being the arrangement, not a fall from one ideal or an approach toward another.

And the tension is not a problem awaiting resolution. This is where the reading and the position agree most closely. A society that had only the boundary would be a permanent siege; one that had only the folding-in would have no members, since membership is a distinction or it is nothing — the ant that cannot tell nestmate from stranger has no colony. The two dispositions need each other to produce anything at all. Whatever balance obtains at a given moment establishes itself out of their working, and shifts, and establishes itself again. Nobody adjudicates it, because there is nobody positioned to.

Which leaves the practical residue, and it is smaller and less satisfying than a programme. If both dispositions are native, then neither the hope of curing tribalism nor the fear that openness is naive describes the situation. What is available is work on the conditions — what the boundary is currently drawn around, what makes folding-in possible here rather than there, which markers are doing the certifying and how much they cost to hold. That is the interesting question, and it is only reachable once the question of which disposition is *really* us has been set down.

Moffett gets there from ants and fieldwork, and stops at the description — he is careful, as ever, about what his framework does and does not settle. I get there from a position about how coexistence is read. The routes do not resemble each other. The place they arrive is recognisably the same.
