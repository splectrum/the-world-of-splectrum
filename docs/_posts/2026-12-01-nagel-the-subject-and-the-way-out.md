A recent interview with [Thomas Nagel](/positioning/persons/n/nagel/), given after he was named laureate of the Berggruen Prize for Philosophy & Culture, drew my attention back to a philosopher whose thinking has been influential in the development of SPLectrum's approach. The interview was about AI, but that is a conversation for another time. Here my attention was drawn back to a pillar of his main work: "how to combine the perspective of a particular person inside the world with an objective view of that same world, the person and his viewpoint included."

In 1974 he asked what it is like to be a bat. A bat finds its way by echolocation, there is something it is like to be that bat, and no account of its neurons or of sonar tells us what. His point was not privacy. "I am not adverting here to the alleged privacy of experience to its possessor," he wrote; the point of view in question "is a type." The barrier lies between kinds of experiencer. And the usual road to knowledge leads away from it: a shift to greater objectivity, "less attachment to a specific viewpoint", does not bring us nearer to experience; "it takes us farther away from it." Here SPLectrum joins him fully. Experience is had by a subject before it is anything else, and no description from outside stands in for it.

His objective is built with the same care. "Objectivity is a method of understanding." We step back from our view of something and form a new conception "which has that view and its relation to the world as its object", and the step can be repeated. It comes in degrees: the standpoint of morality is more objective than that of private life, and less objective than that of physics, so that reality is "a set of concentric spheres, progressively revealed as we detach gradually from the contingencies of the self." The capacity sits in each of us; he called it the objective self. Sharing has its place, but downstream: "where consensus is available, it arises from the convergence among different individuals, all reasoning to get at the truth." Consensus on its own is "the wrong kind of generality." And in *The Last Word* reason has an authority "not merely personal, or societal, but universal."

The stepping back is a form of abstract conceptualisation, and a real insight into how understanding grows. But it is done with concepts and with language, and those were shared, and had converged, before any subject stepped back with them. Nagel saw the objection coming in one form: "the truth of solipsism is not shown by the fact that the language in which I describe the world is my language." That answers *my* language. The question here is about *our* language. The tools belong to the sharing before they belong to the thinker, and a knowledge position can only travel once others hold the parts it is built from.

Take a thought that is correct and never shared. Is it objective? To call it correct, someone has to stand outside the scene and judge the thought against the world directly, without taking part. Nagel grants that "someone would still have to think them"; the question is who judges, and from where. A judge who takes no part is the view from nowhere, the standpoint Nagel himself says no one occupies.

Seen from SPLectrum, content that does not depend on who has it is content that has been shared and has converged, seen in hindsight. Convergence comes in widths: a conversation, a community, a culture, a science, and what gets called universal is the widest there is. That is as far as SPLectrum goes: converged subjectivity as reachable objectivity. It is not a free-for-all. Reality discloses itself through language, not everything converges, and a position has to be built, step by step, from parts already shared. What SPLectrum declines is a seat outside the sharing from which to name what holds it. So the explanation runs the other way round: for Nagel, objectivity explains convergence; here, objectivity is what convergence comes to.

Nagel walked a careful line inside analytic philosophy. Against physicalism he defended the subject; against the relativists and pragmatists, universal reason. Each defence kept the other credible. From outside that system the first half can be taken without the second. The subject he defended is the one SPLectrum starts from. Where we part is the way out of it: for him by detachment, for SPLectrum by ever widening circles of subjective convergence.
