# Martin Heidegger (1889–1976)

Heidegger asked the question of being: not what there is, but what it means *to be*. *Being and Time* (1927) approached it through the being that we ourselves are, which he called Dasein. Dasein is being-in-the-world: it is what it is through its dealings with things, with others and with its own projects, and it meets things first in use. Looking at them as detached objects comes later and depends on that involvement. With this analysis he contested the modern picture, set by Descartes, of a subject standing over against an objective world. The later work turned to being itself and its history, a sequence of epochs in which things show up differently, culminating in the technological age. In each epoch works of art and language take part in opening up the world, and language, in his phrase, is "the house of Being". Trained in [Husserl](/positioning/persons/h/husserl/)'s [phenomenology](/positioning/subjects/p/phenomenology/), he turned it to the question of being, and his work went on to shape existentialism, [hermeneutics](/positioning/subjects/h/hermeneutics/the-ontological-turn/) and [deconstruction](/positioning/subjects/d/deconstruction/). His support for National Socialism makes the relation between his thought and his politics what the *Stanford Encyclopedia* calls "the most controversial issue in contemporary discussions" of his work.

[Martin Heidegger](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heidegger/) (1889–1976). Born in Messkirch in south-west Germany, he studied Catholic theology, the natural sciences and mathematics at Freiburg before settling on philosophy, and took his habilitation in 1915 under the neo-Kantian Heinrich Rickert. He became Husserl's assistant in 1919 and dedicated *Being and Time* to him "in friendship and admiration". At Marburg (1923–28) his students included [Gadamer](/positioning/persons/g/gadamer/), Karl Löwith, [Jonas](/positioning/persons/j/jonas/) and [Arendt](/positioning/persons/a/arendt/). *Being and Time* appeared in 1927, and in 1928 he succeeded to Husserl's chair at Freiburg. In 1929 he gave his inaugural lecture there, "What Is Metaphysics?", which [Carnap](/positioning/persons/c/carnap/) made the test case of his attack on metaphysics, and he met [Cassirer](/positioning/persons/c/cassirer/) in the Davos disputation. Elected rector in April 1933, he joined the Nazi Party on 1 May and resigned the rectorship in April 1934 (see below). After the war the French military government barred him from teaching until 1949; his later work appeared mostly as lectures and essays. He died in Freiburg in 1976.

## Key concepts

**Dasein.** Heidegger's term for the kind of being that we ourselves are — the being for whom its own being is an issue. Dasein is not a subject contemplating objects. It is being-in-the-world: always already involved, always already understanding, always already ahead of itself. Its being as a whole is care (*Sorge*): "Dasein's Being reveals itself as care" (*Being and Time*, §39), the structure of being "ahead-of-itself", "already-in" a world and "alongside" what it meets (§41).

**Being-in-the-world (*In-der-Welt-sein*).** The world is not a container that Dasein is placed into. It is the structure of meaningful relations that Dasein inhabits. Tools, others, projects, moods — these are not additions to a bare existence but constitutive of what it means to be.

**Readiness-to-hand and presence-at-hand (*Zuhandenheit / Vorhandenheit*).** Things are first encountered as equipment — ready-to-hand, used without being noticed. "The less we just stare at the hammer-Thing, and the more we seize hold of it and use it, the more primordial does our relationship to it become" (*Being and Time*, §15). Only when something breaks or goes missing does it become present-at-hand — an object of detached observation. The theoretical attitude is derived from the practical, not the other way around.

**Being-with (*Mitsein*).** Dasein's being is being-with: "the world is always the one that I share with Others. The world of Dasein is a with-world [*Mitwelt*]". Others are not added to a subject that was first alone: "an isolated 'I' without Others is just as far from being proximally given" as a bare subject without a world (*Being and Time*, §§25–26). In everyday being-with, Dasein is mostly absorbed in the "they" (*das Man*): one does what one does, as anyone does. Authentic selfhood is not an escape from the "they" but a modification of it.

**Disclosure (*Erschlossenheit*).** The world is not discovered by a subject — it is disclosed through Dasein's involvement. In *Being and Time*'s words, "Dasein is its disclosedness" (§28): as being-in-the-world it is "cleared in itself, not through any other entity, but in such a way that it is itself the clearing". Understanding, mood, and discourse together open up the world as a field of meaning. Truth, for Heidegger, is in the first place unconcealment (*aletheia*). He does not deny that an assertion is true by agreeing with how things are; it can agree only because entities have already been uncovered and their being disclosed, so propositional truth is one kind of unconcealment and rests on it. Ernst Tugendhat objected that this loses what makes truth truth, its opposition to falsehood, and Heidegger later granted that it was "misleading" to call unconcealment "truth" ("The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking").

**Thrownness (*Geworfenheit*).** Dasein does not choose its starting point. It finds itself already in a situation — a historical moment, a culture, a mood — that it did not create but must take up. Freedom is not the absence of constraint but the taking-over of what is given. Thrownness and projection belong together: "as thrown, Dasein is thrown into the kind of Being which we call 'projecting'" (§31).

**Temporality.** The meaning of Dasein's being is time. Not clock time but the ecstatic unity of past (having-been), present (being-alongside), and future (being-ahead-of-itself) — held together in each moment. Being-toward-death gives Dasein its finitude and its urgency: it is the horizon against which everything matters. Historicity — Dasein's capacity to take up its own past and project toward its future — follows from this temporal structure.

**The turn (*die Kehre*).** After *Being and Time*, Heidegger's thinking shifted. Early Heidegger approached Being through the analysis of Dasein. Later Heidegger turned toward Being itself — its history, its withdrawal, the way it discloses and conceals itself across epochs. The subject recedes; language, poetry, and technology become the sites where Being shows itself. The sharpness of the turn is disputed: scholars "disagree about the precise timing, nature, extent, and even existence of this turn", though talk of an early and a late Heidegger "remains fairly standard" (Lee Braver, as the *Stanford Encyclopedia* quotes him). [Rorty](/positioning/persons/r/rorty/), who drew on him alongside [Wittgenstein](/positioning/persons/w/wittgenstein/) and [Dewey](/positioning/persons/d/dewey/), charged that the later talk of a history of being is vacuous, because Heidegger does not validate it through references to ordinary historical events (*Consequences of Pragmatism*, 1982, as the *Stanford Encyclopedia* reports it). Mark Okrent, as the same entry quotes him, answers that the history of being is "correlated with ordinary history" but "not … simply a function of" it.

**Language.** In *Being and Time* language is founded on a prior, non-linguistic articulation of meaning, to which words "accrue". Beside one such passage Heidegger later wrote in his own copy "Untrue. Language is not another story raised on top", and by 1932 he held that "where there is no language, there is no understanding of being" (as the *Stanford Encyclopedia* quotes him). In the *Letter on Humanism* (1947) he wrote: "Language is the house of Being". In the later work, language is not a tool humans use — it is where Being comes to light. "Language speaks" (*Die Sprache spricht*): we do not master language; we dwell in it. Poetry, for Heidegger, is language at its most disclosive — the place where what is concealed comes to word.

**The age of the world picture.** In "The Age of the World Picture" (1938) man becomes *subiectum* with Descartes: "Man becomes the relational center of that which is as such", and the world picture is "not … a picture of the world but the world conceived and grasped as picture". An appendix ties this to an end: "With Descartes begins the completion and consummation of Western metaphysics … Through anthropology the transition of metaphysics into the event of the simple stopping and setting aside of all philosophy is introduced."

**Technology (*Gestell*).** In "The Question Concerning Technology" (lecture 1953, published 1954) technology is not first of all an instrument: "Technology is therefore no mere means. Technology is a way of revealing." Modern technology reveals everything as "standing-reserve" (*Bestand*), on call for further ordering, and its essence is *Gestell*, Enframing: "that way of revealing which holds sway in the essence of modern technology and which is itself nothing technological". In the history of being the technological age is the epoch in which that history culminates.

**The end of philosophy.** In "The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking" (1964) Heidegger reads the end of philosophy as its completion in the sciences. "Philosophy is metaphysics", and its end "means the completion of metaphysics. However, completion does not mean perfection": the old meaning of "end" is place, the gathering of philosophy's uttermost possibilities. The word translated "completion" is *Vollendung*, which, as David Farrell Krell notes in his edition of the essay, stresses the full rather than the ending. The sciences' independence "looks like the mere dissolution of philosophy, yet in truth is precisely its completion", and they will be regulated, he expected, by cybernetics. What is left is a task for thinking, which "is less than philosophy". The other announced ends are on [The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-end-of-philosophy/).

**What philosophy is.** His lecture "Was ist das – die Philosophie?" (Cerisy-la-Salle, 1955) confined itself, as Dominique Janicaud reports, to the Greek origin of philosophy, starting from "The Greek word philosophia indicates the direction". The *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy* sums up his later metaphilosophy as holding "that: philosophy is metaphysics; metaphysics involves a fundamental mistake; metaphysics is complicit in modernity's ills; metaphysics is entering into its end; and 'thinking' should replace metaphysics/philosophy". His early method, as the IEP quotes it from *The Basic Problems of Phenomenology* (1927), was "a destruction … in which the traditional concepts … are deconstructed down to the … sources from which they were drawn"; the later thinking is, in the IEP's words, "a kind of thoughtful questioning". In the Nietzsche lectures he had said that "All philosophical thinking" is "in itself poetic". What thinking promises is "a new ground and a new foundation upon which we can stand and endure in the world of technology without being imperiled by it" (*Discourse on Thinking*).

## The Nazi period and the *Black Notebooks*

As rector Heidegger was responsible for bringing the university into line with the aims of the party (*Gleichschaltung*). He resigned the office in April 1934 but remained a party member until the end of the regime. After the war, the *Stanford Encyclopedia* reports, he "repeatedly lied or distorted the record" of his political activity, and he "never publicly and explicitly renounced his support for National Socialism". The private notebooks he kept from 1931, the *Black Notebooks*, began to appear in his collected works in 2014 and documented his antisemitism. Their significance for the philosophy is disputed across a wide range. At one end Julian Young (1997) holds that the philosophy does not "stand in any essential connection to Nazism". At the other Emmanuel Faye (2005) holds that it is "impossible to dissociate" the work from his political commitments, and that it is "not at all a 'philosophy'" (both as the *Stanford Encyclopedia* quotes them). Many tie the engagement to his critique of technology: [Habermas](/positioning/persons/h/habermas/) holds that in 1935 Heidegger "still saw the inner truth and greatness of the National Socialist movement in the 'encounter between global technology and modern man'" (as the *Stanford Encyclopedia* quotes him). [Marcuse](/positioning/persons/m/marcuse/), who had come to Freiburg to study with him, parted ways with him in 1932 over his developing interest in National Socialism, a year before he joined the party; [Levinas](/positioning/persons/l/levinas/)'s break is told on his page.

## Key works

- [*Being and Time*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_and_Time) (1927) — the analytic of Dasein, being-in-the-world, temporality
- ["What Is Metaphysics?"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Is_Metaphysics%3F) (1929) — the Freiburg inaugural lecture: anxiety and the nothing
- [*Kant and the Problem of Metaphysics*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant_and_the_Problem_of_Metaphysics) (1929) — his reading of Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason*
- [*The Origin of the Work of Art*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Origin_of_the_Work_of_Art) (1935/36) — truth as unconcealment, the work of art as world-disclosing
- [*Contributions to Philosophy*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contributions_to_Philosophy) (written 1936–38, published 1989) — the history of being and *Ereignis*
- ["The Age of the World Picture"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Age_of_the_World_Picture) (1938) — the world as picture, man as *subiectum*
- [*Letter on Humanism*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_on_Humanism) (1947) — language as the house of Being, against Sartre's existentialism
- ["The Question Concerning Technology"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Question_Concerning_Technology) (1954) — technology as a mode of revealing, the danger of *Gestell*
- *On the Way to Language* (1959) — "language speaks": the essays on the essence of language
- "The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking" (1964; in *Zur Sache des Denkens*, 1969) — philosophy as metaphysics, its end as completion in the sciences, thinking as what remains
