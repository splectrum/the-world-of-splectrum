# A Finite Task?

An end presupposes a task that can be finished. Whether philosophy has one is a question of its own in metaphilosophy, which asks both "Is philosophy historical in some special or deep way?" and "Is philosophy, in some sense, over – or should it be?" (IEP).

- **One philosophy, developing.** [Hegel](/positioning/persons/h/hegel/) states the difficulty: philosophy "aims at understanding what is unchangeable, eternal", yet it has a history. His answer is that the succession of philosophies is the development of one philosophy, the latest "the most developed, richest and deepest" (*Lectures on the History of Philosophy*, tr. Haldane).
- **Questions handed over, and questions kept.** [Russell](/positioning/persons/r/russell/) (1912) has philosophy hand over to the sciences whatever becomes answerable (see [Philosophy and the special sciences](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/philosophy-and-the-special-sciences/)), while some questions stay open, studied "for the sake of the questions themselves".
- **No eternal problems.** [Collingwood](/positioning/persons/c/collingwood/) (1940): "there are no 'eternal' or 'crucial' or 'central' problems in metaphysics", and those who believe in them "are bad historians".
- **Got over, not solved.** [Dewey](/positioning/persons/d/dewey/) (1909), after Darwin: old questions are not answered but abandoned, "We do not solve them: we get over them."
- **Rationality with a history.** [MacIntyre](/positioning/persons/m/macintyre/) (1988): rationality "is a concept with a history", and there are "rationalities rather than rationality", traditions that progress or fail by their own lights.
- **No criterion outside vocabularies.** [Rorty](/positioning/persons/r/rorty/) (1989): "there is nothing beyond vocabularies which serves as a criterion of choice between them".
