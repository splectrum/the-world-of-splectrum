# The Twentieth Century

The *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy* sorts the Western metaphilosophies of the last hundred years into three streams: analytic; continental; and pragmatist, neopragmatist and post-analytic. It does so "solely for organizational purposes", in an account "organized by chronology as much as by theme". This page takes the pragmatists second. Each stream gave its own answers to the three questions: what philosophy is, what its point is, and how it should be done.

---

## Analytic

**What it is.** The pioneers shared the view, in [Russell](/positioning/persons/r/russell/)'s words of 1900, that "all sound philosophy should begin with an analysis of propositions"; his 1914 lecture is titled "Logic as the Essence of Philosophy". For the early [Wittgenstein](/positioning/persons/w/wittgenstein/), "Alle Philosophie ist ‚Sprachkritik'", all philosophy is critique of language (*Tractatus* 4.0031). It "is not one of the natural sciences"; the word "must mean something which stands above or below, but not beside the natural sciences" (4.111). "Die Philosophie ist keine Lehre, sondern eine Tätigkeit": "not a theory but an activity" (4.112, tr. Ogden).

The later Wittgenstein kept philosophy as a critique of language; in the IEP's summary it became "a kind of therapy for what normally passes for philosophy". 

In the second half of the century [P. F. Strawson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._F._Strawson)'s descriptive metaphysics "is content to describe the actual structure of our thought about the world", where revisionary metaphysics aims "to produce a better structure", and for [Quine](/positioning/persons/q/quine/) "it is only within science itself, and not in some prior philosophy, that reality is to be identified and described". Metaphilosophical naturalism, in the IEP's words, "asserts a strong continuity between philosophy and science".

**Its point.** "Der Zweck der Philosophie ist die logische Klärung der Gedanken", "The object of philosophy is the logical clarification of thoughts" (*Tractatus* 4.112). The Vienna Circle's manifesto (1929): "The scientific world-conception serves life, and life receives it. The task of philosophical work lies in … clarification of problems and assertions, not in the propounding of special 'philosophical' pronouncements". The later Wittgenstein: "the clarity that we are aiming at is indeed complete clarity. But this simply means that the philosophical problems should completely disappear. The real discovery is the one that makes me capable of stopping doing philosophy when I want to.—The one that gives philosophy peace" (*Philosophical Investigations* §133, tr. Anscombe). The practical ethicists of the 1960s and 1970s insisted, in the IEP's words, that philosophy "should treat 'real problems'".

**How it is done.** By logical analysis. "The right method of philosophy would be this: To say nothing except what can be said, i.e. the propositions of natural science … and then always, when someone else wished to say something metaphysical, to demonstrate to him that he had given no meaning to certain signs in his propositions" (*Tractatus* 6.53). [Carnap](/positioning/persons/c/carnap/): "Philosophy is to be replaced by the logic of science—that is to say, by the logical analysis of the concepts and sentences of the sciences" (*The Logical Syntax of Language*, 1934; English 1937, Foreword).

The later Wittgenstein turned to description: "We must do away with all explanation, and description alone must take its place … Philosophy is a battle against the bewitchment of our intelligence by means of language" (§109); "The work of the philosopher consists in assembling reminders for a particular purpose" (§127); "There is not a philosophical method, though there are indeed methods, like different therapies" (§133). For [J. L. Austin](/positioning/persons/a/austin-jl/) ordinary language is "the first word", not "the last word". [Rawls](/positioning/persons/r/rawls/)'s reflective equilibrium moves "back and forth between the initial judgments and the principles", and the experimental philosophers, in the IEP's words, "themselves do science", testing the intuitions armchair philosophy relies on. Russell's division of labour with the sciences is on [The residue and the specialisms](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-residue-and-the-specialisms/).

## Pragmatist and post-analytic

**What it is.** The classical pragmatists, [Peirce](/positioning/persons/p/peirce/), [James](/positioning/persons/j/james/) and [Dewey](/positioning/persons/d/dewey/), each held, in the IEP's words, "that philosophy is not fundamentally different to other inquiries", with experimental science as the exemplar. James: "The history of philosophy is to a great extent that of a certain clash of human temperaments" (*Pragmatism*, 1907, Lecture I). [Rorty](/positioning/persons/r/rorty/) rejected the view of philosophy as "a tribunal of pure reason, upholding or denying the claims of the rest of culture" (*Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature*, 1979) and took up [Wilfrid Sellars](/positioning/persons/s/sellars/)'s definition, "an attempt to see how things, in the broadest possible sense of the term, hang together". Post-analytic philosophy, in the IEP's summary, favours "a broad construal of the philosophical enterprise".

**Its point.** James: "The whole function of philosophy ought to be to find out what definite difference it will make to you and me, at definite instants of our life, if this world-formula or that world-formula be the true one" (Lecture II). Dewey: "the task of future philosophy is to clarify men's ideas as to the social and moral strifes of their own day. Its aim is to become so far as is humanly possible an organ for dealing with these conflicts" (*Reconstruction in Philosophy*, 1920), and "Philosophy recovers itself when it ceases to be a device for dealing with the problems of philosophers and becomes a method, cultivated by philosophers, for dealing with the problems of men" (1917). For Rorty the philosopher is not "a 'cultural overseer'" but "an 'informed dilettante'", the aim "edification", finding "new, better, more interesting, more fruitful ways of speaking"; in politics, "putting politics first and tailoring a philosophy to suit". Post-analytic philosophy holds, in the IEP's words, "that Analytic philosophy needs to reconnect itself with the rest of culture".

**How it is done.** James: "The pragmatic method is primarily a method of settling metaphysical disputes that otherwise might be interminable", "THE ATTITUDE OF LOOKING AWAY FROM FIRST THINGS, PRINCIPLES, 'CATEGORIES,' SUPPOSED NECESSITIES; AND OF LOOKING TOWARDS LAST THINGS, FRUITS, CONSEQUENCES, FACTS" (capitals his). Dewey, after Peirce, extended "the 'laboratory habit of mind'" into every field of inquiry. Rorty's hermeneutics: "we play back and forth between guesses about how to characterize particular statements … and guesses about the point of the whole situation, until gradually we feel at ease with what was hitherto strange". Post-analytic philosophy attempts, in the IEP's words, "less to solve and more to dissolve or even discard philosophical problems". Dewey's and Rorty's view of philosophy's task is on [A finite task?](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/a-finite-task/).

## Continental

**What it is.** [Husserl](/positioning/persons/h/husserl/) opens "Philosophy as Rigorous Science" (1911): "From its earliest beginnings philosophy has claimed to be rigorous science", and closes: "Philosophy, however, is essentially a science of true beginnings, or origins" (tr. Lauer). [Heidegger](/positioning/persons/h/heidegger/)'s lecture "Was ist das – die Philosophie?" (Cerisy-la-Salle, 1955) confined itself to the Greek origin, starting from "The Greek word philosophia indicates the direction". His later metaphilosophy, in the IEP's summary, holds "that: philosophy is metaphysics; metaphysics involves a fundamental mistake; metaphysics is complicit in modernity's ills; metaphysics is entering into its end; and 'thinking' should replace metaphysics/philosophy". Critical theory "conceives itself as philosophy's inheritor"; [Habermas](/positioning/persons/h/habermas/)'s "postmetaphysical thinking" rejects "a normative privilege for philosophy". For [Derrida](/positioning/persons/d/derrida/), in the IEP's gloss, "in philosophy the nature of philosophy is always and everywhere at issue".

**Its point.** For Husserl philosophy claims to be the science that "renders possible from an ethico-religious point of view a life regulated by pure rational norms"; in the *Crisis* the loss of that aim belongs to "a 'crisis of European humanity'". For the existentialists, in the IEP's summary, "philosophy must be practical"; the later [Sartre](/positioning/persons/s/sartre/): "Every philosophy is practical … [Every philosophy is] a social and political weapon".

[Max Horkheimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Horkheimer)'s critical theory ("Traditionelle und kritische Theorie", 1937) is "auf diese Emanzipation gerichtet", directed at emancipation, and has "die Veränderung des Ganzen zum Ziele", the transformation of the whole, as its aim; "An der Existenz des kritischen Verhaltens … hängt heute die Zukunft der Humanität", the future of humanity depends today on the critical attitude. For Habermas morality and ethics are matters "for the discourse between citizens", not for the philosopher. Heidegger's thinking promises "a new ground and a new foundation upon which we can stand and endure in the world of technology without being imperiled by it" (*Discourse on Thinking*). From the 1980s Derrida held that "deconstruction is justice".

**How it is done.** Husserl's epochē brackets "the natural attitude", and "The impulse to research must proceed not from philosophies but from things and from the problems connected with them". His successors broke with his method: for [Merleau-Ponty](/positioning/persons/m/merleau-ponty/) "The most important lesson of the reduction is the impossibility of a complete reduction"; Heidegger calls for "a destruction" in which the traditional concepts are taken down to their sources; for [Gadamer](/positioning/persons/g/gadamer/) "The very idea of a definitive interpretation … seems to be intrinsically contradictory". Critical theory is interdisciplinary, reading philosophies as "unknowing reflections of social conditions"; Habermas makes the "communicative turn". The later Heidegger's thinking is "a kind of thoughtful questioning", and "All philosophical thinking" is "in itself poetic". Derrida: "Deconstruction is not a method and cannot be transformed into one" ("Letter to a Japanese Friend", 1983). Heidegger's and Derrida's ends of philosophy are on [The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-end-of-philosophy/).

## The sorting

The IEP calls the later Wittgenstein's placing "particularly moot", notes that some deny analytic philosophy, and some continental philosophy, "any substantial existence", and leaves out traditionalist, feminist and environmental philosophy. It names other possible sorts: by the aim a metaphilosophy gives philosophy; by what it takes as its model ("Science? Art? Therapy? Something else?"); by whether it holds philosophy to be essentially linguistic; or by its stance on metaphysics.

## In one view

| | What it is | Its point | How it is done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analytic | "not a theory but an activity", "critique of language"; continuous with science | "the logical clarification of thoughts"; "peace" | logical analysis; "the logic of science"; "assembling reminders" |
| Pragmatist and post-analytic | not different in kind from other inquiry; how things "hang together" | "what definite difference it will make"; "the problems of men"; edification | the pragmatic method; the "laboratory habit of mind"; dissolving problems |
| Continental | "rigorous science", "true beginnings"; metaphysics, to give way to thinking | "a life regulated by pure rational norms"; emancipation | epochē; destruction; hermeneutics; deconstruction, "not a method" |
