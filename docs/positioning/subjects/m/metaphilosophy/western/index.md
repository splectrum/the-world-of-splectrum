# Western Philosophy

The Western line runs from the Latin Middle Ages to the present. It inherits from [ancient philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/ancient/), as does [philosophy in the Islamic world](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/islamic-world/), which passed much of Aristotle back to the Latin West.

Its claim to the name has been questioned from within. In 2016 [Jay Garfield](/positioning/persons/g/garfield/) and Bryan Van Norden proposed in *The New York Times* ("If Philosophy Won't Diversify, Let's Call It What It Really Is") that "any department that regularly offers courses only on Western philosophy should rename itself 'Department of European and American Philosophy'".

The pages:

- [Medieval philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/medieval/) — from Clement and Augustine to the Renaissance: faith and reason (philosophy one with true religion, its servant, or the best life; Boethius, Eriugena, Aquinas, Abelard, the Paris arts masters and 1277), and the way of life again (Petrarch, Erasmus, Montaigne).
- [Early modern philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/early-modern/) — the turn towards the knower, from the whole system and the under-labourer (Descartes, Bacon, Locke) through reason and the philosophe (Wolff, the *Encyclopédie*, Hume) to Kant's critique of reason.
- [The hinge](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-hinge/) — the nineteenth century: Hegel's science of its time and Comte's positive philosophy; "scientist", the identity crisis, Windelband, Dilthey and the neo-Kantians; Ortega against "the terrorism of the laboratories"; the canon rewritten "from the Greeks to Kant" (Park) and the word sent abroad (*tetsugaku*, *zhexue*, *darśana*).
- [The twentieth century](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/twentieth-century/) — the analytic stream (Russell, Wittgenstein, Carnap, Quine), the pragmatist and post-analytic (James, Dewey, Rorty) and the continental (Husserl, Heidegger, critical theory, Habermas, Derrida), each against the three questions, and how the streams are sorted.
- [The residue and the specialisms](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-residue-and-the-specialisms/) — Russell's residue view, the sciences leaving, the philosophies of the special sciences, specialisation in the university, and the answers that refuse the residue (Husserl, Ortega, Deleuze and Guattari).
- [The name](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-name/) — the word "metaphilosophy": Lazerowitz's coinage (1940), the dispute over the "meta" (Wittgenstein, Williamson, Rescher, the French "after"), metaphilosophy as a field of its own, and the other names it goes by.

## Claims of an end

Since the nineteenth century, when philosophy came to define itself against the sciences ([The hinge](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-hinge/)), there have been claims that it has ended or should end, and questions over whether its task could ever be finished.

- [The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-end-of-philosophy/) — claims that philosophy has ended or should end, from Hegel and Comte to Heidegger and Rorty, the word each uses, what each declares over and what carries on.
- [The scientists' verdict](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-scientists-verdict/) — Hawking's "philosophy is dead", Weinberg, Krauss and Tyson, and the replies from Maudlin, Pigliucci, Rovelli, the reopened foundations of quantum mechanics, and Nietzsche a century earlier.
- [Why then](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/why-then/) — conditions named in the literature around the end claims: Laplace, philosophy's lost role after Hegel, the world picture and the turn to language.
- [A finite task?](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/a-finite-task/) — whether philosophy has a task that could be finished: Hegel, Russell, Collingwood, Dewey, MacIntyre and Rorty.
