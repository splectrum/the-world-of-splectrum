# The Hinge

After Kant, philosophy's place among the kinds of knowledge changed. [Hegel](/positioning/persons/h/hegel/) made it the science that comprehends its own time; [Comte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Comte) made it a specialism in the generalities of the sciences. The natural philosophers took a name of their own, and philosophy had to define itself against the sciences. In the same decades the history of philosophy was rewritten as a line from the Greeks to Kant, and the word went abroad as the name of a European discipline. Each stage gave its own answers to the three questions: what philosophy is, what its point is, and how it should be done.

---

## Hegel

**What it is.** Philosophy as science. Its aim, in Hegel's words, was to "lay aside the name of love of knowledge and be actual knowledge" (see [The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-end-of-philosophy/)). "To apprehend what is is the task of philosophy, because what is is reason … philosophy also is its time apprehended in thoughts" (*Philosophy of Right*, Preface, tr. Dyde).

**Its point.** Not to prescribe: "Philosophy cannot teach the state what it should be". To recognise "reason as the rose in the cross of the present … implies reconciliation with reality. This reconciliation philosophy grants". In the *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy*'s gloss, the point is "to enable people to recognize the embodiment of their ideals in their social and political lives and thereby to be at home in the world". [Marx](/positioning/persons/m/marx/)'s eleventh thesis on Feuerbach (1845): the philosophers have only interpreted the world; the point is to change it.

**How it is done.** By dialectic. "Logical doctrine has three sides: [a] the Abstract side, or that of understanding; [b] the Dialectical, or that of negative reason; [c] the Speculative, or that of positive reason", stages or "moments" in every logical entity (*Encyclopaedia Logic* §79); in the dialectical stage "these finite characterisations or formulae supersede themselves, and pass into their opposites" (§81).

## Comte

**What it is.** In Comte's *Course of Positive Philosophy* (1830–42), knowledge passes through "three different theoretical conditions", and the mind "employs in its progress three methods of philosophizing … Hence arise three philosophies, or general systems of conceptions on the aggregate of phenomena": the theological, the metaphysical and the positive (tr. Martineau, "freely translated and condensed").

**Its point and how it is done.** The positive philosophy needs a new kind of worker: "Let us have a new class of students … whose business it shall be to take the respective sciences as they are, determine the spirit of each, ascertain their relations and mutual connection, and reduce their respective principles to the smallest number of general principles". That means "constituting one more speciality from the study of scientific generalities".

---

## Philosophy and the sciences

**What it is.** The natural philosophers took a name of their own. [William Whewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Whewell) proposed "scientist" in 1833, and it was first printed in 1834. After the collapse of German idealism, philosophy faced what [Herbert Schnädelbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Schn%C3%A4delbach) calls an identity crisis (see [Why then](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/why-then/)). [Wilhelm Windelband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Windelband) took the question as his title, "Was ist Philosophie?" (*Präludien*, 1884), and answered that philosophy in the systematic sense is "die kritische Wissenschaft von den allgemeingiltigen Werthen", the critical science of universally valid values: the values name its objects, "critical" its method. [Dilthey](/positioning/persons/d/dilthey/) found across its history a small number of recurring worldviews.

[José Ortega y Gasset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Ortega_y_Gasset) described what philosophy had become and argued against it (*¿Qué es filosofía?*, 1929, Lesson II). For nearly a century Europe had suffered "el «terrorismo de los laboratorios»", the terrorism of the laboratories. The philosopher "se avergonzó de no ser físico", was ashamed of not being a physicist, reduced philosophy to a minimum, "poniéndola humildemente al servicio de la física", putting it humbly at the service of physics, and decided "que filosofía era sólo teoría del conocimiento", that philosophy was only theory of knowledge, [Kant](/positioning/persons/k/kant/) being the first to take this stance radically. "La filosofía había quedado reducida, o poco menos, a la teoría del conocimiento. Así se titulaban la mayor parte de los libros filosóficos publicados entre 1860 y 1920": philosophy had been reduced, or nearly so, to theory of knowledge, the title of most philosophical books published between 1860 and 1920. The historian [Pierre Hadot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Hadot) draws the line through to the present: "University philosophy therefore remains in the same position it occupied in the Middle Ages: it is still a servant, sometimes of theology, sometimes of science."

**How it is done.** For the Marburg neo-Kantians "philosophy begins with a fact — paradigmatically, the fact of science — and investigates the conditions that make that fact possible"; philosophy of science "is the logic of the sciences". Dilthey gave the human sciences a method of their own, understanding (*Verstehen*) against the natural sciences' explanation (*Erklären*).

The twentieth-century sequel, the residue view and the specialisms, is on [The residue and the specialisms](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-residue-and-the-specialisms/).

## The canon and the word abroad

**What it is.** Peter K. J. Park dates to these decades the rewriting of the history of philosophy, in his publisher's summary, "as a march of progress from the Greeks to Kant"; the verdicts that went with it, Hegel's among them, are on the pages of the traditions they judged ([India](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/india/), [China](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/china/), [Africa](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/africa/), [Latin America](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/latin-america/)).

In the same century the word travelled as the name of a Western academic discipline. In Japan [Nishi Amane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi_Amane) coined *tetsugaku*, first published in 1874, so that Western philosophy would not be confused with Confucianism; *-gaku* was the suffix of the new university disciplines. Chinese took the word over from Japanese as *zhexue* ([Philosophy in China](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/china/)), and modern Indian languages came to use *darśana* for it ([Philosophy in India](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/india/)).
