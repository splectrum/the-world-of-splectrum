# Philosophy in Latin America

The region's name is recent. The *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy* dates its wide use to the twentieth century, "the very same period" as the first histories of Latin American philosophy, and notes that "the very idea of Latin America has been posed as a major problem" (Mignolo). The authors below mostly use other words: *filosofía americana* (Alberdi, Zea), *nuestra América* (Martí, Salazar Bondy), *filosofía hispanoamericana* (Salazar Bondy); the field's large histories say *pensamiento filosófico*, philosophical thought. Before the conquest the words are Nahuatl and Quechua: the *tlamatini*, "the one who knows something", and his *tlamatiliztli*, knowledge or wisdom; the Inca *amauta*. Both were first glossed "philosophers" by chroniclers writing in Spanish: Sahagún wrote "sabios o philosophos" in the margin beside the Nahuatl description of the *tlamatini*, and Garcilaso de la Vega called the *amautas* "los filósofos" (*Comentarios reales*, 1609).

Whether there is a philosophy of this America is, for the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, "perhaps the most discussed subject matter within Latin American philosophy". Each stage below gave its own answers to the three questions: what philosophy is, what its point is, and how it should be done.

---

## The knowers of things

**What it is.** The Nahuatl text Sahagún marked describes the sage: "una luz, una tea, una gruesa tea que no ahúma. Un espejo horadado … Suya es la tinta negra y roja, de él son los códices … Es camino, guía veraz para otros" (in León-Portilla's Spanish from the Nahuatl): a light, a torch that does not smoke, a mirror pierced through; his are the black and red ink and the codices; he is a road, a true guide for others. [Miguel León-Portilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Le%C3%B3n-Portilla) (*La filosofía náhuatl*, 1956) made the equation his title, what they "llamaron tlamatiliztli y nosotros filosofía", and said how: "aplicando anacrónica y análogamente al sabio o tlamatini los términos con que hoy se designan a quienes tienen muy semejantes funciones", applying anachronistically and by analogy the terms used today for those with very similar functions.

Whether this is philosophy "is a disputed matter" (Gracia and Vargas); Susana Nuccetelli calls such work "proto-philosophy of the non-academic variety". James Maffie (*Aztec Philosophy*, 2014) keeps the word but frees it from Western frames, holding that the idea of philosophy as exclusively Western "plays a vital role in the West's conception of itself".

**Its point.** Maffie: the *tlamatinime* "turned to philosophy for practicable answers to what they regarded as the defining question of human existence: How can humans maintain their balance upon the slippery earth?" Their philosophy is "better understood as a 'way-seeking' rather than as a 'truth-seeking' philosophy". The sage, in the Nahuatl text, "hace sabios los rostros ajenos, hace a los otros tomar una cara": he makes the faces of others wise, makes them take on a face.

**How it is done.** By "flower and song", *in xochitl in cuicatl*. León-Portilla: in their poems the *tlamatinime* formulated a theory of metaphysical knowing; despite universal transience, "hay un modo de conocer lo verdadero: la poesía (flor y canto)", there is a way of knowing the true, poetry. Maffie: "song-poems rather than discursive arguments are the appropriate medium of sagely expression". The texts come from informants interviewed after the conquest. Of the Incas, Garcilaso wrote that "La filosofía moral alcanzaron bien, y en práctica la dejaron escrita en sus leyes, vida y costumbres", moral philosophy they attained, and left it written in practice in their laws, life and customs.

## The colonial schools and Sor Juana

**What it is.** The universities of Lima and Mexico were chartered in 1551, and [Alonso de la Vera Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonso_de_la_Vera_Cruz) wrote for Mexico the arts curriculum, logic and physics, in the 1550s. In Gracia and Vargas's account, "for scholastics, the primary objective was the apologetic defense of the faith". [Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juana_In%C3%A9s_de_la_Cruz), in her *Respuesta a sor Filotea* (1691), gives the ladder: she directed her study "a la cumbre de la Sagrada Teología", climbing "por los escalones de las ciencias y artes humanas; porque ¿cómo entenderá el estilo de la Reina de las Ciencias quien aun no sabe el de las ancilas?", how will one understand the style of the queen of the sciences who does not yet know that of her handmaids?

**Its point.** Sor Juana: "Yo no estudio para escribir, ni menos para enseñar … sino sólo por ver si con estudiar ignoro menos", I do not study to write, still less to teach, but only to see whether by studying I am less ignorant. Against "Mulieres in Ecclesia taceant", let women keep silent in the churches, she asks why those women are reproved "que privadamente estudian", who study privately.

**How it is done.** Alone, "teniendo sólo por maestro un libro mudo, por condiscípulo un tintero insensible", with a mute book for master and an unfeeling inkwell for fellow student. And from the kitchen: "what can women know except philosophy of the kitchen? … it is possible to philosophize while preparing dinner. As I often say on observing these little things, if Aristotle had cooked, he would have written much more" (tr. Rappaport).

## An American philosophy

**What it is.** [Juan Bautista Alberdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Bautista_Alberdi), in Montevideo in the early 1840s: "No hay, pues, una filosofía universal, porque no hay una solución universal de las cuestiones que la constituyen en el fondo. Cada país, cada época, cada filósofo ha tenido su filosofía peculiar", there is no universal philosophy, because there is no universal solution to the questions at its root; each country, each age, each philosopher has had a philosophy of its own. Beside the Greek, German and French, "es necesario que exista una filosofía americana", there must be an American philosophy.

**Its point.** "La filosofía americana debe ser esencialmente política y social en su objeto, ardiente y profética en sus instintos, sintética y orgánica en su método, positiva y realista en sus procederes, republicana en su espíritu y destinos": essentially political and social in its object, ardent and prophetic in its instincts, synthetic and organic in its method, positive and realist in its procedures, republican in its spirit and destiny. "Nuestra filosofía, pues, ha de salir de nuestras necesidades", our philosophy must come out of our needs.

**How it is done.** As philosophy applied, "no la filosofía en sí … sino la filosofía aplicada a los objetos de un interés más inmediato para nosotros"; "la América practica lo que piensa la Europa", America practises what Europe thinks.

The text's influence came later: Arturo Ardao and Arturo Roig hold that it was revived only in the 1940s, by José Gaos.

## Order and progress

**What it is.** [Comte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Comte)'s positive philosophy ([The hinge](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-hinge/)) became, in Gracia and Vargas's words, "the official state philosophy of several nations".

**Its point.** "For positivists, the goal was national integration and economic and social progress" (Gracia and Vargas). Comte's *Système de politique positive* (1851) carries on its title page "Ordre et Progrès" and "L'Amour pour principe; L'Ordre pour base, Et le Progrès pour but". The Brazilian republic's flag of 1889, designed by positivists, carries *Ordem e Progresso*. In Mexico Gabino Barreda's *Oración cívica* (1867) closes: "que en lo de adelante sea nuestra divisa libertad, orden y progreso; la libertad como medio; el orden como base y el progreso como fin", liberty as the means, order as the base and progress as the end; where Comte's formula has love as the principle, Barreda's has liberty as the means. Positivism, Gracia and Vargas add, "was even used to justify dictatorial regimes, as in the case of Mexico".

## Is there a philosophy of our America?

**What it is.** Hegel had written that "What has taken place in the New World up to the present time is only an echo of the Old World—the expression of a foreign Life" (*Philosophy of History*, tr. Sibree). In the early twentieth century a generation Francisco Romero called "the founders" broke with positivism, and philosophy came to be "pursued for its own sake" (Gracia and Vargas). [Leopoldo Zea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopoldo_Zea): the questions of being and God belong to every man, but "the solution to them will be given from a Latin American standpoint" (1948, tr. Nuccetelli). [Augusto Salazar Bondy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Salazar_Bondy), *¿Existe una filosofía de nuestra América?* (1968): philosophy "fue traída por los españoles porque éstos vinieron a conquistar y a dominar la tierra americana e importaron con ellos las armas intelectuales de la dominación", it came with the conquerors as the intellectual weapons of domination; "El problema de nuestra filosofía es la inautenticidad", the problem of our philosophy is inauthenticity, rooted in "nuestra condición histórica de países subdesarrollados y dominados", our historical condition as underdeveloped and dominated countries. He called the debate itself "the philosophy of philosophy" (tr. Nuccetelli).

**Its point.** Salazar Bondy: philosophy can be authentic amid inauthenticity by becoming "la conciencia lúcida de nuestra condición deprimida como pueblos y en el pensamiento capaz de desencadenar y promover el proceso superador de esta condición", the lucid consciousness of a depressed condition and the thought that sets off its overcoming; much of its task "es destructiva". Answering Hegel's owl of Minerva, philosophy can be "la mensajera del alba", the messenger of dawn.

**How it is done.** Zea (*América como conciencia*, 1953): "todo intento de hacer filosofía americana, con sólo la pretensión de que sea americana, tendrá que fracasar. Hay que intentar hacer pura y simplemente filosofía, que lo americano se dará por añadidura", any attempt at an American philosophy that only aims to be American will fail; one must do philosophy purely and simply, and the American will come of itself, *filosofía sin más* (the title of his 1969 reply). Gracia and Vargas count four approaches in the debate, "universalist, culturalist, critical, and ethnic": Zea's is culturalist, Salazar Bondy's critical, and Jorge Gracia's ethnic, Latin American philosophy as "the philosophy produced by the Latin American people".

## Liberation

**What it is and its point.** [Enrique Dussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrique_Dussel), *Filosofía de la liberación* (preface dated 1976): "Escrito desde la periferia para hombres de la periferia, sin embargo, se dirige también al hombre del centro", written from the periphery for the people of the periphery, it is addressed also to those at the centre. "La filosofía, patrimonio exclusivo del Mediterráneo, desde los griegos, y en la edad moderna sólo europea, comienza por primera vez su proceso de mundialización real", philosophy, once the property of the Mediterranean and in modern times of Europe alone, begins its real worldwide process. The *Internet Encyclopedia* sums up: it "seeks to develop transformational conceptions and practices leading to liberation" from cultural dependence and socioeconomic oppression. Against Salazar Bondy, the liberation thinkers held, as the editors of the 2013 edition record it, that one cannot wait for the culture to be freed before beginning to think liberation philosophically.

**How it is done.** "Parte, es evidente, de la periferia, pero usa todavía el lenguaje del centro", it starts from the periphery but still uses the language of the centre, "como el esclavo que habla la lengua del señor cuando se rebela", like the slave who speaks the master's language when he rebels.
