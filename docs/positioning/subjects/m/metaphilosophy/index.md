# Metaphilosophy

Metaphilosophy is the study of the nature of philosophy. Its three most general questions, as the *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy* puts them, are:

1. What is philosophy?
2. What is, or what should be, the point of philosophy?
3. How should one do philosophy?

The answers have changed with philosophy's history and differ between traditions: in the Western line an inquiry into nature, a way of life, the servant of theology, the whole system of knowledge, the critique of reason, the residue the sciences leave; elsewhere learning and the Way, inquiry by the means of knowing, the critique of a culture's concepts. Since the nineteenth century there have also been claims that philosophy has ended, and disputes over what counts as philosophy at all.

---

## The name

[Morris Lazerowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Lazerowitz) claimed to have coined the English word in 1940, though equivalents in other languages are older, and defined it (1970) as "the investigation of the nature of philosophy". The name itself is disputed, over what the "meta" means:

- **The philosophy of philosophy.** On the first-order reading, applying philosophy to itself is simply more philosophy ([Wittgenstein](/positioning/persons/w/wittgenstein/), *Philosophical Investigations* §121; [Timothy Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Williamson), *The Philosophy of Philosophy*, 2007). On the second-order reading, metaphilosophy stands to philosophy as philosophy stands to its subject matter ([Nicholas Rescher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Rescher), 2006). Williamson prefers his title to "metaphilosophy" because the word suggests looking "down upon philosophy from above, or beyond".
- **After philosophy.** "Meta" can also mean *after*, and metaphilosophy then means post-philosophy. Lazerowitz sometimes used it for the inquiry Wittgenstein had described as one of the "heirs" of philosophy; some French philosophers used it with reference to Heidegger or Marx. The IEP treats post-philosophy as one position within metaphilosophy rather than its definition.

---

## Explicit and implicit

Explicit metaphilosophy, pursued as a field of its own, is small. Even when it waxes, it "generates much less activity than philosophy", and book-length works on it are "not the rule but the exception". The IEP names three: Williamson's *The Philosophy of Philosophy*, Rescher's *Essay on Metaphilosophy*, and Deleuze and Guattari's *What Is Philosophy?*. It counts as probable causes of the field's growth in the early twenty-first century analytic philosophy's growing awareness of itself as a tradition, the rise of more empirical philosophising, and a softening of the analytic–continental divide.

Much metaphilosophy is implicit. "Any philosophical view or orientation commits its holder to a metaphilosophy that accommodates it": to advance an ontology is to hold that ontology can be done, and to adopt a method is to deem it at least passable. A conception of the nature and point of philosophy, "albeit perhaps an inchoate one, motivates and shapes much philosophy", often without being stated. How metaphilosophies can be sorted is on [The twentieth century](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/twentieth-century/).

## Western philosophy

- [Ancient philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/ancient/) — the word and its origin story; the inquiry into nature (the early Greek thinkers); a way of life (Socrates, Plato, Aristotle, the Hellenistic schools, Hadot).
- [Medieval philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/medieval/) — from Clement and Augustine to the Renaissance: faith and reason (philosophy one with true religion, its servant, or the best life; Boethius, Eriugena, Aquinas, Abelard, the Paris arts masters and 1277), and the way of life again (Petrarch, Erasmus, Montaigne).
- [Early modern philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/early-modern/) — the turn towards the knower, from the whole system and the under-labourer (Descartes, Bacon, Locke) through reason and the philosophe (Wolff, the *Encyclopédie*, Hume) to Kant's critique of reason.
- [The hinge](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-hinge/) — the nineteenth century: Hegel's science of its time and Comte's positive philosophy; "scientist", the identity crisis, Windelband, Dilthey and the neo-Kantians; Ortega against "the terrorism of the laboratories"; the canon rewritten "from the Greeks to Kant" (Park) and the word sent abroad (*tetsugaku*, *zhexue*, *darśana*).
- [The twentieth century](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/twentieth-century/) — the analytic stream (Russell, Wittgenstein, Carnap, Quine), the pragmatist and post-analytic (James, Dewey, Rorty) and the continental (Husserl, Heidegger, critical theory, Habermas, Derrida), each against the three questions, and how the streams are sorted.
- [Two traditions](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/two-traditions/) — the analytic–continental divide: Dummett's common root and two rivers, Glendinning's case that the science question cuts across the line, and the quarrels of 1979 and 1992.

---

## Western philosophy in question

Since the nineteenth century, when philosophy came to define itself against the sciences ([The hinge](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-hinge/)), its standing has itself been in question: what is left to it, whether it has ended, and whether its task could ever be finished.

- [The residue and the specialisms](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-residue-and-the-specialisms/) — Russell's residue view, the sciences leaving, the philosophies of the special sciences, specialisation in the university, and the answers that refuse the residue (Husserl, Ortega, Deleuze and Guattari).
- [The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-end-of-philosophy/) — claims that philosophy has ended or should end, from Hegel and Comte to Heidegger and Rorty, the word each uses, what each declares over and what carries on.
- [The scientists' verdict](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-scientists-verdict/) — Hawking's "philosophy is dead", Weinberg, Krauss and Tyson, and the replies from Maudlin, Pigliucci, Rovelli, the reopened foundations of quantum mechanics, and Nietzsche a century earlier.
- [Why then](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/why-then/) — conditions named in the literature around the end claims: Laplace, philosophy's lost role after Hegel, the world picture and the turn to language.
- [A finite task?](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/a-finite-task/) — whether philosophy has a task that could be finished: Hegel, Russell, Collingwood, Dewey, MacIntyre and Rorty.

## Non-Western philosophy

- [Is it philosophy?](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/is-it-philosophy/) — whether the thought of the other traditions counts as philosophy: the question's origin in the canon of 1780–1830 and Hegel's verdicts, its return in Japan, China, India, Africa, Latin America and the West itself, and Defoort's four answers.
- [Philosophy in the Islamic world](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/islamic-world/) — the same three questions from the ninth century to the seventeenth: al-Kindī's six definitions, al-Fārābī's philosopher-legislator and religion as "an imitation of philosophy", Ibn Sīnā's system, al-Ghazālī's challenge, Ibn Rushd's defence and Maimonides, and the later *ḥikma*; the way into the Latin West.
- [Philosophy in India](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/india/) — no Sanskrit word for "philosophy" and the dispute over the name (Hacker, Halbfass); the Upaniṣads' higher and lower knowledge, the Buddha's unanswered questions and the Jaina standpoints, the investigative science (Kauṭilya, Sāṃkhya, Nyāya, the Cārvāka), the *pramāṇa* debate (Dharmakīrti, Nāgārjuna), the inquiry into dharma and Brahman (Śaṅkara), commentary, and the modern question (Radhakrishnan, Matilal, K. C. Bhattacharyya).
- [Philosophy in China](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/china/) — no classical word for "philosophy": learning (*xue*) and the Way (*dao*), and Jin Yuelin's question, Chinese philosophy or philosophy in China; Confucius, the Mohists' standards, Mencius and Xunzi, the Daoists, the schools surveyed as corners of one Way, Chan, the Learning of the Way (Zhu Xi, Wang Yangming), and the modern question (Feng Youlan, the 1958 manifesto).
- [Philosophy in Japan](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/japan/) — learning (*gaku*) and the Ways before *tetsugaku*, and "Japanese philosophy" against "philosophy in Japan" (Maraldo); Kūkai, Dōgen, the Confucian academies (Jinsai, Sorai), Native Studies (Norinaga), Meiji (Nishi, the Inoues), the Kyoto School (Nishida, Tanabe), and the question after 1945.
- [Philosophy in Africa](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/africa/) — no common word, and "African philosophy" in quotation marks (Hountondji); the Egyptian instructions and the dispute over origins, Zera Yacob's *Hatata*, the scholars of Timbuktu, the ethnophilosophy debate (Tempels, Kagame, Hountondji), Oruka's sages, and Wiredu's conceptual decolonisation.
- [Philosophy in Latin America](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/latin-america/) — a recent name for the region; the Nahua *tlamatinime* and whether they were philosophers (León-Portilla, Maffie), the colonial schools and Sor Juana, Alberdi's American philosophy, positivism's "order and progress", the question of a philosophy of our America (Zea, Salazar Bondy), and Dussel's philosophy of liberation.

## Under other names

- **The philosophy of philosophy** — Williamson's preferred name.
- **What is philosophy?** — the question as a title, from Windelband (1884) to Ortega, Heidegger, and Deleuze and Guattari; see [The hinge](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-hinge/), [The twentieth century](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/twentieth-century/) and [The residue and the specialisms](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-residue-and-the-specialisms/).
- **Post-philosophy** — metaphilosophy read as what comes after philosophy; see [The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/the-end-of-philosophy/).
- **Philosophy as a way of life** — Hadot's phrase for the ancient conception, and the title of the English collection of his essays.
