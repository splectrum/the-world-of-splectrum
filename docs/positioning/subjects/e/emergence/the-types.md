# The Types of Emergence

The word "emergence" names many things. This page catalogues the named types — each with its origin and its definition in a line. The inclusion bar: a type earns an entry for a distinct *definition*; new labels for existing definitions are folded as aliases.

## The catalogue

| Type | Origin | Definition in a line |
|---|---|---|
| [Heteropathic effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_System_of_Logic) | [Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stuart_Mill), 1843 | Joint effects that are not the sum of the separate effects — chemical against mechanical laws |
| Emergent (vs resultant) | [Lewes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Henry_Lewes), 1875 | An effect incommensurable with its components, not traceable as their sum — the coinage |
| [Emergent qualities in a hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space,_Time_and_Deity) | [Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Alexander), 1920 | New qualities as levels in a hierarchy of complexity, to be accepted "with natural piety" — the phrase is his |
| Emergent evolution | [Lloyd Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._Lloyd_Morgan), 1923 | Genuinely new qualities arising in evolutionary sequence — Alexander's natural piety adopted as method |
| British-emergentist emergence | [Broad](/positioning/persons/b/broad/), 1925 | Properties of wholes underivable in principle from the components in other combinations; trans-ordinal laws ultimate |
| [Level-autonomy ("More is Different")](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.177.4047.393) | [Anderson](/positioning/persons/a/anderson/), 1972 | Each level of complexity with its own laws, arising through [symmetry breaking](/positioning/subjects/p/phase-transitions/) — reducible in principle, not reconstructible from the reduction |
| [Emergent interactionism](https://informationphilosopher.com/solutions/scientists/sperry/Sperry_Modified_Consciousness.pdf) | [Sperry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Wolcott_Sperry), 1960s–80s | Consciousness as an emergent brain property exerting downward causal control |
| Two senses of "emergent" | [Searle](/positioning/persons/s/searle/), 1992 | Features explained by element interactions (accepted) vs features with powers beyond them (rejected) |
| [Non-structural emergent properties](https://toconnor.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/OConnor_Emergent-Properties-1994.pdf) | [O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_O%27Connor_(philosopher)), 1994 | An emergent as a non-structural property: causally basic, macro-level, with genuine downward efficacy |
| [Failure of aggregativity](https://philpapers.org/rec/WIMARH) | [Wimsatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_C._Wimsatt), 1986/1997 | A system property is emergent to the degree it fails the four aggregativity conditions (intersubstitution, size scaling, decomposition, cooperativity) |
| [Weak emergence](http://people.reed.edu/~mab/papers/weak.emergence.pdf) | [Bedau](/positioning/persons/b/bedau/), 1997 | Derivable from the micro-dynamics, but only by [simulation](/positioning/subjects/c/complex-adaptive-systems/) — explanatory incompressibility |
| Nominal emergence | [Bedau](/positioning/persons/b/bedau/), 1997/2002 | Any macro-property that no micro-constituent can possess — ubiquitous and innocuous |
| Emergentism formalised for critique | [Kim](/positioning/persons/k/kim/), 1999 | The target definition of the exclusion argument: supervenience + irreducibility (non-functionalisability) + downward causation, jointly |
| Weak emergence (in-principle sense) | [Chalmers](/positioning/persons/c/chalmers/), 2006 | Unexpected given the low-level principles, but deducible in principle |
| Strong emergence | [Chalmers](/positioning/persons/c/chalmers/), 2006 | Not deducible even in principle; entails new fundamental laws — consciousness the one serious candidate claimed |
| Weak metaphysical emergence | [Wilson](/positioning/persons/w/wilson-jm/), 2015/2021 | The dependent feature has a proper subset of the powers of its base — real, and physicalism-compatible |
| Strong metaphysical emergence | [Wilson](/positioning/persons/w/wilson-jm/), 2015/2021 | The dependent feature has at least one power its base lacks — physicalism fails if instantiated |
| Graded taxonomy | [Stephan](https://philpapers.org/rec/STEVOE), 1999 | Weak, synchronic and diachronic emergence with strong variants — the graded sorting the European literature uses |
| [The options grid](https://opessoa.fflch.usp.br/sites/opessoa.fflch.usp.br/files/Van-Gulick-Reduction-and-emergence-2001.pdf) | [Van Gulick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Van_Gulick), 2001 | Modest-to-radical epistemic and ontological variants sorted into a single grid — the taxonomists' taxonomy |
| Fusion emergence | [Humphreys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Humphreys_(philosopher)), 1997/2016 | Base entities fuse, losing their identities in the emergent — against the layered supervenience picture |
| Transformational emergence | [Guay & Sartenaer](https://philpapers.org/rec/GUAANL), 2016 | The later system emerges from the earlier by a spatiotemporally continuous, possibly deterministic transformation |
| Flat emergence | [Sartenaer](https://philpapers.org/rec/SARFE), 2018 | Emergence without levels — novelty in a one-level world-history |
| Contextual emergence | [Bishop & Atmanspacher](http://www.scholarpedia.org/article/Contextual_emergence), 2006 | The lower level supplies necessary but not sufficient conditions; sufficiency comes from the stability conditions of a context |
| Causal emergence | [Hoel et al.](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1314922110), 2013 | The coarse-grained description carries higher effective information than the micro — macro beating micro, quantified |
| [Asymptotic emergence](https://philpapers.org/rec/WBATDI) | [Batterman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Batterman), 2002 | Novel behaviour located in [singular limits](/positioning/subjects/p/phase-transitions/), explained by asymptotic reasoning — neither reduction nor classical emergence |
| Protectorate emergence | [Laughlin & Pines](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.97.1.28), 2000 | Macro-laws insensitive to, and protected from, micro-detail — the [quantum protectorates](/positioning/subjects/p/phase-transitions/) of "The Theory of Everything" |
| [Intrinsic emergence](https://csc.ucdavis.edu/~cmg/papers/CalcEmerg.pdf) | [Crutchfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_P._Crutchfield), 1994 | [Pattern formation](/positioning/subjects/c/complex-adaptive-systems/) that increases the system's own computational capability — not merely in the observer's eye |
| [Complexity working definition](https://www.anecdote.com/pdfs/papers/EmergenceAsAConsutructIssue1_1_3.pdf) | [Goldstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Goldstein_(academic)), 1999 | Novel, coherent macro-structures, patterns and properties arising dynamically; ostensible |
| Emergence vs self-organisation | [De Wolf & Holvoet](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/11494676_1), 2004 | Emergents: novel coherent macro-phenomena from micro-interaction — distinguished from [self-organisation](/positioning/subjects/c/complex-adaptive-systems/), the ordering process; the two combine |
| Four types | [Fromm](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0506028), 2005 | A cross-disciplinary ladder: intentional/predictable → weak → multiple → strong, built for multi-agent systems |
| [Three orders](https://philpapers.org/rec/DEATLO-7) | [Deacon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrence_Deacon), 2003/2011 | Thermodynamic (synchronic) → morphodynamic (self-organising) → teleodynamic (biased across generations: life, mind) |
| [Top-down causation, five classes](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsfs.2011.0062) | [Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_F._R._Ellis), 2008–12 | Five distinct mechanisms of downward influence, from algorithmic control to intelligent causation |
| [Multiscale variety](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cplx.20029) | [Bar-Yam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaneer_Bar-Yam), 2004 | Macro-properties determined only at system scale through multiscale structure, not from the parts pairwise |
| Social emergence | [Durkheim](/positioning/persons/d/durkheim/), 1895; [Sawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._Keith_Sawyer), 2005 | [Social facts](/positioning/subjects/s/social-construction-of-reality/) sui generis — exterior, constraining, irreducible to members; Sawyer maps the philosophical taxonomy onto the social case |
| Spontaneous order | [Hayek](/positioning/persons/h/hayek/), 1940s–70s | Order resulting from human action but not human design, carrying knowledge no participant holds |
| Emergent abilities (LLMs) | [Wei et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07682), 2022 | Capabilities absent at small scale appearing discontinuously at large scale — contested: [Schaeffer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.15004) (2023) argue the discontinuities are artifacts of discontinuous metrics |

## Aliases and near-misses

Labels that recur without a distinct definition are folded into the rows above: "novel," "systemic," and "holistic" properties (the nominal/weak family); "radical emergence" (the strong family); "supervenient emergence" (the British/Chalmers line); "epistemological" and "diachronic emergence" as bare labels (standing distinctions of [the field's own sortings](/positioning/subjects/e/emergence/the-sortings/), instantiated by the rows that take them). One borderline case is noted rather than folded: second-order emergence in social simulation ([Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigel_Gilbert), 2002) — the emergent recognised by the agents themselves and thereby fed back — which adds reflexivity to the definition and may earn a row as the literature settles.
