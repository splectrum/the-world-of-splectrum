# Evolution

The word "evolution" is used of stars, minerals, chemistry, species, languages, cultures, economies, technologies, sciences and civilisations, and of the state of a physical system under a law of motion. Each of those homes uses the word by name and means something by it. This subject gathers what each means, and where the meanings meet.

Readers who arrive expecting biology will find the theory of evolution by natural selection in the [Darwinism](/positioning/subjects/d/darwinism/) bundle: the mechanism, common descent, the synthesis with genetics, and the extensions and disputes that bear its name.

The word had senses before biology took it up. Latin *evolutio* was the unrolling of a scroll; eighteenth-century embryology used "evolution" for the unfolding of a preformed germ; [Lyell](/positioning/persons/l/lyell/) used it in 1832, disapprovingly, for [Lamarck](/positioning/persons/l/lamarck/)'s transmutation of species; [Spencer](/positioning/persons/s/spencer/) made it the name of a universal law in the 1850s; [Darwin](/positioning/persons/d/darwin/) avoided the noun until 1871. [T. H. Huxley](/positioning/persons/h/huxley/)'s article for the ninth *Britannica* (1878) was the first history of the word and already gave it two senses. Biology's technical definition came afterwards, and biologists dispute its breadth among themselves: [Ernst Mayr](/positioning/persons/m/mayr/) called the population-genetic formula, change in the frequency of alleles, "misleading", and Douglas Futuyma's definition runs from slight shifts in gene frequency to the whole passage from the first organisms to dinosaurs, bees, oaks and humans.

Across its homes the word carries two senses. In the first, **unfolding**, a single thing or system changes, with no population, no heredity and no selection. Two readings by the shape of the history: **morphology**, Goethe's word, in which a type runs one course per instance and the course is read off many specimens at different stages, as Herschel read the lifecycle of stars off a sky full of them and Spengler read the lifecycle of cultures off eight; and the **arrow**, one irreversible history with a direction, aimed or aimless, as in Spencer's law, the stage schemes of anthropology, and cosmic evolution. Physics' "time evolution" cuts across both: it names any change given by a rule from a state, reversible or not, and physics disputes with itself which shape its evolution has at root. The contrary of all of this is recurrence, the whole coming back, in Hutton's world-machine or Vico's ricorso; none of its authors used the word, and it is what the unfolding readings are defined against.

In the second sense, **descent with modification**, a population changes across generations. This is the Darwinian sense, and the fields that use it grade their own cases by how much of the Darwinian apparatus they carry, from biology's paradigm case to the marginal cases the social sciences argue over. [The criterion](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/the-criterion/) is biology's instrument for that grading, and the page says what it does not measure.

Homes migrate between the senses, and the pages show the migrations as history: linguistics began with the lifecycle of languages and crossed to descent; chemistry hands over to selection at autocatalysis; mineral and cosmic evolution have pulled selection back across by widening it.

## The pages

<!-- The list grows as pages are built; the hub names only pages that exist. -->

- **[The word](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/the-word/)** — the history of the word: the unrolling of a scroll, the preformed germ, Lyell's hostile first use for species, Spencer's fusion of embryo and species, Darwin's avoidance of the noun, and the dispute over whether the senses were linked or broken.
- **[Progress and direction](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/progress-and-direction/)** — the arrow's inheritance: Spencer's law of progress, Darwin on both sides of his own text, the twentieth-century argument from Julian Huxley to Gould and Ruse, the growth metaphor, and recurrence as the pole progress is defined against.
- **[The criterion](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/the-criterion/)** — biology's instrument for grading the descent sense: Lewontin's three conditions, universal and generalised Darwinism, Godfrey-Smith's space of paradigm and marginal Darwinian populations, and what the instrument does not measure.
- **[Mineral evolution](/positioning/subjects/m/mineral-evolution/)** — the word applied by geologists to a non-living, non-reproducing domain: Earth's mineral diversity as a coupled physical, chemical and biological history, with the authors' own disclaimer of Darwinian mechanism.
- **[Lamarck and the inheritance of acquired characters](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/lamarck/)** — the idea behind the word "Lamarckian": Lamarck's theory, Darwin's use and disuse and pangenesis, Weismann's barrier and the neo-Lamarckians, the Baldwin effect, Kammerer and Lysenko, epigenetics, and the word's uses in culture, economics and technology.
- **[Language change](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/language-change/)** — the comparative method and the family tree before Darwin, the exchange with Darwin, Whitney's institutions, sound laws and waves, Saussure's static and evolutionary axes, and the modern field's answers from utterance selection to the invisible hand.
- **[Cultural evolution](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/cultural-evolution/)** — the descent sense applied to culture: dual inheritance and its transmission biases, memetics and why it did not establish itself, Sperber's cultural attraction, cultural group selection and its critics, and culture's place on the criterion.
- **[Economic and institutional change](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/economic-change/)** — Veblen's question and Marshall's Mecca, Schumpeter's development from within, Alchian's selection without maximisers, Hayek's cultural selection of rules, Nelson and Winter's routines, and generalised Darwinism against the continuity hypothesis.
- **[Technology](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/technology/)** — at the scale of artefacts, Basalla's descent, the Ziman volume, Mokyr's limit and Arthur's combinatorial evolution; at the scale of civilisation, Mumford's technics, Ellul's autonomous technique, Gille's technical systems, Kelly's technium and Haff's technosphere.
- **[Science and knowledge](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/science-and-knowledge/)** — the growth of knowledge as variation and selection: Popper's natural selection of hypotheses, Campbell's blind variation and selective retention, Toulmin's populations of concepts, Kuhn's closing analogy, Hull's selection process, and the dispute over blind variation.
- **[Assembly theory](/positioning/subjects/a/assembly-theory/)** — construction history and selection claimed for objects without the population-genetic apparatus, from molecules to the technosphere.
