# Natural Philosophy in Evolution

The study of nature started as part of philosophy and remained there for most of its history. It was the part that dealt with the natural world, with motion and change, the heavens, living things and the soul. From Aristotle's physics to the nineteenth century it kept its place, while what it covered changed. Its history has two movements. What counted as nature narrowed each time the way of knowing changed, and what fell outside it went to the other parts of philosophy. Natural philosophy itself first grew, until it was the trunk of all knowledge, and then left philosophy as the sciences. What stayed behind, metaphysics, the theory of knowledge, ethics and the rest of the old core, is what Russell called the residue. The measure of knowledge narrowed along the way: from love of knowledge, to actual knowledge, to definite knowledge.

**The whole.** The first Greek thinkers called their work inquiry into nature, *historia peri physeos*, and nature was the whole: the moon, the sun, the stars and the origin of the universe. Pythagoras, asked what human beings are born for, said "to be an observer of the sky". Knowing was seeing and inquiring, and the one who looked was simply inside what was looked at. There was no natural philosophy beside other parts yet: the inquiry into nature was the whole inquiry. ([The inquiry into nature](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/greece-and-rome/inquiry-into-nature/))

**A branch.** [Aristotle](/positioning/persons/a/aristotle/) sorted knowledge, and natural philosophy became one part: physics, one of "three speculative philosophies: mathematics, physics, and theology", beside the practical philosophies of conduct. Physics took things that move; number went to mathematics, and the unmoved to theology. Knowledge rose from perception, through memory and experience, to principles, out of a desire to know that all men share. The knower stood half inside: the student of nature studies the soul only "so much of it as is not independent of matter". ([Philosophy in Greece and Rome](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/greece-and-rome/))

**The curriculum.** In the schools of the Islamic world and the Latin West, natural philosophy became a set of books: the *Physics*, *On the Heavens*, *On the Soul*, read in the arts course with the commentators. Its subject was *ens mobile*, movable being, for [Aquinas](/positioning/persons/a/aquinas/), and revealed knowledge was set apart in a faculty of its own, theology. Knowing nature now meant reading and hearing an author, with experience, in Roger Bacon's word, at the edge. ([The schools and the universities](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/schools-and-universities/), [Natural philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/natural-philosophy/))

**The seventeenth century.** Natural philosophy grew. For [Descartes](/positioning/persons/d/descartes/) physics was the trunk of the tree of philosophy, with metaphysics its roots and the other sciences its branches; for Bacon it was the great mother of the sciences; mathematics, a separate philosophy for Aristotle, moved inside it, and [Newton](/positioning/persons/n/newton/) called his work the *Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy*. In the same century nature narrowed. [Galileo](/positioning/persons/g/galileo/) read the book of nature in the language of mathematics and put tastes, odours and colours into the sensing body; Descartes set the thinking thing apart from extended matter and made physics geometry; Bacon and Newton made experiment and method the way in. God was still inside natural philosophy for [Locke](/positioning/persons/l/locke/) and Newton, but the knower had left. As Collingwood put it, minds came to "form a class of beings outside nature". ([The new philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/new-philosophy/))

**Division.** [Kant](/positioning/persons/k/kant/) made the mathematical the measure: there is "as much science proper … as there is mathematics in it". [Hegel](/positioning/persons/h/hegel/) wanted philosophy itself to "lay aside the name of love of knowledge and be actual knowledge". The workers on nature took a name of their own, "scientist", and natural philosophy came to mean physics. Then it left philosophy under the sciences' own names, and the study of the mind followed it as psychology. Knowledge split into the sciences of nature and the human sciences, laws and events. ([The nineteenth century](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/nineteenth-century/), [The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-end-of-philosophy/))

**The residue and the philosophies of.** [Russell](/positioning/persons/r/russell/) gave the rule for what stayed: as soon as definite knowledge of a subject becomes possible, it "ceases to be called philosophy", and what is left is "the residue which is called philosophy". Philosophy then took up a new place beside what had gone: a general philosophy of science from the 1930s, with its own journals, and from the 1960s and 70s the philosophies of the particular sciences, of biology and of physics. These stand outside the sciences' nature and look in. ([Philosophy and the special sciences](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/philosophy-and-the-special-sciences/))

Read this way, the residue is not a scrap heap. It is the old core of philosophy, and it is measured by a rule that it fails by definition: by the measure of definite knowledge, what stays can only count as what is left. Its shape follows from the narrowed nature, with the knower at its centre. [Collingwood](/positioning/persons/c/collingwood/) said it from inside the history: what is "extruded from the concept of nature must find a lodgement somewhere else … first, qualities in general; secondly, minds". Burtt found that the central place of the theory of knowledge in modern philosophy "is no accident", and [Whitehead](/positioning/persons/w/whitehead/) that science's abstractions had been "foisted onto philosophy". Comte reads the same history differently: for him the mind cannot observe itself, and its study goes to physiology.

The philosophies of the special sciences gave philosophy a place beside each science, but they did not give it back the whole, and the core has not yet found its balance among them. It is still measured as the remainder, and the announcements of philosophy's end, from Comte's positive stage to "philosophy is dead", keep coming back. Voices inside the field say as much. For Frodeman and Briggle the old unity of knowledge "shattered under the weight of increasing specialization by the turn of the 20th century"; [Kitcher](/positioning/persons/k/kitcher/) would turn the discipline inside out, the work on the "periphery" first and the "core areas" only as far as needed; for Nicholas Maxwell natural philosophy "died. It split into science on the one hand, and philosophy on the other". The calls to take natural philosophy up again, from Maxwell to Unger and [Smolin](/positioning/persons/s/smolin/), are answers to that imbalance. ([The end of philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/the-end-of-philosophy/), [Natural philosophy](/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/western/natural-philosophy/))
