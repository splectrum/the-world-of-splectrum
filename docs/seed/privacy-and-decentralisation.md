# The seed, Own Experience and Decentralisation

This page builds on the [original principles](/seed/original/) and [historicity](/seed/historicity/). That a subject's experience is its own, and that communities form without a centre, are not policy choices — they are structural consequences of how subjects and communities arise.

**P0 — Being implies language.**

Differentiation produces a boundary: something is, because it is not its context. That boundary is constitutive — without it, there is no being. The boundary is not a wall — it is a dynamic, sustained from within. Experience being one's own begins here, at the most basic level of what it means to be.

**P1 — Language is relational.**

As per the [original principles](/seed/original/). A relation's reach depends on what it relates to — a community's language reaches its members and does not reach outside without translation.

**P2 — Language is the medium through which a subject experiences reality.**

P2 is where the subject's own experience becomes irreducible. A subject's experience is constitutively its own — no outside access, no view from nowhere.

The totality of what a subject feels and thinks is its own — not as a preference or a right, but as a condition of being a subject at all. Being someone's is not added to experience; experience is always someone's.

**P3 — Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality.**

Sharing does not dissolve this — it operates between subjects whose experience stays their own. Subjects share through language, indirectly, reading each other through effects on a shared environment. What converges is shared reality, and with it shared knowledge, but convergence requires enough common ground. A community forms when subjects carry sufficient shared reality to synchronise. From inside, a circle of its own; from outside, opaque.

This is decentralised by its nature. Communities form from shared reality upward, not from authority downward. Each community is itself a subject (P0 fires again), with its own experience, its own boundary maintained from within. Centralisation — the imposition of a single language game across communities — works against this structure. It does not create shared reality; it replaces it.

**P4 — Languages are interrelational and have equal standing in potential.**

Equal standing in potential means each community's language holds its own ground. No external position can rank one community's reality above another's — to understand a community's language requires entering it, and entering it means converging from within. Where communities overlap, mutual understanding forms through that convergence; where they do not, each community's shared reality stands on its own terms. This is the structural basis for decentralisation: not a policy preference, but the absence of any principled meta-privilege across communities.

**P5 — Together they form a web of growing complexity.**

Decentralisation is not fragmentation — it is the structure through which complexity grows. The web gets richer because there are many communities, not despite it: each community's language opens relations no other language reaches, and the overlaps between them are where new language emerges. One language for all contexts would not produce a simpler web — it would produce a web that has stopped growing.
