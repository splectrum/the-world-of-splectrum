## How to read this note

- **[A]** — the author's claim. Part 1 gives these verbatim or near-verbatim. They are the load-bearing content.

## Part 1 — The position, in the author's sequence

1. **[A]** Everett's many worlds posits one wave function for the world.
2. **[A]** So much can be done with QM on partial systems that are at the same time entangled that "one world = one wave function" is fairly meaningless.
   - **[C→A]** Refinement the author accepted ("yes, but…"): this is not a logical contradiction inside the formalism. The global state is idle. It does no descriptive work.
3. **[A]** The Everettian defence is that the global state is essential for smooth evolution with no collapse. But we cannot observe it, and we do not need it except to satisfy an easy description.
4. **[A]** It also makes the view "weirdly deterministic".
5. **[A]** Many worlds resolves the *disappearing* part of collapse, but the *transformational* part remains, and that part feels discontinuous as well.
6. **[A]** What if the disappearance were likewise continuous?
7. **[A]** The real motive for many worlds is not the collapse problem but determinism. This is "maybe not for Everett" himself. In essence it is an exercise in wanting to eliminate contingency. It started with the observer problem, but that problem does not require a single global state as its solution.
8. **[A]** An observer-free description can still be local. The usefulness of QM is 100% local.
9. **[A]** Many worlds suffers from TOE-type tendencies: something at the lowest level that, applied consistently throughout, describes or explains everything. That goes against the nature of description in language. You do not get a better description by adding more and more detail in the same block.
10. **[A]** There are pointers for "local rules apply" and for "relational". The two combined will join up into a single whole "somehow if one wants to", but that does not make the whole meaningful from a descriptive or insight point of view.
11. **[A]** Wanting to join things up is not foolish, but emergence might be a better way to do it.
12. **[A]** "Emergence from" does not presuppose a final bottom. It lives happily in turtles-all-the-way-down land.
13. **[A]** In a "local rules apply" scenario, hard emergence is not possible.
14. **[A]** Relational holism, constraint, and underivability-through-history are all part of *soft* emergence. The underivability has to do with contingency.
15. **[A]** Soft emergence has derivability. The difference between simulation and logical deduction is determinism.
16. **[A]** QM has a deterministic framework and is able to offer a probabilistic solution, and that is where contingency comes in.
17. **[A]** In essence the probabilistic description is a mechanism of concept creation. It offers grouping over a multitude, and the groupings can then become entities with their own properties attached.
18. **[A]** The lack of knowledge about the participants in the multitude requires something like probability. This always introduces some fuzziness, but the fuzziness does not stop us creating well-defined groupings.
19. **[A]** Touchstone image: a solid is never 100% a solid. It is always in equilibrium with a gas phase, and probably has atoms or molecules in a liquid-like state at the interface.
20. **[A]** In that case the gas and liquid have not collapsed out. They have become insignificant.
21. **[A]** The "reality" of the solid world can live while forgetting about the fuzzed-out states, without treating that as a collapse or a discontinuous calamity. Parts have simply moved from significant to insignificant. Someone with innate deterministic tendencies will not be happy with this.
22. **[A]** That a description holds only within a domain is *not a condition*. It is a reminder that there is always a context.

### Compact statement

**[C]** This was offered several times and never rejected, but it should be read as Claude's compression and not the author's wording:

Local rules, relational. Deterministic between interactions, contingent at them. Soft emergence, no floor, no master block. Grouping over a multitude as the source of entities. Insignificance in place of collapse. Every description is a description in a context.

## Part 2 — Supporting external material, by claim (all [C], all to verify)

### On claims 1–3: the global state is idle

- Everett (1957) titled his work the "relative state" formulation. "Many worlds" is DeWitt's later label (c. 1970).
- There are two layers in Everett. The relative-state layer is relational, and Rovelli's RQM (1996) retains it. The universal-wave-function layer is absolute, and RQM drops it.
- A global state splits into separate part-wave-functions only for product states. Entanglement is the normal condition. The parts get reduced density matrices, which are "improper mixtures", and these do not sum back to the whole: the correlations belong to no part.
- The factorisation (preferred-basis) problem: the universal state supplies no cut into subsystems. Decoherence presupposes a split into system and environment.
- Why partial QM works:
  1. Local observables depend only on the reduced state. This is the same theorem as no-signalling.
  2. Isolation is achieved by experimental craft.
  3. Preparation is a measurement, so every working wave function is a state relative to a lab record.
- Four reasons the global state is idle:
  1. It has never been written down.
  2. It is untestable, because the universe is one unrepeatable instance with nothing outside it.
  3. It has no parts until it is cut.
  4. Canonical typicality (Popescu, Short and Winter, 2006; Goldstein et al., 2006): for almost any global pure state, small subsystems look the same.
- The one job the global state does is guarantee coherence between perspectives. RQM's patch for this is the "cross-perspective links" postulate (Adlam and Rovelli, 2022).
- A quantifier shift separates two claims:
  - Claim A: for every system there is a unitary description. The law is universal.
  - Claim B: there is a unitary description of every-system-at-once. The totality exists.

  Wigner's friend shows that A holds without B.

### On claims 4 and 7: determinism and contingency

- Closing the box around everything leaves nothing outside to interrupt unitary evolution. Determinism and totality go together.
- The Born rule problem. Two attempts to solve it: Deutsch–Wallace decision theory and Vaidman's self-locating uncertainty (also Sebens and Carroll). Both reintroduce a located agent.
- Everett's own stated motive was that the textbook theory had two incompatible dynamics and kept the observer outside the physics. Wheeler's influence added the cosmological use.
- Control case: Bohmian mechanics is deterministic and single-world, and Everettians reject it. Objective-collapse theorists are realists who accept indeterminism.
- Rorty, *Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity* (1989). William James on philosophical temperament (*Pragmatism*, lecture 1).
- Contingency returns one level up: why this initial state, why this law, why this Hilbert space.

### On claims 5–6: two parts of collapse, continuous disappearance

- Decoherence *is* entanglement with the environment. The interference terms decay exponentially and never reach zero. The classical regime is the most entangled regime, not the least.
- Zurek: "environment as witness", quantum Darwinism, redundancy of records.
- Continuous (weak) measurement and quantum trajectories: Murch et al. (2013, *Nature*). Minev et al. (2019, *Nature*) caught and reversed a quantum jump mid-flight.
- Continuous spontaneous localisation (CSL): Pearle; Ghirardi, Pearle and Rimini.
- Wallace: branches are approximate emergent patterns (after Dennett, "Real Patterns"), and the number of branches is not well defined.
- Rovelli: the interaction events are the ontology, and the wave function is bookkeeping between them.

### On claims 8–11: local, TOE tendency, joining up

- In RQM an "observer" is any physical system, with no minds involved. So observer-free and standpoint-free are separate demands.
- Anderson, "More is Different" (1972). Laughlin and Pines, "The Theory of Everything" (2000). Effective field theory, renormalisation-group decoupling, universality. Batterman.
- Borges, "On Exactitude in Science", as the image of the 1:1 map.
- Kochen–Specker (1967), and Abramsky and Brandenburger's sheaf-theoretic treatment of contextuality (2011): the local data is consistent on overlaps, yet no global section exists.
- Atlas analogy: a sphere has no single flat chart. It is described by local charts and transition rules. Relativity has the same form, with local frames, transformation rules, and no global "now".
- Consistency between levels is a real constraint, but it is not derivation.

### On claims 12–15: emergence

- Schaffer, "Is There a Fundamental Level?" (2003).
- Kim's exclusion argument, as the local-rules argument against hard emergence.
- Teller (relational holism) and Humphreys (fusion). Juarrero, Deacon and Ellis on constraint. Gell-Mann's "frozen accidents". Symmetry breaking.
- Bedau on weak emergence as derivability by simulation. Wolfram on computational irreducibility.
- Under determinism, a simulation is a long deduction, so the difference is one of degree. Under indeterminism, a simulation yields *an* outcome and not *the* outcome. The possibilities are derivable. The actual is reached only by running.
- Gisin, and Del Santo and Gisin (2019): classical determinism needs infinite-precision real numbers as initial conditions, which no finite standpoint can hold.
- Many worlds turns simulation back into deduction by keeping every run.

### On claims 16–18: probability as concept creation

- Classical probability is read as ignorance. QM probability is structural. The Born rule is always a probability of an outcome *at an interaction*.
- Examples of entities made by grouping:
  - Temperature, pressure and entropy. A macrostate is an equivalence class of microstates.
  - Quasi-particles such as phonons.
  - Renormalised ("dressed") parameters.
  - Ballentine's ensemble interpretation (1970), on which the quantum state itself is a grouping.
  - Mayr's population thinking.
  - Distributional semantics.
- Law of large numbers: fluctuations scale as 1/√N, so contingent members give sharp aggregate properties.
- Robustness selects which groupings count. This is the same filter as the pointer basis.
- From the group-level description, "detail exists but is not carried" and "detail is not yet settled" look the same. So the epistemic/ontic distinction needs a vantage above both levels.
- Wittgenstein on vague boundaries ("heap"). Fluctuations are where new groupings begin: nucleation, variation within a population, a word's marginal uses.

### On claims 19–22: the solid, insignificance, context

- Every solid has a non-zero vapour pressure above absolute zero.
- Surface premelting. Faraday proposed it in the 1850s. Review: Dash, Rempel and Wettlaufer, *Rev. Mod. Phys.* (2006).
- Equilibrium vacancy concentration in the bulk at any temperature above absolute zero.
- Yang–Lee (1952): phase transitions are mathematically sharp only in the infinite-size limit.
- Anderson on rigidity as an emergent property.
- Membership turns over while the pattern persists. The boundary is a zone of exchange.
- Both suppressions are exponential: the Boltzmann factor in the solid, the decoherence factor in measurement.
- Borges, "Funes the Memorious": forgetting nothing makes thought impossible.
- Collapse and many worlds ask the same yes-or-no question about the remainder, "is it there?", and give opposite answers. The author's position is graded and level-relative.
- Effective theories state a cutoff. See the correction in Part 3 on how to phrase this.

## Part 3 — Corrections and pitfalls

1. **"Contradiction" became "idle".** The author first said "contradiction". Claude argued that the formalism is consistent and that the pragmatist charge of idleness is the stronger one. The author accepted this ("yes, but…"). Do not write that many worlds is self-contradictory.

2. **Decoherence is entanglement.** Claude's first account suggested that pure states imply no entanglement. The author pointed out that decoherence means there *is* entanglement. Correct form: pure states are pure only relative to a heavily entangled commons.

3. **The motive.** Claude proposed "completeness, an observer-free description" as the deeper motive. The author partly accepted this, then corrected it: an observer-free description can still be local. The accurate distinction is between observer-free, which RQM already has, and standpoint-free, which only the totality offers. The author's own formulation is *eliminating contingency*.

4. **[withdrawn] "'Emergence from' presupposes a bottom."** The author's correction: it does not. "From" is relative to the level below, and the series need not end.

5. **[withdrawn] Treating relational holism, constraint and historical underivability as "what the strong emergentists wanted".** The author's correction: all three are soft emergence, and the underivability is contingency.

6. **"Derivable in principle".** The standard definition of weak emergence assumes Laplace. The author's sharper form: soft emergence has derivability, and the difference between simulation and deduction is determinism.

7. **The limit of the solid analogy.** Read literally, "nothing collapses out, it just becomes insignificant" is also what an Everettian says. The difference: the solid models the *disappearance* part (interference becoming insignificant), and *selection* still needs contingency at the interaction. The author confirmed that both parts are needed ("yes, but…"). Do not let insignificance account for selection.

8. **[withdrawn] "Condition".** Claude wrote that forgetting is safe "only within a domain", and that the description "fails" near the melting point. The author's correction: this is not a condition. It is a reminder that there is always a context. Nothing fails. The context shifts and the partition between significant and insignificant moves with it. Avoid proviso-shaped phrasing ("holds provided that…") and failure-shaped phrasing. Context is constitutive, not a limitation added afterwards.

9. **Register reminders arising in the session:**
   - Drop "just" from "just an exercise in eliminating contingency". The drive is legitimate locally.
   - The deterministic temperament is coherent within its own frame.
   - Decoherence remains a *candidate mechanism* for the belonging rhyme, not a dependency.
   - Nothing here refutes Everett. It removes reasons for the posit.

## Part 4 — Connections to existing SPLectrum material

All of these were offered by Claude. The author did not reject them, but most were not explicitly confirmed either. Treat them as suggestions.

- **Refuse the totality, insist on the cut.** The universal wave function as a global outside view. Determinism holds only from the unoccupiable standpoint.
- **Decoherence as belonging rhyme.** The pointer basis as whatever survives redundant copying among witnesses. Stability-without-finality follows from graded definiteness, and the Minev reversal shows the other side of it.
- **Deviation before sharing.** Symmetry breaking and frozen accidents. A contingent outcome becomes the starting state for the next stretch: possibilities, then outcome, then new possibilities. Residual fuzz acts as the reservoir of deviation.
- **The shared voice is built, not given.** Coherence between perspectives is constituted at the overlaps and not delivered from below by a totality. Claude suggested this as the real point of dissonance with Everett.
- **The entity is its relations, bundled and held as a unit.** Probabilistic grouping as that kind of bundling.
- **Emergence is defined within a language.** Emergence as a relation between descriptions, with no privileged floor.
- **Loyalty to what-posits.** The global state is held for the kind of world it promises and not for its yield.
- **The unsaid arrangement of loaded terms.** The "condition" slip is a worked instance of it.

## Part 6 — Conversation record

The author's turns are verbatim. Claude's turns are condensed.

**A1.** "There is the QM many worlds from Evert. If I am not mistaken it says that there is one wave function for the world."
**C1.** Confirmed (Everett, 1957: one universal wave function that never collapses). Set out the two layers, relative-state and universal-state, and said RQM keeps the first and drops the second. Described the universal state as the totality move. Noted the Everettians' "least furnished" argument.

**A2.** "Can this wave function be broken down in a bundle of wave functions for part of the world?"
**C2.** In general, no. Only product states factorise. Covered reduced density matrices, which are lossy; relative states as a decomposition into relations; and the factorisation problem. Concluded that the totality cannot produce its own perspectives.

**A3.** "how come we can get results from QM with wave functions that only describe (small) parts?"
**C3.** Gave three reasons: local predictions need only the local description; isolation; preparation is a measurement, so every working wave function is relative to a lab record. Said the practice is neutral between the two readings, but the global state has no empirical warrant of its own.

**A4.** "decohrence means there is entanglement"
**C4.** Accepted as a correction. Decoherence is entanglement, and the classical regime is the most entangled. Zurek's environment as witness. Pure states are pure relative to an entangled commons.

**A5.** "I think there is a contradiction in all of this. There is so much we can do with QM on partial systems that are at the same time entangled that the one world = one wave function is fairly meaningless"
**C5.** Argued it is not a contradiction but idleness. Gave the four reasons, including canonical typicality. Named the global state's one job, coherence between perspectives, and the contrast between coherence that is built and coherence that is given.

**A6.** "yes, but they say: the global state is essential for the 'smooth' evolution - no collapse. But we can't observe it and we don't need it except to satisfy an easy description"
**C6.** Separated claim A (the law is universal) from claim B (the totality exists) and identified the quantum shift between them. Wigner's friend. The simplicity belongs to claim A.

**A7.** "because they seems to become weirdly deterministic because of it as well"
**C7.** Determinism enters with claim B. Born rule problem. Determinism holds only from the unoccupiable standpoint. No real novelty in the Everettian picture. RQM's openness is also a primitive.

**A8.** "But one thing I do not understand is that many worlds resolves the wave function collapse (the disappearing part) but their remains the transformational part, which in my book feels discontinuous as well."
**C8.** Agreed that collapse has two parts. Branching is continuous only in the global description. The discontinuity is moved into the perspective. Suggested it is discontinuous in kind and not in time. Rovelli's events as the primitive.

**A9.** "And what if wave function disappearance was likewise continuous?"
**C9.** Continuous measurement experiments (Murch; Minev). The motive for many worlds weakens. What remains is openness. The state evolves conditional on the record, so definiteness comes in degrees.

**A10.** "I think the motive for many worlds is not the problem of wave function collapse really, but one of determinism"
**C10.** Called it half right. Everett's stated motive was different, and Bohm serves as a control case. Proposed completeness, an observer-free description, as the deeper motive.

**A11.** "maybe not for everett. But an observer-free description can still be local (and in fact the usefulness of QM is 100% local). And I think it suffers from TOE type tendencies: there is something at the lowest level that when applied consistently throughout describes/explains all. But that is against the nature of description in language - you don't get a better description by adding more and more detail in the same block."
**C11.** Accepted the correction: observer-free and standpoint-free are separate. Anderson; EFT and RG; universality; Borges's map. Wallace needs pattern-language to get worlds out of the block. Consistency without derivation.

**A12.** "from what you say, there are pointers for 'local rules apply'. And for relational. And those two combined will join up into a single whole somehow if one wants to. Doesn't mean it is meaningful from a descriptive / insight point of view."
**C12.** Sometimes the joining-up provably fails: Kochen–Specker and contextuality. The global object is a reified consistency condition. Atlas analogy.

**A13.** "I think it is just an exercise of wanting to eliminate contingency - in essence. I agree it started with the observer problem, but that doesn't require the single global state as solution. Yes, to join up things is not foolish. But emergence might be a better way to do that"
**C13.** Rorty. The elimination fails by its own standard, since contingency returns one level up, and the drive is legitimate locally. Symmetry breaking and frozen accidents. Added the caveat that "emerges from" presupposes a bottom (later withdrawn).

**A14.** "emergence from doesn't presuppose a (final) bottom, it lives happily in 'turtles all the way down' land. BTW I think that in a 'local rules apply' scenario hard emergence is not possible."
**C14.** Withdrew the caveat (Schaffer). Agreed on hard emergence (Kim). Listed three survivors, relational holism, constraint and historical underivability, framed as what the strong emergentists wanted (framing later withdrawn).

**A15.** "those three things are part of soft emergence as well, and the underivability has to do with contingency"
**C15.** Accepted. "Derivable in principle" assumes Laplace, and both sides of the hard/soft debate share that premise. Gisin on real numbers.

**A16.** "soft emergence has derivability, but the difference between simulation and 'logical deduction' is determinism"
**C16.** Under determinism, a simulation is a long deduction. Under indeterminism, a simulation samples *an* outcome. The possibilities are derivable and the actual is not. Many worlds keeps every run.

**A17.** "QM has a deterministic framework and is able to offer a probablilistic solution where contingency comes in"
**C17.** Probability in QM is structural. The Born rule applies at interactions. Everett treats the deterministic half as the whole. Described the loop from outcome to new possibilities.

**A18.** "in essence the probabilistic description is a mechanism of concept creation. It offers grouping over a multitude that then can become entities with their own properties attached."
**C18.** Examples: thermodynamic quantities, quasi-particles, renormalisation, Ballentine, Mayr, distributional semantics. The law of large numbers as the stabiliser. Robustness selects groupings.

**A19.** "the lack of knowledge we have about the participants in the multitude requires something like probability. This is always going to introduce some fuziness. However, that fuziness doesn't stop us of creating well defined groupings."
**C19.** From the group level, epistemic and ontic look the same. 1/√N. Vague boundaries still work. The fuzz is productive.

**A20.** "Look at it this way: a solid is never 100% a solid. It is always in equilibrium with a gas phase and probably has atoms/molecules with a liquid like state at the interface."
**C20.** Vapour pressure, premelting, vacancies, Yang–Lee. Membership turns over. The boundary is a zone of exchange.

**A21.** "In this case the gas and liquid have not collapsed out of it, they become insignificant."
**C21.** Exponential suppression in both cases. Significance is level-relative. The analogy covers disappearance and not selection. Both parts of collapse now have models.

**A22.** "yes, but the 'reality' of the solid world can live forgetting about the 'fuzzed' out states, without having to think of it as a collapse or some sort of discontinuous calamity. It is just that part have moved from being significant, to being insignificant. However, if you have innate deterministic tendencies, you're not going to be happy with this."
**C22.** Forgetting is what makes the description work (Funes). Collapse and many worlds ask one yes-or-no question. James on temperament. Added that forgetting is safe "only within a domain" and called this a condition (later withdrawn).

**A23.** "what you say is not a condition. It is a reminder there is always a context."
**C23.** Accepted. Context is constitutive. Nothing fails, the partition moves. The slip is an instance of the unsaid arrangement of terms.
