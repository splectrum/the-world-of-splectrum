**Checks (done 2026-10-01; wording as found, sources in brackets).**
- *Heraclitus B30:* "This world-order [*kosmos*], the same of all, no god nor man did create, but it ever was and is and will be: everliving fire, kindling in measures and being quenched in measures." [Stanford Encyclopedia, "Heraclitus"]
- *Ekpyrosis dispute:* Aristotle (*On the Heavens* 279b12–17) and the Stoics attribute to Heraclitus a world periodically destroyed by fire and reborn; the Stanford entry argues this contradicts B30's "ever was and is and will be", a point Hegel already made. State as a dispute. [Stanford Encyclopedia]
- *Empedocles:* B57 (D157) "From it blossomed many faces without necks, / Naked arms wandered about, bereft of shoulders, / And eyes roamed about alone, deprived of brows"; B59 (D149); B61 (D156) "Many grew double of face and double of chest, / Races of man-prowed cattle…". A reading of the zoogony as an anticipation of natural selection has been proposed (Sedley 2016) and is contested, along with the single-or-double zoogony question. [Stanford Encyclopedia, "Empedocles"]
- *Anaximander:* life arose from the moisture covering the earth; the first animals were fish-like, with thorny skin; humans were first generated from fishes, nourished like a viviparous shark, because the human child needs long protection. Doxographical reports, attributed as such; the entry warns that reading a "proto-evolutionist theory" into them is anachronistic. Cosmogony: a germ of hot and cold separated from the boundless (DK 12A10). [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Anaximander"; the specific ancient reporters not yet named — Pseudo-Plutarch, Censorinus, Hippolytus to be checked if quoted]
- *Lucretius, De rerum natura* (W. E. Leonard translation): V, the early monsters "powerless were they to reach unto / The coveted flower of fair maturity, / Or to find aliment, or to intertwine / In works of Venus", and many stocks perished, "unable / By propagation to forge a progeny"; IV, "naught is born in body so that we / May use the same, but birth engenders use". Line numbers (about V.837ff., IV.823ff.) still to be confirmed against a numbered text. [Project Gutenberg 785]
- *Timaeus:* the Craftsman, "imitating an unchanging and eternal model, imposes mathematical order on a preexistent chaos"; the cosmos is a living thing with soul and intelligence; the governing principle is teleological. [Stanford Encyclopedia, "Plato's Timaeus"]
- *Physics II.8* (Hardie and Gaye): Aristotle states the opposing argument — "Wherever then all the parts came about just what they would have been if they had come be for an end, such things survived, being organized spontaneously in a fitting way; whereas those which grew otherwise perished and continue to perish, as Empedocles says his 'man-faced ox-progeny' did" — and rejects it: "Yet it is impossible that this should be the true view." [MIT Internet Classics Archive]
- *Darwin's footnote:* first appears in the fourth edition of the *Origin* (1866; absent from the third, 1861; present in the sixth, 1872). It quotes the II.8 argument in Clair Grece's translation and comments: "We here see the principle of natural selection shadowed forth, but how little Aristotle fully comprehended the principle, is shown by his remarks on the formation of the teeth." Reception point: Darwin quotes as Aristotle's view the argument Aristotle states in order to reject it. Rechecked 2026-10-05 (sixth edition, Gutenberg 2009; SEP "Aristotle on Causality" for the structure of II.8): the teeth example is inside the opponent's argument (198b23–32), so Darwin's fault-finding with "his remarks on the formation of the teeth" confirms he read the argument as Aristotle's own. The 10-05 reframing ("Darwin saw Aristotle did not hold it") was wrong and is withdrawn. [Origin texts on archive.org and Project Gutenberg]

## 2. Research round — evolution in non-Western philosophies and cultures

**Purpose.** Find where traditions outside the Western line have ideas of cosmic or living change that the doorway's sort can place: unfolding (morphology or arrow), recurrence, descent, or something the sort does not anticipate. Then decide where any of it belongs in the bundle, if anywhere.

**Leads to check (all unverified; listed so the round has a starting map, not as findings):**
- **India.** Sāṃkhya's *pariṇāma*, the manifestation of the tattvas from *prakṛti*, conventionally translated "evolution"; the cosmic cycles of the yugas and kalpas as recurrence; Buddhist dependent origination; the nineteenth- and twentieth-century readings of the avatars as an evolutionary sequence (to be treated as reception, not as the texts); Aurobindo's evolution of consciousness.
- **China.** The *Zhuangzi* passage on a chain of transformations from seeds to creatures to humans, often cited as anticipating evolution; the *Yijing* and the vocabulary of change (*bian*, *hua*); Neo-Confucian *qi*; and the word's own arrival — Yan Fu's translation of T. H. Huxley as *Tianyan lun* (1898) and the term *jinhua*.
- **The Islamic world.** Al-Jāḥiẓ's *Book of Animals* and the claims made for it; the Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ and Ibn Miskawayh on a graded series from mineral to plant to animal to human; Ibn Khaldūn's passage on the same series in the *Muqaddimah*; Rūmī's "I died as a mineral"; the nineteenth-century Muslim reception of Darwin.
- **Elsewhere.** The Hawaiian *Kumulipo* chant, often compared to an evolutionary sequence; the successive suns of Mesoamerican cosmology as recurrence; Japanese reception of the word (*shinka*); African cosmologies, where the round should look before assuming there is nothing to find.

**Guards.**
- **Word test.** For each case, ask whether the tradition has a term of its own for the change, and separate it from the translator's choice. *Pariṇāma* rendered "evolution", or *tianyan* chosen for Huxley, are facts about translation and reception, and belong with the word's travels, not with the texts' own claims.
- **No priority claims.** Statements that a tradition "had Darwin first" (made for al-Jāḥiẓ, the *Zhuangzi*, the avatars and others) are part of the reception and are reported as such, with their critics. The bundle sorts meanings; it does not award precedence.
- **Neutral register, is-like.** The doorway's voice applies: each tradition in its own terms, placed on the sort only where its own terms support the placement.
- **Backing governs placement.** Decide the destination after the round: a paragraph on existing pages (The word, Progress and direction, Evolution in metaphysics, Civilisation as morphology), a new line page, or the translation-theory bundle for the word's travels. No page by default.

**Optional lens.** For each tradition, note whether its account of change is closed — given in advance, as preformed, cyclical or purposive — or open, in its own terms.

**Placement state (2026-10-05).** The round's note is `plan/evolution-nonwestern-research.md`; its §7 options await review. Progress and direction's recurrence section now has its Greek head (built 2026-10-05, descriptive only); option 4, a non-Western recurrence paragraph there, is decided with the other options, not added by default.

## 3. Blog post — explaining by how things came about

**Thesis.** The modern turn was not to a world that changes — Heraclitus had that — but to one explained by how it came about. Explanation by origin comes to weigh more than explanation by law. No claim about direction or destination.

**Four positions as the spine.**
1. *By what a thing is:* Parmenides; Aristotle's eternal heavens; Einstein adding the cosmological constant in 1917 to keep the universe static; preformation, development as the unrolling of what is already present (and the bridge from 1 to 3).
2. *By the law of its change:* Heraclitus's fire in measures; Hutton's "no vestige of a beginning"; Lyell's steady state.
3. *By what it is for:* Aristotle's telos; Plato's craftsman in the *Timaeus*.
4. *By how it came about:* Empedocles and Lucretius as the ancient voice; Darwin's descent; Hubble and Lemaître; the cosmic background radiation (1965) as the universe's own record; stars and minerals with histories; Prigogine's *From Being to Becoming*.

**Steady-state cosmology** goes with the cosmology material in position 4: the rival to Hubble, Lemaître and the expanding universe that the 1965 background radiation settled against. Its perfect cosmological principle concerns the universe as a whole on the large scale — galaxies form and age, matter is continuously created — so it is not presented as a static picture.

Bergson's diagnosis frames 1–3 together as the closed explanations: mechanism and finalism share the assumption that "all is given" ("Finalism thus understood is only inverted mechanism"). The open reading, 4, is set against them.

**Close.** Short, first person, is-like: the sciences came to explain by origin as well as by law. No advance language, and no claim that a position was vindicated.

**Shape.** About 850 words, no headings, image template. Labels: positioning or conversations (to be decided; positioning keeps it apart from the scheduled emergence and entropy word posts). Park on a 2030 date until placed.

**Depends on.** §1 built and committed: the recurrence head on Progress and direction and the person-page additions. Facts to check before drafting: Einstein 1917 and the cosmological constant; the steady-state theory (Bondi, Gold, Hoyle, 1948) and the 1965 discovery; Hubble 1929 and Lemaître 1927; Prigogine's title and date (1980).

## 4. Further post candidates

Each needs its own backing check before drafting; anything a post says goes onto a reference page first.

**a. Blind or sighted — the first idea and the pursuit.** The bundle's standing dispute, Campbell's blind variation and selective retention against Thagard's reasoned generation of hypotheses (1980), reframed across a lifetime: the guiding idea often arrives blind — a question someone grows up with or stumbles on, with no foresight of where it leads — and is then pursued by sighted means, reasoning, mathematics and experiment, for decades. Thagard describes the pursuit; Campbell the first idea. Worked case: Einstein's question at sixteen about riding alongside a light beam, which he says fed into special relativity (*Autobiographical Notes*, 1949) — used as his own retrospective account, with John Norton's "Chasing the Light" (2013) on what the thought experiment could and could not have shown. Neighbours: Simonton's degrees of blindness; Reichenbach's distinction between the contexts of discovery and justification (not yet on the site; would need backing on the Reichenbach page or the philosophy-of-science subject). Guard: keep Campbell's technical sense of "blind", variation without foreknowledge of which trial will succeed; a question that arrives unbidden is not blind in that sense, so the post either keeps his meaning or says openly that it is stretching it. Register: the two-stage reading is offered as a way of seeing the dispute, is-like, not as its resolution. Series: positioning.

**b. Schleicher — evolution as an exploration.** A post on the episode itself, framed as an attempt rather than a verdict: a Hegelian linguist, drawing language trees since 1853, reads the *Origin* and offers Darwin, in an open letter to Haeckel (1863), what Darwin lacked — a real tree built from evidence; Darwin cites him in the *Descent of Man*. The attempt is an exploration of what "evolution" could mean when carried between fields: the tree travelled and lasted, the organism with its life cycle was set aside, and the attempt to classify human groups by language is part of the record and is stated as such. Backing: the Schleicher page and Language change. Series: conversations.

**c. Mokyr — diversity of ideas as the engine.** Mokyr's account of why sustained growth began in Europe, approached from ideas and freedom of thought rather than politics: a Republic of Letters in which many competing ideas could be voiced, tested and kept or dropped on their merits, a market for ideas in which no single authority settled what could be thought, and the Industrial Enlightenment's belief that open inquiry into nature would yield useful knowledge, carried by many minds rather than one plan. Diversity and freedom of thought as the condition of innovation, read alongside the pluralism material — is-like, not as Mokyr endorsing a position he does not hold, and with Allen's and Pomeranz's rival explanations kept in view. Backing: the Mokyr page; the pluralism subject. Topical after the 2025 Nobel prize. Series: positioning or conversations, to be decided.

**Not yet decided:** a Toynbee portrait (Thucydides in 1914, the rise and fall of his reputation); "Selection widened" (Chaisson and Hazen's widening of selection against Hazen's own "quartz does not out-compete olivine"); "Lamarckian" as the name fields give to inheritance that is not blind. **Dropped:** Lloyd Morgan portrait; physics' undecided arrow.
