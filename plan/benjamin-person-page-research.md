## 3. Death — the disputed points, as content

- **Date.** SEP gives **27 September 1940**. The Portbou doctor's certificate gives **26 September**; Gurland said she found him on the 27th; a burial record gives the 28th; the crossing was the 25th. **Confirmed** as a genuine three-way dispute (LARB 2019; Historical Materialism 2024; Jewish Review of Books; walterbenjaminportbou.org). Page wording: "26 or 27 September 1940 (the record and the witness disagree)".
- **Certificate oddities.** The certificate names him "Benjamin Walter" and gives apoplexy as cause; the signing doctor (Ramon Vila Moreno) is reported to have been away from Portbou the previous day. **Confirmed** as reported (Historical Materialism; historia-viva). State as "reported", not as established.
- **Cause.** Suicide by morphine overdose is the standard account (Wikipedia infobox; SEP "took his life"). Murder hypotheses: Stephen Schwartz (2001, in *Intellectuals and Assassins* / a June 2001 piece — **confirmed** to exist via Washington Examiner) alleging Stalinist agents; David Mauas's documentary *Quién mató a Walter Benjamin…* (2005, **confirmed** IMDb). Unsubstantiated; state as such.
- **The briefcase.** Fittko's memoir carries Benjamin's line that the manuscript "is more important than I am" (**confirmed** wording: "I dare not lose it. The manuscript must be saved. It is more important than I am"). Spanish police records list a briefcase with "newspapers and various other papers of unimportant content"; Gurland and Carina Birman do not mention it. **Confirmed** as contested (Aeon; LRB). "Came to wider attention around 1980" **unverified**.

## 4. Contributions — status by item

- **Kant metacritique (1913/14, 1918).** **Confirmed** at SEP: "Experience" contrasts empty lived experience with a spirit-filled one; the 1918 "Program" holds Kant's experience narrowed to the Newtonian model and proposes widening it via language theory after Hamann. SEP also names theology as part of the widening.
- **"On Language as Such" (c. 1916).** **Confirmed** at SEP, including the "no event or thing in either animate or inanimate nature" sentence.
- **Dissertation.** **Confirmed**: Romantic criticism grounded in Fichtean reflection; criticism as consummation of the work. The critical afterword on formalism **unverified**.
- **Goethe essay.** **Confirmed**: criticism vs commentary, truth content vs material content, the expressionless as a "counter-rhythmic rupture" shattering false totality.
- **Trauerspiel book.** **Confirmed**: Ideas as "dialectical synthesis between phenomenal extremes"; origin excludes coming-to-be and includes after-history; allegory's temporality "dynamic, mobile, fluid" against the symbol's "mystical instant". **Corrected on Schmitt:** SEP holds that Benjamin's live interest was Klages's reactionary vitalism rather than Schmitt's political theology, and treats the Benjamin–Schmitt "encounter" as a later construction (see §6). The page may say the book cites Schmitt on sovereignty, but not that it "engages" him as a central interlocutor.
- **"The Task of the Translator".** SEP mentions it only as the source of his influence on translation theory; the doctrine (way of meaning vs the meant, afterlife, pure language, interlinear preference) is **confirmed at the text** (textlog): *"In ›Brot‹ und ›pain‹ ist das Gemeinte zwar dasselbe, die Art, es zu meinen, dagegen nicht."* Published 1923 as preface to his *Tableaux parisiens*; written 1921 **unverified**.
- **"Critique of Violence" / "Capitalism as Religion".** **Confirmed** at SEP: mythic (law-making and law-preserving) vs divine (law-destroying) violence; Sorel's proletarian general strike as pure means; capitalism as a "cult without theology" making guilt pervasive with no possibility of penance. (Proposal had "cult without dogma"; SEP's phrase is "cult without theology" — **corrected**.)
- **"Little History of Photography" (1931).** **Confirmed**: aura defined there as "a strange weave of space and time: the unique appearance or semblance of distance"; photography's destruction of aura read as "salutary estrangement".
- **Work of Art essay.** **Confirmed**: three versions 1935, 1936, 1939 with rising political urgency; aura restated; fascism's aestheticisation of politics answered by communism's politicisation of art (third version, at source). **Corrected on first/second technology:** the terms are in the **second (1936) version** and do **not** appear in the third; the page must attach them to the 1936 text. The French 1936 text (Klossowski) and Institute editing **confirmed** as Institute editing; Klossowski **unverified**.
- **Mimesis (1933).** SEP mentions the essays without detail. The "archive of non-sensuous similarity" gloss **unverified**.
- **City writing, "Surrealism" (1929), profane illumination.** **Unverified** at source (uncontroversial; a text check suffices).
- **Arcades Project.** **Confirmed**: 26 lettered convolutes; 1935 exposé (six sections) and 1939 French exposé (five); the Baudelaire section came to dominate; the dialectical image as the method's centre; Convolute N "Theory of Knowledge, Theory of Progress". 1938 Baudelaire essay rejected by the Institute; 1940 "Some Motifs" published there — **confirmed**.
- **"On the Concept of History" (1940).** **Confirmed** at SEP: not written for publication; first printed 1942 in the Institute's limited hectograph. Jetztzeit, weak messianic power, Angelus Novus, history of the victors — the terms are in the text; SEP treats "weak messianic power" mainly through Derrida's recovery of a "weaker" messianicity. Title: SEP uses "On the Concept of History" throughout; "Theses on the Philosophy of History" is Zohn/Arendt (**unverified** at SEP; standard).

## 5. Quotations — verified wording

- *"Es gibt kein Geschehen oder Ding weder in der belebten noch in der unbelebten Natur, das nicht in gewisser Weise an der Sprache teilhätte, denn es ist jedem wesentlich, seinen geistigen Inhalt mitzuteilen."* — **confirmed** (full sentence; the proposal truncated it).
- *"Die Ideen verhalten sich zu den Dingen wie die Sternbilder zu den Sternen."* — **confirmed** (Erkenntniskritische Vorrede; preceded by "Ein Vergleich mag deren Bedeutung darstellen.").
- Translator essay: the essay does **not** open with "Keine Dichtung gilt dem Leser…". It opens *"Nirgends erweist sich einem Kunstwerk oder einer Kunstform gegenüber die Rücksicht auf den Aufnehmenden für deren Erkenntnis fruchtbar."* The line the proposal wants is the third sentence and reads *"Denn kein Gedicht gilt dem Leser, kein Bild dem Beschauer, keine Symphonie der Hörerschaft."* — **corrected** ("kein Gedicht", not "Keine Dichtung"; not the opening sentence).
- Aura definition: *"einmalige Erscheinung einer Ferne, so nah sie sein mag"* — **confirmed** in the Work of Art essay (third version). The fuller "Ein sonderbares Gespinst aus Raum und Zeit" form is the photography essay's; **unverified** at the German text (SEP's English has "strange weave of space and time").
- *"was im Zeitalter der technischen Reproduzierbarkeit des Kunstwerks verkümmert, das ist seine Aura"* — **confirmed** (third version; lower-case "was", mid-sentence).
- *"Es ist niemals ein Dokument der Kultur, ohne zugleich ein solches der Barbarei zu sein."* — **confirmed** (thesis VII; textlog and multiple).
- *"Methode dieser Arbeit: literarische Montage. Ich habe nichts zu sagen. Nur zu zeigen."* — **confirmed** (N1a,8).
- *"Der Faschismus läuft folgerecht auf eine Ästhetisierung des politischen Lebens hinaus"* / *"Der Kommunismus antwortet ihm mit der Politisierung der Kunst"* — **confirmed** (third version); usable in place of the proposal's paraphrase.
- Adorno's 18 March 1936 letter — **unverified**; paraphrase only, as the proposal already says.

## 6. Reception — status by item

- **Scholem / Adorno / Brecht; theological vs materialist.** **Corrected:** SEP does *not* say the question is badly posed. It treats the tension as productive, citing Khatib on "the irreducible theological remainder" not reducible to Judaeo-Christian theology. Drop the "badly posed" line or attribute it elsewhere.
- **Estate disputes.** **Confirmed**: Arendt's *Illuminations* (1968) presented a "metaphorical" or "poetical" thinker and marginalised the political; Adorno's *Schriften* (1955) included the philosophical, historical and city writings. The "pearl diver" phrase and the late-1960s / early-2020s resurfacings **unverified** at SEP.
- **Institute and funds.** **Confirmed** verbatim: "Recent scholarship (Erazo-Heufelder 2017; Mulder 2015; Fleck 2023) has offered a more critical view of the Institute's mismanagement of funds and inequitable distribution of financial and administrative support." Arendt's own claim about Brecht endangering the funding **unverified** at SEP.
- **Unity or fragments.** **Confirmed**: Friedlander's description of readers treating the corpus as "brilliant and idiosyncratic insights"; Scholem and Adorno seeking coherence in mysticism or philosophy; Kohlenbach on religiosity; Caygill's "philosophizing beyond philosophy". Andrew Benjamin's name **not in source** on this point.
- **Poststructuralist reception.** **Confirmed**: Derrida, *Force of Law* (1990), on the undecidability of the violence oppositions and the recovery of a weaker messianicity; de Man for his influence on the poststructuralist reception via allegory; Weber (2008) on "nonsynthesis"; Weigel (1996) resisting postmodern assimilation ("resolutely modernist"). "Des Tours de Babel" and de Man's 1983 lecture **not in source**; cite from the reception texts directly if used.
- **Agamben / Schmitt.** **Confirmed** and stronger than the proposal: SEP is critical of the Benjamin–Schmitt affinity claims; Agamben's 1970 essay and *Homo Sacer* (1995); Kostko and Pomreneke on "chronological reversals"; Weber (2008), Hamacher (1991), Tomba (2009) and Avelar (2005) on misreading; Klages more influential on Benjamin than Schmitt. Taubes **not in source**.
- **Habermas.** **Corrected on date:** SEP cites Habermas **1979** (the English version of the 1972 essay); the point (relevance lies in guiding "a theory of linguistic communication") **confirmed**; Moran and Salzani (2015) on the resulting "non-reading" **confirmed**.
- **Fields shaped.** **Confirmed**: philosophy of education (via *Early Writings* 2011 and *Radio Benjamin* 2014), translation theory, film theory, cultural theory and modernity, political philosophy. (A second fetch of the same entry did not surface the education titles; the first did. Treat the education claim as confirmed, the two titles as the entry's examples.)

## 7. Uncertainties — status

- Death date and cause: see §3, all confirmed as disputes.
- Work of Art versions and the English title variants: three German versions **confirmed**; "Mechanical Reproduction" (Zohn) **not in source**, standard elsewhere.
- Theses: manuscript variants and 1942 hectograph **confirmed**.
- Fichte reading questioned: **confirmed** — SEP cites Bullock (1987) and Menninghaus (2005).
- *Kritische Gesamtausgabe*: **confirmed** — 21 volumes, in production since 2008, scheduled completion 2027.

## 8. Corrections to carry into the page

1. First/second technology belongs to the **1936 second version**, not the essay generally.
2. "Cult without **theology**", not "without dogma".
3. The translator-essay line is *"Denn kein Gedicht gilt dem Leser…"*, third sentence, not the opening.
4. The language-essay sentence continues "…denn es ist jedem wesentlich, seinen geistigen Inhalt mitzuteilen."
5. SEP does not call the theological/materialist question badly posed; it calls the tension productive (Khatib).
6. Habermas is cited at 1979 in SEP.
7. Schmitt: cited in the Trauerspiel book, but SEP reads Klages as the live influence and the Schmitt "encounter" as Agamben's construction. The page reports the citation and the dispute, no more.
8. Death date: give both 26 and 27 September with the disagreement; SEP's own date is the 27th.
9. Andrew Benjamin, Taubes, "Des Tours de Babel", de Man 1983, the "pearl diver" phrase, Klossowski, *Der Anfang*, Rickert/Simmel, *Krise und Kritik*, the four exile places, Lukács on Capri: all unverified at the anchor. Each is standard and low-risk, but none should carry a quotation or a date the page cannot back; take them from Eiland & Jennings when the page is written, or state without dates.
