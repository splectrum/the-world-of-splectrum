What the doorway consists of, which pages it needs beneath it, which person pages it asks for, and in what order. ## 1. The doorway

**One subject bundle: Evolution.** Hub plus line pages, on the pattern of the emergence and philosophy-of-history bundles.

**Sorting principle: by meaning, not domain.** Two senses of the word, each with its own internal grading, each grading the fields' own.

- **Unfolding.** A single thing, or a system, changes, with no population, no heredity and no selection. A structural description, and the older sense of the word; its history, from *evolutio* and the preformed germ, is line 18's, and the colouring that history left is line 19's. Within it, two readings by the shape of the history, each named in the field's own word, and one home whose use is defined on a different question:
  - **Morphology.** A type runs one course per instance, and the course is read off many specimens at different stages: Goethe's word, taken over by Spengler, and Herschel's natural-history method for stars. Differs from the arrow on how many times the course runs.
  - **Arrow.** One irreversible history with a direction, aimed or aimless: Spencer's law, the stage and energy schemes, Chaisson's "aimless, meandering" cosmic evolution, Hazen's "deterministic, irreversible" diversification, Kelly's technium. Progress is the arrow's inheritance, not its definition.
  - **Physics' use cuts across the two.** "Time evolution" names any change given by a rule from a state, reversible or not: unitary and Hamiltonian dynamics, but equally master equations, Fokker–Planck, Lindblad evolution of open systems. It answers how the change is given, not what shape the history has, and it can land on either: time-symmetric at root with the arrow emerging, or, as Prigogine held, irreversible at root. Peirce's anancasm is the metaphysical form of the rule-given reading.
  - **The contrary, named but not a reading.** Recurrence — Hutton's world-machine, Lyell's steady state, Vico's ricorso, Eliade's eternal return, Gould's *Time's Cycle* — is what the unfolding readings are defined against, and none of its authors used the word; Gould's pair sets the cycle as geology's anti-evolutionary pole. The hub gives it one sentence as the contrast; the progress page carries it as the other side of the arrow.
- **Descent with modification.** A population changes across generations. The Darwinian sense, graded by how much of the apparatus a case carries. Biology's own instrument for that grading is Lewontin's three conditions and Godfrey-Smith's space of paradigm and marginal Darwinian populations. It is one field's instrument for one sense, and the line pages use it only where a field has used it on itself.

Lines migrate between the senses and between the readings, and the hub says so: linguistics began as morphology (Schleicher's Hegelian *Entwicklung*, growth to maturity to decay) and crossed to descent; chemistry hands over at autocatalysis and molecular selection; cosmic and mineral evolution pull selection back across by widening it, and Smolin's cosmological natural selection carries the whole Darwinian mechanism into cosmology without widening anything; physics' time evolution never touches descent, since Prigogine brings self-organisation rather than heredity, but the far-from-equilibrium programme meets chemical evolution at Eigen, one more crossing to show; and physics' own dispute is which shape its evolution has at root. The hub shows the migrations; it does not rule on them.

**Access.** A single list to begin with, the lines in the order below, each with one line of description. Further access angles (by mechanism, by what is inherited, by scale) can be added as hub sections later. No separate index pages.

**Hub contents.** Intro on the word's range across its homes and on its history: senses before biology (Huxley's 1878 account of two senses, and the Bowler–Richards dispute over how they relate), biology's technical definition as one event in that history, and biologists' own dispute over its breadth (Mayr against the allele-frequency formula, Futuyma's expansive version). The reader sees the specialisation; the hub does not name it as one. Then the two senses and their readings stated in the fields' terms, two sentences for unfolding's readings by shape, one for physics' rule-given use, one for the contrary; the migrations named; the list of lines; a persons block; see-also to the Darwinism bundle, assembly theory, mineral evolution, philosophy of history, process philosophy, emergence, thermodynamics, and translation theory for the linguistics border.

## 2. The line pages

Sixteen lines and three cross-cutting pages. Each line page: what "evolution" means in this home, in the home's own words; who carries it; where the home itself places its process on the cut and the grading; the standing disputes. Length by the touchstone principle, most at 500–900 words.

**Unfolding — the rule-given use**

1. **Time evolution in physics.** Any change given by a rule from a state: the Schrödinger equation's unitary evolution and Hamiltonian flow, the "evolution operator", and equally the irreversible dynamics of master equations, Fokker–Planck and Lindblad evolution of open quantum systems. The word is defined by how the change is given, not by its shape, so the line's standing dispute is which shape physics' evolution has at root: time-symmetric fundamental dynamics with the arrow emerging (Boltzmann's H-theorem and the Loschmidt objection, decoherence), or irreversibility as fundamental, Prigogine's position with Nicolis and Babloyantz's "Thermodynamics of Evolution" (1972) and *From Being to Becoming* (1980). Peirce's anancasm as the metaphysical form. The line's second strand is where that programme reaches: the 1972 paper is titled "Thermodynamics of Evolution" because it carries far-from-equilibrium dynamics toward biological evolution, order through fluctuations, prebiotic self-organisation, and a direct engagement with Eigen's molecular selection, which is where this line meets line 5. Sits next to the thermodynamics bundle's away-from-equilibrium page, the arrow-of-time material, and the Schrödinger, Prigogine and relational-quantum-mechanics pages.

**Unfolding — morphology**

2. **Stellar evolution.** Herschel's garden of specimens (1789), a lifecycle read off many stars at different stages; Lockyer's meteoritic scheme and *Inorganic Evolution* (1900), borrowing the meaning from "organic evolution"; Russell reading the H–R diagram as a contraction track; Eddington and Bethe; the modern "evolutionary track" as one star's irreversible path; Baade's Populations I and II as age classes, not lineages. Morphology per instance.
3. **Civilisation as morphology.** Goethe's morphology as Spengler's declared method; Spengler's eight Cultures, "contemporaneous" epochs, civilisation as a Culture's old age, anti-Darwinian by declaration and against progress; Toynbee's genesis, growth, breakdown, disintegration, rejecting Spengler's fixed lifespan; Quigley's *Evolution of Civilizations* and what its title word means; Tainter's diminishing returns; Diamond's collapse as choice. Nisbet's critique of the growth metaphor. Vico and Ibn Khaldun belong to the recurrence contrary and stay with the philosophy-of-history bundle, which already separates them from Spengler and Toynbee.

**Unfolding — arrow**

4. **Cosmic evolution.** Chaisson's definition and his widening of selection to "preferential interaction of any object with its environment"; Shapley, Sagan, Jantsch as antecedents; big history's use of the phrase; the aimless-process reading against the arrow's inheritance. Then the sharpest migration on this side: Smolin's cosmological natural selection (1992; *The Life of the Cosmos*, 1997), universes reproducing through black holes, constants inherited with small variation, differential reproduction — the full descent apparatus in cosmology, with the word unchanged. Smolin has a page.
5. **Chemical evolution and the hand-over.** Oparin and Haldane; Calvin's four periods (1955) and his book (1969), already importing variation and selection via autocatalysis; Eigen's molecular Darwinism (1971) and hypercycles; the point where the arrow hands over to descent. Links down to assembly theory, Walker, Cronin.
6. **Mineral evolution.** Built. The hub links the existing subject and the line page is short: Hazen's own disclaimer ("quartz does not out-compete olivine"), Bowen's 1928 precedent, the Rosing and Eldredge objections to the word, and Wong–Hazen's 2023 law of increasing functional information as the reverse move.
7. **Social evolutionism and the stage schemes.** Comte's stages ("progress", not "evolution"); Spencer's militant to industrial; Morgan's ethnical periods keyed to the arts of subsistence, and Engels on Morgan; Tylor; Boas's rejection and historical particularism; the neo-evolutionist return: White's energy law, Steward's multilinear evolution, Sahlins and Service's general and specific, Service's band to state, Lenski's technological information; Childe's chosen word "revolution". Steward and Sahlins as the border case toward descent.

**Descent with modification**

8. **Biology.** The narrow technical sense: the population-genetic definition (attribution to Dobzhansky 1937 unverified; von Baer's embryology as the other root), Futuyma's expansive one, Mayr's objection to the allele-frequency formula, the modern synthesis. Short; points down to the Darwinism bundle, which is the biology backing in full.
9. **Lamarck and the inheritance of acquired characters.** Lamarck's own theory (1809), its place in Lyell's hostile 1832 summary where the species sense of the word first appears, Darwin's own use of use-and-disuse, Weismann's barrier, the neo-Lamarckians, Lysenko as the political afterlife, and the word "Lamarckian" as it runs through the cultural, economic and technological lines (Nelson & Winter, Ziman, Mokyr) and through epigenetics (Jablonka). The line the descent-sense homes keep reaching for when their inheritance is not blind.
10. **Language change.** Jones, Rask, Bopp, Grimm; Schleicher's tree (1853), his Hegelian lifecycle vocabulary and his open letter to Haeckel (1863); the mutual borrowing with Darwin (*Descent* vol. 1 ch. II, quoting Müller); Whitney's language-as-institution; the neogrammarians' sound laws as regularity of transmission; Schmidt's wave model; Saussure's "static/evolutionary" axes becoming synchronic/diachronic; the modern ladder: Croft's utterance selection, Pagel, phylogenetic linguistics (Gray & Atkinson, Bouckaert), Keller's invisible hand, Lass's scepticism. Faculty evolution kept separate.
11. **Cultural evolution.** Dual inheritance (Cavalli-Sforza & Feldman, Boyd & Richerson); memetics and why it did not establish; Sperber's attractors; cultural group selection (Henrich; Turchin's *Ultrasociety*); Fracchia & Lewontin and Ingold; Godfrey-Smith's placement of culture as marginal. Draws on the cultural-extensions page and the deep-time branch; does not repeat them.
12. **Economic and institutional change.** Veblen 1898; Marshall's Mecca; Schumpeter's development from within and his rejection of biology; Alchian's selection without maximising agents; Hayek's cultural selection of rules; Nelson & Winter's routines, "unabashedly Lamarckian"; Dosi, Metcalfe, Witt; generalised Darwinism (Hodgson & Knudsen) against Witt's continuity hypothesis, with Nelson among the sceptics.
13. **Technology.** Artefact scale: Basalla, Ziman's volume, Mokyr's limit (no feedback from artefact to knowledge in nature), Arthur's combinatorial evolution "not Darwinian". Civilisation scale: Mumford's technics and megamachine, Ellul's autonomous technique, Gille's technical system, Kelly's technium as arrow, Haff's technosphere, and the assembly-theory lineage claim.
14. **Science and knowledge.** Popper's natural selection of hypotheses; Campbell's blind variation and selective retention (1960, 1974); Toulmin's intellectual ecology; Kuhn's closing analogy and his retreat from it; Hull's replicator and interactor applied to science.
15. **Evolutionary computation.** Holland's genetic algorithms; the word applied to a deliberately engineered process; paradigm on every parameter by construction. Short; points down to Holland and complex adaptive systems.
16. **Assembly theory and the technosphere.** Built. The hub links it as the line where construction history and selection are claimed without the population-genetic apparatus.

**Metaphysics — the test case, placed on neither side**

17. **Evolution in metaphysics.** Peirce's "Evolutionary Love" (1893) sorts the word's meanings from inside the field: tychastic (chance variation, Darwin), anancastic (mechanical necessity), agapastic (growth by attraction). Bergson's *L'Évolution créatrice* (1907) rejects both finalism, "inverted mechanism", and Darwinian mechanism, and gives the word a third sense the doorway must be able to place without ruling on it. Emergent evolution (Alexander, Lloyd Morgan, 1920s) beside the emergence bundle. Whitehead's process. Teilhard as the arrow reading in theological form. The page states the three positions on the cut as their own: Peirce's sort is a sort, Bergson's refusal is a refusal, and the doorway records both. Links to process philosophy.

**Cross-cutting**

18. **The word.** *Evolutio*; 1670 for Swammerdam; Haller 1744 and Bonnet for preformation; Lyell 1832 as first species-sense use (hostile, of Lamarck); Chambers's "development"; Spencer 1852 and 1857 fusing embryo and species, the *First Principles* formula in its 1862 and 1867 forms; Darwin's avoidance of the noun until 1871, reasons marked as historians' inference; T. H. Huxley's 1878 Britannica article as the first history of the word; Richards against Bowler on whether the senses were linked or broken.
19. **Progress and direction.** The arrow's inheritance. Opens on the question page 18 leaves: why the historians think Darwin avoided the noun, which is largely the progress colouring. Points back to 18 for preformation rather than repeating it. Spencer's "Progress: Its Law and Cause" (1857) as the law of progress; Darwin two-sided in his own text ("no innate tendency to progressive development" beside "will tend to progress towards perfection"; the "never say higher or lower" note unconfirmed as to location); Julian Huxley's evolutionary progress; Gould's *Full House*; Ruse's *Monad to Man*; the Richards–Lewontin exchange (*NYRB*, 2005) as the argument in its most direct recent form; Nisbet on developmentalism; and the contrary of the whole arrow, recurrence, in Hutton's world-machine, Lyell's steady state and Gould's *Time's Cycle*, as the pole progress is defined against. Neutral: the positions, not a verdict.
20. **The criterion.** Lewontin 1970 and the any-level generalisation; universal Darwinism (Dawkins 1983, Dennett 1995); Campbell before both; generalised Darwinism; Godfrey-Smith's Darwinian populations, the parameters, paradigm and marginal cases. Biology's instrument for grading the descent sense; the page says so and says what it does not measure.

## 5. Register guards

- Outside voice throughout. The position's reading of evolution is on the reality side and is not cited on these pages.
- Every placement on the cut or a grading is the field's own, quoted or attributed. Where a field has not placed itself, the page says so rather than placing it. Godfrey-Smith's space is applied only where a field has applied it to itself.
- No line is "loose talk", and "narrowing" and "specialisation" do not appear on the hub: biology's definition is one event in the word's history, and the view that other uses are loose talk appears only attributed, if a source is found holding it. The unfolding sense is the older sense, reported as such, with its two readings by shape in the fields' own words, morphology (Goethe, Spengler, Herschel) and arrow (Spencer, Chaisson), and physics' rule-given use stated as cutting across them with Prigogine's dispute reported; recurrence is named as their contrary, not as a reading, because none of its authors used the word.
- The migrations are reported as history, not as a thesis about the word.
- Progress and teleology: both sides of Darwin's text, both sides of the twentieth-century debate, no verdict.
- The metaphysics line is placed on neither side and says why in the philosophers' own terms.
- No SPLectrum vocabulary. No "is-like" commentary. The doorway is reference material.
