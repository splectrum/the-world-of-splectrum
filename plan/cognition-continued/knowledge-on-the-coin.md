## 1. The source

**[C, checked against the review]** "How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Drop Justified True Belief", *Freiburger Zeitschrift für Philosophie und Theologie* 72, no. 2: 530–537 (final preprint; author not named on it). A review essay of Antognazza, *Thinking with Assent*. Page numbers below are the book's, as the review cites them; **the book itself has not been read.**

- **Three claims rejected.** That knowledge is justified true belief (JTB); that JTB has been standard since Plato; that Gettier (1963) overturned it.
- **Knowledge is not a species of belief.** Knowledge and belief are different, mutually exclusive mental states. Knowledge is prompted by direct intellectual perception or grasp of its object, analogous to seeing; belief is prompted by something other than its object — "a mode of cognition in which something which is not present, is nevertheless affirmed on the basis of reasons, symptoms, traces, signs" (110). Knowledge can no more be a kind of belief than seeing a kind of non-seeing (362).
- **Belief is graded, knowledge on/off.** The attempt to reach knowledge by grading belief up commits what David Bentley Hart calls the *pleonastic fallacy*: the "belief that an absolute qualitative difference can be overcome by a successive accumulation of extremely small and entirely relative quantitative steps" (Hart, *The Experience of God*, 2013, 98).
- **The history.** Plato distinguishes knowledge and belief by their objects and has Socrates reject a JTB-like analysis as circular (*Theaetetus* 201a–b); Aristotle: belief of what can be otherwise, knowledge of what cannot; the Pyrrhonists and Hume ("contrary natures") keep the distinction; Kant reverses Plato and keeps it. Gettier's target "was, simply put, a strawman".
- **The positive picture.** Knowledge is "an under-lived contact with 'what is', or an intellectually 'seeing'" (30), "a primitive, undefinable mental state by which we are in conscious cognitive contact with an object" (85). Scepticism is defeated by denying the gap between mind and world, not by bridging it (94). Drawn from Aquinas, Ockham and Olivi: the mind is assimilated to the world, knower and known united (144) — the tree outside and the tree in the mind two aspects of one thing (137).
- **First-personal.** Knowledge is not a "parcel" handed from one person to another (209); knowledge by testimony is a contradiction in terms — though testimony is often indispensable for *obtaining* knowledge (follow the lab instructions, run the experiment, learn something new).
- **Objects, not propositions.** Knowledge is of things, expressible propositionally but not itself propositional (175–179). Small children and some animals, with or without propositions, have a basic knowledge (182) — against Davidson. Knowledge is everywhere; the world constantly impresses itself on us.
- **Judgment below propositions.** Even the most primitive mental act contains a minimal judgment, that what is perceived exists (158) — against Frege's content stroke; the review links it to Kimhi and Rödl on the unity of thought and judgment.
- **Giving belief its due.** Belief is not "botched knowing" (Williamson's phrase, *Knowledge and its Limits*, 2000, 47) but a successful mode of cognition in its own right (190): the indispensable way of extending assent beyond what can strictly be known. Faith belongs to the "doxastic family" — things not seen (Hebrews 11:1), linked with hope — not knowledge, and so open to free choice; against Plantinga's faith-as-warranted-knowledge.
- **[C, unchecked]** The strawman point has a precedent: Julien Dutant, "The Legend of the Justified True Belief Analysis", *Philosophical Perspectives* 29 (2015).

## 2. Convergence is belief

- **[A]** The article can be drawn on to deepen the definition of knowledge in our epistemology.
- **[A]** Convergence and belief are quite similar: convergence expresses the view of the others in the community — the shared. In that sense we haven't moved away from beliefs; philosophy has a lot of them. We have added more languages to achieve convergence.
- **[C]** Mapped onto the two poles of knowing: the private pole (disclosed, from a seat no one else holds) is where Antognazza's *knowledge* falls; the shared pole (what holds is what survives convergence) is, in her terms, *belief* — assent moved by others' reports, reasons and signs rather than by the object. Converged subjectivity is then honestly named belief; nothing is lost, it is only no longer presented as knowledge.
- **[C]** More languages make the shared more robust — agreement across independent languages is stronger than agreement inside one tribe — without changing its kind.

## 3. What knowledge is here

- **[corrected]** Claude set Antognazza's account against the seed as "direct contact, the Scholastic union of knower and known, *with no medium*". **[A]** Direct contact is a language in itself — a way convergence gets expressed. The union of knower and known is a formulation converged in one tradition, with its own vocabulary (form, species, in-forming); under equal standing it cannot describe what happens beneath all language.
- **[corrected]** Claude applied Hart's point as "no amount of convergence turns belief into knowledge". **[A]** What is knowledge here? The sentence presupposed knowledge as an absolute contact that convergence falls short of — a privileged target above the system, the outside view the position refuses.
- **[C]** Knowledge in the position's own terms is already stated in the second principle: "Knowledge is what the subject comes to recognise within that accumulation" — recognition within the subject's own retained experience, through the medium, partial, with nothing making it absolute.
- **[C]** The knowledge/belief difference then becomes a difference of **seat**, not of contact against no contact:
  - **Knowing** — the recognition happens in *my* accumulation: my experience converging with the language tools I hold.
  - **Believing** — holding what was recognised in *another's* accumulation, reaching me as expression.
  - Both are convergence: within the subject (experience with tools) and between subjects (expressions into the shared). The tools are common either way; what differs is whose contact the assent rests on.
- **[C]** Hart's point returns in a form that holds: no amount of *others'* convergence becomes *my* recognition, because the difference is one of seat, not strength. The line is crossed differently — by the subject's own experience: take the lab instructions as belief, run the experiment, and it is in one's own accumulation.

## 4. Knowledge on the coin

- **[A]** Knowledge is a seed concern (mainly), lifted into content because that is where epistemology lives (at the moment).
- **[A]** A language-medium convergence definition of knowledge would sit 100% within the seed. What does that mean for SPLectrum, and what else is there in epistemology? It might still be seen as SPLectrum's content interpretation of the (common to all) seed principles.
- **[C] The seed face — knowledge itself, kept thin.** Knowing as recognition within one's own accumulation; belief as holding what was recognised at another's seat; convergence within and between subjects; access always partial; no outside from which knowledge could be certified — which is also why the sceptic's demand cannot be met by anyone, the sceptic included. This holds for every voice: Antognazza's direct contact and Plato's knowledge of the forms are expressions made in the medium, so the seed describes the medium they speak in rather than contradicting them. That is the coin's test, and it passes.
- **[C] The condition.** The seed definition must not take sides in the field's debates — whether knowledge is a mental state, whether its objects are things or propositions, JTB against Antognazza's contact against Williamson's knowledge-first. Settling those would make it SPLectrum's own voice claiming seed status, a privileged meta-language, which the fourth principle rules out. The structural account is seed; any formulation that competes on the field's own terms is content — SPLectrum interpreting the principles.
- **[C] The content face — a stance toward knowing.** What is left for SPLectrum's own epistemology is mostly the normative side the field files under the ethics of belief, epistemic virtue, trust and testimony, disagreement and epistemic injustice. There the core values speak:
  - **Privacy** — knowing cannot be handed over, so a subject cannot outsource its own recognition: a stance as well as a fact.
  - **Belonging** — giving belief its due: holding the community's convergence as belief, honestly, and trusting it on that footing rather than dressing it as knowledge.
  - **Creativity** — new recognition expressed and offered to the shared; more languages for a more robust convergence.
  - And the positions others may reject — the equal-footing discipline, reading on merit, a philosopher's discourse not standing outside — content by definition, because they take sides.
- **[C]** So SPLectrum's epistemology becomes a stance toward knowing rather than a theory of what knowledge is: the theory thin and common, the stance the own voice.

## 5. The realms question

- **[C]** The reality side's epistemology pillar is described as "the process of experience and learning" — "We learn from the feedback from our private experience into the (shared) language tools": almost entirely seed-level already. The same may hold for ontology ("the concepts of the language tools") and for parts of aesthetics ("the dynamics of meaning and language"). All three form the realm of *realisation* — the subject with reality through the medium — which may be largely seed seen from the content side. The realm of *coexistence* (ethics, politics) takes positions by nature, and may be where the content face is most at home. If so, the pillars would divide along the realms more than along the field's subject divisions. Recorded, not settled.
- **[C]** A related observation: on 2026-10-12 bare principle labels were removed from two content-face reality pages (discovery, evolution). The seed vocabulary showing through there may be a symptom of the same thing — seed material living on content pages.

## 6. Open questions

1. **Where knowledge lives on the seed side.** A seed page of its own, or within the seed's treatment of cognition — the site defines cognition as "the process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience", so knowledge is already cognition's product on the seed side.
2. **The third principle's wording.** "Language is where subjects share knowledge about reality." On the seat reading knowledge cannot be shared — not a parcel. What is shared is expression, the occasion to know for oneself, and belief. Whether the wording changes is a seed-level decision.
3. **The content pillar.** If the definition moves to the seed, what the reality side's epistemology becomes — the stance in the core values' vocabulary (§4) — and whether the same move applies to ontology and aesthetics (§5).
4. **Is the seed version thin enough?** A test to run: can each major epistemology — JTB, Antognazza's contact, reliabilism, knowledge-first, the pragmatists' inquiry — be read as an expression in the medium without being contradicted on its own terms?
5. **Truth.** Where truth, objectivity and correctness sit on the coin, given that convergence is "the only objectivity available": seed (the structure) or content (a reading of it).
6. **Common understanding.** How the knowing/believing seat reading meets the finding that common understanding is itself a language.
7. **Sources to read.** Antognazza, *Thinking with Assent* (OUP, 2024), itself — the review is the only thing read so far; Dutant 2015; Hart on the pleonastic fallacy in context.
