# Life and alive: the position, and how it stands against the field

*Working document, opened 2026-11-05. Keeps Jules's position apart from the analysis of it. The position is in § 1, the analysis in §§ 2 and 3, the open points in § 4.*

- **[A]** — the author's position, verbatim or near-verbatim.
- **[C]** — analysis or a formulation supplied by Claude in discussion, to be confirmed.
- **[Walker]** — her words, reported, not the position.

---

## 1. The position

- **[A]** Alive is the process running within a context.
- **[A]** Life is the artifacts created so far: the historicity of a process.
- **[A]** The contexts are processes running within a boundary, in relation to their environment.
- **[A]** A dead cat is life and not alive at the level of the cat. But within the dead cat there are plenty of contexts that are alive.
- **[A]** The universe is the perimeter: the furthest out we can imagine.
- **[A]** The position is stated within a context. It is not offered as the definition of life.
- **[A]** The boundary needs the full self-contained ecosystem. The river is out: its ecosystem would have to include the reverse flow from the river's output to its spring.
- **[A]** A phone has an internal battery that keeps a process going, much like life as we know it, and if it charges (eats) it keeps going.
- **[A]** The seed principles fit perfectly over entities that are considered alive.
- **[A]** The eventual definition of alive will be different from "assembly theory in action".
- **[A, as a question]** Would alive be akin to being an active agent in the CAS: a player in the game of "assembly theory in action"?
- **[A, as a question]** Cognition may play a key role here: the evolutionary drive to language complexity expressing the relational, the experience of reality.
- **[A]** Not all life goes after its food; some waits for it to arrive.
- **[A]** The players in the game, through their play, push it in a certain direction. That is not causal in the sense of intent.
- **[A]** A process of evolution and selection is not necessarily Darwinian. Darwinian is a narrowing of evolution and selection.
- **[A]** What Darwin thought differs from the mainstream interpretation. His thoughts were closer to agents in a CAS than to "selection of the fittest" or the "selfish gene" interpretation.
- **[A]** For the definitions of life we use the mainstream interpretation of Darwinian, as that is what is alive within our discussion context.
- **[A]** The main difference is the reference point. The mainstream centres on agents, but it is the landscape evolution that really matters.
- **[A]** A lineage is a line traced through a changing landscape.
- **[A]** Life consists of things that are alive and things that are not alive. That is not about a dead cat: things that are alive need the right not-alive substances. Evolution has created more and more of these, and on the early Earth a whole activity of generating not-alive substances made life as we know it possible. That makes the origin of life fuzzier.

## 2. Against Walker

- **[Walker]** "By 'life,' I mean all objects that can only be produced in our universe through a process of evolution and selection. Being 'alive,' by contrast, is the active implementation of the dynamics of evolution and selection" ("AI Is Life", 2023). "Some objects — like a dead cat — are representative of 'life'... but not themselves 'alive'" (same essay).
- **[C]** Kept from her: the split between life and alive, one word no longer doing two jobs.
- **[C]** Changed: both terms are redefined, so the position is Walker-inspired and must not be reported as hers.
  - Her context is "our universe"; here each context is local, and the universe is the perimeter.
  - Her "only" carries a threshold (the assembly index). Here the question is whether something came out of the process.
  - She starts from evolution and selection. Here alive comes first, and life is derived as its record.
  - Her dead cat is "not alive" as a whole object. Here alive is indexed to a boundary, so the answer depends on the boundary.

## 3. The field's test cases

**[C]** Read through the position. Alive is asked of a boundary, not of an object.

| Case | Alive | Life |
|---|---|---|
| Dead cat | not at the cat's boundary; yes within it (cells for a while, gut bacteria, decomposers) | yes |
| Living cat | yes at the cat's boundary, and within it | yes, its own record too |
| Sterile mule | yes | yes |
| Virus | only while running in a cell (one of the field's three camps, Mariscal) | yes |
| Phone, switched off | no | yes |
| Phone, running | in on the battery; open how far as a player (§ 4) | yes |
| River | no: the ecosystem isn't self-contained at the river's boundary | open |
| Water cycle | open: the reverse flow is inside this boundary (§ 4) | open |
| Mineral | no, a piece in the game rather than a player, unless a boundary is found (§ 4) | yes |

**[C]** Consequences:

- "Is it alive?" becomes "what is the boundary?". The question has no answer until the boundary is named.
- Death is a context ending, not a line through everything inside it.
- Contexts nest in both directions, up to the perimeter.

## 4. Open

- **[C]** Self-contained: the water cycle closes the river's loop, and the Gaia question sits one boundary further out. Is closure enough, or does alive need the player as well?
- **[C]** Player: what separates it from a piece. Candidate: it engages in a language of its own and has a stake in the outcome. Field neighbours: agents in complex adaptive systems (adaptive units with internal structure), Kauffman's autonomous agent ("can both reproduce itself and do at least one thermodynamic work cycle"; ant workers are Gershenson's objection).
- **[C]** Alive and cognition: identity (Maturana: "living as a process is a process of cognition"), or one process described twice, alive as the player seen from outside and cognition as the same process lived from within.
- **[C]** "Artifacts": usually read as human-made. Keep it, or use another word.
- **[C]** Emergence: kept out of the post. In the definitions note only Friston uses it for what life is ("an inevitable and emergent property"); the note did not search for it. If the meta subject wants it, it is probably an axis across the families, not a family: it names how something stands to what it arises from, not a mark.
- **[C]** Bichat is recalled as describing death as staged, the animal life ending before the organic. Unchecked; the research note quotes only his definition. If it holds, the oldest definition on the list has the nesting in it.
