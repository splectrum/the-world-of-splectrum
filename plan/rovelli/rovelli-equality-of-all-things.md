# Carlo Rovelli, *On the Equality of All Things* — discussion capture

*Chat capture, saved 2026-09-15. A book Jules encountered the day the English edition landed. Research and reading from that conversation, held for a placement decision. Provisional: the book has not been read, only its apparatus, lectures and first reviews.*

## Publication facts

- Italian original: *Sull'eguaglianza di tutte le cose*, Adelphi 2025, 214 pp., Piccola Biblioteca Adelphi 823. Listed mid-October 2025; Milan presentation at Teatro Dal Verme 22 October 2025; first reviews (ANSA, *Il Fatto*, *L'Espresso*) 17–30 October 2025.
- Subtitle *Lezioni americane* deliberately echoes Calvino — Rovelli wrote to Calvino's family for permission.
- Origin: lectures given in Princeton's philosophy department, November–December 2024.
- English edition trans. Simon Carnell: Penguin (UK, 9780241733707); Scribner (US, subtitled *Lessons on Physics and Philosophy*, 9781668092422). NPR review dated 14 September 2026.

## The title

From the preface: "On the Equality of All Things" (齊物論) is the title of the second chapter of the *Zhuangzi*, and points to the anti-foundationalism Rovelli takes modern science to lead us to. The publisher's compression: electrons and minds, stones and laws, judgements and galaxies are not essentially different in kind but notions that illuminate each other.

The preface credits **Bas van Fraassen**'s early interest in the relational interpretation (his 2010 "Rovelli's World"), and thanks Hans Halvorson, Gideon Rosen and Mark Johnston — noting the discussions were best when they disagreed.

## Structure (Italian table of contents)

1. The relational structure of reality — Earth, velocity, quanta, perspectives. The relational interpretation of QM.
2. From inside the circle — knowledge, certainty, information, circularity. The physical nature of information; "iteration of relativity".
3. Spacetime is a quantum process — time, space, gravity, networks. Neither presentism nor block universe; timeless mechanics; the conceptual structure of quantum gravity.
4. Living in the course of time — flow, memory and causes, freedom. The arrow of time; compatibility of physics with free will as commonly understood.
5. What exists? — multiplicity, unity, morals. Neither reductionism nor emergentism; unity/disunity of science; realism.
6. Without certainty or foundations — relations, fish (the *Zhuangzi* fish-happiness exchange), foundations.
- Appendix: How does science work? — continuity with common sense, historical continuity, "the obvious is misleading", modal tools not models, concepts evolve, we think in metaphors, "physics off the road". He adds that he is not convinced by how science is generally presented today.

*(Provenance note: the English back cover, ANSA and the Adelphi TOC agree on six lectures plus appendix; one Italian review says seven, counting the appendix.)*

## The argument, from the lectures and reviews

The fullest public walk-through is the Berggruen lecture at Peking University (May 2026, Roger Ames commenting).

- Physics and philosophy are reciprocal: philosophical concepts shape discovery, scientific knowledge reshapes concepts.
- Quantum properties are not properties of systems but of one system relative to another; we can only describe the world from within it.
- Three figures anchor the anti-absolutist line: **Wittgenstein, Nāgārjuna, Zhuangzi** — each questioning an absolute, externally observable reality.
- The "equality of all things" is glossed as the dissolution of metaphysical gaps: no gap between heavens and earth, human and non-human, living and non-living. Use the level of description fitting the circumstances.
- **Circularity replaces contradiction**: "the world I know is in my mind" and "I am in the world" are self-referential rather than opposed — and the same move is offered as dissolving the Confucian/Daoist tension.
- **Morals are relative but not arbitrary**: they arise through human relations and must keep evolving toward a universalism that is a shared goal rather than a single assumed perspective.

**Reviews.** Adam Frank (NPR) frames it as an explicit rejection of any path to "ultimate reality" or a God's-eye view; quotes Rovelli: "Knowing the physical laws of the world does not amount to knowing the world." Frank's substantive objection is the neglect of the primacy of experience and a hand-wave dismissal of Bergson on lived time. *Publishers Weekly* notes he builds on Kierkegaard to assert there is no independent objective reality, and singles out the final chapter — science as a constantly evolving way of organising how to live in the world. Italian reception foregrounds the Calvino lightness theme and the line that we are children of the Enlightenment but it is time to move on.

## The reading, and the difference worth keeping

The structural test passes almost line for line: "described only from its inside" against refusing the totality; no ultimate ground against how-over-what; the dissolution of metaphysical gaps as P4 in a physicist's voice; circularity-not-contradiction against reality as its expression in language; morals relative-but-not-arbitrary against values scoped to the language game.

**Two differences, located before the resonance smooths them over.**

1. **~~His equality is a *what*-claim~~ — WITHDRAWN (Jules, 2026-09-15).** The reading was that he says *is* where the discipline says *is-like*, and that keeping realism on the table in lecture five makes a metaphysical inventory. **This is a red herring.** He is not a claimer by nature — he writes *is* as any physicist writing about their subject does, and a book arguing there is no God's-eye view and no ultimate ground is not smuggling an inventory in through its verbs. Reading it as a fence would be finding a disagreement in grammar.

    The general point, which matters beyond this case: **the is-like discipline is a guard on our own writing, not a criterion for judging others.** It exists because the slide from "this resonates" to "this shows" is a failure mode on our side. Turning it into a test other thinkers pass or fail is the scoring-against-the-seed move the method notes warn against, and would manufacture fences everywhere, since almost nobody writes with that particular restraint. *(Frank's complaint about the Bergson hand-wave stands on its own as reception; it is not evidence for the withdrawn reading.)*

2. **The shape of the levelling.** His examples run "biology is a particular kind of chemistry, which can be described in physics; pick the level that fits" — equality by permission to zoom, with every level available and physics still the floor one zooms down to. Equal standing on the position's side is deviation-first: no game is the floor, and the shared is what sediments out of differences taken up. His "neither reductionism nor emergentism" is close, but the instinct for unity remains unity-of-nature, and nature arrives furnished with physics' vocabulary. His from-within is from within physics.

**Where he may be closer than the book advertises:** chapter two, on information, circularity and stable facts. His earlier work with Di Biagio on relative facts becoming stable through decoherence is very nearly the decoherence-as-belonging rhyme — minus the reading that the environment is other subjects.
