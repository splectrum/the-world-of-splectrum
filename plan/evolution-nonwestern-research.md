# Evolution outside the Western line: research note

Purpose: to record how traditions outside the Western line conceive cosmic or living change, what their own words for that change are, how the English word "evolution" was attached to them, and where (if anywhere) the evolution doorway could place any of it. This is a working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a fetched source, named in brackets by its key in the Sources list. **[U]** = unverified (memory, a search-engine summary, or a secondary paraphrase I could not open). English renderings of Chinese passages marked "my rendering" are mine, from the fetched Chinese text.

The sort used throughout is the doorway's: **unfolding** (a single thing or system changes; read as **morphology** or **arrow**), its contrary **recurrence**, and **descent with modification** (a population across generations, graded by Darwinian apparatus). The optional lens: is the account of change **closed** (given in advance: preformed, cyclical, purposive) or **open**, in its own terms.

## 1. India

### Sāṃkhya: pariṇāma

- Sāṃkhya derives twenty-three principles (tattvas) from prakṛti, the unmanifest material cause; IEP's author (Ferenc Ruzsa) renders the process "evolution (*pariṇāma*)" and says "the character of this evolution (*pariṇāma*) is somewhat vague" [C, S1].
- The causal theory behind it is satkāryavāda: in Ruzsa's words, "there is nothing absolutely new in the product: everything in it was determined by its causes" [C, S1]. On the lens, this is the clearest **closed** case in the survey: the effect pre-exists in the cause.
- Word test, verse 22 of the *Sāṃkhya Kārikā*. The Sanskrit is "prakṛteḥ mahān, tato 'haṅkāraḥ, tasmād gaṇaś ca ṣoḍaśakaḥ; tasmād api ṣoḍaśakāt pañcabhyaḥ pañca bhūtāni" — a chain of ablatives ("from prakṛti, mahat; from that, ahaṃkāra; from that, the group of sixteen; from five of those sixteen, the five elements") with **no verb at all**. A widely circulated English rendering supplies "evolves" three times ("From the Primordial Prakriti evolves the intellect…") [C, S2 for the Sanskrit; C, S3 search summary for the "evolves" rendering]. The verb is the translator's.
- Where the word pariṇāma itself appears, verse 16, it is "pariṇāmataḥ salilavat": the unmanifest operates "by transformation, like water", the one water taking different forms in different places [C, S4]. Pariṇāma in its own context is *transformation / modification of one substance into its states*, not a history of kinds.
- Pariṇāma is a technical term defined against its rival in Vedānta: **pariṇāmavāda** (the world is a real transformation of its cause) against **vivartavāda** (the cause only appears to change; "no real change takes place") [C, S5]. Sāṃkhya argues for pariṇāmavāda [C, S5].
- Is the manifestation cosmic (the world emerging once, or per cycle) or psychological (each person's experience unfolding)? Ruzsa: the cosmogonic reading "seems to predominate in later accounts" and "nicely conforms to the standard Hindu image of cosmic cycles of creation and destruction", while Īśvarakṛṣṇa himself appears "intentionally silent on this conflicting issue" [C, S1]. So even the *scale* of the process is a matter of reading.
- Placement: in its own terms pariṇāma is **unfolding** of a single substance, closest to the preformationist sense of the English word (the germ unrolling what it already contains), which is presumably why nineteenth-century translators reached for "evolution". Where read cosmogonically within the cycles, it is the unfolding phase of a **recurrence**. It has no population and no heredity.

### Purāṇic cosmic cycles: yugas, kalpas, pralaya

- Four yugas (Kṛta/Satya, Tretā, Dvāpara, Kali) in the ratio 4:3:2:1, totalling 4,320,000 years; a kalpa (a day of Brahmā) is 1,000 such cycles; each is followed by a pralaya (dissolution) of equal length; the cycle repeats indefinitely [C, S6, Wikipedia, which cites Manusmṛti, Mahābhārata and Purāṇas; the primary texts themselves not fetched].
- Within the cycle the direction is **decline**: dharma diminishes from yuga to yuga, and at the end of the Kali yuga a cataclysm restores the Kṛta age [C, S6].
- Placement: the clearest Indian case of **recurrence**, with a built-in arrow of decline inside each turn. **Closed** (the course is fixed in advance, by number).

### Buddhist accounts

- **Pratītyasamutpāda** (dependent origination). General formula: "When this exists, that comes to be. With the arising of this, that arises…" (Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.61) [C, S7]. Its twelve-link form (ignorance … birth, ageing-and-death) was read by the commentarial tradition across three lives [C, S7]. The early texts present it as standing "whether or not there are Buddhas who see it", with no creator [C, S7]. It is an account of how suffering and rebirth are conditioned, not of how kinds of living things arose. On the sort it is neither sense; it is closest to the doorway's "metaphysical uses" in that it is a general principle of conditioned change, and its twelve-link application is a **recurrence** (the round of rebirth). **Open** or **closed** does not map well: it is lawlike but has no fixed goal except its cessation.
- **Aggañña Sutta** (Dīgha Nikāya 27) — a better lead than dependent origination for "cosmic change", not in the original lead list. The world "shrinks" and expands over very long periods; at the start of an expansion beings are "mind-made, feeding on delight, self-luminous"; a "savory earth" spreads over the waters; as beings eat it their bodies grow coarser; sexual difference, property, theft and finally kingship and the four social classes follow [C, S8, Wikipedia summary of the sutta; the Pāli/translation not fetched — SuttaCentral blocked]. The direction is **descent from a higher state**, not ascent. Richard Gombrich reads it as satire of brahminical claims about the divine origin of the varṇas; Steven Collins as a parable with "good humoured irony" [C, S8]. Some modern readers compare the sequence to evolution; Wikipedia records that this is debated [C, S8].
- Placement: **recurrence** (expanding and contracting worlds) with **devolution** inside the turn. It is a counter-case worth having on the page if anywhere: an origin story whose arrow points down.

### Daśāvatāra read as evolution (reception)

- Word test: *avatāra* is from *ava-* "down" + *tṛ* "to cross": a **descent** of a deity into a form; the term is post-Vedic, Purāṇic [C, S9]. The Bhagavad Gītā (4.7–8) gives the purpose: whenever dharma declines, "I body Myself forth … I manifest Myself from age to age" [C, S9]. In its own terms the sequence fish, tortoise, boar, man-lion, dwarf … is a series of divine descents to restore order within the yuga cycle, not a series of ascents of animal forms. The lists also vary (Buddha or Balarāma, regional substitutes) [C, S10].
- Reception, with dates: Blavatsky, *Isis Unveiled* (1877), presented the avatars as "an allegorical presentation of Darwinian evolution" [C, S10]; Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura (1873–80) proposed ascending complexity [C, S10]; Keshub Chunder Sen's Calcutta lecture of 1882, "That Marvellous Mystery, the Trinity": "The Hindu Avatar rises from the lowest scale of life through the fish, the tortoise, and the hog up to the perfection of humanity" [C, S10; the lecture itself not fetched]. Monier-Williams wrote that Hindus were "Darwinians centuries before the birth of Darwin" [C, S10]. J. B. S. Haldane gave it as a "rough idea" of vertebrate sequence [C, S10].
- Critics and historians: C. Mackenzie Brown names the view "avataric evolutionism" and traces it to Western roots in colonial India, Keshub being apparently the first Indian to develop it; a second article follows N. B. Pavgee and Aurobindo [C, S11, abstracts via search; articles not opened — Wiley 403]. Meera Nanda counters that Hinduism teaches "endless cycles of creationism" [C, S10]. Some Vaiṣṇavas reject avataric evolutionism as ranking Rāma and Kṛṣṇa in a hierarchy [C, S10]; ISKCON's founder was openly anti-Darwinian [C, S12].
- Placement: a **reception** case for "The word's travels", not a tradition's own account of evolution. No priority.

### Aurobindo: the evolution of consciousness

- Aurobindo (1872–1950) wrote in English and used "evolution" as his own word. Involution is the Divine concealing itself by stages down to the apparent "Inconscience" of Matter; evolution is "the inverse action of the involution", consciousness emerging through Matter, Life, Mind toward Supermind [C, S13, Wikipedia article "Evolution (Sri Aurobindo)"]. A much-cited sentence from the first chapter of *The Life Divine*: "If it be true that Spirit is involved in Matter and apparent Nature is secret God, then the manifestation of the divine in himself and the realisation of God within and without are the highest and most legitimate aim possible to man upon earth" [U — found only as a search-result quotation; check against the book].
- Brown treats Aurobindo as an elaboration of avataric evolutionism [C, S11 abstract]. Vivekananda and Aurobindo are both described as joining an involution of the Divine to an evolution over many lives [C, S12].
- Placement: Aurobindo belongs with the **metaphysical uses** on the existing [Evolution in metaphysics](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/metaphysics/) page (beside Bergson, Teilhard, emergent evolution): an arrow, **closed** (the end is what was involved at the start). Because he wrote in English and chose the word, he is not a word-test problem; he is the one non-Western case in the survey that *uses* the word in the doorway's own sense of a metaphysical evolution.

## 2. China

### The Zhuangzi "Zhile" chain and its readers

- The passage (end of chapter 18, 至樂 "Perfect Happiness/Enjoyment"): "種有幾，得水則為㡭 … 久竹生青寧，青寧生程，程生馬，馬生人，人又反入於機。萬物皆出於機，皆入於機。" [C, S14]. My rendering, kept literal: "Seeds/kinds have *ji*; getting water they become [a water plant] … old bamboo produces *qingning*, *qingning* produces *cheng*, *cheng* produces the horse, the horse produces man; man again returns into the *ji*. The myriad things all come out of the *ji* and all go back into the *ji*."
- What the chain contains: plant becoming insect, insect becoming bird, butterfly turning into a grub, things "born from" rot and from old bamboo [C, S14]. It is a catalogue of metamorphosis and spontaneous generation, ending in a **return**, not a lineage.
- Word test, Legge (1891): "Man then again enters into the great Machinery (of Evolution), from which all things come forth (at birth), and which they enter at death" [C, S15]. "(of Evolution)" is Legge's parenthetical insertion; the Chinese has only 機 *ji*. Legge's own footnote compares the passage to Buddhist teaching on rebirth and calls it somewhat "absurdly stated" [C, S15] — so the first English translator both supplied the word and did not think the passage was a theory of descent.
- Word test, Hu Shi. In his *墨家哲學* (Mohist Philosophy, c. 1919) Hu Shi speaks of "a school of the theory of biological evolution" (一派生物進化論) of the Zhuangzi period, quoting the Yuyan chapter "萬物皆種也，以不同形相禪" and the Zhile "種有幾" [C, S16]. He emends the text: "萬物皆出於幾（今作機，誤）" — "now written 機, wrongly" — reading 幾 as "an extremely minute seed" whose graph "originally depicts an embryo" [C, S16]. He reads the chain as progress "step by step from the lowest micro-organisms to the highest, man" and adds a potential/manifest (潛性/顯性) gloss [C, S16]. The emendation is what makes 幾 a germ.
- Omitted context: the Yuyan sentence Hu Shi quotes continues "始卒若環，莫得其倫，是謂天均" — "beginning and end are like a ring; none can find its order; this is called the Heavenly Potter's Wheel/Equaliser" (my rendering) [C, S17]. In its own context the line is a figure of **recurrence**, not of ascent.
- Hu Shi's *Outline of the History of Chinese Philosophy* (中國哲學史大綱, 1919) has a chapter "莊子時代的生物進化論" ("The theory of biological evolution in Zhuangzi's time"), and is reported to say that Zhuangzi "knew the principle of evolution but unfortunately regarded it as the natural course of Heaven", leaving human effort no role [U — chapter title and sentence from search summaries; the book not fetched]. A modern study (OUP, "Hu Shi: Biological Evolutionism and Zhuangzi") treats Hu Shi's reading as May Fourth reception, Hu ultimately calling Zhuangzi an obstacle to progress [U — chapter abstract not opened; search summary only].
- Placement: the Zhuangzi passage is a reception case, with a clean word-test story at both ends (Legge's parenthesis, Hu Shi's emendation). In its own terms it is **transformation (化) with return**: a recurrence of forms out of and back into *ji*. **Open** in one sense (no fixed sequence or goal is claimed; the chain is wild) and cyclical in another. The site already has a [Zhuangzi](/positioning/persons/z/zhuangzi/) person page.

### The Yijing and the vocabulary of change

- The *Xici* commentary: "生生之謂易" ("generating and generating again is called change/the Changes"), "變化者，進退之象也" ("*bianhua* is the image of advancing and withdrawing"), "易窮則變，變則通，通則久" ("when change reaches its limit it alters; altering, it gets through; getting through, it endures"), "變動不居，周流六虛" ("it alters and moves without rest, flowing round the six empty places") [C, S18; renderings mine].
- The vocabulary therefore names change as alternation and circulation: *bian* 變 (alteration), *hua* 化 (transformation), *bianhua* 變化 explicitly glossed as advance-and-withdraw, *shengsheng* 生生 continual generation. SEP's entry describes the cosmos of the *Yijing* as "an organismic process without beginning or end" [C, S19].
- Placement: neither unfolding of one history nor descent; a **process cosmology of alternation**, closest to recurrence but without a fixed cycle length or a decline. Better placed (if anywhere) as a contrast on the progress-and-direction page's recurrence section than as an "evolution".

### Neo-Confucian qi (Zhang Zai)

- Zhang Zai (1020–1077): all things are condensations of *qi*; dispersed *qi* is the Great Vacuity; "What looks like creation and destruction is just the never-ending movements of *qi*"; there is no creation *ex nihilo* [C, S20, IEP].
- Shao Yong's numerical world-cycle (the *yuan* of 129,600 years) is a further Song-dynasty recurrence scheme [U — memory; not checked].
- Placement: **recurrence** of condensation and dispersal; no arrow. Not a candidate for the evolution doorway except as the Chinese background against which *tianyan* and *jinhua* were coined.

### The word's arrival (summary; detail in "The word's travels")

Yan Fu's *Tianyan lun* (1898) rendered "evolution" as 天演; the Japanese coinage 進化 (*jinhua*) displaced it. See below.

## 3. The Islamic world

The four medieval cases below are all cited, in modern apologetic and popular literature, as having anticipated Darwin. The most thorough philological critique found is Shoaib Ahmed Malik's chapter "Old texts, new masks" (*Islam and Evolution*, Routledge 2021, open access), which reads each passage in context and argues that all belong to the scala naturae / Great Chain of Being, not to evolution [C, S21]. Malik's caution is that "what seems like indications of evolution are in fact decontextualised interpretations" [C, S21].

### al-Jāḥiẓ, *Kitāb al-Ḥayawān* (9th century)

- The claim: Mehmet Bayrakdar, "Al-Jahiz and the Rise of Biological Evolutionism" (*Islamic Quarterly*, 1983), credits al-Jāḥiẓ with struggle for existence, transformation of species and environmental factors, and influence on Lamarck and Darwin [C, S22]. Conway Zirkle (1941) had cited a passage of his as the only relevant Arabic one he found on struggle [C, S23, Wikipedia].
- What the text says, on the transformation claim. Bayrakdar's key quotation (vol. IV p. 23): "People said different things about … al-miskh (= original form of quadrupeds). Some accepted its evolution and said that it gave existence to dog, wolf, fox…" [C, S22]. Malik gives the full paragraph from the 1938 edition: "The people said different things about the miskh. Some of them said that the miskh doesn't reproduce or doesn't remain … except as a lesson to mankind … And some of them said that the miskh does remain. So they considered lizards, eels, rabbits, dogs, and other animals from the descendents of those that were metamorphosed into that form" [C, S21]. Malik's three corrections: *miskh/maskh* is not "quadruped" but the **metamorphosis** of people into animals, from the Qur'anic account of a group turned into apes and pigs as divine punishment (Q 5:60, 2:65, 7:166); the discussion is confined to that topic; and al-Jāḥiẓ is reporting two opinions, not stating his own [C, S21].
- Word test: Bayrakdar's "Some accepted its **evolution**" renders a report about divine metamorphosis-as-punishment. The original term is *maskh*; its meaning in context is a transformation imposed on sinners, the opposite of a natural descent.
- Al-Jāḥiẓ's own position on species, as summarised by M. Manṣūr (1977, quoted in Malik): resemblance between man and monkey "does not go beyond the limit of resemblance"; "al-Jahiz completely rejected the possibility of the transformation of one species to another" [C, S21].
- What survives scrutiny: food chains ("neither can the hunting animal escape being hunted in his turn", via Egerton 2002) and attention to environmental effects [C, S21]; Egerton calls the early-evolutionist claim "unconvincing" but the narrower environmental claim valid [C, S23]. Rebecca Stott (2013): al-Jāḥiẓ "was not trying to work out how the world began or how species had come to be. He believed that God had done the making"; historians who credit him with evolution "have misunderstood" [C, S23]. Herbert Eisenstein: the book's goal was "a proof of the existence of the Creator that is evident from his creation" [C, S24 search summary; Eisenstein not opened — treat as U for exact wording]. Paul Braterman, writing for a Muslim-heritage site, still reads the dogs–wolves–foxes lines as common descent, "a few lines out of his massive written output" [C, S25] — Malik's reading of the same passage answers this.
- Placement: on the sort, the passage is about neither unfolding nor descent but about *maskh*, a third thing (punitive metamorphosis). Report as reception with critics. No priority.

### Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ (Brethren of Purity, 10th century)

- Their cosmos emanates through intermediaries down to the three kingdoms (mineral, plant, animal) and man; "A rigid hierarchy of universe links each level of existence … to that which precedes and that which follows it"; the SEP entry notes that its account "may question the idea of 'evolution' in the Ikhwān that some scholars have ascribed to them" [C, S26].
- They do give a **temporal** order (plants before animals, sea animals before land animals, lower before more perfect), which Majid Fakhry called "amounting almost to an anticipation of Darwinian evolution" [C, S21 quoting Fakhry 2004]. But they hold species fixed: "The species and genus are definite and preserved. Their forms are in matter. But the individuals are in perpetual flow" (quoted via Nasr 1978) [C, S21].
- Placement: an ordered **unfolding** of a created hierarchy, temporal as well as ontological, with fixed kinds; **closed** (purposive: everything below exists for man, and man's "evolution" is inward, a return to God, per Nasr quoted in Malik) [C, S21]. The temporal order is the genuine point of interest and should be stated as theirs, without "anticipation".

### Ibn Miskawayh (d. 1030)

- Attributed works: *al-Fawz al-Aṣghar* and/or *al-Fawz al-Akbar*; sources disagree on which carries the graded series [C, S27 (Wikipedia names *al-Fawz al-Akbar*); C, S28 (Sultan Shah names *al-Fawz al-Aṣghar*)]. The series runs from intellect emanating from God through minerals, plants, animals (ape at the "frontier of humanity") to man, and back to the source — Wikipedia's phrase is "a cyclic spiritual evolution back to the source" [C, S27]. Muhammad Hamidullah and Sami Hawi are the modern readers who called this evolution [C, S27, S23]. 
- I did not reach a translation of Miskawayh himself [U for any wording attributed to him]. The emanation-and-return frame places him with the Ikhwān.

### Ibn Khaldūn, *Muqaddimah* (1377)

- The passage (Rosenthal translation): "One should then look at the world of creation. It started out from the minerals and progressed, in an ingenious, gradual manner, to plants and animals. The last stage of minerals is connected with the first stage of plants … The word 'connection' with regard to these created things means that the last stage of each group is fully prepared to become the first stage of the next group. The animal world then widens, its species become numerous, and, in a gradual process of creation, it finally leads to man … The higher stage of man is reached from the world of the monkeys … This is as far as our (physical) observation extends" [C, S29]. Rosenthal's note on "stage": "Lit., 'horizon'" (Arabic *ufuq*) [C, S29].
- Context: the passage sits under the heading "The real meaning of prophecy" in the sixth prefatory discussion of chapter 1; it opens with the elements arranged "in an ascending order, from earth to water, … to air, and … to fire" and the spheres, and the next paragraph continues upward: the soul "must be prepared to exchange humanity for angelicality" [C, S29]. The series is the set-up for an account of how prophets cross into the angelic and return.
- Reception and critique: Malik et al. (2017) took it as Ibn Khaldūn's belief "that humans themselves evolved specifically from an ape/monkey ancestor"; Malik (2021) answers that advocates of that reading "would have to demonstrate what relevance souls and angels have" and concludes he is describing the gradation of the Chain of Being [C, S21]. Wikipedia's article summarises the same critique ("the species … themselves are eternal and fixed") [C, S23].
- Word test: "connected" (*ittiṣāl*) and "horizon" (*ufuq*) are the original idiom; "fully prepared" (*istiʿdād*, preparedness) [U for the Arabic of the last term — Rosenthal's English checked, the Arabic not]. None is a word for descent.
- Placement: an ordered **ladder** read *upward*, the scala naturae; **closed**, purposive. The site already carries Ibn Khaldūn's dynastic cycle on [the speculative tradition](/positioning/subjects/p/philosophy-of-history/the-speculative-tradition/) page; that is his recurrence, a separate matter from this passage.

### Rūmī, *Masnavī* III 3901–3906

- Nicholson-based translation (Gamard): "I died to the mineral state and became a plant; I died to the vegetable state and reached animality; I died to the animal state and became a man; then what should I fear?" continuing to death to humanity "so that I may lift up (my) head and wings (and soar) among the angels", and finally to non-existence, which says "Truly, to Him is our return" [C, S30] (the phrase echoes Qurʾān 2:156 [U]). The key Persian terms: *jamādī* (mineral state), *nāmī* (growing), *ḥaywān*, *bashar*, *malāʾik*, *ʿadam* [C, S30].
- The translator's note: "This is not transmigration, reincarnation, or an early view of biological evolution" but the descent of the spirit into matter and its re-ascent, after William Chittick [C, S30]. Malik quotes Chittick: "every 'evolution' demands a prior 'devolution'"; the frame is *mabdaʾ wa maʿād*, origin and return [C, S21].
- A second oft-quoted Rūmī stanza circulates in a double translation (Persian → Arabic dissertation → English) missing four couplets about the disciple and the master; Nicholson's version includes them [C, S21].
- Placement: **recurrence** at the scale of the soul (descent and return). Not a claim about species.

### Nineteenth- and early-twentieth-century reception of Darwin

- John William Draper, *History of the Conflict between Religion and Science* (1874/5), spoke of "the Mohammedan theory of the evolution of man from lower forms" and said "our modern doctrines of evolution and development were taught in their schools" [C, S21 quoting Draper]. This is an early **Western** source of the priority claim, later cited approvingly by Muslim advocates (Shanavas 2010) [C, S21].
- Jamāl al-Dīn al-Afghānī, *al-Radd ʿalā al-dahriyyīn* (Refutation of the Materialists, 1881, in Persian), opposed Darwin [C, S31]; Wikipedia gives his line "Only the imperfect resemblance between man and monkey has cast this unfortunate man into the desert of fantasies", and records that he later attributed evolution to medieval Muslim thinkers [C, S23]. Malik names al-Afghānī as the precedent for that strategy and cites Elshakry for its history [C, S21].
- Shiblī Shumayyil published an Arabic rendering of Ludwig Büchner's lectures on Darwin in 1884 [C, S31]. Its title differs between sources: *Falsafat al-nushūʾ wa-l-irtiqāʾ* [C, S31] or *Sharḥ Bukhnir ʿalā madhhab Dārwīn*, with *Falsafat al-nushūʾ wa-l-irtiqāʾ* the title of the 1910 edition [U — search summary of a Zygon article]. Unresolved.
- Ismāʿīl Maẓhar translated the first chapters of the *Origin* in 1918; the full translation appeared only in 1964 [U — search summary of Elshakry material; not opened].
- Ḥusayn al-Jisr (Ottoman-Lebanese Sunni scholar) held there is no contradiction between evolution and scripture, since the Qurʾān does not say whether species were created at once or gradually [C, S23]; his treatise was translated into Ottoman Turkish in 1891 [C, S32].

## 4. Elsewhere

### Hawaiʻi: the Kumulipo

- A cosmogonic genealogy (*mele koʻihonua*) in sixteen *wā* (eras), the first part in *pō* (night/darkness), the later in *ao* (light) [C, S33]. It was composed for the chief Ka-ʻĪ-i-mamao (Lonoikamakahiki); the 1897 title page gives its composer as Keaulumoku "in 1700" [C, S34 — the date is the 1897 edition's, not independently established]. Kalākaua printed the Hawaiian text in 1889 to establish his descent from the ancient chiefs; Liliʻuokalani translated it during her imprisonment (1895) and published it in 1897 [C, S34].
- Its own idiom is birth: "Kumulipo was born in the night, a male. / Poʻele was born in the night, a female. / A coral insect was born, from which was born perforated coral …"; species are paired across sea and land ("The Akaha's home was the sea; / Guarded by the Ekahakaha that grew in the forest") [C, S34]. The sequence moves through sea creatures to land plants and animals and eventually to gods and the chiefly line [C, S33].
- Reception: Liliʻuokalani herself wrote in 1897 that "curious students will notice in this chant analogies between its accounts of the creation and that given by modern science or Sacred Scripture" [C, S34] — the comparison is the translator's, made in a political setting (her preface's editor notes the publication refuted the annexationist claim that Hawaiians had no culture) [C, S34]. Adolf Bastian, the first European to publish on it (*Die heilige Sage der Polynesier*, 1881), read it through a European world-conflagration motif [U — search summary]. The 1997 foreword by Pualani Kanakaʻole Kanahele uses "evolution" herself: the chant "maintained the theme of survival, urgency for life and preservation of the species with procreation and evolution as the sinew" [C, S34]. So the word now has an insider user, which the note should report as such.
- Placement: this is the one case whose **own idiom is descent** — literal genealogical descent by birth, lineage by lineage — and it does sequence sea before land and simple before complex. But it is a chiefly genealogy whose purpose is to connect a ruler to the origin, with no variation, heredity-as-mechanism or selection; and the foreword stresses a cycle ("returns again to repeat the cycle with the sky and earth") [C, S34]. On the sort it sits between: genealogical descent in form, cosmogony in purpose. **Closed** in the sense that it ends where it is aimed, the chief.

### Mesoamerica: successive suns and failed humanities

- Nahua "Five Suns": four earlier worlds, each ended by catastrophe, the present (fifth) sun destined to end in earthquake [C, S35]. Per the Wikipedia summary, people of the second sun were "turned into monkeys", survivors of the third "turned into birds", people of the fourth flood "into fish" [C, S35]. The order of the suns differs between sources (the *Leyenda de los Soles*, 1558, and the *Historia de los mexicanos por sus pinturas*) [U — not checked].
- K'iche' Maya *Popol Vuh* (manuscript copied by Francisco Ximénez c. 1701): attempts at humans from mud (crumbled) and from wood (destroyed by flood); "those that survived became monkeys"; humans successfully made from maize [C, S36].
- Placement: **recurrence** (successive creations and destructions), **closed** (each world ends by divine act). The striking feature is the **inverse direction**: animals as the remnants of earlier, failed humanities. A counter-case worth stating plainly if the recurrence section is expanded. No one I found presents these as anticipations of evolution in serious literature.

### Africa

Looked at properly; the result is that the leads are about **recurrence** and creation, not about evolution, and the best-known "cosmology" (Dogon) is unreliable.

- **Dogon.** Walter van Beek's restudy (*Current Anthropology* 32, 1991) found the cosmology of Griaule's *Dieu d'eau* (1948) and *Le renard pâle* "impossible to replicate in the field": "The Dogon know no proper creation myth; neither the version of Ogotemmêli nor that of the Renard pâle is recognizable to informants … That Sirius is a double star is unknown … The water spirit Nommo is not a central figure in Dogon thought" [C, S37]. Any placement built on Griaule's material should be discarded.
- **Yoruba.** Creation is a single act: Ọbàtálá (or, in the Ifẹ version, Odùduwà) descends a chain with sand and a hen that scatters it over the primordial water, founding Ilé-Ifẹ̀ [C, S38 search summary of several sources; treat as U for detail]. Change at the scale of a person is **familial reincarnation**, *àtúnwáyé*; names such as Babátúndé ("father has come again") and Yétúndé ("mother has come again") mark an ancestor's return [C, S38]. This is recurrence along a lineage.
- **Kongo.** The *dikenga* cosmogram ("the turning") maps the sun's four moments onto conception, birth, maturity and death, and the process repeats: the righteous are reborn "like the sun" [C, S39, via the Wikipedia/Thompson summary; Fu-Kiau and Thompson's *The Four Moments of the Sun* (1981) not opened]. A **recurrence** model of all becoming.
- **Mbiti and African time.** John Mbiti's *African Religions and Philosophy* (1969) held that time is two-dimensional, a long past (*zamani*) and a dynamic present (*sasa*), with "virtually no future" [C, S40 search summaries — Mbiti's own text not opened]. Kwame Gyekye (Akan time is three-dimensional) and D. A. Masolo rejected this as overgeneralisation from East African languages [C, S40]. Relevant only as a warning: generalisations about "African conceptions of change" are themselves contested.
- No pre-colonial African tradition was found that has been claimed, in serious literature, to anticipate evolution, and none whose own terms support placement on the evolution sort. The reception of Darwin in African languages (e.g. Swahili *mageuzi*, "transformation") was not researched [U — memory].

## 5. The word's travels

The cleanest finding of the round: in every language checked, the word chosen for "evolution" carried a **direction** in its own root — ascent, perfecting, advance — which the Darwinian theory does not require. The choices are facts about translators.

- **Sanskrit and Indian languages.** Nineteenth-century English writers rendered *pariṇāma* as "evolution" (Ruzsa still does, as convention) [C, S1]; the reverse direction, Darwin's word into Indian languages, uses new compounds: Hindi *vikās* / *vikāsvād* ("development, expansion" / "-ism") and *kramik vikās* ("gradual development"); Marathi *utkrānti* / *utkrāntivād*; Bengali *bibartan* (বিবর্তন) / *bibartanbād* [C, S41, dictionary summaries]. Bengali *bibartan* shares its root (*vi-vṛt*) with Vedānta's *vivarta*, the "apparent transformation" defined *against* pariṇāma [C, S5 for vivarta; the etymological link and who coined *bibartan* are U]. If confirmed, Bengali chose for Darwin the root that Indian philosophy used for change that is not real.
- **Chinese.** Yan Fu's *Tianyan lun* 天演論 (1898) rendered Huxley's *Evolution and Ethics* (1893–94) with heavy commentary drawing on Spencer [C, S42 for title and translation; C, S43 for Spencer and Daoist reinterpretation via Laozi and Wang Bi, search summaries]. *Tian* is heaven/nature; *yan* is to perform, unfold, develop [C, S44]; the title reads something like "the unfolding of Heaven". The preface to the same book states his three difficulties of translation, faithfulness, comprehensibility, elegance (信達雅) [C, S42]. 天擇 *tianze* ("Heaven's selection") rendered natural selection [C, S42]. Wang Guowei, in "On the importation of new learned terms" (論新學語之輸入, 1905), set 天演 against 進化 and judged the Japanese term clearer [C, S45]. Most of Yan Fu's coinages lost to Japanese ones; Shen Guowei notes Yan Fu himself already used 進化 several times in *Tianyan lun* [C, S45]. Wiktionary now gives 天演 as "a dated term for evolution" [C, S44].
- **Japanese.** 進化 *shinka* (advance + transform). Candidates in the early Meiji years included 変遷 *hensen* (change), 化醇 *kajun* and 進化; Inoue Tetsujirō is reported first to use 進化 in May 1878; the *Tetsugaku jii* (1881) lists both 化醇論 and 進化論 for "theory of evolution"; Katō Hiroyuki's *Jinken shinsetsu* (1882) used 進化 [C, S46 (Ohori essay), S47 (Li Dongmu), with S47 holding that 進化 is almost certainly Katō's coinage "in the course of his argument" — the two sources disagree on the coiner]. Edward S. Morse lectured on Darwin in Tokyo from 1877; his student Ishikawa Chiyomatsu published the lectures as *Dōbutsu shinkaron* (1883) [C, S48 search summary — treat as U for exact date]. The word arrived through social theory more than biology: by 1888 there were 31 translated works of Spencer against 4 of Darwin [C, S46]. And the directional load was felt at once: Ōhara Shōichi (1902) warned "Though the terms shinka and shimpo [progress] are at times used as synonyms, this is a great error … Things both progress (shinpo) and degenerate (taika), this is the condition of evolution (shinka)" [C, S49]. 進化 then passed into Chinese as *jinhua*, a Japanese-made Sino-Japanese word (wasei kango) [C, S44, S47].
- **Arabic.** Terms used include *taraqqī* and *irtiqāʾ* ("ascent, progress"), *nushūʾ* ("growth, development"), the phrase *madhhab al-nushūʾ wa-l-taraqqī/irtiqāʾ*, and later *taṭawwur*, the standard modern term; also *taraqqī ilā l-kamāl* ("progress toward perfection") [C, S31 for the terms as listed; that *taṭawwur* became standard is C via a search summary of Elshakry, S50]. Elshakry's article "Knowledge in Motion" (*Isis* 2008) is the authority on the choices [not opened].
- **Ottoman Turkish.** Daniel Kolland (*Modern Intellectual History*, 2024) documents the sequence. Earlier renderings: *inkılābāt* ("total transformations"), *neşv ü nemā* ("emergence and growth" — "Darwin's theory of evolution [neşv ü nemā nazarīyesi] in which the simple progresses to the complex", 1889); a 1891 technical dictionary gave *devir* and *inkişāf* [C, S32]. Abdullah Cevdet in 1892: "previously translated this word [évolution] as 'continuous growth' [nemā-yı mütevālī] … I now prefer the word 'mutual perfecting' [tekāmül]" [C, S32]. *Tekâmül* derives from the Arabic root *k-m-l*, "to become perfect", the root of the Sufi *al-insān al-kāmil* (the perfect human); it was not a technical term in Islamic philosophy and is absent from Ottoman Sufi vocabulary; its first dictionary entry is Şemseddin Sami's 1905 dictionary (*tekâmül-i uzvî* for *évolution organique*) [C, S32]. Kolland's three explanations for the choice: the root's resonance, perfecting in Lamarck and Spencer, and the translators' preference for "a much more teleological and normative reading of the evolutionary process" — while the evolutionists themselves explicitly *silenced* the Sufi layer, presenting *tekâmül* as natural law from Western science alone [C, S32]. Modern Turkish *evrim* replaced *tekâmül* in the language reform [U — not checked].

Shape of the finding: Chinese chose *Heaven's unfolding*, Japanese *advance-transform*, Arabic *ascent/growth* then *development*, Ottoman *perfecting*, Hindi *development*, Bengali possibly a root of *apparent change*. The English word already carried two senses (unrolling and descent, per the site's [The word](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/the-word/) page); the receiving languages mostly translated it as the **arrow**, and the Japanese warning about *shinka* versus *shinpo* repeats, within a decade, the Western argument on the [Progress and direction](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/progress-and-direction/) page.

## 6. Where the sort fits and where it does not

**Fits, on the tradition's own terms:**
- **Recurrence** is the dominant shape outside the Western line: yugas and kalpas; Aggañña's expanding and contracting worlds; the Zhuangzi's ring (始卒若環) and return into *ji*; Zhang Zai's condensation and dispersal; Nahua suns; Kongo *dikenga*; Yoruba *àtúnwáyé*; Rūmī's origin and return. The doorway already names recurrence as the contrary of unfolding; these cases are well-attested members of it.
- **Unfolding of a single substance** fits Sāṃkhya pariṇāma (closed, satkāryavāda), close to the preformationist sense of the English word.
- **Metaphysical evolution** fits Aurobindo, who chose the word.

**Does not fit:**
- **The scala naturae** (Ikhwān, Miskawayh, Ibn Khaldūn, Rūmī's ladder) is neither unfolding nor descent: an ordered hierarchy of fixed kinds, read upward, often with a temporal order of appearance (the Ikhwān) but no transformation of kinds. It is the sort's blind spot. The Western scala naturae (Lovejoy's great chain) has the same status, so this is not a non-Western peculiarity — but the doorway does not currently name the ladder at all.
- **Devolution** — Aggañña, the yuga decline, Rūmī's/Chittick's "prior devolution", the Nahua and Maya humanities turned into animals — is an arrow pointing down. The doorway's arrow is "aimed or aimless" but tacitly upward; descent-from-perfection is not named.
- **Punitive metamorphosis** (*maskh*) is a third kind of change of kind: one-off, divinely imposed, individual. It fits nowhere on the sort and should not be forced.
- **Genealogical descent** (Kumulipo) has descent's form (birth from parents across generations) without the population or the mechanism. The doorway's descent sense is graded by Darwinian apparatus; the Kumulipo would sit at zero apparatus while being, in idiom, the most "descent-like" text in the survey.
- **Alternation without a cycle** (*Yijing* 變化 as advance-and-withdraw, 生生) is neither an arrow nor a fixed recurrence.

**Lens result:** closed accounts dominate (satkāryavāda, numbered yugas, purposive chains, return to God, genealogy aimed at a chief). The Zhuangzi chain is the most open in texture (wild, unsystematic), but it closes in a return.

## 7. Placement proposal (options, not decisions)

1. **Nowhere for the traditions themselves; one place for the word.** The evolution doorway is a doorway on the *word*; none of the traditions used the word, and the sort mostly does not fit them. The defensible minimum is to place nothing about them and to carry only the word's travels (section 5), which is squarely about the word.

2. **The word's travels on the translation-theory bundle.** [The words or the sense](/positioning/subjects/t/translation-theory/the-words-or-the-sense/) already has Yan Fu's three difficulties, stated in the preface of the very book that coined 天演. A paragraph there on *tianyan* versus *jinhua* (Wang Guowei 1905), with *tekâmül* as a second case of a translator's root choice, would be well sourced (Li Dongmu, Kolland). Alternatively [The foreign and the home](/positioning/subjects/t/translation-theory/the-foreign-and-the-home/), since *tekâmül* is a domestication its users then denied. A link from the evolution doorway's see-also already exists (Translation theory).

3. **The word's travels on the evolution page [The word](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/the-word/).** A closing section, "The word abroad", of 200–300 words: the receiving languages chose the arrow (Heaven's unfolding, advance-transform, perfecting, ascent), with Ōhara's *shinka/shinpo* warning as the mirror of the Western progress argument. This keeps the doorway self-contained on its own subject.

4. **Recurrence material on [Progress and direction](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/progress-and-direction/).** Its "contrary: recurrence" section currently has Hutton and Vico. One paragraph could add that recurrence is the more widely attested shape outside the Western line (yugas/kalpas, Aggañña, Zhang Zai, the Nahua suns), with Aggañña or the yuga decline as the case of an arrow pointing *down* inside the cycle. Guard: keep it short and descriptive; the page is about the arrow's inheritance, not a world survey.

5. **Aurobindo on [Evolution in metaphysics](/positioning/subjects/e/evolution/metaphysics/).** A short entry beside Bergson and Teilhard: involution and evolution, Matter–Life–Mind–Supermind. He uses the word, so the word test is satisfied. Check *The Life Divine* directly before writing.

6. **A new line page, "Anticipations" or "Read back into older texts".** One page on the reception pattern: Draper's "Mohammedan theory of evolution", avataric evolutionism (Blavatsky, Keshub, Monier-Williams), Hu Shi's Zhuangzi, al-Jāḥiẓ via Bayrakdar, each with the textual correction (*maskh*, the Yuyan ring, Legge's parenthesis, avatāra as descent, Ibn Khaldūn's prophecy context). It is strong material and well sourced, but it is a page *about* priority claims and risks reading as adjudication; the site's register would need it to describe the readings and the texts side by side and let the texts' own terms stand. Weigh against option 1.

7. **The scala naturae as a gap in the doorway.** Independently of the non-Western material, the doorway may want to name the ladder (Aristotle, the great chain, the Ikhwān and Ibn Khaldūn) as the thing that most often gets mistaken for evolution's arrow. This is a question for the doorway's own design rather than for this round.

Discard list (do not place): Griaule's Dogon cosmology; Sāṃkhya, the Zhuangzi, al-Jāḥiẓ, Ibn Khaldūn, Rūmī, the Daśāvatāra or the Kumulipo as "evolution" in their own terms; any "first" or "before Darwin" framing.
