# Proposal: wider landscape piece on evolution

Research basis: `evolution-homes-analysis.md`, which grids every home against the questions below and cross-reads the grid. The findings it numbers are cited here as (A1), (A2), and so on.

## The subject and its centre of gravity

This is the first wider-landscape piece. It takes the homes gathered on the evolution subject and classifies them by putting the same questions to every home. The homes are tied together without being unified. All of them answer the same questions, but their answers stay plural, and the disputes within and between homes stay open.

The questions, in this order:

1. **Where does change come from?** The source, mechanism or process of change.
2. **What changes and what does it carry, read from where?**
3. **How does change gain traction?**
4. **Which other homes is this one coupled to?**

Question 2 carries the centre of gravity. The direction change takes and the past it carries are read from the thing that changes, and every answer depends on where it is read from:

- **Along the thing's own course.** Following one thing through time.
- **From compared instances.** One course read off many instances at different stages.
- **Along a lineage.** A population or lineage tracked across generations.
- **Over the whole.** The whole taken as one unit.
- **By a rule.** A law or designed procedure describing the change from outside.

The piece records the vantage with each claim. No vantage is privileged.

None of the questions has a direction built into it:

- Sources, mechanisms and processes carry no direction of their own.
- Traction is a relation between a change and its surroundings, not something done by a taker.
- The direction change takes is an outcome, and it is contingent, as is the change itself.
- Contingent is not unconstrained: the material holds hard limits beside the contingency.

The piece itself reads from outside. It reports each home's account and never speaks for the thing that changes. It has no SPLectrum opening statement, no SPLectrum vocabulary and no upward links. Its vantage point shows only in three things: which questions are asked, the order they are asked in, and how the homes are arranged by their answers. A reader meeting the piece cold sees a classification of the uses of "evolution".

## Relation to the hub's classification

The hub sorts by sense (unfolding, or descent with modification) and by shape (morphology, arrow, recurrence). This piece sorts on other axes, and its answers cut across the hub's line (A10). That crossing is material for the piece:

- **Lineage inside unfolding homes.** Star generations enriched in heavy elements; Smolin's universes.
- **The hand-over in chemical evolution.** A period in a longer history becomes descent, within a single home.
- **Widened selection.** Chaisson, and Hazen and colleagues (2023), bring stars and minerals under a selection idea while insisting the process is not Darwinian.
- **Lenski.** Intersocietal selection inside a stage scheme.
- **Schumpeter.** Development "from within", the unfolding of a system from its own resources, inside economics.
- **Lamarck.** The hinge between a body's own course and its lineage.
- **Language change.** Holds every vantage.

The piece can note where its sort and the hub's part ways. It does not re-sort the hub or argue with it.

## Material by question

Each section takes one question and places the homes by their answers. The piece is organised by the questions, not by home, because home-by-home treatment is the hub's job.

### 1. Where change comes from

The question asks for sources, mechanisms and processes, so the homes where change brings nothing new answer it as directly as the others.

- **A law of motion.** Physics: "from the state at one moment and the law of motion, the state at any later moment follows." The word "says how the change is given, not what shape the history takes".
- **Physical process within one body.**
  - Stars: contraction, then "stages of nuclear burning".
  - Minerals: "a sequence of deterministic, irreversible processes"; each stage "opens new chemical and physical regimes".
- **A course run from within.**
  - Spengler's Cultures run "from springtime to the winter of Civilisation".
  - Schumpeter's development is "the unfolding of a system from its own resources".
- **Copying error.** Eigen's molecules "copy themselves with errors". Mutation in genetic algorithms.
- **Fluctuation.** Prigogine: "order arises through fluctuations in flows of energy". Jantsch's self-organising systems far from equilibrium.
- **Combination.**
  - Arthur: "The base mechanism is not Darwinian. Radically novel technologies do not come into existence by the cumulation of small changes in earlier technologies."
  - Schumpeter's "new combinations".
  - Holland's recombination.
- **Search and design.**
  - Nelson and Winter: "the timely appearance of variation under the stimulus of adversity".
  - Ziman's question, in what sense invention is blind.
- **Response and decision.**
  - Toynbee's creative response.
  - Diamond: "the product of decisions rather than a predetermined course".
  - Morgan: each stage is opened by an advance in the arts of subsistence.
- **Innovation in use.**
  - Croft: "innovation has functional causes and propagation social ones".
  - The Neogrammarians locate sound change in individual speakers.
- **Chance as one mode among others.**
  - Peirce: "evolution by fortuitous variation, evolution by mechanical necessity, and evolution by creative love".
  - Calvin: "there is no selection; it is a random process, so far".
- **Creativity as general.**
  - Bergson: forms "in no sense contained in what preceded them".
  - Whitehead: creativity, "the production of novelty".

**Blind versus guided is a gradient, not a line.**

- Simonton treats blindness as a matter of degree.
- Campbell answers Thagard's "guided rather than blind" by making guidance "itself the product of earlier selection".
- "Lamarckian" is a placement a field makes of itself when what it passes on was not produced blindly (Gould; Nelson and Winter).
- The Baldwin effect produces Lamarckian-looking results without Lamarckian inheritance.
- Evolutionary computation has the full Darwinian apparatus "by construction" and is wholly purposive.

### 2. What changes and what it carries, read from where

This is the centre of gravity. It holds four strands, and vantage runs through all of them.

**2a. The thing that changes, and where it is read from.**

Most homes build their account from more than one place (A1):

- **Stars.** The course is "read off many stars observed at once at different stages", now "with the help of models that follow a single star through time". Generations of stars are tracked through enrichment.
- **Language.** The comparative method reconstructs "the parent language from its daughters". Schleicher's languages "grow in prehistory and decline in history". The Neogrammarians' laws, and Croft's, Mufwene's and Pagel's populations, give the remaining vantages.
- **Technology.** The phrase works at "two scales": "every artefact has antecedents", and technology as a whole is "one system with a history and a direction" for some authors.

The fields name vantage in their own words (A2), and the piece should use them:

- "laying the cases side by side at corresponding stages" (civilisations)
- "relocate complexity from object-properties to object-trajectories" (assembly theory)
- "the experimenter's judgement where the environment stands" (evolutionary computation)
- a "participant-observer's history" (Hull)
- "the people inside it cannot control" (Haff)
- Saussure's static and evolutionary axes
- Veblen's equilibrium against process

Many disputes over the unit are disputes over vantage (A4). They are content here, with each side standing:

- Lewontin's units of selection.
- Hull's replicators and interactors.
- Boas against the ladder ("each culture had to be understood through its own", in the page's wording).
- Sahlins and Service on general against specific evolution.
- Bradie's two programmes, which "can succeed or fail independently".
- Memetics "from the meme's point of view" against populations, and against Sperber's reconstruction "in each mind".

**2b. What is carried.**

Accumulation is the most widespread answer (A7), and it leads:

- "species accumulate against the stock built by earlier ones" (minerals)
- later stars "form from gas enriched by earlier ones"
- Tainter's layers of organisation
- Lenski's technological information
- Basalla's antecedents and Arthur's components
- Veblen's "cumulative change"
- "each stage building on the last" (cosmic evolution)

Copying is one case among others:

- Genes and alleles.
- Replicating molecules, capped by the error threshold.
- Smolin's inherited constants.
- Designed heredity, "how faithfully a solution is copied".

The other carriers:

- **Reconstruction.** Sperber: ideas "are reconstructed in each mind from partial evidence, and reconstruction transforms them".
- **Institutions, routines and rules.** Veblen's habits of thought; routines that "play the role that genes play"; Hayek's rules by imitation; Whitney's languages as institutions.
- **The state.** Physics, where unitary evolution "preserves the information" and open systems let it leak away.
- **Several carriers at once.** Dual inheritance; Jablonka and Lamb's four systems. Weismann's barrier is the claim that one carrier is sealed from another.
- **Taking up the past.** Whitehead's occasions, "each coming into being by taking up its past".

Godfrey-Smith's fidelity of heredity belongs here as biology's measure of carrying, placed beside the others and not used as their scale.

**2c. Direction, read from where.**

Direction is reported with its vantage, never as a property of a source or of traction. The material shows a pattern, not a rule (A3):

- **Built-in directions are mostly read over the whole, by a rule, or from compared instances:**
  - Spencer's law.
  - Comte and White.
  - Julian Huxley's "directional and essentially irreversible process".
  - Teilhard's convergence.
  - Kelly's technium "toward complexity, diversity and specialisation".
  - The stage ladder.
  - Mineral evolution's "progressively more diverse and complex phase assemblages".
- **Along a thing's own course or a lineage, direction is mostly an outcome, or denied:**
  - Darwin to Hyatt: "no innate tendency to progressive development exists".
  - Gould: "the mean drifts upward while the mode stays with the bacteria".
  - Kuhn: "not a process of evolution toward anything", with Darwin showing "directed-looking change … with no goal set in advance".
  - Bergson's "no plan".
  - Toynbee: "depends on how it responds".
  - Lenski's "blind and purposeless".
  - Chemistry, where the best copiers "take over".
- **The exceptions stay in view.** A star's "single irreversible path … fixed mainly by its mass", and Spengler's fixed course within each Culture. Both fix the course per instance and claim no history beyond it.

The material records the move from one vantage to another in its own words:

- Spencer "took the law of individual development from von Baer's embryology … and made it the law of everything".
- Nisbet: "the direction came with the metaphor, and the metaphor came from the organism".
- The biology page: "Embryology also supplied the pattern the species sense later took over".

Homes that claim a direction for the whole are placed with that claim as their answer, with its vantage, and are not refuted. Gould's *Full House* is the clearest single case of one history giving different directions from different vantages.

**2d. Where contingency enters, and its constraints.**

The material locates contingency in three places (A5):

- **Starting conditions, in the law-given and per-instance homes:**
  - the initial state, and on one reading the early universe's low-entropy state, where Prigogine instead puts irreversibility in the law (the dispute stands);
  - a star's mass;
  - the early atmosphere;
  - Gould's starting wall.
- **Response, decision, position and circumstance, in the human homes:**
  - Toynbee and Diamond;
  - Quigley's reform or circumvention;
  - Edith Penrose's deliberate choices;
  - firms that "happen to realise positive profits";
  - Godfrey-Smith's placement of culture, in the page's wording: "success often depends on position rather than on the intrinsic character of a variant";
  - Hayek: "Cultural selection is not a rational process".
- **Chance and variation:**
  - copying errors;
  - Smolin's slight variations;
  - tychasm;
  - Bergson's "unpredictable in principle";
  - the dice of the first evolution strategies.

Constraints stand beside the contingency, in three kinds (A6):

- **Fixed by a starting quantity:** mass and the stellar path; Gould's wall.
- **Caps on carrying:** the error threshold; low-fidelity transmission; Weismann's barrier; epigenetic marks possibly "reset each generation".
- **Limits on range:**
  - mineral evolution "not open-ended";
  - three stellar endpoints;
  - reducing conditions before oxygenation;
  - Basalla's "no radical breaks";
  - Witt's economic evolution "constrained by" biological evolution.

The two mineral pages differ here, and the difference is content. The hub branch cites "deterministic, irreversible processes". The subject page stresses "cumulative path-dependence".

**2e. What persists alongside.**

Earlier forms continuing beside later ones, read along lineages and instances:

- Gould's mode staying with the bacteria.
- Baade's Population I and II side by side.
- Tylor's survivals and Darwin's rudiments in language.
- Campbell's levels, "each level a shortcut that selects vicariously on behalf of the level below".
- Emergent evolution's levels "resting on the level below".
- Spengler's "contemporaneous" epochs across Cultures.
- Steward's multilinear regularities.

### 3. How change gains traction

Traction is a relation between a change and its surroundings. The section names no taker and implies no sequence in which something new appears first and is then selected.

- **Differential reproduction.** Lewontin's three conditions.
- **Widened selection.**
  - Chaisson: "selection can be generally taken to mean preferential interaction of any object with its environment".
  - Hazen and colleagues: the persistence of stable configurations.
  - Root-Bernstein disputes it.
- **Attraction.** Sperber: much of the stability usually credited to selection comes from the bias of transformation itself. The balance with selection "varies case by case".
- **Biases in adoption.** Content, conformist and success biases. Richerson and Boyd: "determining who is a success is much easier than determining how to be a success".
- **Social propagation.**
  - Croft's propagation.
  - Schmidt's waves.
  - Pagel's frequency of use.
  - Keller's invisible hand, "the result of human action but not of human design".
- **Imitation and market selection.**
  - Hayek: "the selection by imitation of successful institutions and habits".
  - Alchian's market, selecting firms "without maximising agents".
- **Group competition.**
  - Cultural group selection, with its critics standing (Pinker, Sterelny, Birch).
  - Lenski's intersocietal selection.
- **Judgment and design.**
  - Turing's "judgment of the experimenter".
  - Basalla: "Selection on this account is directed by people."
- **Accumulation and response.**
  - Minerals and stars accumulate.
  - Toynbee's civilisations respond.

What counts as traction is itself historical in several homes:

- Toulmin's standards are "local to the discipline and subject to the same change".
- Campbell's vicarious selectors.
- Hayek's selection that "creates reason".

Where change is given by law or accumulation (physics, stars, minerals), the traction answer is thin (A8). That is an edge to show, not a gap.

Biology's criterion appears here as biology's account of traction, one entry among the others.

### 4. Homes coupled to homes

- **Hand-overs.**
  - Chemistry to descent: "the history of molecules hands over to descent", with Calvin's "the essence of the process of selection within the chemical system itself". Where it happens is disputed: RNA world, metabolism-first, assembly theory.
  - Physics to chemistry, where Prigogine's school met Eigen.
- **Shared histories.**
  - "Mineral evolution and biosphere evolution are not parallel processes but a single coupled history."
  - Dual inheritance.
  - Mineral evolution as "a specific example" of cosmic evolution.
- **Continuity or analogy, with both sides standing.**
  - Witt against Hodgson and Knudsen.
  - Assembly theory's lineage from biology into technology "without a break".
  - Haff's "new stage in the geologic evolution of the Earth".
  - Kelly's seventh kingdom.
- **Borrowing in both directions.**
  - Linguistics had "genealogies of descent before biology did".
  - Peirce read Darwinian selection as political economy.
  - Marshall: "the Mecca of the economist lies in economic biology".

## Edges

At some homes the questions get unusual answers. Show these as the edges of the classification, not as shortfalls:

- **Evolutionary computation.** Built by rule throughout. Contingency is located in design, and every ingredient is chosen by the designer.
- **Metaphysics.** The unit is the world as a whole.
- **Physics, stars and minerals on question 3.** The traction answer is thin.
- **Assembly theory.** Its landing records no direction and holds its contingency claim on a sub-page.

## Editorial directions

- **Opening.** Start from the word's many homes and the fact that the piece asks them all the same questions. No thesis about evolution, and no claim about what the questions reveal.
- **Closer.** No summary verdict and no "evolution is…". If a closer is needed, it should leave the answers plural, or end on an edge.
- **Vantage with every claim.**
  - Direction and the carried past are always reported with where they are read from.
  - No vantage is treated as the true one.
  - The piece reads from outside and never voices the thing that changes.
  - Use the fields' own vantage wording where it exists. Otherwise use plain phrases: "read along its own course", "read off many instances", "along a lineage", "over the whole", "given by a rule".
- **Tie, don't unify.** No master definition. Keep the senses plural. Disputes stay standing:
  - Bowler against Richards.
  - Hodgson and Knudsen against Witt.
  - Hazen against Root-Bernstein.
  - Memetics against dual inheritance.
  - Cultural group selection against its critics.
  - Albert, Penrose and Carroll against Prigogine on the arrow.
- **The criterion is not the ruler.** Darwinian grading appears as one home's way of sorting, beside the others. Do not place homes by how much of the apparatus they carry.
- **No foils.** The ladder and progress schemes are placed by their own answers, with their vantage. They are not held up as the contrast to the cases where direction is an outcome.
- **Placements come from the home's own page.** Each placement must be supported there and voiced in the field's own words. Where a home resists the word itself, that is content:
  - Comte's word was "progress".
  - Childe chose "revolution".
  - Spengler set *Entwicklung* against Darwin.
  - Schumpeter refused the biology.
  - Hazen's group calls the word "odd, perhaps even inappropriate", then defends it.
- **Grammar.** Homes and their authors are the subjects of the sentences.
- **No built-in direction.** No question or heading presumes a direction, a taker, or a sequence from new to selected.
- **Contingent, not unconstrained.** Where contingency shows, the constraints the material records stay in view.
- **Vocabulary.** No SPLectrum terms, including the plain-looking ones: deviation, sediment(ation), historicity, belonging, resonance, the common, language game, register, equal standing, subject, perimeter. Use instead: change, source, mechanism, process, carried, read from, traction, coupled.
- **Links.** Downward only: into the hub's branches, the subject pages they cite, and person pages. Nothing upward.

## Source-level uncertainties

- **Quotations.** All quotations were transcribed from the live pages through a fetch tool. Most were confirmed on a second fetch. Some passages on the stage-schemes, biology, Lamarck, language and economics pages rest on a single fetch. The pages are the authority on wording.
- **Boas.** "each culture had to be understood through its own" is the page's wording about Boas, not a quotation from him.
- **Nisbet on the civilisation page.** That sentence did not transcribe cleanly. The progress-and-direction wording is confirmed.
- **Hayek.** The page attributes "Cultural selection is not a rational process…" to the span from *The Constitution of Liberty* (1960) to *The Fatal Conceit* (1988), not to a specific work.
- **The 2024 mineral study.** The study is not named on the page, and the mineral subject page does not mention it or the 2023 law.
- **Homes the hub does not yet hold.** Major transitions (Maynard Smith and Szathmáry), media evolution and software evolution are not on the hub. The piece walks only the homes the field pages hold, so these enter only once they exist there.
