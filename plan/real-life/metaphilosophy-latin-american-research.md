# Metaphilosophy in Latin America: research note

Purpose: to gather the stages for a page on Latin America beside the India and China pages of the Metaphilosophy subject. Each stage gets the subject's three questions (1. What is philosophy? 2. What is its point? 3. How is it done?), in the tradition's own words where they exist. Headlines only. Salazar Bondy 1968, Zea 1969 and the philosophies of liberation were checked in an earlier round from the IEP and SEP entries; those lines are carried below and marked as before, with new lines from the books added where they could be opened.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a source I opened, named by its key in the Sources list. **[C as quoted]** = the wording is checked as it appears in a secondary source I opened; the primary was not opened. **[U]** = unverified. "My rendering" = my English from the Spanish or French I fetched.

**Access.** León-Portilla's English *Aztec Thought and Culture* (1963) and the English Sor Juana translations by Peden (1982) and Arenal and Powell (1994) are lending-only on archive.org (401). mdpi.com returned 403. studylib.es is behind a Cloudflare challenge. Spanish Wikisource, archive.org full texts, the SEP and the IEP were reachable.

## 1. The words and the name (word test)

- **The region's name is recent and contested.** The IEP entry dates the wide public and academic use of "Latin America" to the twentieth century, "the very same period" as the first histories of Latin American philosophy, and notes that "the very idea of Latin America has been posed as a major problem (Mignolo 2005)" [C, L3]. A name taken from the colonisers' Latin languages fits the pre-Conquest stage least well; that is my observation, not a source's.
- **The authors' own words differ from the page's:**
  - Alberdi (1840/42): *filosofía americana*, *la América del Sur* [C, L10].
  - Martí (1891): *nuestra América* [C, L12].
  - Salazar Bondy (1968): *filosofía hispanoamericana*, title *nuestra América*, and he limits his subject to "la América hispanoindia" [C, L14].
  - Zea: *filosofía americana* (1953, and the 1969 title) [C, L15]; *La filosofía latinoamericana* is a 1987 title [C, L22 list].
  - Gracia: "Latin American philosophy (and more broadly, Hispanic and Latino/a philosophy)" [C as quoted, L2].
  - The field's large histories say *pensamiento filosófico* (Beorlegui 2006; Dussel, Mendieta and Bohórquez 2009) [C, titles in L3 bibliography].
- **Nahuatl.** *tlamatini*, "el que sabe algo" (plural *tlamatinime*), and *tlamatiliztli*, knowledge or wisdom. León-Portilla closes his 2006 preface on the equation itself: what the ancient Mexicans "llamaron tlamatiliztli y nosotros filosofía" [C, L4 p. VII]. Sahagún wrote "sabios o philosophos" in the margin beside the Nahuatl description of the *tlamatini* (Códice Matritense de la Real Academia, f. 118r–v), and León-Portilla reproduces the margin and invites the reader to "juzgar si fue o no un acierto" [C, L4 p. 65]. Maffie glosses *tlamatinime* as "knowers of things," "sages," "philosophers" and warns that metaphysics, epistemology and the rest "demarcate categories for which there are no precise, noncontroversial synonyms in Nahuatl" [C, L5].
- **Quechua.** Garcilaso, *Comentarios reales* (1609): "sus amautas, que eran los filósofos" (Book II ch. 2), and "hombres de buenos ingenios, que llamaron Amautas, que filosofaron cosas sutiles" (II.21) [C, L11]. The gloss "philosophers" is the mestizo chronicler's, writing in Spanish, just as the marginal note is Sahagún's.
- **Page name.** The series names places ("Philosophy in India", "Philosophy in China"), so the option is **"Philosophy in Latin America"**, with the intro saying that the name came late and is not the authors' own. The alternatives are worse. "Latin American philosophy" prejudges the identity question the page reports, and "Philosophy in Our America" takes Martí's side.

## 2. The knowers of things (Nahua thought; Andean aside)

- **What it is.** The Nahuatl text Sahagún marked: "El sabio: una luz, una tea, una gruesa tea que no ahúma. Un espejo horadado … Suya es la tinta negra y roja, de él son los códices … Es camino, guía veraz para otros" (lines 1–5; tr. from the Nahuatl by León-Portilla) [C, L4 pp. 65–66]. León-Portilla's own summary flags its method: "aplicando anacrónica y análogamente al sabio o tlamatini los términos con que hoy se designan a quienes tienen muy semejantes funciones, diremos que era un maestro, un psicólogo, un moralista, un cosmólogo, un metafísico y un humanista" [C, L4 pp. 71–72]. Garibay's prologue gives the broad definition the book rests on: philosophy "no es sino el conato de explicar los sumos problemas de la existencia", and "Todo hombre de necesidad filosofa, sin necesidad de ajustarse a los moldes de Platón y Aristóteles" [C, L4 prologue p. X].
- **Its point.** Maffie (IEP): the *tlamatinime* "turned to philosophy for practicable answers to what they regarded as the defining question of human existence: How can humans maintain their balance upon the slippery earth?" Nahua philosophy is "better understood as a 'way-seeking' rather than as a 'truth-seeking' philosophy"; wisdom is "non-propositional 'know how'" [C, L5 §3]. Sahagún's text again: "Hace sabios los rostros ajenos, hace a los otros tomar una cara" (line 10) [C, L4 p. 66].
- **How it is done.** By "flower and song", *in xochitl in cuicatl*. León-Portilla: "los tlamatinime llegaron a formular en sus poemas una auténtica teoría acerca del conocer metafísico. No obstante la transitoriedad universal, hay un modo de conocer lo verdadero: la poesía (flor y canto)" [C, L4 p. 146]. Maffie: "song-poems rather than discursive arguments are the appropriate medium of sagely expression, and sages are perforce singer-songwriter-poets" [C, L5 §4d]. The sages' reply to the twelve friars in the *Coloquios* rests on what the ancestors handed down: "Ellos nos dieron sus normas de vida" (lines 947–8, tr. León-Portilla from Lehmann's Nahuatl) [C, L4 p. 131].
- **The source problem (one clause on the page).** The *Coloquios* survive in the version Sahagún had put "en lengua mexicana bien congrua y limada" at Tlatelolco in 1564, forty years after the 1524 meeting (his own note, quoted by León-Portilla) [C, L4 p. 15]. The other texts come from informants interviewed after the Conquest. Bierhorst's reading of the *Cantares* as post-Conquest "ghost songs" [U, search summary].
- **The dispute (the headline).**
  - At the 1956 thesis defence the neo-Kantian Francisco Larroyo called the question anachronistic. Margarita Vera: call them sages, not philosophers, since "no necesitamos asumir el discurso del dominador, así sea el del fraile Sahagún". López Austin objected to renderings such as *mictlanmatini*, "conocedor de la región de los muertos", turned into "metafísico" [C as quoted, L7, which reports them via Bordes Sevilla 2002; two removes].
  - Maffie keeps the word but drops the frame: he "respectfully unbinds" León-Portilla's reading from "the trappings of Western discourses" [C as quoted, L8 review]. He holds that the idea that philosophy is exclusively Western "plays a vital role in the West's conception of itself" (*Aztec Philosophy*, p. 6) [C as quoted, L9 review].
  - Nuccetelli calls such works "proto-philosophy of the non-academic variety"; Gracia defers the question to future historical work [C, L2 §1.2]. SEP (Gracia and Vargas): "Whether this body of work is rightly characterized as philosophy or something else is a disputed matter" [C, L1].
- **Andean.** Garcilaso: the Incas "La filosofía moral alcanzaron bien, y en práctica la dejaron escrita en sus leyes, vida y costumbres", but of natural philosophy "poco o nada", having no letters (II.21) [C, L11]. Miró Quesada: "The Maya and the Inca had thought that, without abusing the terms, may be considered of philosophical character" (1978, tr. Nuccetelli) [C as quoted, L2]. That is enough for one sentence, not a stage.

## 3. The schools of New Spain and Peru (colonial scholasticism)

- **What it is / its point.** The universities: Lima chartered 12 May 1551, Mexico by decree of 21 September 1551 [C, L19 Wikipedia]. Alonso de la Vera Cruz "composed the first fully philosophical treatises in Latin America" [C, L1]; *Recognitio summularum* and *Dialectica resolutio* (1554), *Physica speculatio* (1557), an arts curriculum for the new university [C, L19 Wikipedia]. Gracia and Vargas: "For scholastics, the primary objective was the apologetic defense of the faith" [C, L1 §2.1]. Romero noted "the prevalence [then] of pedagogical over philosophical concerns" (1941, tr. Nuccetelli) [C as quoted, L2].
- **Valladolid (1550–51).** Las Casas against Sepúlveda over the justice of the conquest [C, L19 Wikipedia, dates]. It bears on Q2 only as philosophy put to a juridical-theological question, and Nuccetelli calls Lascasianism "a prime borderline case" [C, L2]. A clause at most, not a stage.
- **Sor Juana, *Respuesta a sor Filotea de la Cruz* (1691).** All [C, L13 Spanish text; edition unstated on Wikisource]:
  - *Its point*: "Yo no estudio para escribir, ni menos para enseñar (que fuera en mí desmedida soberbia), sino sólo por ver si con estudiar ignoro menos."
  - *What it is*: a ladder. She directs her study "a la cumbre de la Sagrada Teología; pareciéndome preciso, para llegar a ella, subir por los escalones de las ciencias y artes humanas; porque ¿cómo entenderá el estilo de la Reina de las Ciencias quien aun no sabe el de las ancilas?"
  - *How it is done*: alone, "teniendo sólo por maestro un libro mudo, por condiscípulo un tintero insensible". She also learns from the kitchen: "¿qué podemos saber las mujeres sino filosofías de cocina? Bien dijo Lupercio Leonardo, que bien se puede filosofar y aderezar la cena. Y yo suelo decir viendo estas cosillas: Si Aristóteles hubiera guisado, mucho más hubiera escrito." Rappaport's translation (2005): "what can women know except philosophy of the kitchen? … it is possible to philosophize while preparing dinner. As I often say on observing these little things, if Aristotle had cooked, he would have written much more" [C, L18]. The circulating "Had Aristotle prepared victuals, he would have written more" is Peden's [U].
  - *The defence of study*: against "Mulieres in Ecclesia taceant", "¿por qué reprenden a las que privadamente estudian?"
  - *Reception*: SEP calls her "retrospectively regarded as the first Latin American feminist writer and philosopher" [C, L1]. That is reception, and the page should say "regarded".

## 4. An American philosophy (independence and after)

- **Alberdi, "Ideas para presidir a la confección del curso de filosofía contemporánea".** All [C, L10, my renderings]:
  - *What it is*: "No hay, pues, una filosofía universal, porque no hay una solución universal de las cuestiones que la constituyen en el fondo. Cada país, cada época, cada filósofo ha tenido su filosofía peculiar". Hence, beside the Greek, German and French, "es necesario que exista una filosofía americana".
  - *Its point*: "la filosofía americana debe ser esencialmente política y social en su objeto, ardiente y profética en sus instintos, sintética y orgánica en su método, positiva y realista en sus procederes, republicana en su espíritu y destinos". "Nuestra filosofía, pues, ha de salir de nuestras necesidades."
  - *How it is done*: as applied philosophy, "no la filosofía en sí … sino la filosofía aplicada a los objetos de un interés más inmediato para nosotros". "Si es posible decirlo, la América practica lo que piensa la Europa."
  - *Date*: SEP has a Montevideo newspaper article of 1840 [C, L2]; the Wikisource note says it was read at the Colegio de Humanidades in Montevideo in 1842 [C, L10]. Open check.
  - *Reception*: Ardao and Roig hold that its influence came only in the 1940s, its "most decisive apologist" being Gaos [C as quoted, L2]. The page should not present it as founding at the time.
- **Martí, "Nuestra América" (1891)**, if a line is wanted: "Injértese en nuestras repúblicas el mundo; pero el tronco ha de ser el de nuestras repúblicas"; "Nuestra Grecia es preferible a la Grecia que no es nuestra" [C, L12; Wikisource text, source flagged as missing].

## 5. Order and progress (positivism)

- **Comte's mottoes.** The title page of the *Système de politique positive* (1851) carries "Ordre et Progrès. — Vivre pour autrui" and "L'Amour pour principe; L'Ordre pour base, Et le Progrès pour but" [C, L16, 1879 reprint "conforme à l'édition de 1851"]. The *Catéchisme positiviste* (1852) calls the three-part line "la formule sacrée du positivisme" [C, L17].
- **Brazil.** The flag carrying *Ordem e Progresso* was adopted on 19 November 1889, its concept the work of the positivist Raimundo Teixeira Mendes with Miguel Lemos and others [C, L19 Wikipedia]. Wikipedia derives the motto from the three-part formula. Comte's own two-word masthead, "Ordre et Progrès", matches the flag word for word [C, L16]; that comparison is mine.
- **Barreda, *Oración cívica* (1867).** He reads Mexican history as "la emancipación mental … Emancipación científica, emancipación religiosa, emancipación política", and closes: "que en lo de adelante sea nuestra divisa libertad, orden y progreso; la libertad como medio; el orden como base y el progreso como fin" [C, L20]. Barreda puts liberty where Comte had love (my observation). Place and date (Guanajuato, 16 September 1867) and his founding of the Escuela Nacional Preparatoria [U].
- **Its point.** Gracia and Vargas: "for positivists, the goal was national integration and economic and social progress"; positivism became "the official state philosophy of several nations" and "was even used to justify dictatorial regimes, as in the case of Mexico" [C, L1].
- **What it is.** Comte's positive philosophy; the subject already covers Comte on `the-hinge`, so a pointer is enough.

## 6. The founders and "normality"

- **The founders.** "The generation of founders", Romero's name for Korn, Deústua, Vasconcelos, Caso, Molina, Vaz Ferreira and Farias Brito [C, L1 §2.1]. (SEP's "a generation of philosophers born around 1910" for them is a slip; their dates in the same paragraph run from 1849 to 1883.) They broke with positivism on "purely philosophical" grounds, "concerned, for example, with freedom", and drew on Boutroux and Bergson; Ortega's 1916 visit brought Scheler and Hartmann [C, L1].
- **Its point.** "The concern of these thinkers was more systematically philosophical in motivation", philosophy "pursued for its own sake", where earlier generations took up European ideas "with pre-established ends in mind" [C, L1].
- **"Normality".** Romero's term for 1940–60, when the profession was institutionalised [C, L1; L3 §"normalization"]. His definition, "el ejercicio de la filosofía como función ordinaria de la cultura, al lado de las otras ocupaciones de la inteligencia" [U, search summary only]. Open check before quoting it.

## 7. Is there a philosophy of our America?

- **The backdrop.** Hegel, *Philosophy of History*, Introduction: "America is therefore the land of the future … What has taken place in the New World up to the present time is only an echo of the Old World—the expression of a foreign Life" (tr. Sibree) [C, L21]. Salazar Bondy answers Hegel's owl of Minerva: philosophy can be "la mensajera del alba" [C, L14 ch. 3].
- **Zea.**
  - *What it is*, a philosophy from the circumstance: "Even though being, God, etc., are issues appropriate for every man, the solution to them will be given from a Latin American standpoint" (1948, tr. Nuccetelli) [C as quoted, L2].
  - *How it is done*: he quotes himself from an earlier text: "todo intento de hacer filosofía americana, con sólo la pretensión de que sea americana, tendrá que fracasar. Hay que intentar hacer pura y simplemente filosofía, que lo americano se dará por añadidura". He then asks "si hasta ahora hemos hecho auténtica filosofía, Filosofía sin más" (*América como conciencia*, 1953, ch. I) [C, L15]. The "sin más" of the 1969 title is already there.
  - The 1969 book itself was not reachable. Finola reports that it takes Fanon as the epigraph of its last chapter and moves towards dependency theory and liberation [C as quoted, L23].
- **Salazar Bondy, *¿Existe una filosofía de nuestra América?*** (1968; checked in the 3rd ed. 1975):
  - *What it has been*: "La filosofía fue traída por los españoles porque éstos vinieron a conquistar y a dominar la tierra americana e importaron con ellos las armas intelectuales de la dominación" (ch. 1) [C, L14].
  - *The problem*: "El problema de nuestra filosofía es la inautenticidad. La inautenticidad se enraiza en nuestra condición histórica de países subdesarrollados y dominados" (ch. 3) [C, L14].
  - *Its point*: philosophy can be authentic amid inauthenticity by becoming "la conciencia lúcida de nuestra condición deprimida como pueblos y en el pensamiento capaz de desencadenar y promover el proceso superador de esta condición"; much of its task "es destructiva" [C, L14 ch. 3].
  - The earlier round's IEP lines stand: authenticity "inseparable from the concrete socioeconomic conditions" [C, L3].
  - He calls the debate "the philosophy of philosophy" (1968, p. 13) and sorts its questions as descriptive, prescriptive and about identity [C as quoted, L2].
- **The frames of the modern debate.**
  - Gracia and Vargas count four approaches: "universalist, culturalist, critical, and ethnic". Zea is culturalist; Salazar Bondy is critical; the ethnic view (Gracia) takes Latin American philosophy as "the philosophy produced by the Latin American people", one "because an ethnic group has produced it" [C, L1 §3.4]. The debate is "perhaps the most discussed subject matter within Latin American philosophy" [C, L1].
  - Nuccetelli's spectrum runs from "strong universalism" to "strong distinctivism" with "middle-way theories" between, and includes the "new skeptics", for whom there is "no regular philosophical dialogue among Latin American philosophers" [C, L2].
  - Gracia's ethnic account is "neo-Wittgensteinian", modelled on family resemblance (Gracia 2008, p. 140) [C as quoted, L2].

## 8. The philosophies of liberation

- **Dussel, *Filosofía de la liberación*** (written 1975, preface dated México 1976; first published 1977 [U]; checked in the 2013 Docencia *Obras selectas* XI, whose body text is revised, e.g. it mentions the Gulf War, so quote only the 1976 preface):
  - *What it is and its point*: "Escrito desde la periferia para hombres de la periferia, sin embargo, se dirige también al hombre del centro"; "La filosofía, patrimonio exclusivo del Mediterráneo, desde los griegos, y en la edad moderna sólo europea, comienza por primera vez su proceso de mundialización real". The book would "iniciar un diálogo mundial de la filosofía".
  - *How it is done*: "Parte, es evidente, de la periferia, pero usa todavía el lenguaje del centro", "como el esclavo que habla la lengua del señor cuando se rebela" [C, L22 pp. XIV–XV].
- **Against Salazar Bondy.** The volume's introduction (by Solís Bello Ortiz and others) records the liberation thinkers' objection: "No se puede esperar a que nuestra cultura se libere para después comenzar el proceso de pensar filosóficamente la liberación" [C, L22 introduction; theirs, not Dussel's].
- **IEP.** Dussel "seeks to develop transformational conceptions and practices leading to liberation" from cultural dependence and socioeconomic oppression [C, L3]. IEP dates the English translation (Martinez and Morkovsky) 1980, Wipf & Stock [C, L3]; the Orbis edition of 1985 [U]. Open check.

## 9. Placement: draft outline (options, not decisions)

**Option A: a page "Philosophy in Latin America"** in the India/China shape: an intro on the words (late name; *americana*, *nuestra América*, *hispanoamericana*, *pensamiento*; *tlamatiliztli* and *amauta* glossed as "philosophy" by Sahagún and Garcilaso), then stages, then "In one view". A draft of the table:

| Stage | What it is | Its point | How it is done |
|---|---|---|---|
| The knowers of things | the *tlamatini*, "a light, a torch"; "what they called *tlamatiliztli* and we philosophy" (and the dispute) | keeping one's balance on the slippery earth (Maffie); giving others "a face" | flower and song, poetry as the way to say true words; the ancestors' "rules of life" |
| The schools | the arts curriculum; theology as summit, the sciences its "steps" (Sor Juana) | defence of the faith (Gracia and Vargas); for Sor Juana, "to see if by studying I am less ignorant" | teaching texts; alone with "a mute book"; "philosophy of the kitchen" |
| An American philosophy | "no universal philosophy"; each country its own | "essentially political and social in its object" | applied: "America practises what Europe thinks" |
| Order and progress | positive philosophy | "liberty as means, order as base, progress as end"; national integration | — |
| The founders and normality | philosophy for its own sake | — | philosophy "normalised", an ordinary profession |
| A philosophy of our America? | the debate as "the philosophy of philosophy"; universalist, culturalist, critical, ethnic | Salazar Bondy: "lucid consciousness" of domination | Zea: "purely and simply philosophy", the American "added" |
| Liberation | philosophy's first "real worldwide" process | from the periphery to the periphery | speaking "the language of the centre" while rebelling |

Two choices to discuss: whether "The founders and normality" stands alone or merges into the next stage (its Q2 and Q3 are thin), and whether Sor Juana gets her own stage. Her lines answer all three questions, which none of the other colonial material does.

**Option B: fold into an Americas section of a wider page.** Not recommended; the identity debate is its own headline, as on the China page.

## 13. Discard list (do not place)

- **Priority claims, all reception:**
  - Dussel's 1996 preface: the book was written "cuatro años antes que el conocido libro de Lyotard".
  - The 2013 introduction: liberation philosophy as "el primer movimiento filosófico que comienza la descolonización epistemológica".
  - Sor Juana as "the first" feminist except as SEP's "retrospectively regarded".
  - "First university / first philosophical book in the Americas" as achievement rather than date.
- **Nahua thought equated with Greek or Heideggerian thought.** León-Portilla cites García Bacca on Heidegger; Gingerich reads *neltiliztli* as *alētheia*. Report these as readings if at all, not as what the Nahua held.
- **Alberdi's text presented as founding in 1840/42**, without the Ardao and Roig note on its 1940s revival.
