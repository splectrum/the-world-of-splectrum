# "Philosophy is dead": research note

Purpose: to record who declared the end of philosophy and why. It has two parts: the scientists' verdicts (1987–2014) with the replies, and the philosophers' own announcements, sorted by kind of reason. Each claim is checked for exactly what it says ended, in the author's own words. Material for a blog post and the planned Real Life philosophy page. This is a working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a source I opened, named by key (Sources list at the end). **[U]** = unverified (memory, a search summary, or a secondary paraphrase I could not open). Quotations are given as found in the named translation.

## 1. The scientists

### Hawking, 1988: a lament, not a death notice

- At the end of *A Brief History of Time*, in the chapter "Conclusion", Hawking writes that "the people whose business it is to ask why, the philosophers, have not been able to keep up with the advance of scientific theories". In the eighteenth century philosophers "considered the whole of human knowledge, including science, to be their field". In the nineteenth and twentieth, science "became too technical and mathematical for the philosophers", who "reduced the scope of their inquiries so much that Wittgenstein, the most famous philosopher of this century, said 'The sole remaining task for philosophy is the analysis of language.' What a comedown from the great tradition of philosophy from Aristotle to Kant!" [C, S1; Bantam 1988, scanned export printing 1989, around pp. 185–86 of that printing; the page in the 1988 hardcover is not checked].
- The same passage closes in hope: with a complete theory "we shall all, philosophers, scientists, and just ordinary people, be able to take part in the discussion of the question of why it is that we and the universe exist … for then we would know the mind of God" [C, S1].
- Word test on the Wittgenstein line. That sentence is not in the *Tractatus*. The nearest wordings are 4.0031, "All philosophy is 'Critique of language'" (*Sprachkritik*), and 4.112, "The object of philosophy is the logical clarification of thoughts … Philosophy is not a theory but an activity" (Ogden) [C, S14]. This is my comparison of the texts; I found no secondary source on the misquotation.

### Hawking and Mlodinow, 2010–11: the death notice

- *The Grand Design* (Bantam, 2010), p. 5: "Traditionally these are questions for philosophy, but philosophy is dead. Philosophy has not kept up with modern developments in science, particularly physics. Scientists have become the bearers of the torch of discovery in our quest for knowledge." The questions meant are "What is the nature of reality? Where did all this come from? Did the universe need a creator?" [C, S2: quoted with page in W. L. Craig's review; the book itself was not opened. S5 quotes "these *were* questions": open check].
- Hawking said the same in a talk at Google's Zeitgeist conference in May 2011 [C, S5, as reported].
- The book itself goes on to take philosophical positions, which reviewers were quick to point out. Thagard lists "There is no picture- or theory-independent concept of reality" and "A well-constructed model creates a reality of its own" (the authors' "model-dependent realism") and remarks that these "are all philosophical in making general claims about the nature of knowledge and reality" [C, S3].

### Weinberg, 1987 and 1992: "against philosophy"

- In a 1987 speech printed in *Nature*, Weinberg said: "I've heard the remark (although I forget the source) that the philosophy of science is just about as useful to scientists as ornithology is to birds" [C, S6 quoting *Nature*, 3 Dec 1987, p. 433].
- *Dreams of a Final Theory* (1992), chapter VII, "Against Philosophy" [chapter number C, S9 reference list]: "The insights of philosophers have occasionally benefited physicists, but generally in a negative fashion—by protecting them from the preconceptions of other philosophers." And: "I know of no one who has participated actively in the advance of physics in the postwar period whose research has been significantly helped by the work of philosophers." He sets this against Wigner's phrase as "the unreasonable ineffectiveness of philosophy" [C, S7, a transcript of the chapter; the book's pages were not seen].
- His scope is narrower than Hawking's, and he says so: "This is not to deny all value to philosophy, much of which has nothing to do with science. I do not even mean to deny all value to the philosophy of science, which at its best seems to me a pleasing gloss on the history and discoveries of science. But we should not expect it to provide today's scientists with any useful guidance" [C, S7]. His main case is positivism, which he says helped Einstein and Heisenberg but later did "as much harm as good" [C, S7].

### The ornithology line is not Feynman's

- Quote Investigator found "no substantive support" for the Feynman attribution. The line is absent from *The Quotable Feynman* (2015). The earliest close match is Weinberg's anonymous version of 1987. The family of sayings goes back to the painter Barnett Newman ("aesthetics is for the artist like Ornithology is for the birds", reported in *Art in America*, 1955). The earliest printed link to Feynman found is Philip Kitcher (1998), who called it "famous (perhaps apocryphal)" [C, S6].

### Krauss, 2012

- Interview with Ross Andersen, *The Atlantic*, 23 April 2012: "Philosophy is a field that, unfortunately, reminds me of that old Woody Allen joke, 'those that can't do, teach, and those that can't teach, teach gym.' And the worst part of philosophy is the philosophy of science". He continued: "people in philosophy feel threatened, and they have every right to feel threatened, because science progresses and philosophy doesn't" [C, S4]. The context was David Albert's review of *A Universe from Nothing* in the *New York Times* [C, S4].
- Four days later he narrowed the claim (*Scientific American*, 27 April 2012, "The Consolation of Philosophy"). Dennett had told him the interview was read "as implying a blanket condemnation of philosophy as a discipline, something I had not intended" [C, S8].

### Tyson, 2014

- On the Nerdist podcast, when a host said he had majored in philosophy, Tyson replied: "That can really mess you up." He went on: "My concern here is that the philosophers believe they are actually asking deep questions about nature. And to the scientist it's, what are you doing? Why are you concerning yourself with the meaning of meaning?" [C, S10, Pigliucci's transcript; the podcast itself not heard; episode date U]. Rovelli quotes Tyson saying the community of philosophers "was rendered essentially obsolete" by the expanding universe and quantum physics [C, S9].

### Replies (headline)

- **Maudlin**, *The Atlantic*, 19 January 2012: "Hawking is a brilliant man, but he's not an expert in what's going on in philosophy, evidently. Over the past thirty years the philosophy of physics has become seamlessly integrated with the foundations of physics work done by actual physicists, so the situation is actually the exact opposite of what he describes" [C, S5].
- **Pigliucci**, Scientia Salon, 12 May 2014, replying to Tyson: asking what philosophy of science has done for science lately is "a category mistake". Its "main objective … is to understand how science works and, when it fails to work … why"; it is "epistemology applied to the scientific enterprise" [C, S10].
- **Rovelli**, "Physics Needs Philosophy. Philosophy Needs Physics", *Foundations of Physics* 48 (2018): 481–491. The reply names Weinberg, Hawking and Tyson. It uses Aristotle's *Protrepticus* against Isocrates and argues that "Those who deny the utility of philosophy, are doing philosophy". When Weinberg and Hawking write "philosophy is dead", "they were not doing physics … They were reflecting on the best way to develop science". He holds that "the current anti-philosophical ideology has had damaging effects on the fertility of science", and that scientists who deny philosophy's role "are the ones trapped in the ideology of their time" [C, S9]. The reply cuts both ways: "Those philosophers that discount science, and there are many of them, do a serious disservice to intelligence and civilisation" [C, S9].
- **Thagard** (*Psychology Today*, 24 Nov 2010): "those who ignore philosophy are condemned to repeat it" [C, S3]. **Craig** (a philosopher writing as a Christian apologist): "effrontery and condescension" [C, S2].

## 2. The philosophers: five kinds of reason

The section's main finding: almost none of these thinkers say philosophy as such is over. Each names something narrower that ends: metaphysics, the theological and metaphysical stages, philosophy-as-love-of-knowing, interpretation without action, or Philosophy with a capital P. Most keep something that goes on (a method, an activity, "thinking", a lowercase philosophy). The word each uses for the ending is part of the claim.

### (a) The sciences take over its questions

- **Comte**, *Cours de philosophie positive*, first lesson (vol. 1, 1830 [U, date]). In Martineau's version (*freely translated and condensed*, Bell printing 1896 [C]): "each branch of our knowledge, —passes successively through three different theoretical conditions: the Theological, or fictitious; the Metaphysical, or abstract; and the Scientific, or positive". In the positive state "the mind has given over the vain search after Absolute notions, the origin and destination of the universe, and the causes of phenomena, and applies itself to the study of their laws" [C, S11]. Word test: Comte does not end *philosophy*. The three stages are "three methods of philosophizing … three philosophies", and his own project is called positive philosophy [C, S11]. What ends is theology and metaphysics.
- **Engels** on the Marxist side. "Modern materialism … no longer needs any philosophy standing above the other sciences … That which still survives, independently, of all earlier philosophy is the science of thought and its laws — formal logic and dialectics. Everything else is subsumed in the positive science of nature and history" (*Anti-Dühring*, Introduction) [C, S12].
- **Heidegger**, "The End of Philosophy and the Task of Thinking" (1964). The essay first appeared in French (*Kierkegaard vivant*, Gallimard 1966). The German is in *Zur Sache des Denkens* (Niemeyer 1969), pp. 61–80; Stambaugh's English is in *On Time and Being* (1972) [C, S13 editor's note; the 1964 Paris UNESCO occasion is U]. Krell's text:
  - "Philosophy is metaphysics." The end "means the completion of metaphysics. However, completion does not mean perfection". "The old meaning of the word 'end' means the same as place … End as completion means this gathering" [C, S13].
  - "With the reversal of metaphysics that was already accomplished by Karl Marx, the uttermost possibility of philosophy is attained" [C, S13].
  - The sciences' independence "looks like the mere dissolution of philosophy, yet in truth is precisely its completion … the sciences now establishing themselves will soon be determined and regulated by the new fundamental science that is called cybernetics … Philosophy is ending in the present age. It has found its place in the scientific attitude of socially active humanity" [C, S13].
  - What remains is "thinking", which "is less than philosophy" [C, S13].
  - Word test: *Ende* / *Vollendung*. Krell notes that the French edition rendered "end" as *achèvement* and that *Vollendung* stresses the "full" rather than the "ending" [C, S13 headnote]. Heidegger's German wording is not checked [U].

### (b) Completion

- **Hegel's own wording.** The *Phenomenology* preface, §5: his aim is "to help to bring philosophy nearer to the form of science – that goal where it can lay aside the name of love of knowledge and be actual knowledge" (Baillie) [C, S15]. What is set aside is the *name* philo-sophia, love of knowing; philosophy continues as science (*Wissenschaft*). The German wording is not checked [U].
- The close of the *Lectures on the History of Philosophy*: "This then is the standpoint of the present day, and the series of spiritual forms is with it for the present concluded" [C, S15]. Note the hedge: "for the present".
- The *Philosophy of Right* preface: philosophy "always comes too late … The owl of Minerva, takes its flight only when the shades of night are gathering" [C, S15]. This is philosophy as retrospect, not philosophy ended.
- **The reception reads more into it than Hegel says.** Engels (*Ludwig Feuerbach*, 1886): "At any rate, with Hegel philosophy comes to an end", because Hegel summed up its development and "showed us the way out of the labyrinth of systems to real positive knowledge of the world"; there is "an end to all philosophy in the hitherto accepted sense of the word" [C, S12]. The "end of history" reading (Kojève, Fukuyama) is a further reception [U]; the site's philosophy-of-history subject already carries Fukuyama.

### (c) Its questions are confusions of language

- **Carnap**, "Überwindung der Metaphysik durch logische Analyse der Sprache", *Erkenntnis* 2 (1932). The English is by Arthur Pap, "The Elimination of Metaphysics through Logical Analysis of Language" [C, S16; the translation appeared in Ayer (ed.), *Logical Positivism*, 1959, U]. Word test: *Überwindung* means "overcoming", and "Elimination" is the translator's choice. The target is metaphysics, not philosophy. Carnap asks: "But what, then, is left over for philosophy …? What remains is not statements, nor a theory, nor a system, but only a method: the method of logical analysis". That is "scientific philosophy" in contrast to metaphysics [C, S16]. And: "Metaphysicians are musicians without musical ability" [C, S16].
- **Wittgenstein, early.** Preface to the *Tractatus*: "the problems have in essentials been finally solved", and the book's value "consists in the fact that it shows how little has been done when these problems have been solved" (Ogden) [C, S14]. 6.53: "The right method of philosophy would be this: To say nothing except what can be said, i.e. the propositions of natural science, i.e. something that has nothing to do with philosophy … This method would be unsatisfying to the other … but it would be the only strictly correct method" [C, S14]. 6.54: the ladder [C, S14].
- **Wittgenstein, later** (*Philosophical Investigations*, Anscombe, Blackwell 1958 [C, S17]):
  - §109 opens: "It was true to say that our considerations could not be scientific ones … we may not advance any kind of theory … These are, of course, not empirical problems; they are solved, rather, by looking into the workings of our language" [C, S17]. Its sentence "Philosophy is a battle against the bewitchment of our intelligence by means of language" is lost in the scan's OCR [U].
  - §133: "the philosophical problems should completely disappear. The real discovery is the one that makes me capable of stopping doing philosophy when I want to.—The one that gives philosophy peace … There is not a philosophical method, though there are indeed methods, like different therapies" [C, S17].
  - §309: "What is your aim in philosophy?—To shew the fly the way out of the fly-bottle" [C, S17].
  - Note for the post: Wittgenstein's line runs against Hawking's. Philosophy is not to become science (§109; Tractatus 4.111, "Philosophy is not one of the natural sciences", Ogden [C, S14]).

### (d) It should act, not interpret

- **Marx**, thesis XI (spring 1845, Brussels; first published by Engels in 1888, edited, as an appendix to *Ludwig Feuerbach* [U, via a fetch summary of the German Wikipedia]): "The philosophers have only interpreted the world, in various ways; the point is to change it" [C, S18]. The manuscript German as reported: "Die Philosophen haben die Welt nur verschieden interpretirt, es kömmt drauf an sie zu verändern" [U, same route].
- **Marx's earlier, sharper form**, in the *Introduction* to the *Contribution to the Critique of Hegel's Philosophy of Right* (1843–44 [U, date]): "You cannot abolish [aufheben] philosophy without making it a reality"; "Philosophy cannot realize itself without the transcendence [Aufhebung] of the proletariat, and the proletariat cannot transcend itself without the realization [Verwirklichung] of philosophy" [C, S18]. Word test: *aufheben* (abolish / preserve / raise) is the Hegelian word, so the "end" is also a realisation.

### (e) The foundational project failed

- **Nietzsche** does not declare philosophy over. He ends the "true world": "The 'true world'—an idea that no longer serves any purpose … let us abolish it! … In abolishing the true world we have also abolished the world of appearance!" ("How the 'True World' Ultimately Became a Fable", *Twilight of the Idols*, Ludovici) [C, S19]. He calls for new philosophers: "THE REAL PHILOSOPHERS, HOWEVER, ARE COMMANDERS AND LAW-GIVERS" (*Beyond Good and Evil* §211, Zimmern) [C, S20].
  - He also met the scientists' version in 1886 and protested it. "The declaration of independence of the scientific man, his emancipation from philosophy", is "one of the subtler after-effects of democratic organization", and science, having resisted theology, "now proposes … to lay down laws for philosophy, and in its turn to play the 'master'—what am I saying! to play the PHILOSOPHER on its own account" (§204) [C, S20]. This is a dated fact, not a priority claim.
- **Rorty.** *Philosophy and the Mirror of Nature* (1979) argues that the mind-as-mirror picture, and with it philosophy as the foundational discipline that judges other knowledge, should be dropped [U at quotation level: book not opened; the site's Rorty page carries the headline]. The *Consequences of Pragmatism* introduction (1982), as quoted with page numbers in a peer-reviewed article (S26): "'philosophy' like 'truth' and 'goodness' is ambiguous. Uncapitalized, 'truth' and 'goodness' name properties of sentences, or of actions and situations. Capitalized, they are the proper names of objects – goals or standards which can be loved with all one's heart and soul and mind, objects of ultimate concern" (p. xiv); "Both traditions are now in a period of doubt about their own status. Both are living between a repudiated past and a dimly seen post-Philosophical future" (p. xxi); he "raised the question of whether a culture could get along without Philosophy, without the Platonic attempt to sift out the merely contingent and conventional truths from the Truths which were something more than that" (p. xxxviii) [C secondary, S26: quotations with pages, book not opened; a search for the xxi phrase found no second source]. In the passages quoted, Rorty's own phrase is "post-Philosophical *future*"; "post-philosophical culture" is the secondary literature's term (S26, S21), and Rorty's exact use of "post-Philosophical culture" stays [U]. Philosophy with a capital P is the Platonic attempt to find Truths beyond the contingent; the lowercase sense (Sellars's "hang together") is [U]. SEP calls him "an anti-philosopher's philosopher" [C, S22].
- **Derrida**, *Of Grammatology* (1967; Spivak 1976). The epoch is one "of which we merely glimpse the closure. I do not say the end" (Exergue, p. 4). "The age of the sign is essentially theological. Perhaps it will never end. Its historical closure is, however, outlined" (p. 14) [C, S23, Spivak, Motilal reprint pagination]. Word test: *clôture*, not *fin* [U, French not checked]. What closes is the "historico-metaphysical epoch", and the reading works within it.
- **Quine**, "Epistemology Naturalized" (in *Ontological Relativity and Other Essays*, Columbia 1969): "Epistemology, or something like it, simply falls into place as a chapter of psychology and hence of natural science". "The old epistemology aspired to contain, in a sense, natural science … Epistemology in its new setting, conversely, is contained in natural science, as a chapter of psychology" [C, S24, a course-reader excerpt; page 82 is U]. What ends is foundational epistemology. "Or something like it" keeps a successor.

### How the field frames the debate

- The field's own frame is "end *or transformation*". *After Philosophy: End or Transformation?*, ed. Baynes, Bohman and McCarthy (MIT Press, 1987) [C, S25 catalogue]. Its parts are reported as "The End of Philosophy" (Rorty, Lyotard, Foucault, Derrida) and two "Transformation" parts (Davidson, Dummett, Putnam, Apel, Habermas; Gadamer, Ricoeur, MacIntyre, Blumenberg, Taylor) [U, search summary of contents].

## 3. Which philosophy is said to be dead? (how commentators read it)

- **The scientists** mostly mean the usefulness of philosophy, especially philosophy of science, to working physicists. Weinberg says so explicitly. Krauss withdrew the "blanket condemnation". Tyson speaks of "deep questions about nature" [C, S7, S8, S10]. Hawking alone claims the big questions themselves ("What is the nature of reality?") for physics [C, S2]. In 1988 his complaint was the opposite: philosophy had shrunk itself to language analysis [C, S1].
- **The repliers** read Hawking as misinformed about the discipline (Maudlin). Others read him as doing philosophy while denying it (Rovelli, Thagard) [C, S5, S9, S3]. Pigliucci says the critics expect from philosophy of science a product it never aimed to make [C, S10].
- **The philosophers'** ends almost all target metaphysics or the foundational project: Comte, Carnap, Nietzsche, Heidegger ("Philosophy is metaphysics"), Derrida, Rorty, Quine. Hegel's end is the change from love of knowing into science. Marx's is the change from interpretation into realisation. Most name a successor: positive philosophy, the method of logical analysis, clarification as an activity, "thinking", lowercase philosophy, a chapter of psychology [C as marked above; Rorty C secondary, S26]. Heidegger is the one who reads the takeover by the sciences as the completion of philosophy itself, dissolved into them and steered by cybernetics [C, S13].
- On the brief's question, "specialised/academic form or the foundational project?": in these sources the scientists aim mainly at the specialised, academic form (philosophy of science, the profession), Hawking excepted. The philosophers aim mainly at the foundational project. The bundle plan's wording, "nearly all are about philosophy in its specialised form", fits the scientists better than the philosophers. This is a neutral reading of the sources. Whether to adjust the plan wording is for discussion.

  - (iii) Which philosophy: the scientists aim at the profession, the philosophers at the foundations.
