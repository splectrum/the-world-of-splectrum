# Vaiśeṣika, Yoga, Mīmāṃsā and one Lokāyata check: research note

Purpose: checked material for three school pages of the India section of the metaphilosophy subject. Each page answers three questions in running prose: what the activity (the love of knowledge and wisdom) is for the school, what its point is, and how it is done. Vaiśeṣika is for the page "Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika". Yoga is for "The investigative science", which covers Kauṭilya's grouping of Sāṃkhya, Yoga and Lokāyata. Mīmāṃsā is for "Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta", which so far shows Mīmāṃsā only through Śaṅkara. This is a working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a source I opened, keyed to the Sources list at the end. **[C as quoted]** = wording checked as it appears in a secondary source I opened; the primary was not opened. **[U]** = unverified. "My rendering" = my English, from Sanskrit I fetched.

## 1. Vaiśeṣika

### 1.1 The opening sūtras, and the recension problem

The headline is that two recensions differ on exactly the sūtra that matters here.

- **The commonly printed recension.** This is the text Śaṅkara Miśra's *Upaskāra* comments on, Sinha's base. GRETIL has it as an unproofread Sansknet file [C, VS-alt]:
  - 1.1.1 *athāto dharmaṃ vyākhyāsyāmaḥ*
  - 1.1.2 *yato 'bhyudayaniḥśreyasasiddhiḥ sa dharmaḥ*
  - 1.1.3 *tadvacanād āmnāyasya prāmāṇyam*
  - 1.1.4 *dharmaviśeṣaprasūtād dravyaguṇakarmasāmānyaviśeṣasamavāyānāṃ padārthānāṃ sādharmyavaidharmyābhyāṃ tattvajñānān niḥśreyasam*
- **Sinha's translation** [C, SIN pp. 3–8]:
  - 1.1.1: "Now, therefore, we shall explain Dharma."
  - 1.1.2: "Dharma (is) that from which (results) the accomplishment of Exaltation and of the Supreme Good."
  - 1.1.3: "The authoritativeness of the Veda (arises from its) being the Word of God [or being an exposition of dharma]."
  - 1.1.4: "The Supreme Good (results) from the knowledge, produced by a particular dharma, of the essence of the Predicables, Substance, Attribute, Action, Genus, Species, and Combination, by means of their resemblances and differences."
- **The other recension.** GRETIL's main file (input by Nozawa Masanobu, edition not named in the file) has 1.1.1–1.1.3 with the same wording (1.1.3 as *āmnāyaprāmāṇyam*), and then goes straight to the list of substances as 1.1.4. **The sūtra linking knowledge of the categories to *niḥśreyasa* is absent.** The words *tattvajñāna* and *niḥśreyasa* occur nowhere else in that file [C, VS-N].
  - Its numbering in Book 9 matches SEP's citation of "VS 9.18; Jambuvijaya (ed), 1961", the edition with Candrānanda's commentary [C, SEP-L, against VS-N 9.18]. So the file is very probably Candrānanda's recension [U as to the identification; inferred from the match].
- **Word test.**
  - *dharma*: Sinha keeps "Dharma" and glosses it "piety, religion" [C, SIN p. 5].
  - *niḥśreyasa*: "Supreme Good" (Sinha); "highest bliss" and "final beatitude" (Jha, on Praśastapāda) [C, SIN; JHA-P pp. 13–16].
  - *abhyudaya*: "Exaltation" (Sinha). The *Upaskāra*, as Sinha translates it, glosses *abhyudaya* as "knowledge of the essences" and *niḥśreyasa* as "final cessation of pain" [C, SIN p. 5].
  - "Philosophy" (Sinha's running head "Vaiśeṣika Philosophy") is the translator's word.
  - The sūtras' own word for the end is *mokṣa*: *tadabhāve saṃyogābhāvo 'prādurbhāvaḥ sa mokṣaḥ* (VS-N 5.2.20; VS-alt 5.2.18 without *sa*) [C, VS-N; VS-alt].
- **For the page:** the opening frames the treatise as an explanation of *dharma*, defined by its result (*abhyudaya* and *niḥśreyasa*). In the printed recension, knowledge of the six categories is what yields *niḥśreyasa*. In the other recension that link is not a sūtra; it is stated by Praśastapāda (next). Stated plainly, this is a detail that touches the headline.

### 1.2 Praśastapāda's opening: the purpose of the work

- **Sanskrit** (GRETIL, Dvivedin's 1895 edition) [C, PDS]:
  - Verse: *praṇamya hetum īśvaraṃ muniṃ kaṇādam anvataḥ / padārthadharmasaṃgrahaḥ pravakṣyate mahodayaḥ*
  - Then: *dravyaguṇakarmasāmānyaviśeṣasamavāyānāṃ ṣaṇṇāṃ padārthānāṃ sādharmyavaidharmyatattvajñānaṃ niḥśreyasahetuḥ*, and *tac ceśvaracodanābhivyaktād dharmād eva*.
- **Jha's translation (1916)** [C, JHA-P]:
  - Verse (p. 1): "Having bowed to Īśvara, the Cause, and then to the sage Kaṇāda, I am going to describe the nature of things (or, compose the work called 'Padārthadharmasaṅgraha') leading to the best of results."
  - Text (1) (p. 13): "A knowledge of the true nature of the six categories — Substance, Quality, Action, Generality, Individuality and Inherence — through their similarities and dissimilarities, — is the means of accomplishing the highest bliss."
  - Text (2) (p. 16): "This knowledge proceeds from dharma manifested by the injunction of the Lord."
- **The commentator's framing.** In Jha's version, Śrīdhara's *Nyāyakandalī* argues that a reader will not attend to a work until "the connection and the purpose" are understood, "as the activity of all intelligent persons is always preceded by a full recognition of the purpose to be fulfilled by that act" [C, JHA-P pp. 5–6].
  - It glosses *mahodaya* as "that work from which results the highest result, in the shape of Deliverance" [C, JHA-P p. 6].
  - **Word test:** "Deliverance" is Jha's English for Śrīdhara's word, and the gloss is Śrīdhara's, not Praśastapāda's.

### 1.3 Means of knowing

- **The sūtras: two.**
  - *tayor niṣpattiḥ pratyakṣalaiṅgikābhyām* (VS-N 10.4; VS-alt 10.1.3): "the two [doubt and ascertainment] arise from perception and from inference" [C, VS-N; my rendering].
  - *etena śābdaṃ vyākhyātam* (VS-N 9.19; VS-alt 9.2.3). Sinha: "Hereby verbal (cognition is) explained", which the *Upaskāra* reads as "by the characteristic of being inferential" [C, SIN p. 283].
- **Praśastapāda states it outright:** *śabdādīnām apy anumāne 'ntarbhāvaḥ samānavidhitvāt*. Jha (Text 105): "Word, and the other means of knowledge, are included under 'Inference', the process of these being similar to that of Inference" [C, PDS; JHA-P p. 418].
  - The Veda keeps its authority through this route. Its authority depends on the speaker's: *āmnāyo vaktṛprāmāṇyāpekṣaḥ*, citing VS 1.1.3 [C, PDS; my rendering].
- **Knowledge (*vidyā*) has four kinds** for Praśastapāda: *pratyakṣa-laiṅgika-smṛty-ārṣa*, "perception, inference, memory and the seers' cognition" [C, PDS; my rendering]. The sūtras also name *ārṣaṃ siddhadarśanaṃ ca* (VS-N 9.28) [C, VS-N]. Only perception and inference are *pramāṇa*s; the other two are kinds of knowing.

### 1.5 Natural philosophy first, liberation added later: Frauwallner's reading

- **Frauwallner's own words** (Bedekar's English translation) [C, FRA pp. 12–13]:
  - "The doctrine of deliverance … was introduced into the Vaiśeṣika system externally, without any inner connection with the rest of the system. As a matter of fact, it shows itself to be a later supplement which had originally nothing to do with the system. It was, on the contrary, a purely Nature-philosophy."
  - Vaiśeṣika is "a philosophical system — a phenomenon rare in India — which sets before it as its final goal not Deliverance, but the attempt to understand and explain the phenomenal world."
  - He sets classical Nyāya and classical Vaiśeṣika side by side: in both, deliverance was made to depend on knowing the categories "without changing, on that account, anything of the system" [C, FRA p. 12].
  - In his foreword, these schools "did not create a philosophy out of longing for Deliverance but … endeavoured to explain the world in a scientific manner out of pure striving for philosophical knowledge" [C, FRA, foreword, pp. vii–x].
- **Halbfass's assessment:**
  - Frauwallner "has tried to interpret the old Vaiśeṣika system as a fundamentally unsoteriological doctrine." His approach "is obviously influenced by the ideals of 'pure theory' and 'objective science' … and it can hardly be justified in its entirety. Nevertheless, it raises important and legitimate questions" [C, H p. 271].
  - Halbfass grants that there were "attempts … to strengthen and emphasize the religious and soteriological dimension of the system", for instance in the development of *adṛṣṭa* [C, H pp. 271–272].
  - Elsewhere he describes Frauwallner as having found "the spirit of 'real philosophy'" as "a kind of hidden layer" in Indian thought [C, H p. 158].
- **Word test:** "Deliverance" and "Nature-philosophy" are Bedekar's English for Frauwallner's German. The sūtra word is *mokṣa* (section 1.1).
- **Placement note:** this is a reading with a named critic, so it belongs on the page as a contested reception. The page should not state it as fact.

### 1.6 How Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika came to be one

- **The headline.** SEP (Ganeri): early Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika "developed in parallel until, at some point in the 11th or 12th century, they merged to form a new school, called 'Navya-Nyāya'" [C, SEP-EM].
  - SEP (Phillips): "In a dozen treatises, Udayana merges the separate literature and worldview of Vaiśeṣika … with Nyāya's", and adds absence as a seventh category [C, SEP-G].
  - IEP (Dasti): Nyāya's categories were "largely adapted, with some modifications, from its sister school Vaiśeṣika" [C, IEP-N].
- **The syncretist manuals.** Gurumurti (1932) follows Keith in speaking of "the syncretist school", writers "who tried to combine the Vaiśeṣika Metaphysics with the Nyāya Logic". He calls the two *samāna tantra*, "allied systems", with shared features from the start. He places Śivāditya's *Saptapadārthī* first, "as the earliest known model of the syncretist school" [C, GUR pp. xxviii–xxxi].
  - Śivāditya's date "is enshrouded in obscurity": editors disagree whether he preceded Udayana, and Gaṅgeśa quotes him [C, GUR pp. lxiv–lxv].
  - Gurumurti is dated scholarship; use him for the term and the genre, not for dates.
- **The doxographic side.** Haribhadra (8th c.) mentions those who merge Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika [U here; not re-opened for this note].

## 2. Yoga

### 2.1 *Yogasūtra* 1.1–1.4

- **Sanskrit** (GRETIL, Maas's input of Āgāśe's 1904 Ānandāśrama edition) [C, YS]:
  - 1.1 *atha yogānuśāsanam*
  - 1.2 *yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ*
  - 1.3 *tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam*
  - 1.4 *vṛttisārūpyam itaratra*
- **Woods (1914)** [C, WOODS p. xxx]: "1 Now the exposition of yoga [is to be made]. 2 Yoga is the restriction of the fluctuations of mind-stuff. 3 Then the Seer [that is, the Self] abides in himself. 4 At other times it [the Self] takes the same form as the fluctuations [of mind-stuff]."
- **The *Bhāṣya* on 1.1:** *athety ayam adhikārārthaḥ. yogānuśāsanaṃ śāstram adhikṛtaṃ veditavyam. yogaḥ samādhiḥ* [C, YSB].
  - Woods: "The expression 'now' indicates that a distinct topic commences here. The authoritative book which expounds yoga is to be understood as commenced. … yoga is concentration" [C, WOODS pp. 3–4].
- **Word test:**
  - *anuśāsana* is "instruction, teaching"; Woods renders it "exposition".
  - *citta* is "mind-stuff" and *vṛtti* "fluctuations" (both Woods).
  - *draṣṭṛ*, "the seer", is the sūtra's word; "[that is, the Self]" is Woods's bracket.

### 2.2 YS 1.7: the means of right knowledge

- *pratyakṣānumānāgamāḥ pramāṇāni* [C, YS]. Woods: "Sources-of-valid-ideas are perception and inference and verbal-communication" [C, WOODS p. xxx].
- **The point for the page:** in Yoga the *pramāṇa*s are listed as one of the five kinds of *vṛtti* (1.6), that is, among the fluctuations that 1.2 says are to be restricted [C, YS 1.5–1.7]. Knowing by the means of knowledge is itself an activity of the *citta* that the practice brings to rest.
- **Beyond the three means:** a further insight (*prajñā*) is said to have a different object from that of testimony and inference: *śrutānumānaprajñābhyām anyaviṣayā viśeṣārthatvāt* (1.49) [C, YS].

### 2.3 The goal: *kaivalya*, and how it relates to Sāṃkhya

- **YS 4.34** (final sūtra): *puruṣārthaśūnyānāṃ guṇānāṃ pratiprasavaḥ kaivalyaṃ svarūpapratiṣṭhā vā citiśaktir iti* [C, YS]. Woods: "Isolation is the inverse generation of the aspects, no longer provided with a purpose by the Self, or it is the Energy of Intellect grounded in itself" [C, WOODS p. xli].
- **The *Bhāṣya* on 4.34** explains *kaivalya* as the power of consciousness "alone" (*citiśaktir eva kevalā*), with no further connection to *buddhisattva* [C, YSB].
- **The Sāṃkhya link in the sūtras:**
  - 2.25 *tadabhāvāt saṃyogābhāvo hānaṃ taddṛśeḥ kaivalyam*. Woods: "This is the escape, the Isolation of the Seer" [C, YS; WOODS p. xxxv].
  - 2.26 *vivekakhyātir aviplavā hānopāyaḥ*. Woods: "The means of attaining escape is unwavering discriminative discernment" [C, YS; WOODS p. xxxv].
  - 3.55 *sattvapuruṣayoḥ śuddhisāmye kaivalyam* [C, YS].
  - So discrimination (*vivekakhyāti*) between *puruṣa* and the *guṇa*-made mind is the means, and *kaivalya* is the end.
- **The work calls itself an exposition of Sāṃkhya.** Each chapter colophon reads *iti śrīpātañjale sāṃkhyapravacane yogaśāstre …* [C, YSB]. Woods: "Of Patañjali's Yoga-treatise entitled Exposition of Sāṃkhya (Sāṃkhya-pravacana)" [C, WOODS p. 100].
  - Maas notes the same title in an interview [U, search summary of the History of Philosophy podcast transcript; not opened].
  - Bryant (IEP): "Yoga is not to be considered as a school distinct from Sāṁkhya until well after Patañjali's time"; "Sāṁkhya provides the metaphysical or theoretical basis …, and Yoga the technique or practice itself" [C, IEP-Y].
- **Word test, important here:**
  - *kaivalya* (from *kevala*, "alone") is "Isolation" for Woods, but "separation" for Maas, whose title for book 4 is "'on separation' (*kaivalyapāda*)" [C, MAAS].
  - Maas glosses the aim more broadly as "spiritual liberation during the present life of the yogi" [C, MAAS]. "Liberation" is his umbrella word, not the sūtra's.
  - The *Bhāṣya*'s own word for the goal in the fourfold scheme (2.4) is *mokṣa*.

### 2.4 How it is done, and what the treatise is for

- **YS 1.12:** *abhyāsavairāgyābhyāṃ tannirodhaḥ*. Woods: "The restriction of them is by [means] of practice and passionlessness" [C, YS; WOODS p. xxx].
  - 1.13 defines practice as effort toward steadiness (*tatra sthitau yatno 'bhyāsaḥ*). 1.15–16 define detachment, highest when it comes from discernment of *puruṣa* (*puruṣakhyāti*) [C, YS].
- **YS 2.29**, the eight limbs (*aṅga*): *yamaniyamāsanaprāṇāyāmapratyāhāradhāraṇādhyānasamādhayo 'ṣṭāv aṅgāni* [C, YS]. Woods: "Abstentions and observances and postures and regulations-of-the-breath and withdrawal-of-the-senses and fixed-attention and contemplation and concentration" [C, WOODS p. xxxv].
  - 2.28 says the limbs work "up to discriminative discernment" (*ā vivekakhyāteḥ*) [C, YS].
- **What the treatise is for: the *Bhāṣya* on 2.15.** This is the clearest self-statement. *yathā cikitsāśāstraṃ caturvyūham — rogo rogahetur ārogyaṃ bhaiṣajyam iti. evam idam api śāstraṃ caturvyūham eva. tadyathā — saṃsāraḥ saṃsārahetur mokṣo mokṣopāya iti* [C, YSB].
  - Woods: "Just as a system of medicine has four divisions, [on] Disease [and on] Cause of Disease [and on] Health [and on] Remedy, so this system also has four divisions, [on] the Round-of-Rebirth [and on] the Cause of the Round-of-Rebirth [and on] Release [and on] the Way to Release" [C, WOODS p. 135].
  - The means is *samyagdarśana*, "focused-insight" in Woods [C, YSB; WOODS p. 135].
  - **Word test:** Woods's running head for this passage, "Medical analogues to the Four Noble Truths", is his own comparison. The text does not mention the Buddhist truths.
  - The four terms map onto sūtras 2.16–2.26: *heya*, *heyahetu*, *hāna*, *hānopāya* [C, YS].

### 2.6 Placement flag: is Kauṭilya's *yoga* Patañjali's Yoga?

- **The list:** Kauṭilya: *sāṃkhyaṃ yogo lokāyataṃ ca-ity ānvīkṣikī* [C as quoted, H p. 274].
- **Halbfass on the word:** "What Kauṭilya means by *yoga*, the second item in his list, is not easy to determine. … the word *yoga* is by no means exclusively associated with the Yoga system of Patañjali." He notes that *yoga* "is occasionally used to refer to disciplines of 'reasoning' and 'argumentation,' such as Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika".
  - Vātsyāyana (on NS 1.1.29) mentions *pratitantrasiddhānta* of "Yogas" that differ from Pātañjala Yoga.
  - In Jaina doxographies Naiyāyikas and Vaiśeṣikas are often called "Yogins" [C, H p. 278].
- **Why it matters:** if the page "The investigative science" presents Patañjali's Yoga as Kauṭilya's second school, it states as fact a reading that Halbfass marks as uncertain. The *Arthaśāstra*'s date relative to the *Pātañjalayogaśāstra* (c. 400) is a further question [U, not checked].
  - **Option:** give Patañjali's Yoga its own account on that page, and say in a clause that what Kauṭilya meant by *yoga* is uncertain.

## 3. Lokāyata (one check): Jayarāśi

- **Date:** "most likely flourished between 800–840 probably in Southern India" [C, SEP-JAY].
  - Franco (1994) had 770–830. Balcerowicz places him after Akalaṅka (c. 720–780) and before Vidyānanda (c. 850) [C, SEP-JAY].
- **Only surviving work:** "The only preserved work of Jayarāśi is the *Tattvôpaplava-siṁha*"; it "was preserved without any commentary". The text was published in 1940 [C, SEP-JAY].
- **"The only surviving Cārvāka text" is contested; that is the headline.** SEP reports:
  - Saṃghavī and Pārīkh (1940) took it as "a work of the Lokāyata or Cārvāka school, or to be more precise – of a particular division of that school".
  - Franco speaks of a "radical change from a rather primitive materialism to a highly sophisticated form of scepticism".
  - Others class Jayarāśi "as a sceptic, either loosely affiliated to the Cārvāka / Lokāyata tradition or not at all", and Chattopadhyaya and others deny the affiliation outright [C, SEP-JAY].
  - Balcerowicz's own label is "a 'qualified sceptic' … affiliated to the materialist Cārvāka / Lokāyata school" [C, SEP-JAY].
  - **Safe wording:** the only complete work that survives from anyone associated with the materialists, and its author is classed variously as a materialist, a sceptic, or both.
- **Stance:** "it is not possible to arrive at true knowledge with standard means recognized by the traditionally adopted model of cognitive procedures (*pramāṇa*)", because "all valid cognitive procedures are either fundamentally flawed and ultimately unreliable or they require further valid cognitive procedures, and these stand in the same need" [C, SEP-JAY].
- **Primary text, opening.** Jayarāśi takes up the materialist sūtra's own opening, *athātas tattvaṃ vyākhyāsyāmaḥ / pṛthivy āpas tejo vāyur iti tattvāni*, and turns it [C, TUS 0.2]:
  - *pṛthivyādīni tattvāni loke prasiddhāni / tānyapi vicāryamāṇāni na vyavatiṣṭhante / kiṃ punar anyāni?* "The principles, earth and the rest, are well known in the world; even these, when examined, do not hold. How much less the others?" [C, TUS 0.2; my rendering]
  - Then: *sallakṣaṇanibandhanaṃ mānavyavasthānam / mānanibandhanā ca meyasthitiḥ*, "the establishing of a means of knowledge depends on a sound definition, and the standing of what is known depends on the means of knowledge" [C, TUS 0.3; my rendering].
  - The first target is the Nyāya definition of perception (NS 1.1.4) [C, TUS 1].
- **Title word test:** *tattvopaplava* is "dissolution / upset of the principles (categories)". SEP gives both "The Lion of the Dissolution of [all] Categories" and "The Lion [Destroying] the Delusion of Categories" [C, SEP-JAY].

## 4. Mīmāṃsā in its own voice

### 4.1 Śabara on MS 1.1.1–1.1.5: why the inquiry, and how *dharma* is known

- ***Mīmāṃsāsūtra*** (GRETIL, from B. D. Basu's edition, Allahabad 1923–25) [C, MS]:
  - 1.1.1 *athāto dharmajijñāsā*
  - 1.1.2 *codanālakṣaṇo 'rtho dharmaḥ*
  - 1.1.3 *tasya nimittaparīṣṭiḥ*
  - 1.1.4 *satsaṃprayoge puruṣasyendriyāṇāṃ buddhijanma tatpratyakṣam animittaṃ vidyamānopalambhanatvāt*
  - 1.1.5 *autpattikas tu śabdasyārthena saṃbandhas tasya jñānam upadeśo 'vyatirekaś cārthe 'nupalabdhe tatpramāṇaṃ bādarāyaṇasyānapekṣatvāt*
- **Jha's translations** (*Śabara-Bhāṣya*, GOS 1933, vol. I) [C, JHA-S]:
  - 1.1.1 (p. 1): "Next therefore (comes) the enquiry into Dharma."
  - 1.1.2 (p. 4): "Dharma is that which is indicated by (known by means of) the Veda as conducive to the highest good."
  - 1.1.3 (p. 7): "The examination of its means [follows]."
  - 1.1.4 (p. 8): "That cognition by a person which appears when there is contact of the sense-organs is 'Sense-perception'; and it is not a means (of knowing Dharma), as it apprehends only things existing at the time."
  - 1.1.5 (pp. 8–9): "The relation of the word with its denotation is inborn. — Instruction is the means of knowing it (Dharma) — infallible regarding all that is imperceptible; it is a valid means of knowledge, as it is independent, — according to Bādarāyaṇa."
- **Why the inquiry is undertaken (Śabara, p. 4).** The objection is that *dharma* is either known, so no inquiry is needed, or unknown, so no inquiry is possible. Śabara answers: "learned men have held divergent views regarding Dharma; some people regard one thing as 'Dharma', some regard a totally different thing as 'Dharma'. Under the circumstances, if a man were to undertake an act without due consideration, he might take up any one act at random and would thereby ruin himself and also incur evil consequences. For this reason it is necessary to carry on enquiry into Dharma; specially as we declare that Dharma endows man with the highest good" [C, JHA-S p. 4].
- **What the inquiry is (pp. 1–2).** It follows the reading of the Veda, and the student, instead of returning home, should stay at the teacher's house and "carry on an investigation into the meaning of Vedic texts" [C, JHA-S p. 2].
- **Why only the Veda (on 1.1.2 and 1.1.4).**
  - "It is the Vedic Injunction which is capable of making known … what is past, present or future, also what is subtle or hidden or remote …; this cannot be done by any organ of sense" [C, JHA-S p. 4].
  - On 1.1.4: "Dharma however is something that is yet to come, and it does not exist at the time that it is to be known; — while Sense-perception is the apprehending of an object that is actually present". Inference, analogy and presumption "presuppose (are based upon) Sense-perception", so they cannot be the means either [C, JHA-S p. 8].
- **The secondary frame.** SEP (Arnold): *dharma* "denotes a constitutively future state of affairs"; Mīmāṃsakas say it is always "yet to be" (*bhaviṣyat*). In early commentary it is "what 'connects a person with the highest good'" [C, SEP-K].
- **Word test:**
  - *codanā*: "Injunction" (Jha); "Vedic 'injunction'" (Arnold).
  - *dharma*: Jha keeps "Dharma" in the Śabara translation but renders it "Duty" in his *Ślokavārttika* translation (MS 1.1.2 there: "Duty is a purpose having Injunction for its sole authority") [C, JHA-SV p. 21].
  - *mīmāṃsā*: "literally means something like 'investigation' or 'inquiry,' but here refers particularly to hermeneutical inquiry" [C, SEP-K].
  - "Highest good" is Jha's English for Śabara's gloss of *artha*. Śabara's Sanskrit was not opened (section 6).

### 4.2 Kumārila: intrinsic validity and the authorless Veda

- **Primary (Jha's translation, *Ślokavārttika*, Codanā chapter, vv. 47–48)** [C, JHA-SV p. 28]:
  - 47: "You must understand that authoritativeness is inherent in all Means of Right Notion. For a faculty, by itself non-existing, cannot possibly be brought into existence by any other agency;"
  - 48: "since it is only for the sake of its birth (origination) that a positive entity requires a cause. And when it has once been born …, its Application to its various effects proceeds naturally out of itself."
  - The Sanskrit of v. 47 is usually given as *svataḥ sarvapramāṇānāṃ prāmāṇyam iti gamyatām / na hi svato 'satī śaktiḥ kartum anyena śakyate* [U; the GRETIL files I opened carry the commentary, not the root verse]. Sucarita's *Kāśikā* restates the result: *sarvapramāṇānāṃ svataḥpramāṇatvaṃ doṣataś cāpramāṇatvam*, "all means of knowledge are valid of themselves, and invalid from a fault" [C, SUC; my rendering].
- **The secondary.** SEP (Arnold): *svataḥ prāmāṇya* holds that *pramāṇa*s "must be accepted as having their reliability just 'intrinsically' (*svataḥ*)…, because any demonstration must itself presuppose reliable ways of knowing; … it's not demonstration but falsification that's ultimately decisive" [C, SEP-K].
  - Kumārila develops it in the "*codanā sūtra*" chapter, on Śabara's comment on MS 1.1.2 [C, SEP-K].
  - Arnold also renders the term "immediate justification" [C, SEP-K].
- ***apauruṣeya*:**
  - Arnold: Jaimini's fifth sūtra (the relation of word and referent is *autpattika*) is "meant to underwrite the claim that the Vedas themselves are transcendent or 'impersonal' (*apauruṣeya*)" [C, SEP-K].
  - Deshpande (SEP): "The Mīmāṃsā notion of the authority of the authorless Veda also depends upon their epistemic theory, that claims that all received cognitions are intrinsically valid (*svataḥ pramāṇa*), unless and until they are falsified" [C, SEP-LANG].
  - Phillips and Vaidya (SEP): "The words of the Veda are not spoken by anyone originally. Speakers are subject to error but not the Veda" [C, SEP-EPI].
- **Word test:** *apauruṣeya* is "not of a person"; "authorless" (Deshpande) and "impersonal" (Arnold) are both English choices. For *svataḥ*, Jha has "inherent" and "self-sufficient"; Arnold has "intrinsically" and "immediate justification".
- **Critics, as reported:**
  - Nyāya and most Vedāntic theists hold the Veda is authored, by the Lord [C, SEP-EPI].
  - Arnold notes the doctrine "will surely admit of ideological critique", and quotes Pollock that other Brahmanical disciplines "had perforce to conform to this special model of what counts as knowledge" [C as quoted, SEP-K].

### 4.3 Method: the *adhikaraṇa* as the unit of exegesis

- **A primary statement (Mādhava's *Jaiminīyanyāyamālāvistara*, 14th c.).**
  - *ekaikasyādhikaraṇasya viṣayaḥ, saṃdehaḥ, saṃgatiḥ, pūrvapakṣaḥ, siddhāntaś ceti pañcāvayavāḥ*: "each topic has five members: the subject, the doubt, the connection, the prima facie view and the conclusion" [C, JNMV 1.1.8; my rendering; the e-text reads *paścāvayavāḥ*, a typo for *pañca-*].
  - Mādhava's verses then give the four members other than connection, which he says the reader can supply: *viṣaya-saṃśaya-pūrvapakṣa-siddhānta* [C, JNMV 1.1.7–9, 1.1.29].
  - The work names Bukka of Vijayanagara as patron [C, JNMV 1.0.2]. That supports a 14th-century date [U for the exact date].
- **The five-part list in the brief** (subject, doubt, prima facie view, reply, conclusion) matches the commonly quoted verse *viṣayo viśayaś caiva pūrvapakṣas tathottaram / nirṇayaś ceti pañcāṅgaṃ śāstre 'dhikaraṇaṃ smṛtam* [U; source of the verse not traced].
  - **Headline either way:** each topic is settled by stating the text in question, the doubt, the opponent's view and the established view.
- **The practice is visible in Śabara.** Jha's chapter headings number each topic as an *Adhikaraṇa*: "Adhikaraṇa (1): Propounding of the Enquiry"; "(2): Definition of Dharma"; "(4): Dharma not amenable to such means of Cognition as Sense-perception"; "(5): Dharma cognisable by means of Verbal Injunctions". Objections are answered in turn [C, JHA-S pp. 1–8].
- **Arnold on the activity:** Mīmāṃsā's "constitutive focus … is on interpreting the corpus of texts (the 'Vedas') that enjoin the ritual performances of Vedic religion". Kumārila's insights were influential "for Indian theorists of hermeneutics, poetics, and jurisprudence" [C, SEP-K].

## 5. Placement options (not decisions)

- **"Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika":**
  - The activity is *dharma* explained by its fruit (VS 1.1.1–2).
  - The point is *niḥśreyasa* through knowing the six categories (VS 1.1.4 in the printed recension; Praśastapāda's opening in any case).
  - The method is analysis by likeness and difference (*sādharmya-vaidharmya*), with two means of knowledge and testimony folded into inference.
  - Frauwallner's "nature-philosophy first" goes in as his reading with Halbfass's criticism. The merger is one sentence: Udayana in the 10th century; the syncretist manuals; Navya-Nyāya.
- **"The investigative science":**
  - Yoga in its own words: 1.1–1.4, 1.7 with the *pramāṇa*s placed among the *vṛtti*s, *kaivalya* through *vivekakhyāti*, *abhyāsa-vairāgya*, the eight limbs, and the *Bhāṣya*'s four-part medical scheme.
  - The colophon *sāṃkhyapravacana* ties it to Sāṃkhya on the text's own terms.
  - Halbfass's caution about Kauṭilya's *yoga* should be stated in a clause.
  - Jayarāśi goes under Lokāyata with the affiliation dispute as the headline.
- **"Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta":**
  - The activity is *dharmajijñāsā*, an investigation into the meaning of Vedic texts after study.
  - The point is *dharma*, known only through *codanā*, because it is "yet to be".
  - The method is the *adhikaraṇa*.
  - The epistemology is *svataḥ prāmāṇya* and the authorless Veda, with Nyāya's and modern critics' objections as reported.
- **Person pages:** none proposed by default. Candidates for discussion, with the importance case:
  - **Kumārila:** the SEP says virtually all later Indian philosophers "found it necessary to engage" his positions [C, SEP-K].
  - **Patañjali:** the author-redactor of the founding Yoga text.
  - **Udayana:** the merger, and his arguments for God.
  - Kaṇāda (legendary), Praśastapāda, Śabara and Jayarāśi probably stay as names on the school pages.
