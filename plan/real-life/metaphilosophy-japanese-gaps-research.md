# Metaphilosophy in Japan, the gaps: research note

Purpose: to fill eight gaps for the Japan section of the Metaphilosophy subject, which is being rebuilt as a landing page plus one page per line. The gaps are Tominaga Nakamoto; Hōnen and Shinran; Miura Baien and Andō Shōeki; Ishida Baigan; Nishitani Keiji; Watsuji Tetsurō; Maruyama Masao; and one or two other post-1945 voices. For each the note asks the section's three questions: what the activity is (philosophy, the love of knowledge and wisdom; before Meiji, *gaku* and *michi*/*dō*), what its point is, and how it is done. The answers come from the tradition's own texts and thinkers. Scholars are used for facts only: dates, what a word meant, translations. This is a working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a source I opened, named by its key in the Sources list. **[C as quoted]** = the wording is checked as it appears in a secondary source I opened; the primary was not opened. **[U]** = unverified. "My rendering" = mine, from the Japanese or Chinese text I fetched.

**Access.**
- Most Japanese primary text here comes from the full-text OCR of the National Diet Library's digitised books (NDL Digital Collections, searched through NDL Lab). It is marked [C], with the edition and NDL id. **This is OCR, not the page image.** Check the wording against the image before a page quotes it in Japanese.
- Pye's translation of Tominaga, Mercer's of Baien, Yasunaga's of Shōeki and the Iwanami text of Maruyama's *Nihon no shisō* could not be opened. They are listed under "Sources to fetch".
- Aozora Bunko has none of these texts. Japanese Wikisource has only Hōnen's *Ichimai kishōmon*, plus a 1923 paraphrase (not the original) of the *Tannishō*.

## 1. Tominaga Nakamoto 富永仲基 (1715–1746)

**Dates and setting.**
- 1715–1746 (正徳5–延享3) [C, G3]. He was a son of one of the five Osaka merchants who founded the Kaitokudō (懐徳堂), the "townsman academy" (町人学問所), and studied there under Miyake Sekian [C, G3].
- The story that his early *Setsuhei* (説蔽), a historical critique of Confucianism written at about fifteen, got him expelled is reported as a legend (伝説) [C, G1]. The book is lost [C, G1].
- *Shutsujō kōgo* 出定後語 is in two volumes and 25 chapters [C, G1, G2]. Its preface is dated 「延享元年秋八月」 (1744) [C, G4, manuscript copy], and it was printed in 1745 [C, G1, G2]. That is why dictionaries give either 1744 or 1745; it is a detail.
- *Okina no fumi* 翁の文, in plain Japanese, was printed in 1746 [C, G1, G3]. Tominaga died that year.
- The title means "words after coming out of *samādhi*" (定を出て後語る). Tominaga styles himself the 出定如来 [C as quoted, G1].

**What the inquiry is: *kajō* 加上, "adding on top".**
- *Okina no fumi* states it plainly (1746 print, NDL): 「おほよそ古より道をとき法をはじむるもの、必ずそのかこつけて祖とするところありて、我より先にたてたる者の上を出んとするがその定まりたるならはしにて、後の人は皆これをしらずして迷ふことをなせり」. My rendering: "All who from of old have expounded a way and founded a teaching have had someone they fasten it to as founder, and have sought to rise above those who stood before them. That is the fixed custom, and later people, not knowing it, have gone astray" [C, G5a; also G5b].
- *Shutsujō kōgo* ch. 1, in Japanese reading: 「これ諸教興起の分かるるはみな、もとそのあひ加上するに出づ。そのあひ加上するにあらずんば、則ち道法何ぞ張らん。乃ち古今道法の自然なり。しかるに後世の学者、みないたづらに謂へらく、諸教はみな金口親しく説く所…と」. My rendering: "The divisions in the rise of the teachings all come from their adding on top of one another. Without that, how would ways and teachings spread? It is the natural course of ways and teachings, past and present. Yet later scholars all idly say that every teaching was spoken by the Buddha's own golden mouth" [C as quoted, G1, from *Nihon shisō taikei* 43 pp. 19–20].
- Kanno's summary of chapter 1: Buddhism adds on top of the non-Buddhist heavens; Mahāyāna adds on top of the Hīnayāna; and the *Lotus*, the *Avataṃsaka*, the sudden teaching and the esoteric sūtras each add on top of the one before [C, G1]. Mizuta: the three are 〈加上〉, 立論心理の法則的把握, "a law-like grasp of the psychology of argument" [C, G3].

**How it is done: "speech has three things" (言有三物), ch. 11.**
- 「凡言有類有世有人、謂之言有三物。一切語言解以三物者、吾教学之立也。苟以此求之、天下道法一切語言未嘗不錯然而分也。故云三物五類立言之紀是也」 [C, G4, 1802 and 1805 prints, OCR].
- My rendering: "Every saying has its kind, its age and its person: this is what I call 'speech has three things'. Reading all speech by the three things is what my teaching stands on. Seek by it, and all the ways, teachings and words in the world, tangled as they are, never fail to come apart. So I say: the three things and the five kinds are the guideline for setting forth words." The rendering of 錯然而分 is uncertain [U].
- Mizuta glosses the three as person (部派, the school), age (時代) and kind (言語の用法, how the word is used) [C, G2].
- Also: 「夫言有物道為之分、国有俗道為之異」, "speech has its things, and by them ways divide; countries have their customs, and by them ways differ" (my rendering). The Buddhist teaching 「所貴在幻」, prizes magic; the Ru prize 文, rhetoric [C, G4, 1805 print, OCR]. In *Okina no fumi* Japan's habit is secrecy and the guarding of transmissions (神秘・秘伝・伝授好み, Kanno's gloss) [C as quoted, G1, citing §§14–16]. The Japanese word Tominaga uses for this is [U].

**What it is for: the Way of sincerity (*makoto no michi* 誠の道).**
- *Okina no fumi* §1: 「此の三教の道は、皆誠の道に、かなはざる道也としるべし。いかにとなれば、仏は天竺の道、儒は漢の道、国ことなれば、日本の道にあらず。神は日本の道なれども、時ことなれば、今の世の道にあらず。…道の道といふ言の本は、行はるるより出たる言にて、行はれざる道は、誠の道にあらざれば…」 [C, G5a; C as quoted, G1].
- My rendering: "Know that the ways of these three teachings all fail to accord with the Way of sincerity. Why? The Buddha's is the way of India and the Ru's the way of Han China; the country differs, so they are not the way of Japan. Shinto is the way of Japan, but the time differs, so it is not the way of the present age … The word 'way' in 'the way that can be called a way' comes from being practised, and a way that is not practised is not the Way of sincerity …"
- §6 then lists the content. It is plain conduct: 「唯物ごとそのあたりまへをつとめ、今日の業を本とし、心をすぐにし…今の文字をかき、今の言をつかひ、今の食物をくらひ…今の掟を守…もろもろのあしきことをなさず、もろもろのよき事を行ふを、誠の道ともいひ…」, "just do what is ordinary in each thing, take today's work as the root, keep the heart straight … write today's script, speak today's language, eat today's food … keep today's laws … do no evil and do all good" (my rendering) [C as quoted, G1, NKBT 97 pp. 551–553; the 1746 print has the same opening, C, G5a].
- Its source: 「本天竺より来りたるにもあらず。漢より伝へたるにもあらず。又神代のむかしに始りて、今の世に習ふにもあらず。天よりくだりたるにもあらず。地より出たるにもあらず。只今日の人の上にて…人のあたりまへより出来たる事にて」, "it did not come from India, was not handed down from China, did not begin in the age of the gods; it did not come down from heaven or up from the earth; it comes from what is ordinary for people today" (my rendering) [C, G5a, G5b].
- *Shutsujō kōgo*, the close: 「諸法あひ万すといへども、その要は善をなすに帰す。…仏もまた可なり。儒もまた可なり。苟に善をつくるをなせる者は、乃ち一家なり」, "the teachings differ ten-thousandfold, but their point comes back to doing good … Buddhism will do, Confucianism will do; whoever does good is of one house" (my rendering) [C as quoted, G1, NST 43 p. 105]. Tominaga himself says in §7 that this ethic is nothing new [C as reported, G1].

**On the three questions.** Q1: a historical sorting of all doctrine by *kajō* and by the three things. Q2: to clear the way to a practised, present-day Way of sincerity: do good. Q3: read every saying by its person, its age and its kind.

**Reception, facts only.**
- Norinaga praised the book in *Tamakatsuma* 8 (1799): 「見るにめさむるここちする事共おほし」, "much in it is eye-opening". Of the monk Musō Bun'yū's rebuttal *Hi-shutsujō* (1759) he wrote that it only shouted abuse (ひたぶるに大声を出して、ののしりたるのみ) and refuted not a line [C as quoted, G1].
- Hirata Atsutane hunted the book down after reading that, and wrote *Shutsujō shōgo* [C, G1].
- Buddhist rebuttals followed in 1746, 1759 and 1819 [C, G1].
- **Priority claims to discard.** Britannica calls it 大乗非仏説の先駆; Mizuta calls it 文化人類学的発想を先取; Kanno speaks of "already proposing" what modern Buddhist studies made common sense [C, G1–G3]. All are reception.
- **Detail, one clause at most.** Kanno: *kajō* is "said to be" influenced by Sorai (徂徠の思想の影響を受けたものともいわれている) [C, G1]. The Nipponica has him study under Tanaka Tōkō, "a close friend of Sorai" [C, G3].

**Grouping.** Tominaga sorts all three teachings from outside each. That makes him the landing page's best example of Japan sorting its own teachings, beside Kūkai's ranking of ten states of mind. The contrast is by mechanism: ranking versus historical layering. The other option is an "outside the schools" page (§9). His link to Native Studies runs through Norinaga and Atsutane.

## 2. Hōnen 法然 (1133–1212) and Shinran 親鸞 (1173–1263)

**Dates** [C, G11]. Hōnen founded the Jōdoshū. His *Senchaku hongan nembutsu shū* is in *kanbun*, "addressed to an audience versed in Buddhist erudition", and argues "logically and cogently on the basis of scriptural evidence" (Hirota) [C, G11].

**Hōnen: what learning is for.**
- On himself, a monk "renowned in his period as a Tendai monk of extensive learning": "Although Buddhism is vast, in essence it is composed of no more than the three learnings [of precepts, meditation, and wisdom.] … But as for precepts, I myself do not keep a single one. In meditation, I have not attained even one. In wisdom, I have not attained the right wisdom…". The translation is Hirota's, from *Jōdoshū zensho* 7: 95–96 [C as quoted, G11]. The "three learnings" are 三学.
- ***Ichimai kishōmon*** 一枚起請文, dated 建暦二年正月二十三日 (1212) [C, G9]:
  - 「又學文をして念の心を悟リテ申念佛ニモ非ズ」. Inagaki: "nor is it the recitative Nembutsu practiced with full understanding of the meaning of 'nen' that becomes clear from study" [C, G9, G10].
  - 「念佛を信ゼン人ハたとひ一代ノ法を能々學ストモ。一文不知ノ愚どんの身ニナシテ…ちシャノふるまいヲせずして。只一かうに念佛すべし」. Inagaki: "Those who accept the Nembutsu in faith, however well versed in the lifetime teachings of the Buddha, should consider themselves as illiterate, stupid persons, and without pretensions to wisdom, should single-heartedly recite the Nembutsu" [C, G9, G10].
  - Word test: 一文不知, "not knowing a single letter", is Hōnen's phrase. "Illiterate" is Inagaki's.

**Shinran, through the *Tannishō* 歎異抄.**
- The *Tannishō* is a record of Shinran's words made by a disciple, who names himself Yuien-bō in the text (Shinran addresses him so in ch. 13). The text survives in Rennyo's copy, with his note [C, G6]. That Yuien compiled it, and the date (c. 1288), are [U].
- **Ch. 2, the scholars.** 「南都北嶺にもゆゆしき學生たちおほく座せられて候ふなれば、かの人人にもあひたてまつりて、往生の要よくよく聞かるべきなり」 (Akegarasu ed. 1932, OCR) [C, G7]. CWS: "since there are many eminent scholars in the southern capital of Nara or on Mount Hiei to the north, you would do better to meet with them and inquire fully about the essentials for birth. As for me, I simply accept and entrust myself to what my revered teacher told me, 'Just say the nembutsu and be saved by Amida'" [C, G6].
- **Ch. 10.** 「念仏には無義をもて義とす」 [C as quoted, G8 Matsubara 1936]. CWS: "Concerning the nembutsu, no working is true working. For it is beyond description, explanation, and conceptual understanding" [C, G6]. Kasulis renders 無義の義 as "working of nonworking (*mugi no gi*)" [C, G12]. Word test: 義 is *gi*, "working" for both CWS and Kasulis. Shin commentators gloss it as はからひ, calculation (義とははからひで) [C, G8 Matsubara].
- **Ch. 12, study and the Path of Sages, the most direct statement on learning.**
  - Heading: 「經釋をよみ學せざるともがら、往生不定のよしのこと、この條すこぶる不足言の義といひつべし」 (Chikazumi ed. 1906, OCR) [C, G7]. CWS: "On the assertion that for practicers who do not read the sutras and commentaries and engage in study, birth is not settled. This statement must be declared hardly worth mentioning" [C, G6].
  - CWS: "Apart from this, what learning is essential for birth?"; "nothing is more to be pitied than failure to understand the fundamental intent of the sacred teachings even though one reads and studies the sutras and commentaries" [C, G6].
  - 「學問をむねとするは聖道門なり、難行となづく。あやまて學問して、名聞利養のおもひに住するひと、順次の往生いかヾあらんずらん…」 [C, G7]. CWS: "It is the Path of Sages that takes learning to be essential; it is called 'difficult practice.' As for those who engage in study with wrong intentions, dwelling in thoughts of fame and profit, is there not the authoritative passage: 'I wonder if their birth in the next life is really settled'?" [C, G6].
  - On debate. CWS: "Where disputation takes place, blind passions arise. The wise keep their distance" [C, G6].
  - Learning's one use. CWS: "If one studies, more and more one realizes Amida's fundamental intent … so that one can explain, to those who anxiously wonder how birth is possible for the wretched people like themselves, that the Primal Vow does not discriminate … Only then is there meaning in being a scholar" [C, G6]. The original has 「…聞かせられ候はばこそ、學生のかひにても候はめ」 [C, G7]. *Gakushō no kai*, the worth of being a scholar.
- **Postscript.** "I know nothing at all of good or evil … all matters without exception are empty and false, totally without truth and sincerity. The nembutsu alone is true and real" (CWS) [C, G6].

**Shinran's own hand: *Jinen hōnishō* 自然法爾章.**
- CWS heads it "A writing by Shinran at age eighty-eight" [C, G6b]. A transcript printed in 1935 carries 「愚禿親鸞八十五歲 正嘉二歲十二月日 … 顯智これをかくなり」 [C, G8 Uno, OCR]. The age differs between the copies. That is a detail; say "late in life".
- 「自然といふは、自はおのづからといふ、行者のはからひにあらず、然といふはしからしむといふことばなり」 [C, G8 Umehara]. CWS: "*Ji* means 'of itself' – not through the practicer's calculation. It signifies being made so" [C, G6b].
- The close is a direct statement on discussing the point. 「この道理をこころえつるのちには、この自然のことはつねにさたすべきにはあらざるなり。つねに自然をさたせば、義なきを義とすといふことは、なほ義のあるになるべし。これは佛智の不思議にてあるなり」 [C, G8, OCR]. CWS: "After we have realized this, we should not be forever talking about jinen. If we continuously discuss jinen, that no working is true working will again become a problem of working. It is a matter of inconceivable Buddha-wisdom" [C, G6b].
- Hirota glosses *jinen* as "'naturalness' or 'being made to become so of itself'" [C, G11].
- **Word test.** *Tariki* 他力 (Other Power) and *jiriki* 自力 (self-power) are the tradition's words. "Other Power" is the usual English. *Hakarai* はからひ is "calculation" in CWS.

**On the three questions.** Q1: the Path of Sages (*shōdōmon*) "takes learning to be essential"; the Pure Land path does not. Q2: birth in the Pure Land, by Other Power, not self-power. Learning has a point only if it makes the Vow plain to those who doubt. Q3: say the nembutsu; study only to see the Vow's intent; keep away from disputation. Even *jinen* should not be talked about endlessly.

**Grouping.**
- These belong on a "Buddhism in Japan" page beside Kūkai (teachings ranked as states of mind) and Dōgen (study the Buddha Way: study the self).
- The three give three answers to "what is study for": rank, self-study, and study set aside for entrusting.
- The Shinran–Tanabe link (*tariki*) is a natural cross-reference to the Kyoto School page.
- **Reception to discard.** Hirota's SEP entry reports Barth's view of Shin as a "parallel" to Reformation Christianity [C, G11]. That is a Western framing; leave it out.

## 3. Miura Baien 三浦梅園 (1723–1789) and Andō Shōeki 安藤昌益 (1703–1762)

### Miura Baien

**Dates and setting** [C, G14].
- 1723–1789. A physician (儒医) in Kunisaki, Bungo (Ōita), mostly self-taught. He left home only for one pilgrimage to Ise and two trips to Nagasaki, and declined every invitation to serve a domain.
- The "three words" (梅園三語), all in *kanbun*: *Gengo* 玄語 (completed at 53), *Zeigo* 贅語 and *Kango* 敢語.
- His learning is *jōrigaku* 条理学, "the learning of *jōri*". Mypedia: around thirty he grasped that there is 〈条理〉 in natural phenomena, and he named the learning that inquires into it 〈条理学〉.

**The letter to Taga Bokkyō** (多賀墨卿君にこたふる書). Shimada Kenji calls it his own Japanese summary of *Gengo* [C, G14]. Text: *Baien zenshū* vol. 2 (1912), p. 503ff., and Saegusa ed. 1946 [C, G13, OCR]. Its date is [U].

- **Doubt.** 「かく物に不審の念をさしはさまば、月日のゆきかへり、造化の推遷るは更にして、己が有と占め置ける目のみえ耳のきこゆるも、態をなす手足も、物をおもふ心もひとつとして合點ゆきたる事はあるまじく候」 [C, G13].
  - My rendering: "Once one puts doubt into things, not only the coming and going of sun and moon and the changes of creation, but the eyes' seeing and the ears' hearing that one claims as one's own, the hands and feet that do things, the very heart that thinks: not one of them will be understood."
- **The "should" (*hazu* 筈).** 「そを世の人いかがすますとなれば筈といふものをこしらへてこれにかけてしまふ也。其筈とは、目は見ゆる筈、耳は聞ゆる筈、重き物は沈む筈、かろき物は浮ぶ筈、是はしれたる事也とすますなり。然れば其次手に雷は鳴る筈にて鳴り地震は動く筈にて動き枯木に花さかんもさけばさく筈石の物いはんもいへばいふ筈とすまし度物なり」 [C, G13].
  - My rendering: "How do people settle this? They make up a 'should' and hang everything on it: the eye should see, the ear should hear, heavy things should sink, light things should float. This is known, they say, and settle it. By the same token they would settle that thunder rumbles because it should, the earth shakes because it should, a dead tree that flowers flowers because it should, a stone that speaks speaks because it should."
  - **This is the source of the popular "withered tree" saying.** The text has 枯木に花さかん; the wording in circulation is a later paraphrase, not Baien's [U on the paraphrase's source].
- **The learning of his day.** 「又少し書讀るなどいふ人は雷は陰陽の鬪などいひて人をさとすなり其人に陰陽といふものをとへばしらず」 [C, G13].
  - My rendering: "Those who have read a few books explain to people that thunder is the battle of yin and yang; ask them what yin and yang are, and they do not know."
  - Then: 「この故に、智を天地に達せんとならば、雷をあやしみ、地震をいぶかる心を手がゝりとして、此天地をくるめて一大疑團となしたき物に候」, "therefore, if you would carry knowing through to heaven and earth, take as your handhold the heart that wonders at thunder and questions the earthquake, and make the whole of heaven and earth one great mass of doubt" (my rendering) [C, G13].
  - **Word test.** あやしむ is "to find strange, to wonder at". That 疑團 (a mass of doubt) echoes the Zen "great doubt" (大疑團) is [U].
- **Method.** 「其達觀する處の道は、則條理にて、條理の訣は反觀合一、捨心之所執、依徴於正のみに候。捨心之所執とは、習氣を離るゝ事にして、依徵於正とは、徴と見えながら徴にあらざる徵あり。たとへば、日月は慥に西にゆくの徵あれども、其實は東に行く」 [C, G13].
  - My rendering: "The way one sees through to is *jōri*; and the secret of *jōri* is only this: *hankan gōitsu* (反観合一, 'look by reversing, and unite'), let go of what the heart clings to, rely on correct evidence. Letting go of what the heart clings to means leaving one's habitual bent (習気). As for relying on correct evidence: there are signs that look like evidence and are not. The sun and moon plainly show signs of going west, but in fact they go east."
- **Opposites.** 「反するに因て一に合す。天地のなる處なり。反して一なるものあるによりて、我これを反して觀合せて觀て、其本然を求むるにて候」, "because they oppose, they unite in one: that is how heaven and earth come about. Because there are things that are one by opposing, I look at them reversed and look at them joined, and seek what they are of themselves" (my rendering) [C, G13]. Also 「天地の道は陰陽にして陰陽の體は對して相反す」, "the way of heaven and earth is yin and yang, and yin and yang stand opposed" [C, G13].
- **Heaven and earth as teacher, not people.**
  - 「古人經〓の名目をば設けながら…是又慣るるに安んじ書藉の習氣を執し徵に正による事能はず其造言の始の人を神聖とたてこれを造物者の位に置き候是即天地を師とせず人を師とするの弊にて御座候」 [C, G13]. My rendering: "Content with what they are used to, clinging to the habits of books, unable to rely on correct evidence, they set up the first man who made the words as a divine sage and put him in the creator's place. This is the harm of taking people, not heaven and earth, as teacher."
  - 「天地を師とする時は、古の聖賢より諸子百家今日蒭蕘狂夫の言葉にいたる迄、等の隔てはあるべけれども、ともに文を以て友を會する位にして、取舍は各あるべき事に候」, "when heaven and earth is the teacher, the words of the ancient sages and worthies, the hundred schools, down to today's grass-cutters and madmen, differ in rank, yet all stand as friends met through writing, each to be taken or left" (my rendering) [C, G13].
  - 「天地をしるは我私の意を入れず、あるまゝに天地に從ひて、天地を師とするにしくはなく候。されども天地物いはず…口舌を以て爭はんには盡期なく自得にしくはなく候」, "to know heaven and earth, nothing is better than to put in nothing of one's own, follow heaven and earth as they are, and take them as teacher. But heaven and earth do not speak … argue with the tongue and there is no end; nothing is better than getting it oneself (*jitoku*)" (my rendering) [C, G13].
- **Word test.** *Jōri* 条理 is Baien's own word. "Logic of opposites" (the brief's phrase) is a gloss, and Saegusa Hiroto's reading of it as dialectics (弁証法) is reception [C, G14]. *Hankan gōitsu* 反観合一 is Baien's.

**On the three questions.** Q1: *jōrigaku*, the learning of *jōri* in heaven and earth. Q2: to "carry knowing through to heaven and earth" (智を天地に達せん). Q3: doubt, leaving one's habitual bent, correct evidence, *hankan gōitsu*; heaven and earth as teacher; *jitoku*.

### Andō Shōeki

**Dates and setting.**
- 1703–1762 [C, G15, Nipponica]. Heibonsha gives "1703?"; Britannica gives "1707?" with the death year unknown [C, G15]. Use 1703–1762 and note the uncertainty in a clause.
- A town physician in Hachinohe in 1744–46, he died in Niida, Akita [C, G15].
- *Shizen shin'eidō* 自然真営道 exists as a manuscript of 101 fascicles and as a three-volume print of 1753 [C, G15]. Fifteen fascicles of the manuscript survive (現存十五册), on the 1936 editor's count [C as found, G16]. Saegusa notes that the social critique is in the manuscript, not the print [C, G16 as found].
- Fact: Kanō Kōkichi acquired the manuscript around 1899, and the work was "wholly buried" until then [C, G15, Bitō].

**What he says of learning** (manuscript text as quoted; locator [U]):
- 「一切ノ文字ハ、己レガ得手勝手ニ私作シ、書学ト為シ、之レヲ以テ上ニ立チ、下ヲ教ユルト為テ私法ヲ立テ、不耕貪食シテ、直耕ノ転道ヲ盗ミ、盗乱ノ根ヲ植ユルヲ、転下ヲ治ムルト為ス。…故ニ字書・学問ハ転道ヲ盗ムノ器具ナリ。…故ニ文字・書学ヲ用ユル者ハ、転真ノ大敵ナリ。此ノ故ニ予、文字・書学ヲ採ラザル所以是レナリ」 [C as quoted, G18; no locator given there].
  - My rendering: "All letters were privately made up [by the sages] to suit themselves and turned into book-learning; with it they stood above and taught those below, set up private laws, ate greedily without cultivating, stole the heaven-earth Way of direct cultivation, and planted the root of theft and disorder, and called this governing the world … So letters and learning are tools for stealing the Way … whoever uses letters and book-learning is the great enemy of the true … This is why I do not take up letters and book-learning."
  - Word test: 転 for 天 (and 転定 for 天地) is Shōeki's own spelling [C, G15, Bitō].
- 「儒書佛書共に自然眞道の本體なる直耕の妙序を顯はすこと無く、之を埋めて、妄りに天地、日月、男女、君民、佛衆、上下、尊卑、善惡等凡て二別の〓門を立て、二にして一眞氣なる眞道を盜み天下を迷はす」, "Confucian and Buddhist books alike never reveal the wondrous order of direct cultivation, the body of the true Way of nature, but bury it, and idly set up the twofold divisions of heaven and earth, sun and moon, man and woman, lord and people, buddha and beings, high and low, noble and base, good and evil; they steal the true Way that is two and yet one true *ki*, and lead the world astray" (my rendering) [C as quoted, G17]. One character is lost in the OCR.
- **On the sages** (*Nihon tetsugaku zensho* 9, OCR) [C, G16]: they set up the five relations and the four classes, 「君相を以て己れ衆人の上に立ち、不耕にして安食安衣し、衆に敬れんが爲に…上下を決め、之れを法として、君相を以て上に立つ」, "made themselves lord and minister above the people, ate and dressed at ease without cultivating, and, to be revered, fixed high and low and made it law" (my rendering).
- **Terms**, all Shōeki's own [C, G15]:
  - 直耕 *chokkō*, "direct cultivation": in Miyake's gloss both the self-moving of 活真 *kasshin* and human farming.
  - 不耕貪食 *fukō tanshoku*, "eating greedily without cultivating".
  - 自然世 *shizen no yo* (the natural world) against 法世 *hōsei* (the world of made laws). 法 here means 〈コシラヘ〉, contrivance (Bitō).
  - 互性 *gosei*, "mutual nature".
  - 二別 *nibetsu*, twofold division.

**On the three questions.** Q1: all received learning, Confucian and Buddhist, is 書学, book-learning made by the sages, and a tool of theft. Q2: to restore direct cultivation and the natural world. Q3: Bitō reads it "from a physician's standpoint", to show "how a person sound in body and mind should live" [C, G15]. Shōeki refuses letters while writing a hundred fascicles; that tension is plain on the text, and no source I opened states it.

**Reception to keep out or hold to a clause.** E. H. Norman's *The Forgotten Thinker* made Shōeki widely known after 1945 as "the only thinker in Japan to criticise the status system from the root" (Bitō) [C, G15]. The Nagata 1930s reading of him as a materialist forerunner, and Kita's of him as an "agrarian socialist", are reception [C as found, G17]. Discard the priority framings.

## 4. Ishida Baigan 石田梅岩 (1685–1744)

**Dates and setting** [C, G19].
- 1685–1744. Born to a farming family in Tanba. He served in Kyoto merchant houses and studied Shinto, Confucian and Buddhist thought on his own, then under Oguri Ryōun.
- In 1729 he opened lectures at his home, "free to attend, no fee" (聴講自由で席料無料).
- *Tohi mondō* 都鄙問答: four volumes, sixteen sections, 1739. The school became Sekimon Shingaku 石門心学.
- Nipponica: one section, 「或学者商人ノ学問ヲ譏ノ段」 ("a scholar mocks the learning of a merchant"), argues that the merchant's way is equal to the others'. Another section is on 「学者行状心得難キ」.

**What learning is and is for** (text from *Yūhōdō bunko* [78], 1914, OCR; Nagata for the volume).
- 「學問の至極といふは、心を盡し性を知り、性を知れば天を知る。天を知れば、天卽孔孟の心なり、孔孟ノ心ヲ知レバ宋儒ノ心モ一ナリ、一ナル故ニ註モ自合フ」, "the utmost of learning is to exhaust the heart and know one's nature; knowing one's nature one knows Heaven; knowing Heaven, Heaven is the heart of Confucius and Mencius; knowing their heart, the heart of the Song scholars is one with it, and being one, the commentaries agree of themselves" (my rendering) [C, G20; Nagata places it in vol. 2, C as quoted, G17]. The opening is Mencius 7A1 (盡其心者知其性也), which Baigan cites as Mencius's.
- 「今言ふ所は性を知るを先とす。…孟子も人を導き給ふは性を知るを先とし」, "what I teach puts knowing one's nature first … Mencius too, in guiding people, put knowing one's nature first" (my rendering) [C, G20].
- Adachi's 1899 summary: Baigan 「心を知るを學問の初めとし」, makes knowing the heart the beginning of learning [C as quoted, G20 Adachi]. The brief's *kokoro o shiru* is attested as Baigan's term in the edition I opened (心を知る) [C, G20]. 心を知るも其如く、闇夜の忽に明け、一天照然として明なるが如し: "knowing the heart is like that, like a dark night suddenly dawning and the whole sky shining clear" (my rendering) [C, G20].
- **Against letter-learning.** 「然るに文字ばかりを知るは、一藝なるゆゑに文字藝者と言ふ」, "but knowing only letters is a single art, so we call such a one a *moji geisha*, a performer of letters"; 「德とは心に得て身に行ふを云ふ」, "virtue means getting it in the heart and doing it with the body" (my renderings) [C, G20]. The questioner then asks for proof that reading books can be split in two, and Baigan answers with *Analects* 5.4 (女器也, "you are a vessel") [C, G20].
- **Word test.** *Shingaku* 心学 is the school's name (石門心学); "learning of the heart/mind" is the usual English. *Shingaku* is also one of the older general names for Confucian learning, as Tucker lists them [C, G12]. Whether *moji geisha* is Baigan's coinage is [U].

**On the three questions.** Q1: learning (*gakumon*) is knowing the heart and one's nature, not knowing letters. Q2: knowing Heaven; virtue "got in the heart and done with the body", in each person's station, the merchant's included. Q3: question and answer, open lectures, Shinto, Confucian and Buddhist sources used freely.

**Grouping.** The fact that decides it: the Nipponica says he took Zhu Xi as his base (朱子学に拠りながら) while drawing on the others [C, G19]. He can sit on the Confucians page as the townsman's line, or on an "outside the schools" page (§9).

## 5. Nishitani Keiji 西谷啓治 (1900–1990)

**Dates** 1900–1990 [C, G23; G27]. *Shūkyō to wa nanika* 宗教とは何か appeared in 1961 [C, G27]. The English title is *Religion and Nothingness*.

**He speaks to the question directly.**
- **"Encounter with Emptiness"** (1984 address, published 1989) [C, G21]:
  - "When I say 'philosophy,' for a person such as myself, born and living in Japan, I first of all mean Western philosophy, since this is the most influential one."
  - "We are faced with the ineluctable necessity of re-thinking once more Oriental thought and philosophy with the help of what we learned from Western philosophy."
  - "Daily life is the basic problem and the last key to all problems. That is the standpoint of Zen and, more generally, the core of the Buddhist standpoint. To think that standpoint by way of philosophy is my basic concern."
  - "I am thinking of making philosophy work as the thinking of basic non-thinking, just like the everyday labor of working the field and pulling the weeds…" He takes "non-thinking" from the monk's answer in zazen ("I am thinking about non-thinking"), a dialogue he says "is often quoted by Dōgen".
  - He also names the method: "to seek the solution through a thoroughgoing intuition (*naikan* 内観)".
  - Whether he wrote the address in English or it was translated is [U].
- **"The Standpoint of Zen"** (禅の立場, 1967; tr. Maraldo 1984, revised by the author with the translator) [C, G22]:
  - "In Zen one is called upon to investigate the most urgent matter of the self, to elucidate the great matter of life and death"; "Zen is the standpoint which exhaustively investigates the self itself". The phrase is *koji kyūmei* 己事究明, from Daitō Kokushi's admonitions (translator's note).
  - On philosophy: for Augustine, as for Socrates, "the love of knowledge which gives rise to philosophy was backed up by a practice of self-investigation which sought a resting place for his own existence"; and in Descartes "the method of philosophy was implicitly a method of self-investigation."
- **On the Kyoto School's ground**, of Nishida and Tanabe: "[Their] philosophies share a distinctive and common basis that sets them apart from traditional Western philosophy: absolute nothingness … the fact that it has also been posited as a foundation for philosophical thought represents a new step virtually without counterpart in the history of Western philosophy" [C as quoted, G23, NKC IX 225–26].
- *Religion and Nothingness*' opening ("What is religion?") is not opened [U]; see Sources to fetch.

**On the three questions.** Q1: philosophy, "first of all Western philosophy", used to rethink Eastern thought. Q2: the self-investigation that seeks "a resting place" for one's existence; daily life as "the last key". Q3: "the thinking of basic non-thinking"; *naikan*.

**Grouping.** Kyoto School page.

## 6. Watsuji Tetsurō 和辻哲郎 (1889–1960)

**Dates** 1889–1960 [C, G24]. *Ningen no gaku to shite no rinrigaku* 人間の学としての倫理学 is dated 1934 by the NDL record [C, G25]. Carter and McCarthy say he "completed" it in 1935 [C, G24]; detail. *Rinrigaku* came out in three volumes, 1937, 1942 and 1949 [C, G24].

**He does say what the discipline is.**
- *Ningen no gaku*, ch. 1 (as quoted in a 1934 Ministry of Education digest): 「第一章 人間の學としての倫理學の意義　倫理學は「倫理とは何であるか」といふ問である」, "Ethics is the question 'what is *rinri*?'" (my rendering) [C as quoted, G25]. The manuscript OCR has 倫理学は自然の学ではなくして人間の学である, ethics is not a study of nature but a study of *ningen*; the OCR is garbled, so this is [U].
- *Rinrigaku* vol. 1 (1937), Introduction §1 opens: 「倫理學を『人間』の學として規定しようとする試みの第一の意義は、倫理を單に個人意識の問題とする近世の誤謬から脫却することである」, "the first significance of the attempt to define ethics as the study of *ningen* is to escape the modern error that makes ethics merely a problem of individual consciousness" (my rendering) [C, G25].
- **The word method.** 「さて倫理といふ言葉は、『倫』『理』の二語から成つてゐる。倫は『なかま』を意味する。『なかま』とは一定の人々の關係體系としての團體であると共に、この團體によつて規定せられた個々の人々である」, "the word *rinri* is made of *rin* and *ri*; *rin* means *nakama*, fellows: the group as a system of relations among certain people, and also the individuals defined by that group" (my rendering) [C, G25]. Carter and McCarthy: *ri* means "reason" or "principle"; *ningen* is *nin* (person) and *gen* (between), and Watsuji traces *gen* to *aida*/*aidagara* [C, G24].
- **A Japanese contemporary's objection to the method.** Tosaka Jun (1900–1945), *Nihon ideorogī ron* (1935; NDL copy 1936 ed.): 「日本語や支那語で「倫理」といふ言葉を造り得ても、その倫理といふ言葉の分析の結果は、倫理といふ事物關係そのものが亦さうあることの證明になるものではない」, "even if Japanese or Chinese could make the word *rinri*, analysing the word does not prove that the thing itself is so" (my rendering) [C, G26; dates C, G27]. He grants the word-analysis only as "an illustration, an intuitive symbol" (一つの引例として、直觀的な象徵として) [C, G26].

**On the three questions.** Q1: ethics is the question "what is *rinri*", asked as the study of *ningen*, not of the isolated individual. Q2: to escape the "modern error" of individual consciousness. Q3: start from the words, *rinri* and *ningen*, as ordinary language has them. Tosaka contests this.

**Grouping.** Watsuji taught at Kyoto until his move to Tokyo in 1934 [C, G24]; whether he counts as a Kyoto School member is [U]. Options: a paragraph on the Kyoto School page, or on an "academy between the wars" stretch of a Meiji-to-1945 page. The Tosaka exchange is the strongest Q3 material and keeps it a dispute between Japanese voices.

## 7. Maruyama Masao 丸山眞男 (1914–1996)

**Dates** 1914–1996 [C, G27]. *Nihon no shisō* 日本の思想 (Iwanami shinsho, 1961) [C, G27, G32]. Word test: his word is *shisō*, thought, not *tetsugaku*. He was a political scientist and historian of political thought [C, G27].

**What I could check (paraphrases with page references; his wording not opened):**
- Karatani (1998), citing *Nihon no shisō*: Maruyama "pointed out the enduring absence of a principle that functions as a coordinate axis for various thoughts. That is to say that there is no orthodoxy that marks and excludes heresy; all imported thoughts are indiscriminately accepted into a space of cohabitation. And, because there is no confrontation between principles, neither is there a development nor an accumulation of thought" [C as paraphrased, G29].
- Najita (2000): Maruyama called Japanese thinking "structureless" and spoke of the habit of mind that "shunned open, public debates and found comfort instead in the solace of isolated 'octopus pots'" [C as paraphrased, G28].
- Hagiwara (2001), citing pages:
  - Maruyama rejected Katō Shūichi's "hybridity" (雑種性) and held that the mark of Japanese culture is 雑居性, cohabitation (pp. 63–64).
  - In the *sasara* (ササラ型) West, a common base of thought joins the specialised organisations; Japan is *takotsubo* (タコツボ型) (p. 148).
  - Maruyama answered that he had not judged Japan's tradition by its gap from an idealised Western modernity (p. 185) [C as paraphrased, G30].
- **The Japanese terms**, attested in the paraphrases: 座標軸 *zahyōjiku*, 雑居 *zakkyo*, タコツボ型 *takotsubo-gata*, ササラ型 *sasara-gata* [C, G29, G30]. The exact sentences are [U].

**Japanese responses, for the dispute.**
- Karatani: Maruyama's "Western thinking … arranged … according to a coordinate axis of principle" holds only "for a certain length of time". Returning to an ancient substratum "tends to structurally merge into the nationalist project" [C, G29].
- Hagiwara: Maruyama never criticised the assumed "purity" of Western culture [C, G30].
- Karatani quotes a Maruyama line on Shinto as a "long cloth tube" that "interwove itself with various religions". His footnote assigns it to another essay [C as quoted, G29]. Which text it is in is [U]; don't use it until checked.

**On the three questions.** This answers a different question: why thought in Japan does not accumulate. Q1: *shisō* with no coordinate axis; Q3 (as diagnosis): ideas "cohabit" without confrontation, in closed "octopus pots". Maruyama's own positive programme belongs to political thought, not to this subject [U].

**Grouping.** After 1945, as a Japanese diagnosis of how thought is held, with Karatani's reply. Hold quotation until the Iwanami text is opened.

## 8. Other post-1945 voices

**Fujita Masakatsu 藤田正勝** (b. 1949 [U]). From *Nihon tetsugaku nyūmon* (Kōdansha gendai shinsho, 2024), as excerpted by the publisher [C, G31]:
- A student asked whether, since philosophy aims at universal truth, it makes sense to add "Japanese" to it. Fujita answers:
  - 「確かに哲学は、その成立以来、普遍的な原理の探究をめざしてきた。しかし普遍的な原理の探究であることは、ただちに使用される言語の制約から自由であるということを意味しない」, "philosophy has indeed aimed from its beginnings at inquiring into universal principles; but being an inquiry into universal principles does not at once mean being free of the limits of the language used" (my rendering).
  - 「ギリシアの哲学と、それを受け継ぐヨーロッパの哲学こそが唯一の哲学であるという考え方もあるが、私はギリシアの哲学もフランスの哲学もドイツの哲学も、それぞれの言語を用いてそれぞれの文化・伝承の枠のなかでなされる営みであり、その制約から自由ではないと考えている」, "some hold that Greek philosophy and the European philosophy that inherits it are the only philosophy; I think Greek, French and German philosophy alike are activities carried on in their own languages within their own cultures and traditions, and are not free of those limits" (my rendering).
  - 「重要なのは、異なった見方を否定したり、排除したりすることではなく、それと対話することである。日本の哲学はその対話に大きな寄与をすることができる」, "what matters is not to deny or exclude a different view but to enter into dialogue with it; Japanese philosophy can contribute much to that dialogue" (my rendering).
- He also discusses why Japan uses *shisō* as distinct from *tetsugaku* [C, G31, chapter outline only]; the content is unopened.
- Q1: an inquiry into universal principles that cannot leave its language. Q2 and Q3: dialogue between traditions.

**Tosaka Jun** (§6) is a second post-Meiji Japanese voice. His critique is prewar (1935).

**Not reached:** Nakamura Hajime (*Ways of Thinking of Eastern Peoples*), Ōmori Shōzō and Ueda Shizuteru. I opened nothing by them; no claims.

## 9. Placement (options, not decisions)

**Lines and pages.**

1. **Buddhism in Japan**: Kūkai, Hōnen and Shinran, Dōgen. The three give distinct answers to "what is study for": ranking teachings as states of mind; study as study of the self; study set aside for entrusting (Hōnen's 一文不知, *Tannishō* 12). Order: chronological (Kūkai, Hōnen, Shinran, Dōgen), or by answer.
2. **The Confucians (Edo)**: as now. Baigan can go here as the townsman's Shingaku, on the Nipponica fact that he built on Zhu Xi.
3. **Outside the schools** (working title; neutral alternatives: "The independents", "Townsmen and physicians"). Tominaga (Osaka merchant academy), Baigan (Kyoto merchant lecturer), Baien (country physician), Shōeki (town physician).
   - What they share, on the texts: each sets aside the received teachings' authority. Tominaga sorts all three by *kajō*; Baien takes heaven and earth, "not people", as teacher; Shōeki rejects letters as tools of theft; Baigan rejects the *moji geisha*.
   - Risk: a page defined by being outside may read as a residue. Alternative: put Tominaga on the landing page (Japan sorting its own teachings), Baigan with the Confucians, and Baien and Shōeki as a pair on one page.
4. **Native Studies**: unchanged. Tominaga–Norinaga–Atsutane is a cross-link, not a move.
5. **Meiji**: unchanged.
6. **The Kyoto School**: add Nishitani (§5); cross-link Tanabe's *tariki* to Shinran. Watsuji here or on a separate prewar stretch, with Tosaka.
7. **After 1945**: Maruyama with Karatani's reply (hold quotes), and Fujita. If the page keeps only Japanese voices, the Kasulis and Maraldo framings now on the Japan page come off by the brief; that is a call for the page.

**Landing page.** Tominaga is the strongest candidate for "how Japan sorted its own teachings", set beside Kūkai's ten stages. That is a pair of methods, not a ranking of them.

**Backing.** No person pages exist for any of these names, and none is proposed here. All stay external links (Wikipedia) or unlinked.

## 12. Discard list (do not place)

- Every priority claim: Tominaga as "forerunner" of the thesis that the Mahāyāna is not the Buddha's word, or as anticipating cultural anthropology (Britannica, Mizuta); Baien's *jōri* as dialectics before Hegel (Saegusa); Shōeki as Japan's first materialist or socialist (Nagata, Kita, Norman's reception); Maruyama's Sorai as the germ of modernity.
- Barth's Shin–Reformation parallel (Hirota reports it).
- Western framings of whether Japan has philosophy: Kasulis's "*Wissenschaft* or Way", Maraldo's four senses. By the brief these are not for the line pages.
- Tominaga's *Setsuhei* expulsion beyond "said to"; the Ōbaku *Tripiṭaka* collation story (Kanno: the dates don't fit; the Nipponica states it as fact).
- Shōeki's *sonnō* reading and his transitional society (Miyake); the 1764 stone-stele incident.
- Baigan's ethics of merchant profit and thrift, beyond one clause on learning for commoners.
- Watsuji's *fūdo*, *aidagara* ethics and *Sonnō shisō* (1943). These are ethics and politics, not the discipline question. The SEP's "selfless morality" summary goes too.
- Karatani's Cornford and Oedipal arguments; Najita's *kosō* discussion.
- The Jinen hōni age discrepancy beyond "late in life"; the 1744/1745 date for *Shutsujō kōgo* beyond a clause.
