# Metaphilosophy, the Western line: research note

Purpose: to gather the Western thinkers' own words for the rework of the Western line. That means what each said philosophy is, what its point is and how it is done, and the West's own words for the work and its own sortings of knowledge. Scholars are used for facts only. Where a current page lets a scholar frame a period, the note gives the thinkers' own lines that could replace the framing. Out of scope: the West reading the other traditions, and the Greek and Islamic material. Headlines only. This is a working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a source I opened, named by key (Sources at the end). **[C as quoted]** = the wording as a named secondary source quotes it; the primary text was not opened. **[U]** = unverified (memory, a search summary, or a source I could not open). "My rendering" marks my own English. OCR spellings are normalised silently (long s, *ſ*, ligatures).

## 1. Landing page: the words and the West's own sortings

### The words

- **Latin *philosophia*.** Hugh of St Victor gives the etymology and two definitions. "Philosophia est amor sapientiae … philos enim Graece, amor dicitur Latine, sophia, sapientia" (*Didascalicon* II.1) [C, W11]. The wider one: "Philosophia est disciplina omnium rerum humanarum atque divinarum rationes plene investigans" (I.4), my rendering "the discipline that fully investigates the reasons of all things human and divine". In II.1 he gives it with *probabiliter*, "probably", in place of *plene* [C, W11]. The first sentence of the book: "Omnium expetendorum prima est sapientia", of all things to be sought, wisdom is the first (I.1) [C, W11].
- **"Natural philosophy".** The term is in the title *Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica* (1687) [C, W61 catalogue]. In the General Scholium (Motte, 1729), hypotheses "have no place in experimental philosophy. In this philosophy particular propositions are inferr'd from the phænomena, and afterwards render'd general by induction"; to discourse of God "from the appearances of things, does certainly belong to Natural Philosophy" [C, W15]. Bacon divides philosophy into "divine philosophy, natural philosophy, and human philosophy or humanity" (*Advancement*, Bk II) [C, W7]. Comte, in his 1830 foreword, notes the overlap of his term with "ce que les savans anglais entendent, depuis Newton surtout, par *philosophie naturelle*", what English scholars have meant by natural philosophy since Newton especially. He chose neither that name nor "*philosophie des sciences*", because neither yet extends to all orders of phenomena, the social ones included [C, W38]. Russell (1912) uses the Principia's title as his example of a subject that left philosophy [C, W45].
- ***Weltweisheit*.** *Deutsches Wörterbuch* (Grimm), "weltweisheit": attested from Middle High German (*werltwîsheit*). Sense 1 is "irdische weisheit, erkenntnis aus menschlichem bemühen und vermögen im ausdrücklichen gegensatz zur weisheit gottes", earthly wisdom from human effort and capacity, in express contrast to God's wisdom [C, W14]. Then "philosophy", popularised by Thomasius and Christian Wolff. Wolff's own definition as the entry cites it: "die weltweiszheit ist eine wissenschafft aller möglichen dinge, wie und warumb sie möglich sind" (German Logic, cited from the 1719 printing) [C as quoted, W14]. It was "häufig der theologie gegenübergestellt", often set against theology, and used in titles: "magister, doktor, lehrer, professor der weltweisheit" [C, W14]. **Word test:** the word itself carries the contrast "of the world" as against divine wisdom.
- **Gottsched, *Erste Gründe der gesammten Weltweisheit*** (first ed. 1733–34 [C that the entry gives these dates, W14]; 1736 printing opened). Einleitung §1: "Die Weisheit überhaupt ist eine Wissenschaft der Glückseligkeit; wie Leibnitz dieselbe zuerst beschrieben hat" [C, W13]. §3: "Die Weltweisheit nenne ich die Wissenschaft von der Glückseligkeit des Menschen; in so weit wir sie, nach dem Maaße unsrer Unvollkommenheit, erlangen und ausüben können. Sie ist also nichts anders, als eine unvollkommene Weisheit". Pythagoras wished to be called "ein Philosoph, das ist, ein Liebhaber der Weisheit, oder nach unsrer Art, ein Weltweiser" [C, W13]. §4: "Die Wissenschaft ist ein gründliches Erkenntniß eines Dinges"; the Weltweise is not content with probability but "strebet allezeit nach dem größten Grade der Gewißheit" [C, W13]. My rendering of §3: "I call world-wisdom the science of human happiness, so far as we can attain and practise it within the measure of our imperfection. It is therefore nothing but an imperfect wisdom." **Word test:** this is not Wolff's "science of the possibles". Gottsched joins Wolff's *Wissenschaft* to a happiness definition he credits to Leibniz.
- ***Wissenschaft*.** Fichte renames the subject in a title: *Ueber den Begriff der Wissenschaftslehre oder der sogenannten Philosophie* (1794), on the concept of the doctrine of science, "or so-called philosophy" [C, W20 catalogue]. Hegel, *Phänomenologie* (1807), Vorrede: "Daran mitzuarbeiten, daß die Philosophie der Form der Wissenschaft näher komme, — dem Ziele, ihren Nahmen der Liebe zum Wissen ablegen zu können und wirkliches Wissen zu seyn, — ist es, was ich mir vorgesetzt" [C, W16]. Kant before both: whether metaphysics goes "den sicheren Gang einer Wissenschaft", the sure path of a science (KrV B VII, AA III 7) [C, W2], and the title *Prolegomena zu einer jeden künftigen Metaphysik, die als Wissenschaft wird auftreten können* (AA IV 253) [C, W5]. **Word test:** *Wissenschaft* is any systematic, grounded body of knowledge (Gottsched §4 above), not "science" in the later English natural-science sense.

### The West's own sortings

- **The arts and the arts faculty.** Hugh: "Philosophia dividitur in theoricam, practicam, mechanicam et logicam. hae quattuor omnem continent scientiam" (II.1), philosophy divides into the theoretical, the practical, the mechanical and the logical, and these four contain all knowledge [C, W11]. The theoretical divides into theology, physics and mathematics, and mathematics into the four arts of the quadrivium (III.1) [C, W11]. On the seven liberal arts: "hinc trivium et quadrivium nomen accepit, eo quod his, quasi quibusdam viis, vivax animus ad secreta sophiae introeat. nemo tunc temporis nomine magistri dignus videbatur, qui non harum septem scientiam profiteri posset" (III.3), they are called trivium and quadrivium because by these, as by roads, the lively mind enters the secrets of wisdom; no one in those times was thought worthy of the name of master who could not profess these seven [C, W11; my rendering]. The fact for the university: "In 1255, the statutes of the Parisian arts faculty declared all known works of Aristotle mandatory reading for the students" (Hasse) [C, W27]. Hugh's date (1120s) [U].
- **Bonaventure, *De reductione artium ad theologiam*.** Four lights from the Father of lights (James 1:17). The outer light is the mechanical arts, the lower is sense knowledge, the inner is "lumen cognitionis philosophicae", and the higher is grace and Scripture (§1) [C, W12]. The inner light "interiores causas et latentes inquirit", inquires into inner and hidden causes. It is threefold, rational, natural and moral (§4) [C, W12]. The close: "Patet etiam, quomodo omnes cognitiones famulantur theologiae", it is clear how all kinds of knowledge serve theology. "Et hic est fructus omnium scientiarum, ut in omnibus aedificetur fides, 'honorificetur Deus', componantur mores …", the fruit of all the sciences: that faith be built up, God honoured, conduct ordered (§26) [C, W12; my renderings]. Edition: unattributed Latin on documentacatholicaomnia (presumably Quaracchi V) [U]. Authorship has been questioned: Dieter Hattrup proposed Roger Bacon [U, search summary]. That is a detail; attribute "traditionally Bonaventure's" if it goes on the page. The *Bacon* collision applies (Roger, not Francis).
- **Descartes's tree** (letter-preface to the French *Principes*, 1647). "Ainsi toute la Philosophie est comme un arbre, dont les racines sont la Metaphysique, le tronc est la Physique, & les branches qui sortent de ce tronc sont toutes les autres sciences, qui se reduisent à trois principales, à sçavoir la Medecine, la Mechanique & la Morale". The highest morals, presupposing all the other sciences, are "le dernier degré de la sagesse" [C, W6 (1706 printing; the 1892 Charpentier text agrees)]. The fruit is gathered "seulement des extrémités de leurs branches" [C, W6]. Veitch: "the word PHILOSOPHY signifies the study of wisdom" [C, W6c].
- **Bacon's three faculties.** "The parts of human learning have reference to the three parts of man's understanding, which is the seat of learning: history to his memory, poesy to his imagination, and philosophy to his reason" (*Advancement*, Bk II, i.1, 1605) [C, W7]. "Knowledges are as pyramids, whereof history is the basis" [C, W7].
- **The *Encyclopédie*'s map.** D'Alembert: "la mémoire, la raison proprement dite, & l'imagination, sont les trois manieres différentes dont notre ame opere". The three branches: "l'Histoire, qui se rapporte à la mémoire ; la Philosophie, qui est le fruit de la raison ; & les Beaux-arts, que l'imagination fait naître" [C, W9]. "Nous devons principalement au Chancelier Bacon l'Arbre encyclopédique" [C, W9]. The tree is "une espece de Mappemonde" showing the main countries of knowledge and their mutual dependence [C, W9]. The article PHILOSOPHIE: "toutes les sciences, tous les arts ont-ils leur philosophie" [C, W10].
- **Kant, *Der Streit der Fakultäten*** (1798 [U]). The lower faculty is the class of the university that deals only with teachings "welche nicht auf den Befehl eines Oberen zur Richtschnur angenommen werden" (AA VII 27) [C, W1]. Philosophy must stand "nur unter der Gesetzgebung der Vernunft, nicht der der Regierung" [C, W1]. Why "lower"? Because "der, welcher befehlen kann, ob er gleich ein demüthiger Diener eines andern ist, sich doch vornehmer dünkt als ein anderer, der zwar frei ist, aber niemandem zu befehlen hat" (VII 20) [C, W1]. The handmaid image, answered: "Auch kann man allenfalls der theologischen Facultät den stolzen Anspruch, daß die philosophische ihre Magd sei, einräumen (wobei doch noch immer die Frage bleibt: ob diese ihrer gnädigen Frau die Fackel vorträgt oder die Schleppe nachträgt)" (VII 28) [C, W1]. My rendering: one may grant the theology faculty its proud claim that philosophy is its maid, though the question remains whether she carries the torch before her lady or the train behind. Its two departments are historical knowledge (history, geography, languages, the humanities, empirical natural knowledge) and pure rational knowledge (pure mathematics, pure philosophy). It takes in "alle Theile des menschlichen Wissens", but makes the upper faculties' teachings "zum Gegenstande ihrer Prüfung und Kritik" (VII 28) [C, W1]. **This is the strongest own-voice replacement for "servant" as a label** (section 5).
- **The histories of philosophy.** Brucker's title, *Historia critica philosophiae a mundi incunabulis ad nostram usque aetatem deducta* (1742–44) [C, W61 catalogue]. Gottsched's appended "kurze philosophische Historie" opens with the *Weltweisheit* of the first men before the Flood, then the Chinese, Hebrews, Indians, Persians, Arabs, Phoenicians and Ethiopians [C, W13 1756, chapter headings]. That belongs to the West reading the other traditions and is noted here only as a fact. Tennemann (*Manual*, tr. Johnson, Oxford 1832), §13: "the history of philosophy is the science which details the efforts of the human understanding to realise the idea of philosophy, by exhibiting them in their mutual dependency: it is a systematic arrangement of facts illustrating the continual development of philosophy, as a science" [C, W19]. Hegel: "Die Geschichte der Philosophie stellt uns die Galerie der edlen Geister dar, welche durch die Kühnheit ihrer Vernunft in die Natur der Dinge, des Menschen und in die Natur Gottes gedrungen [sind]" (Vorlesungen, Einleitung) [C, W18]. His "one philosophy at different stages" (Enzyklopädie §13) [U this round].
- **Kant's own sorting of the past** (KrV, "Die Geschichte der reinen Vernunft", AA III 550–552). It sorts by object (sensual philosophers, Epicurus; intellectual philosophers, Plato), by origin ("Aristoteles kann als das Haupt der Empiristen, Plato aber der Noologisten angesehen werden. Locke … und Leibniz … haben es gleichwohl in diesem Streite noch zu keiner Entscheidung bringen können") and by method (naturalistic against scientific; the scientific either dogmatic, "den berühmten Wolff", or sceptical, "David Hume"). It closes: "Der kritische Weg ist allein noch offen" [C, W2]. Useful on the landing page and for the "Reason and experience" pair (section 4).

## 2. The line, page by page

### (a) The schools and the universities

- **Bridge.** Augustine, *De vera religione* 5.8, "non aliam esse philosophiam, id est sapientiae studium, et aliam religionem" [U this round; the current page has it]. Boethius closes his second theological tractate to John the Deacon: "fidem si poterit rationemque coniunge"; Stewart and Rand: "if possible, reconcile faith and reason" [C, W24]. The *Consolation*'s physician lines are on the current page [not reopened].
- **Eriugena**: "veram esse philosophiam veram religionem conversimque" [U this round; on the current page].
- **Anselm**, *Proslogion*, proem: the work's first title was "Fides quaerens intellectum", faith seeking understanding [C, W23]. Ch. 1: "Neque enim quaero intelligere ut credam, sed credo ut intelligam. Nam et hoc credo: quia 'nisi credidero, non intelligam'" (Is 7:9) [C, W23].
- **Abelard**, *Sic et Non* prologue (Lewis): "through doubting we come to questioning and through questions we perceive the truth" [U this round; on the page].
- **Aquinas, *Summa* I q.1.** a.1: "It was necessary for man's salvation that there should be a knowledge revealed by God besides philosophical science built up by human reason". The Latin objection runs "praeter philosophicas disciplinas" [C, W21]. a.1 ad 2: "theologia quae ad sacram doctrinam pertinet, differt secundum genus ab illa theologia quae pars philosophiae ponitur", "differs in kind" [C, W21]. a.5: sacred doctrine "non accipit ab aliis scientiis tanquam a superioribus, sed utitur eis tanquam inferioribus et ancillis"; "Other sciences are called the handmaidens of this one" [C, W21]. a.8: "Cum enim gratia non tollat naturam, sed perficiat, oportet quod naturalis ratio subserviat fidei"; "natural reason should minister to faith" [C, W21].
- **The Latin reception of the Arabic texts (facts).** Michael Scot and William of Luna were the most productive Averroes translators. "It was only about thirty years after Averroes' death in 1198 that Latin Averroes translations became available in the newly developing universities". The 1255 statutes then "contributed to the rise of Averroes' commentaries as the principal secondary literature of Latin university culture" [C, W27]. "Averroes as the commentator had become an exceptional authority of university education" [C, W27]. Dante: "Averoìs, che 'l gran comento feo" (*Inf.* IV.144) [C, W29]. Aquinas, *De unitate intellectus*, writes "Commentator" for Averroes and calls him "non tam … Peripateticus, quam philosophiae Peripateticae depravator", not so much a Peripatetic as a corrupter of Peripatetic philosophy (cap. 2) [C, W22].
- **"Latin Averroism" as a term.** "The term averroista came into use in the later thirteenth century, but on rare occasions. The first appearance … is in Thomas Aquinas' treatise *On the Unicity of the Intellect*". The added title phrase "contra averroistas appears only in the later manuscript tradition". "Modern scholarship is still divided about the significance of the current of Averroism in the thirteenth century" [C, W27]. Renan, *Averroès et l'averroïsme* (1852) [C, W61 catalogue]; Mandonnet's *Siger de Brabant et l'averroïsme latin* (1899) as the source of "averroïsme latin" [U]. On Boethius of Dacia: "The epithet 'heterodox' or 'radical Aristotelian' comes with the tacitly understood counterpart 'orthodox and moderate like Aquinas', and thus only makes sense from a certain religious point of view" (Ebbesen) [C, W28]. **Headline:** the label is a later reader's, and disputed. On the page, use the term only as "what modern scholars call 'Latin Averroism'".
- **Aquinas against the arts master's "two truths".** He quotes his opponent: "per rationem concludo de necessitate, quod intellectus est unus numero; firmiter tamen teneo oppositum per fidem", and answers that faith would then be "de falso impossibili" (cap. 5) [C, W22]. His own method: "non per documenta fidei, sed per ipsorum philosophorum rationes et dicta" (end) [C, W22].
- **The arts masters.** Boethius of Dacia, *De summo bono*: "Haec est vita philosophi, quam quicumque non habuerit non habet rectam vitam. Philosophum autem voco omnem hominem viventem secundum rectum ordinem naturae"; "Ideo philosophus ducit vitam valde voluptuosam"; few men "studio sapientiae vacant" [C, W25]. *De aeternitate mundi*: "fides non est scientia" [C as quoted, W28]. SEP (fact): "a leading philosopher at the faculty of arts in Paris about 1270–1275"; English translation by Wippel (1987) [C, W28].
- **1277.** Chartularium no. 473, 7 March 1277. Art. 40 "Quod non est excellentior status, quam vacare philosophie"; art. 152 "Quod sermones theologi fundati sunt in fabulis"; art. 153 "Quod nichil plus scitur propter scire theologiam"; art. 154 "Quod sapientes mundi sunt philosophi tantum" [C, W26]. Prologue: masters of arts "proprie facultatis limites excedentes" say things "vera secundum philosophiam, sed non secundum fidem catholicam, quasi sint due contrarie veritates" [C, W26]. **Word test:** the Chartularium heading, "Opiniones … Sigeri de Brabantia, Boetii de Dacia aliorumque", is Denifle's editorial heading, not the decree's [C, W26]. The decree names no one.

### (b) The humanists

- **Petrarch**, *On His Own Ignorance* (Nachod): "It is better to will the good than to know the truth"; Aristotle's moral books left him "more learned … but not better"; "the true moral philosophers and useful teachers of the virtues are those whose first and last intention is to make hearer and reader good" [C, W30]. Latin "Satius est autem bonum velle quam verum nosse" [C as quoted, W33 note]. The "professors sitting in a chair" line is Hadot's paraphrase, citing *De vita solitaria* II.7 (Martelotti, *Prose*, pp. 524–526) [C that Hadot cites it, W33]. **Replace** with Petrarch's own words, or open the *De vita solitaria* Latin.
- **Erasmus**, *Paraclesis* (1516), in the anonymous English of 1529: "this kind of philosophy doth rather consist in the affects of the mind, than in subtle reasons. It is a life rather than a disputation. It is an inspiration rather than a science. And rather a new transformation, than a reasoning" [C, W31]. Latin title: *Paraclesis, id est adhortatio ad sanctissimum ac saluberrimum Christianae philosophiae studium* [U, catalogue records via search]. Latin "philosophia Christi" and "vita magis quam disputatio" (Holborn ed.) [U].
- **Montaigne** (1595 text, Michaud ed.). I.19 is titled "Que philosopher, c'est apprendre à mourir" and opens "Cicero dit que Philosopher ce n'est autre chose que s'aprester à la mort". Reason's whole work should "tendre en somme à nous faire bien viure" [C, W32]. I.25: "la Philosophie est celle qui nous instruict à viure" and the "ergotismes" that made philosophy "vn nom vain et fantastique" [C, W32]. II.6: "Mon mestier et mon art, c'est viure" [C, W32]. That is Montaigne's own text for the line the medieval page now quotes through Hadot. Villey numbers the chapters I.20 and I.26 [U].

### (c) The new philosophy

- **Descartes**: the tree and wisdom (section 1) [C, W6]. *Discourse* rules [on the current page; not reopened].
- **Bacon**: "the real and legitimate goal of the sciences is the endowment of human life with new inventions and riches" (NO I.81) [C, W8]. NO I.95: "Those who have treated of the sciences have been either empirics or dogmatical. The former like ants only heap up and use their store, the latter like spiders spin out their own webs. The bee, a mean between both … The true labor of philosophy resembles hers" [C, W8]. *Philosophia prima* as "the main and common way, before we come where the ways part" (*Advancement*) [C, W7].
- **Locke**: to "examine our own abilities"; the master-builders ("a Boyle or a Sydenham … Huygenius"); the under-labourer "clearing the ground a little, and removing some of the rubbish that lies in the way to knowledge"; the "historical, plain method" [C, W36].
- **Spinoza**, *Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect* (Elwes). §1: "After experience had taught me that all the usual surroundings of social life are vain and futile … I finally resolved to inquire whether there might be some real good" [C, W34]. §13: the chief good is "the knowledge of the union existing [between] the mind and the whole of nature" (Gutenberg misprints "being") [C, W34]. §14: "it is part of my happiness to lend a helping hand, that many others may understand even as I do". §§15–16: moral philosophy, education, medicine and mechanics, but first "a means must be devised for improving the understanding"; "I wish to direct all science to one end and aim" [C, W34]. The Latin was not opened [U].
- **Leibniz**: "quando orientur controversiae, non magis disputatione opus erit inter duos philosophos, quam inter duos Computistas. Sufficiet enim calamos in manus sumere sedereque ad abacos, et sibi mutuo (accito si placet amico) dicere: calculemus" (Gerhardt VII 200) [C, W35]. The undated piece sits among the writings on the general characteristic; the date (c. 1685?) [U].
- **Newton**: "experimental philosophy" (section 1) [C, W15].

### (d) Enlightenment to Kant

- **Wolff**: Latin §29 "Philosophia est scientia possibilium, quatenus esse possunt" and §6 [U this round; on the current page]. The German definition is in section 1 [C as quoted, W14].
- ***Encyclopédie*, "Philosophe"** (t. 12, pp. 509–511; Wikisource lists "Dumarsais, Venel") [C, W10]. "La raison est à l'égard du philosophe, ce que la grace est à l'égard du chretien"; "Notre philosophe ne se croit pas en exil dans ce monde"; "La société civile est, pour ainsi dire, une divinité pour lui sur la terre"; "Le philosophe est donc un honnête homme qui agit en tout par raison" [C, W10]. "PHILOSOPHIE" adopts Wolff: "la science des possibles en tant que possibles" [C, W10].
- **Hume**: "Be a philosopher; but, amidst all your philosophy, be still a man" (E 1.6) and "mental geography" [C, W37].
- **Kant, school and world concepts** (KrV A838–839/B866–867 = AA III 542–543 [the A/B correspondence U, standard]). "Bis dahin ist aber der Begriff von Philosophie nur ein Schulbegriff … Es giebt aber noch einen Weltbegriff (conceptus cosmicus) … In dieser Absicht ist Philosophie die Wissenschaft von der Beziehung aller Erkenntniß auf die wesentlichen Zwecke der menschlichen Vernunft (teleologia rationis humanae), und der Philosoph ist nicht ein Vernunftkünstler, sondern der Gesetzgeber der menschlichen Vernunft" [C, W2]. Kant's note: Weltbegriff "heißt hier derjenige, der das betrifft, was jedermann nothwendig interessirt" [C, W2]. "Man kann nur philosophiren lernen" (III 542) [C, W2]. Its two objects are "Natur und Freiheit" (III 543) [C, W2]. *Logik* (Jäsche), AA IX 24–25: in the scholastic sense philosophy aims at skill, in the world sense at usefulness, "eine Lehre der Weisheit"; then the four questions, ending "Was ist der Mensch?", and "Im Grunde könnte man aber alles dieses zur Anthropologie rechnen" [C, W3]. Philosophy "läßt sich aber nur durch Übung und selbsteigenen Gebrauch der Vernunft lernen" (IX 25) [C, W3]. **Word test:** "cosmical" (Meiklejohn) renders *Weltbegriff*. The *Logik* has "in dieser weltbürgerlichen Bedeutung", whence "cosmopolitan" [C, W3].
- **"What is Enlightenment?"** (AA VIII 35): "Aufklärung ist der Ausgang des Menschen aus seiner selbst verschuldeten Unmündigkeit … sapere aude! habe Muth dich deines eigenen Verstandes zu bedienen!" [C, W4].

### (e) The nineteenth century

- **Hegel**: "Das was ist zu begreifen, ist die Aufgabe der Philosophie, denn das was ist, ist die Vernunft … so ist auch die Philosophie ihre Zeit in Gedanken erfaßt". "Um noch über das Belehren, wie die Welt sein soll, ein Wort zu sagen, so kommt dazu ohnehin die Philosophie immer zu spät". Reason as "die Rose im Kreuze der Gegenwart" is "die Versöhnung mit der Wirklichkeit, welche die Philosophie … gewährt". "die Eule der Minerva beginnt erst mit der einbrechenden Dämmerung ihren Flug" (*Grundlinien*, Vorrede) [C, W17]. Love of knowing becoming actual knowing (1807) is in section 1 [C, W16].
- **Comte**: the word, "j'emploie le mot *philosophie* dans l'acception que lui donnaient les anciens, et particulièrement Aristote, comme désignant le système général des conceptions humaines"; "positive" names "cette manière spéciale de philosopher" that takes theories as having for their object "la coordination des faits observés" (Avertissement, 18 December 1829) [C, W38]. The three states and the "trois méthodes de philosopher" (Leçon 1) [C, W38]. The new worker: "faire de l'étude des généralités scientifiques une grande spécialité de plus. Qu'une classe nouvelle de savans … sans se livrer à la culture spéciale d'aucune branche particulière de la philosophie naturelle" [C, W38]. Comte's own "philosophie naturelle" here means the sciences.
- **Whewell.** In 1834, in the anonymous review now attributed to him (attribution [U]), science "loses all traces of unity". The British Association members had "no general term"; "*Philosophers* was felt to be too wide and too lofty a term, and was very properly forbidden them by Mr. Coleridge"; "some ingenious gentleman proposed that, by analogy with *artist*, they might form *scientist*" [C, W39]. The meetings were at "York, Oxford, and Cambridge, in the last three summers", so the proposal was made by 1833 [C, W39]. In 1840: "We need very much a name to describe a cultivator of science in general. I should incline to call him a Scientist" (*Philosophy of the Inductive Sciences* I, Aphorisms on the language of science) [C, W40].
- **Windelband, "Was ist Philosophie?"** (*Präludien* 1884): "die kritische Wissenschaft von den allgemeingiltigen Werthen. Die Wissenschaft von den allgemeingiltigen Werthen: das bezeichnet die Gegenstände; die kritische Wissenschaft: das bezeichnet die Methode der Philosophie" [C, W42].
- **Windelband, "Geschichte und Naturwissenschaft"** (Strassburg rectorial address, 1 May 1894; 3rd unaltered ed. 1904 opened). **Answer to the brief's question: no, he does not frame the period as nature against history.** He rejects the material division: the current split of Naturwissenschaften and Geisteswissenschaften "ich halte sie in dieser Form nicht für glücklich". "Natur und Geist" is "ein sachlicher Gegensatz" from late antiquity and modern metaphysics "von Descartes und Spinoza bis zu Schelling und Hegel" [C, W41]. He puts a methodological one in its place. The sciences of the real are "Gesetzeswissenschaften" or "Ereigniswissenschaften; jene lehren, was immer ist, diese, was einmal war"; scientific thinking is "in dem einen Falle nomothetisch, in dem andern idiographisch". "In diesem methodischen Sinne" psychology belongs wholly to the natural sciences [C, W41]. On philosophy itself (opening): "für die Philosophie wesentlich, dass ihr eigentliches Untersuchungsobjekt eben die Prinzipien selbst sind … Für die Philosophie gibt es streng genommen überhaupt keine Spezialuntersuchung" [C, W41]. That is a strong own-voice line for the nineteenth century and for the specialisms page. Rickert's *Kulturwissenschaft und Naturwissenschaft* (1899) continues the line [title C, W61; content U].
- **Dilthey**, *Einleitung in die Geisteswissenschaften* I (1883). On the name: the knowledge of reality is divided in two, one half called natural science, and for the other "ist, merkwürdig genug, eine allgemein anerkannte Bezeichnung nicht vorhanden. Ich schließe mich an den Sprachgebrauch derjenigen Denker an, welche diese andere Hälfte des globus intellectualis als Geisteswissenschaften bezeichnen". The usage became familiar "nicht am wenigsten durch die weite Verbreitung der Logik J. St. Mill's"; it is "die mindest unangemessene" of the names on offer [C, W43]. Preface: the answers of "Comte's und der Positivisten, St. Mill's und der Empiristen" seemed to him "die geschichtliche Wirklichkeit zu verstümmeln, um sie den Begriffen und Methoden der Naturwissenschaften anzupassen" [C, W43]. "Die Natur erklären wir, das Seelenleben verstehen wir" (*Ideen*, 1894) [U]. The worldview types (1911) [U].
- **Fichte** and the *Wissenschaftslehre* (section 1) [C, W20].

### (f) The twentieth century, split three ways

**Analytic.**
- **Russell.** The chapter "Logic as the Essence of Philosophy" (*Our Knowledge*, 1914): "every philosophical problem, when it is subjected to the necessary analysis and purification, is found either to be not really philosophical at all, or else to be, in the sense in which we are using the word, logical" [C, W46]. 1912: "Philosophy, like all other studies, aims primarily at knowledge. The knowledge it aims at is the kind of knowledge which gives unity and system to the body of the sciences"; the residue; "The value of philosophy is, in fact, to be sought largely in its very uncertainty". It is to be studied "for the sake of the questions themselves", and through it the mind "becomes capable of that union with the universe which constitutes its highest good" [C, W45].
- **Wittgenstein**, *Tractatus* (German, Ogden, Pears–McGuinness). 4.111: "Die Philosophie ist keine der Naturwissenschaften". 4.112: "Der Zweck der Philosophie ist die logische Klärung der Gedanken. Die Philosophie ist keine Lehre, sondern eine Tätigkeit"; Ogden "not a theory but an activity", Pears–McGuinness "not a body of doctrine". 4.1121: "Erkenntnistheorie ist die Philosophie der Psychologie". 4.113: "Die Philosophie begrenzt das bestreitbare Gebiet der Naturwissenschaft" [C, W44]. **Word test:** *Lehre* is "theory" (Ogden) or "body of doctrine" (Pears–McGuinness). The page should name its translator.
- **Carnap**: "Philosophy is to be replaced by the logic of science—that is to say, by the logical analysis of the concepts and sentences of the sciences, for the logic of science is nothing other than the logical syntax of the language of science" (*Logical Syntax*, 1937, Foreword). §72 is titled "Philosophy Replaced by the Logic of Science" [C, W49].
- **Quine**: "it is only within science itself …" ("Things and Their Place in Theories", 1981) [U]. "Epistemology Naturalized" (1969) [U this round].

**Pragmatism.**
- **James**, *Pragmatism* (1907). "The history of philosophy is to a great extent that of a certain clash of human temperaments" (I); "The whole function of philosophy ought to be to find out what definite difference it will make to you and me … if this world-formula or that world-formula be the true one"; "The pragmatic method is primarily a method of settling metaphysical disputes that otherwise might be interminable" (II) [C, W47].
- **Dewey**, *Reconstruction* (1920), ch. I. Philosophy "has sprung from a clash of social ends"; "the task of future philosophy is to clarify men's ideas as to the social and moral strifes of their own day. Its aim is to become so far as is humanly possible an organ for dealing with these conflicts". "Philosophy which surrenders its somewhat barren monopoly of dealings with Ultimate and Absolute Reality will find a compensation in enlightening the moral forces which move mankind" [C, W48].
- **Rorty**: the lines on the current page (1979) [U this round; PMN not opened]. Rorty against "experience" in Dewey ("Dewey between Hegel and Darwin", *Truth and Progress*, 1998) [U, search summary of Margolis 2014].

**Continental.**
- **Husserl**, "Philosophy as Rigorous Science" (1911; Lauer 1965). "From its earliest beginnings philosophy has claimed to be rigorous science … and renders possible from an ethico-religious point of view a life regulated by pure rational norms". "I do not say that philosophy is an imperfect science; I say simply that it is not yet a science at all, that as science it has not yet begun". "Weltanschauung philosophy and scientific philosophy are sharply distinguished as two ideas". "The impulse to research must proceed not from philosophies but from things and from the problems connected with them. Philosophy, however, is essentially a science of true beginnings, or origins" [C, W50].
- **Heidegger**: "Was ist das – die Philosophie?" (1955) and "Das Ende der Philosophie und die Aufgabe des Denkens" (1964) [U; not opened].
- **Horkheimer** (1937): the German lines on the current page [U this round; not reopened].
- **Habermas**: "postmetaphysical thinking" (*Nachmetaphysisches Denken*, 1988) [U; not opened].
- **Derrida**: "Deconstruction is not a method and cannot be transformed into one" ("Letter to a Japanese Friend", 1983) [C, W52, excerpt].

**Hadot and Foucault (twentieth-century French metaphilosophy, in their own words).**
- **Hadot, the thesis in his own words** (*What Is Ancient Philosophy?*, tr. Chase, Harvard 2002; original © Gallimard 1995). "Philosophical discourse, then, originates in a choice of life and an existential option—not vice versa". "There can never be a philosophy or philosophers outside a group, a community—in a word, a philosophical 'school'". "The task of philosophical discourse will therefore be to reveal and rationally justify this existential option" [C, W33]. He extends it beyond antiquity: "I also think that this is ultimately true of all philosophy … practical reason takes primacy over theoretical reason". He reads Kant's "that which interests reason" as "the choice of a way of life", and the *Tractatus* as resting on an existential choice (ch. 12) [C, W33]. On the university: "University philosophy therefore remains in the same position it occupied in the Middle Ages: it is still a servant, sometimes of theology, sometimes of science" [C, W33]. He takes up Kant's *Weltbegriff* as "the concept of cosmic philosophy" (ch. 12) [C, W33]. Dates: Hadot calls "Exercices spirituels et philosophie antique" "my 1977 article" [C, W33]; the book of that title (Études augustiniennes, 1981) [U; catalogue copies seen, W61]. The English collection *Philosophy as a Way of Life* (Oxford, 1995) [C that Hadot cites it, W33].
- **Hadot on Foucault, and Foucault quoted.** Hadot: his 1977 article "had some influence on Michel Foucault's idea of the 'culture of the self', presented in his book *Le souci de soi* (1984)" [C, W33]. Foucault, as Hadot quotes him: "Before Descartes, a subject could have access to the truth only by carrying out beforehand a certain work upon himself which made him susceptible of knowing the truth"; for Descartes "it is enough that I be any subject capable of seeing what is evident … Evidence has been substituted for askésis" [C as quoted, W33]. Hadot departs from him: each *Meditation* "is indeed a spiritual exercise" [C, W33]. **This is the headline dispute between the two, and it bears on the early-modern page** (is Descartes the end of philosophy as practice?).
- **Foucault, "What is Enlightenment?"** (*The Foucault Reader*, ed. Rabinow, 1984, pp. 32–50; translator Catherine Porter [U]). He would "envisage modernity rather as an attitude than as a period of history … a mode of relating to contemporary reality; a voluntary choice made by certain people". What links us to the Enlightenment is "the permanent reactivation of an attitude—that is, of a philosophical ethos that could be described as a permanent critique of our historical era". The critical ontology of ourselves is "not, certainly, as a theory, a doctrine, nor even as a permanent body of knowledge that is accumulating; it has to be conceived as an attitude, an ethos, a philosophical life", and it is "a historico-practical test of the limits that we may go beyond, and thus as work carried out by ourselves upon ourselves as free beings" [C, W51]. *The Hermeneutics of the Subject* (Collège de France 1981–82; epimeleia heautou, the "Cartesian moment") was not opened [U]; the course outline lists "the Cartesian moment" under 6 January 1982 [U, search summary].
