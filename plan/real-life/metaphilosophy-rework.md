# Metaphilosophy subject: rework

Plan for restructuring the metaphilosophy subject (`/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/`). Draft 2026-10-22, for discussion; nothing on the pages changes until the structure is agreed.

## Why

The subject is now arranged by period and tradition, each page answering the three questions (what is philosophy, what is its point, how should it be done) for its own time or place. What it does not show is the line between them: how and why one self-understanding gave way to the next. That line is what later work needs from the subject. The subject stays neutral: every change in the line is attributed to the source that describes it.

Three further reasons:

- **Greek is not the start of "Western".** The story "from the Greeks to Kant" is itself a construction of 1780–1830 (Peter K. J. Park). The Greeks had at least two heirs, the Latin West and the Islamic world, which carried Aristotle and passed him back. A Greek page of its own makes Greece a shared source and puts the Islamic world beside the Latin West as a sibling.
- **"Non-Western" goes as a heading.** All traditions sit on the same footing on the landing page. That also leaves room to expand any of them later (India, China or the Islamic world could become a sequence of pieces of its own) without restructuring.
- **Neutral names.** Several current titles are coloured: they carry a reading, not a description. Renamed below.

## Proposed structure

2. **The philosophies** — an overview, one entry per tradition, on an equal footing: Greek, the Islamic world, Western (Latin), India, China, Japan, Africa, Latin America. Order to decide (chronological by first texts, or geographical).
3. **How the conception changed** — a pointer and a few lines leading into the overview piece (below).
4. **Claims of an end** — the pieces now under "Western philosophy in question".
6. No Persons list and no See also unless a page relates directly (done 2026-10-23); the name, explicit metaphilosophy and other names moved to a Western piece, The name.

### The new piece: how the conception changed

Follows the changes in philosophy's self-understanding in order, with the cause of each change as a source gives it. Working sequence, in neutral wording:

1. The inquiry into nature (the early Greek thinkers).
2. A way of life (Socrates to the Hellenistic schools).
3. The branching: the Latin West and the Islamic world.
4. Reason and faith (the Latin and Islamic debates; the two components of the mixture, philosophy and revealed religion).
5. The complete system (Descartes's tree, Hegel).
6. The critique of reason (Kant).
7. Philosophy defined against the sciences (the nineteenth century: "scientist", the identity crisis, the neo-Kantians).
8. What the sciences leave (Russell, 1912).
9. The philosophies of the special sciences (the twentieth-century fields; the post-Kuhn turn, Machamer and Muller).
10. The end claims (Hegel to Derrida; the scientists' verdict).

### The mixture of each period

Each period is named by its mixture: the two components it held together, and what grew out of the mixture leads into the next period (from reason and faith, for instance, to Descartes, whose system rests on God's guarantee and whose method is new). A first set, agreed as a start (2026-10-22). The pairs go on the evolution piece and as the heading of each period page's core section; the page titles keep the period names, so readers still find "medieval philosophy".

| Period | Mixture | Why this pair |
|---|---|---|
| Early Greek | **Nature and reason** | *Physis*, the inquiry into nature, worked out by argument (*logos*) rather than story. Not "myth and reason": "from mythos to logos" is a contested narrative. |
| Socrates to the Hellenistic schools | **Knowledge and life** | Philosophy as a way of life: knowing as a way of living well (Hadot; the schools' own claim). |
| Medieval | **Reason and faith** | Decided. |
| Early modern, to Kant | **Reason and experience** | The period's own quarrel, rationalists and empiricists, with the new science behind both. Kant's critique tries to settle it and closes the period. |
| Nineteenth century | **Nature and history** | The sciences take nature; philosophy's remaining claims gather round history and the human world (Hegel, Dilthey's *Geisteswissenschaften*, Windelband). The most interpretive pair: needs a source framing the period this way; Windelband's rectorial address "Geschichte und Naturwissenschaft" (1894) is the first candidate. |
| Twentieth century | **Language and experience** | The analytic turn to language; phenomenology's and pragmatism's experience. Holds across all three streams. |
| The special sciences | **The core and the specialisms** | The mixture that comes apart and feeds back. |

The chain: nature is reasoned about, reason is lived, it meets faith, it meets experience, experience splits into nature and history, history turns to language, and the whole divides into specialisms. Each pair is to be checked against the sources of its page before it is used.

Where other traditions meet the line it says so: the word sent abroad (*tetsugaku*, *zhexue*), the canon narrowed. Scope to begin with: the Greek line and its branchings, where the sources are solid. Lines for other traditions can be added when a tradition is expanded.

Word test: the piece says "evolution" only where a source does; otherwise "how the conception changed".

### Decided 2026-10-23

- **No "Is it philosophy?" page.** Any general page on what counts as philosophy ends up judging by one tradition's standard. In its place, a few very general paragraphs on the hub: the subject takes philosophy as the love of knowledge and wisdom, the activity under whatever name (knowledge first, the Western sense; wisdom added for traditions less driven by matters of fact); what knowledge and wisdom are, what it is for and how it is sought are answered within each tradition, none the measure for another; explicit and implicit. The tradition-specific material moved to the tradition pages (India, China, Japan; Garfield and Van Norden to the Western page).
- **Local discussions.** Each tradition page carries its own discussion of what the love of wisdom is there, and how that understanding changed within the tradition. To be developed as each tradition page is reworked.
- **The name** (the word "metaphilosophy", its dispute, explicit metaphilosophy as a field, other names) is a Western piece.
- **No Persons list and no See also** on the hub.
- **The Western pages live in `western/`.** All Western pieces sit in that folder; "Claims of an end" stays a heading on the Western page, not a folder of its own. Each tradition becomes a folder (`india/index.md` and so on) when it is reworked.
- **Work goes where its context is.** A tradition page is an exposé of what that tradition's philosophy consists of, told by its texts and its thinkers, not a review of it from outside. Western opinion about another tradition (verdicts on whether it "had philosophy", judgements on it, disputes among Western scholars about it) is Western metaphilosophy and lives in the Western section, the nineteenth century most naturally (the canon narrowed, the word sent abroad). Scholars of any origin are used for facts: dates, what a word meant, which text says what, translations. Thinkers who speak from within a tradition (Matilal, Ganeri, Daya Krishna) count as its own voices wherever they hold a post.

### Tradition pages follow the landing page's brief (decided 2026-10-23)

Every page in a tradition's section answers the three questions of the landing page (what the activity is, what its point is, how it is done) for its school or period, as running text, without a heading per question. A page that does not answer them has no place in the section; material that belongs to no single school goes on the tradition's landing page. Implicit metaphilosophy, as on the landing page, is read off each school's position.

### India (decided 2026-10-23)

- **Landing page** — the thinkers' own words for the work (*tantra*, *mata*, *vāda*, *siddhānta*, *śāstra*; the *jijñāsā* openings; *ānvīkṣikī*), inquiry for a purpose (Dharmakīrti, Dharmottara), commentary as the shared form (root text, commentary, treatise; *pūrvapakṣa* and *uttarapakṣa*; Jayanta, the *navya* scholars), the sorting of the schools (*āstika/nāstika* from Pāṇini and Manu to Haribhadra and the Vedāntic surveys; the six as a late list); then the pages.
- **School pages** — the Upaniṣads; the Buddhists (the Buddha, Nāgasena's scholar's talk and king's talk, Nāgārjuna, Dignāga and Dharmakīrti); the Jainas (the path, the standpoints, the four-part debate, Haribhadra's survey); Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika (the means of knowing, release, the forms of debate, Navya-Nyāya); the investigative science (Kauṭilya's grouping: Sāṃkhya, Yoga, the materialists); Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta (the two inquiries, Śaṅkara, Mādhava's survey).
- **Modern India** — the answers to Europe from around 1800: *darśana* for "philosophy", Rammohun Roy, Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan, Matilal, K. C. Bhattacharyya, Daya Krishna's *saṃvāda*. Dārā Shikōh (1655) to place: here as the earlier case, or out.
- **Moves to the Western section**: Hegel's verdict, Jacobi and Hacker on *ānvīkṣikī*, Halbfass's judgements, the Western scholars' dispute over Hindu engagement with Islam, Europe's reception of India (Schlegel, Schopenhauer).
- **Person pages**: none proposed; for discussion when the pages are drafted: Haribhadra, Daya Krishna, K. C. Bhattacharyya, Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan.
