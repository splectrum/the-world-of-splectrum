# Metaphilosophy subject: rework

Plan for restructuring the metaphilosophy subject (`/positioning/subjects/m/metaphilosophy/`). Draft 2026-10-22, for discussion; nothing on the pages changes until the structure is agreed.

## Why

The subject is now arranged by period and tradition, each page answering the three questions (what is philosophy, what is its point, how should it be done) for its own time or place. What it does not show is the line between them: how and why one self-understanding gave way to the next. That line is what later work needs from the subject. The subject stays neutral: every change in the line is attributed to the source that describes it.

Three further reasons:

- **Greek is not the start of "Western".** The story "from the Greeks to Kant" is itself a construction of 1780–1830 (Peter K. J. Park). The Greeks had at least two heirs, the Latin West and the Islamic world, which carried Aristotle and passed him back. A Greek page of its own makes Greece a shared source and puts the Islamic world beside the Latin West as a sibling.
- **"Non-Western" goes as a heading.** All traditions sit on the same footing on the landing page. That also leaves room to expand any of them later (India, China or the Islamic world could become a sequence of pieces of its own) without restructuring.
- **Neutral names.** Several current titles are coloured: they carry a reading, not a description. Renamed below.

2. **The philosophies** — an overview, one entry per tradition, on an equal footing: Greek, the Islamic world, Western (Latin), India, China, Japan, Africa, Latin America. Order to decide (chronological by first texts, or geographical).
4. **Claims of an end** — the pieces now under "Western philosophy in question".
6. No Persons list and no See also unless a page relates directly (done 2026-10-23); the name, explicit metaphilosophy and other names moved to a Western piece, The name.

### The overview piece: dropped (2026-10-24)

The planned "How the conception changed" piece and the period mixtures are dropped. The line now shows on the pages themselves, the mixtures were mostly interpretive (only reason and faith, and reason and experience, had sources), and a single line from Greece onward would retell the "from the Greeks to Kant" story the reading-other-traditions pages describe. In its place the Western landing page has one paragraph on the causes of change the thinkers themselves give (Kant, Hegel, Comte, Whewell, Windelband, Ortega, Russell).

### Decided 2026-10-23

- **No "Is it philosophy?" page.** Any general page on what counts as philosophy ends up judging by one tradition's standard. In its place, a few very general paragraphs on the hub: the subject takes philosophy as the love of knowledge and wisdom, the activity under whatever name (knowledge first, the Western sense; wisdom added for traditions less driven by matters of fact); what knowledge and wisdom are, what it is for and how it is sought are answered within each tradition, none the measure for another; explicit and implicit. The tradition-specific material moved to the tradition pages (India, China, Japan; Garfield and Van Norden to the Western page).
- **Local discussions.** Each tradition page carries its own discussion of what the love of wisdom is there, and how that understanding changed within the tradition. To be developed as each tradition page is reworked.
- **The name** (the word "metaphilosophy", its dispute, explicit metaphilosophy as a field, other names) is a Western piece.
- **No Persons list and no See also** on the hub.
- **The Western pages live in `western/`.** All Western pieces sit in that folder; "Claims of an end" stays a heading on the Western page, not a folder of its own. Each tradition becomes a folder (`india/index.md` and so on) when it is reworked.
- **Work goes where its context is.** A tradition page is an exposé of what that tradition's philosophy consists of, told by its texts and its thinkers, not a review of it from outside. Western opinion about another tradition (verdicts on whether it "had philosophy", judgements on it, disputes among Western scholars about it) is Western metaphilosophy and lives in the Western section, the nineteenth century most naturally (the canon narrowed, the word sent abroad). Scholars of any origin are used for facts: dates, what a word meant, which text says what, translations. Thinkers who speak from within a tradition (Matilal, Ganeri, Daya Krishna) count as its own voices wherever they hold a post.

### Tradition pages follow the landing page's brief (decided 2026-10-23)

Every page in a tradition's section answers the three questions of the landing page (what the activity is, what its point is, how it is done) for its school or period, as running text, without a heading per question. A page that does not answer them has no place in the section; material that belongs to no single school goes on the tradition's landing page. Implicit metaphilosophy, as on the landing page, is read off each school's position.

### India (decided 2026-10-23)

- **Landing page** — the thinkers' own words for the work (*tantra*, *mata*, *vāda*, *siddhānta*, *śāstra*; the *jijñāsā* openings; *ānvīkṣikī*), inquiry for a purpose (Dharmakīrti, Dharmottara), commentary as the shared form (root text, commentary, treatise; *pūrvapakṣa* and *uttarapakṣa*; Jayanta, the *navya* scholars), the sorting of the schools (*āstika/nāstika* from Pāṇini and Manu to Haribhadra and the Vedāntic surveys; the six as a late list); then the pages.
- **School pages** — the Upaniṣads; the Buddhists (the Buddha, Nāgasena's scholar's talk and king's talk, Nāgārjuna, Dignāga and Dharmakīrti); the Jainas (the path, the standpoints, the four-part debate, Haribhadra's survey); Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika (the means of knowing, release, the forms of debate, Navya-Nyāya); the investigative science (Kauṭilya's grouping: Sāṃkhya, Yoga, the materialists); Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta (the two inquiries, Śaṅkara, Mādhava's survey).
- **Modern India** — the answers to Europe from around 1800: *darśana* for "philosophy", Rammohun Roy, Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan, Matilal, K. C. Bhattacharyya, Daya Krishna's *saṃvāda*. Dārā Shikōh (1655) to place: here as the earlier case, or out.
- **Moves to the Western section**: Hegel's verdict, Jacobi and Hacker on *ānvīkṣikī*, Halbfass's judgements, the Western scholars' dispute over Hindu engagement with Islam, Europe's reception of India (Schlegel, Schopenhauer).
- **Person pages**: none proposed; for discussion when the pages are drafted: Haribhadra, Daya Krishna, K. C. Bhattacharyya, Vivekananda, Radhakrishnan.

### China (outline agreed 2026-10-23)

- **School pages** — the Confucians (Confucius, Mencius, Xunzi); the Mohists; the Daoists; Buddhism in China; the Learning of the Way (with the Qing dispute between Han and Song learning); Modern China (*zhexue* from Japan, Hu Shi, Feng Youlan, Jin Yuelin, the 1958 manifesto, Zheng Jiadong's legitimacy debate). Grouping to settle after the gap research: the School of Names, the Legalists, Dark Learning (Wang Bi), the Buddhist doctrinal classifications, Wang Chong, Qing evidential learning.
- **Moves to the Western section**: Hegel on Confucius, Defoort's four positions, Derrida's remark (kept on Modern China only as a clause, as what Zheng's debate answered).
### Japan (decided 2026-10-24)

- **Open**: Maruyama's *Nihon no shisō* to be opened before he is quoted directly; the Japanese lines from NDL scans to be checked against the page images.

### Greece and Rome, the Islamic world, Western (decided 2026-10-24)

The three remaining sections get the same treatment as the five above: a folder each, a landing page with the tradition's own words for the work and its own sortings, pages answering the three questions in running text, told by the tradition's own texts; scholars for facts; the "In one view" tables and per-question labels go. This supersedes the rows for these pages in the table above.

- **Philosophy in Greece and Rome** (`greece-and-rome/`, replacing `ancient/`). Landing page: the words (*philosophia* and its late date, *historia peri physeos*, *sophia*, *theōria*, *scholē*), the Greeks' own sortings (Aristotle's theoretical, practical and productive; the Stoic division into logic, physics and ethics; Diogenes Laertius's successions). Pages: the inquiry into nature; the sophists and Socrates; Plato and the Academy; Aristotle and the Lyceum; the Hellenistic schools; Rome (Lucretius, Cicero's Latin vocabulary, Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius); late antiquity (Plotinus, Porphyry, Proclus, the commentators, the Alexandrian prolegomena's six definitions, which reappear in al-Kindī), closing on a short paragraph on Byzantium (Psellos, Plethon).
- **Hadot.** "Philosophy as a way of life" is Hadot's thesis, a twentieth-century Western reading; on the Greek pages the ancients make the case in their own words (the *Gorgias*, the *Phaedo*, Epicurus, Seneca). Hadot stays for facts only; his thesis goes on the Western twentieth-century pages.
- **Philosophy in the Islamic world** (`islamic-world/`). Landing page: the words (*falsafa*, *ḥikma*, *ʿilm*, *ʿaql*, *kalām*), the tradition's own sortings (al-Fārābī's *Enumeration of the Sciences*, Ibn Sīnā's division of the sciences, al-Shahrastānī's *al-Milal wa-l-niḥal*, Ibn Khaldūn's *Muqaddima*). Pages: the reception and al-Kindī; kalām; al-Fārābī; Ibn Sīnā; al-Ghazālī and Ibn Rushd; al-Andalus (Ibn Bājja, Ibn Ṭufayl, Maimonides); the later *ḥikma* (Suhrawardī, Ṭūsī, Mullā Ṣadrā); a modern page (al-Afghānī and ʿAbduh, Iqbal, al-Jābirī, Ṭāhā ʿAbd al-Raḥmān, Nasr). Adamson's dispute over the name moves to the Western section; "Into the Latin West" moves to the Western medieval page.
- **Islamic world, as drafted (2026-10-24):** al-Ghazālī and Ibn Rushd share a page; Ibn Khaldūn sorts the sciences on the landing page and his refutation closes the later *ḥikma*; Ṭāhā ʿAbd al-Raḥmān is left out until a line of his own is checked; al-Jābirī rests on a summary for now. To move in the Western rework: the Latin reception of the Arabic texts to the schools-and-universities page; Renan, the decline narrative, Adamson on the name, Griffel, Sabra, Gutas on Ibn Sīnā, Strauss, Rizvi after Hadot, and Europe reading *Ḥayy* to the West reading the other traditions (both in the Islamic-world research note).
- **Western.** Landing page: the words (Latin *philosophia*, natural philosophy, *Weltweisheit*, *Wissenschaft*), the West's own sortings (the arts faculty, Descartes's tree, the *Encyclopédie*'s map after Bacon, Kant's *Conflict of the Faculties*, the histories of philosophy from Brucker to Hegel). The line: the schools and the universities (with the Latin reception of the Arabic texts); the humanists; the new philosophy; Enlightenment to Kant; the nineteenth century (renamed from the hinge); analytic philosophy, pragmatism, continental philosophy (the twentieth-century page split, Hadot added); philosophy and the special sciences; the name. Claims of an end stays a group of its own, with the renames above.
- **The West reading the other traditions.** A group of its own on the Western landing page, outside the Western line, with its own timeline: open interest (*Confucius Sinarum Philosophus*, Leibniz, Wolff's Halle lecture, Brucker), the narrowing of 1780–1830 (Park; Tennemann, Hegel; Schlegel and Schopenhauer alongside), the colonial frame (Lévy-Bruhl, Tempels as read in Europe, Hegel's "echo"), comparative philosophy (the East–West Philosophers' Conferences, *Philosophy East and West*), the present disputes (the review material from the five tradition pages, Adamson on the name, Garfield and Van Norden). Dates to be checked in research. The other direction, each tradition receiving the West, stays on the tradition pages.
- **Western, as drafted (2026-10-24):** the line in ten pages (schools-and-universities, humanists, new-philosophy, enlightenment-to-kant, nineteenth-century, analytic-philosophy, pragmatism, continental-philosophy with Hadot and Foucault, philosophy-and-the-special-sciences as its own page, the-name); `western/reading-other-traditions/` with five pages by period (open-interest, philosophy-proper, colonial-frame, comparative-philosophy, present-disputes); claims of an end renamed (scientists-on-philosophy, context-of-the-end-claims). Kant's handmaid retort sits on enlightenment-to-kant. Person pages: none. **Backing to flow down** to person pages: `hegel` ("Philosophy proper commences in the West", the exclusion and Arabian-philosophy sentences, Confucius "no speculative philosophy", Africa in Sibree), `kant` (the *Logik* passage), `leibniz` (the *Novissima Sinica* preface), `schopenhauer` (the 1818 preface, the Oupnek'hat).
