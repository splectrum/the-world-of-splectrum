# Metaphilosophy in China: research note

Purpose: to fill the gaps for a page on China beside the Greek and Latin, Islamic and Indian pages of the Metaphilosophy subject. For each stage it asks the subject's three questions (1. What is philosophy? 2. What is its point? 3. How is it done?), with the tradition's own words. The legitimacy debate (Hegel, Derrida 2001, Zheng Jiadong, Defoort's four positions) is covered here only as an intro headline. Already checked and not redone here: *zhexue* via Nishi Amane; the Jesuit *fei-lu-suo-fei-ya* and its match with *lixue*; Defoort 2001; Hu Shi 1919 and Feng 1931/34 as quoted by Huang Yushun; Feng's *Short History* ch. 1 (definition, *neisheng waiwang*, "elevation of the mind", suggestiveness); Hegel on Confucius; Zheng Jiadong; Derrida 2001. This is a working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a source I opened, named by its key in the Sources list. **[C as quoted]** = the wording is checked as it appears in a secondary source I opened; the primary was not opened. **[U]** = unverified. "My rendering" = mine, from the fetched Chinese text. Analects numbers are given as standard / Legge where they differ; the conversion is mine [U, see open checks].

**Access.** ctext.org is behind a Cloudflare challenge, and the page says automated access is not authorised. I did not try to get past it. sacred-texts.com returned 403. In their place I used Chinese Wikisource for the texts, Gutenberg and archive.org for Legge, Dubs, Mei, Giles and Feng, and the SEP.

## 1. The words and the name

**The tradition's own words for the enterprise** (all attested, all [C]):
- ***xue* 學, learning.** It is the first word of the *Analects*: 學而時習之 (1.1) [C, S2]. Every later name for a movement is a *xue* compound:
  - *Daoxue* 道學, "the Learning of the Way", the Song movement's own name [C, S16 Tiwald].
  - *Lixue* 理學 and *Xinxue* 心學, the "Learning of Pattern" and the "Learning of Heartmind" [C, S16 Tiwald].
  - *xuanxue* 玄學, "the dark or mystic learning" [C, S18].
  - *Hanxue* / *Songxue*, Han learning against Song learning [C, S18].
  - *xinxing zhi xue* 心性之學, the learning of heart-mind and nature (1958 Manifesto) [C, S20].
- ***dao* 道, the Way.** It is what learning seeks, and so it names it: *Daoxue*. Feng: "known as the Tao hsüeh chia and their philosophy as the Tao hsüeh, i.e., the Study of the Tao or Truth. **The term Neo-Confucianism is a newly coined western equivalent for Tao hsüeh**" [C, S18 ch. 24]. A strong word-test headline.
- ***daoshu* 道術, the art of the Way, against *fangshu* 方術, a method.** This is the oldest native account of what the masters were doing, in the *Zhuangzi*'s last chapter, "Tianxia" (ch. 33, a Miscellaneous chapter, so not Zhuang Zhou's own) [C, S11]. See stage 5.
- ***jia* 家, house or school.** Sima Tan's essay is 論六家之要指, "on the essential points of the six *jia*" [C, S12 Shiji 130]. Feng gives the title as "On the Essential Ideas of the Six Schools" [C, S18 ch. 3].
  - Word test: *jia* is literally a house or lineage, and "school" is the translators' word. Dubs renders 百家 as "the hundred schools **of philosophy**"; "of philosophy" is his addition [C, S6 Book XXI; text 百家 in S5].
- ***zhuzi* 諸子, the masters.** It is a library category. Liu Xin's *Seven Summaries* (in *Hanshu* 30) files the *zhuzi lüe* 諸子略 (masters) apart from the *liuyi lüe* 六藝略 (the Six Arts, i.e. the classics). It counts "諸子十家，其可觀者九家而已", "ten schools of masters, of which only nine are worth looking at" [C, S12; my rendering].
- ***bian* 辯, disputation.** This is the Mohist word for the field of argument. Fraser: "interpretable as 'dialectics,' 'distinction drawing,' 'discriminating,' or 'disputation'" [C, S9 Mohist Canons]. Mencius, accused of being 好辯 ("fond of disputing"), says he is not [C, S3].
- **Nearest words.** The earlier round left it [U] whether *dao* and *xue* are the nearest words. They can now be stated as attested: *xue* names the activity and *dao* its object, and the two join in *Daoxue*. Calling them the "nearest" equivalents of "philosophy" is still a judgement. It should be attributed: Feng makes it for *Daoxue*; the Manifesto places *xinxing zhi xue* inside "what was anciently called 義理之學" (the learning of meaning and principle) [C, S20 §6].
- **Translators' words to flag.**
  - *junzi* 君子 (literally "son of a lord"): Legge gives "man of complete virtue" (1.1), "superior man", and "man of real talent and virtue" (7.26/VII.25); SEP gives "gentleman" [C, S1, S16 Confucius].
  - *xin* 心: Legge has "mind" and "mental constitution"; Tiwald has "heartmind" [C, S4, S16].
  - *li* 理: "Pattern, Principle" (Tiwald), "Pattern" (Van Norden) [C, S16].

**Naming headline: Jin Yuelin's question.** Correction to the brief: **the distinction is Jin Yuelin's, not Feng's.** It comes in Jin's review report (審查報告) on Feng's *History of Chinese Philosophy* [C as quoted, S19]:
- The question: 所謂中國哲學史是中國哲學的史呢？還是在中國的哲學史呢？ ("Is the so-called history of Chinese philosophy a history of Chinese philosophy, or a history of philosophy in China?"; my rendering) [C as quoted, S19].
- His two attitudes: treat Chinese philosophy as "a special kind of learning within China's national learning" (中國國學中之一種特別學問), or as "philosophy discovered in China" (發現於中國的哲學) [C as quoted, S19].
- His verdict: Feng's and Hu Shi's books take the second road. On Hu Shi's book he writes that one sometimes feels "its author is an American studying Chinese thought" [C as quoted, S19; my rendering].
- Zheng Jiadong's first statement of the legitimacy problem (1999) was titled 「"中国哲学"与"哲学在中国"」. That picks up the same pair [C, checked in the earlier round against Zheng's own introduction to 《"中国哲学"的牟宗三时代》].
- That the report is appended to vol. 2 of Feng's *History* (1934) is [U, search summary].

## 2. Confucius (*Analects*)

- **What it is: learning.**
  - 1.1: 學而時習之，不亦說乎, "Is it not pleasant to learn with a constant perseverance and application?" (Legge). Word test: 時習 is "to practise / rehearse at the proper times"; "constant perseverance and application" is Legge's expansion [C, S1, S2].
  - On knowing, 2.17: "When you know a thing, to hold that you know it; and when you do not know a thing, to allow that you do not know it;-- this is knowledge" (Legge) [C, S1, S2].
- **Its point: for oneself, toward the sage.**
  - 14.24 / XIV.25: 古之學者爲己，今之學者爲人, "In ancient times, men learned with a view to their own improvement. Now-a-days, men learn with a view to the approbation of others" (Legge; literally "for themselves … for others") [C, S1, S2].
  - The arc of a life, 2.4: "At fifteen, I had my mind bent on learning … At seventy, I could follow what my heart desired, without transgressing what was right" (Legge) [C, S1, S2].
  - The sage is beyond him: "The sage and the man of perfect virtue;-- how dare I rank myself with them? It may simply be said of me, that I strive to become such without satiety, and teach others without weariness" (7.34 / VII.33) [C, S1, S2].
  - "A sage it is not mine to see; could I see a [*junzi*], that would satisfy me" (7.26 / VII.25) [C, S1]. Headline: the *junzi* is the reachable aim, the sage the horizon.
- **How it is done.**
  - 2.15: "Learning without thought is labour lost; thought without learning is perilous" (學而不思則罔，思而不學則殆) [C, S1, S2].
  - Learning outweighs thinking alone, 15.31 / XV.30: "I have been the whole day without eating, and the whole night without sleeping:-- occupied with thinking. It was of no use. The better plan is to learn" [C, S1, S2].
  - He transmits rather than makes, 7.1: "A transmitter and not a maker, believing in and loving the ancients" (述而不作，信而好古) [C, S1, S2].
  - He is not born knowing, 7.20 / VII.19: "I am not one who was born in the possession of knowledge; I am one who is fond of antiquity, and earnest in seeking it there" [C, S1, S2].
  - A thread through it, 15.3 / XV.2: "I seek a unity all-pervading" (予一以貫之) [C, S1, S2].
  - Also usable, 7.2: "The silent treasuring up of knowledge; learning without satiety; and instructing others without being wearied" [C, S1].

## 3. Mencius and Xunzi

**Mencius.**
- **Its point.** 6A11: 學問之道無他，求其放心而已矣, "The great end of learning is nothing else but to seek for the lost mind" (Legge, VI.i.11). The setting: people seek their lost fowls and dogs, but not their lost mind [C, S3, S4].
- **What it is.** 7A1: "He who has exhausted all his mental constitution knows his nature. Knowing his nature, he knows Heaven" (盡其心者，知其性也。知其性，則知天矣) [C, S3, S4].
- **How it is done.**
  - Disputation is reluctant and defensive. Asked why outsiders call him "fond of disputing", he answers 予豈好辯哉？予不得已也, "Am I fond of disputing? I cannot help it" (3B9; my rendering). His target is Yang Zhu and Mozi: "I … address myself to the defence of the doctrines of the former sages, and to oppose Yang and Mo" (Legge) [C, S3; Legge's wording C as quoted in his own prolegomena, S4].
  - On texts, 7B3: "It would be better to be without the Book of History than to give entire credit to it" (盡信《書》，則不如無《書》) [C, S3, S4].

**Xunzi.**
- **How it is done: it never stops.** Ch. 1 "Encouraging Learning" (勸學) opens 君子曰：學不可以已, "The superior man says: Study should never stop" (Dubs) [C, S5, S6].
- **Its point: from scholar to sage.** 學惡乎始？惡乎終？曰：其數則始乎誦經，終乎讀禮；其義則始乎爲士，終乎爲聖人. Dubs: "It begins by reciting the Classics and ends in learning the rites. Its purpose begins with making the scholar, and ends in making the sage" [C, S5, S6]. Dubs's note gives the three grades: "scholar, superior man, and sage" [C, S6].
  - He repeats *Analects* 14.24, and adds: 君子之學也，以美其身；小人之學也，以爲禽犢, "the *junzi* learns to make himself beautiful; the petty man learns to make gifts" (literally "calves and birds" offered as presents; my rendering) [C, S5].
- **What it is: against the one-sided.** Ch. 21 "Dispelling Obsession" (解蔽) opens 凡人之患，蔽於一曲，而闇於大理, "the common affliction is to be blinkered by one corner and in the dark about the great pattern" (my rendering). Dubs is loose: "Everything that men suffer is from being prejudiced by one false thing; and so the great principles are hidden from them" [C, S5, S6].
  - He then names the masters, each blinkered: "Motze was prejudiced towards utility and did not know the elegancies of life … Hueitze … towards words and did not know reality. Chuangtze was prejudiced towards Nature, and did not know man" (Dubs). Confucius alone "仁知且不蔽", humane, wise and not blinkered [C, S5, S6].
  - The remedy is a state of mind, 虛壹而靜, "empty, unified and still" (my rendering) [C, S5].
  - **This is the Chinese counterpart to the Jaina standpoint line on the India page.** Here the rival schools are each a corner of one Way.

## 4. The Mohists

- **How it is done: the three standards.** *Mozi* 35 "Against Fate (I)": 言必立儀 … 故言必有三表, "speech must set up a standard … so speech must have three gauges" [C, S7]. The three are 本之, 原之, 用之. Mei: "Its basis, its verifiability, and its applicability":
  1. based on "the deeds of the ancient sage-kings";
  2. verified "by the senses of hearing and sight of the common people";
  3. applied "by adopting it in government and observing its benefits to the country and the people" [C, S8].
  - Speaking without a standard is "similar to determining the directions of sunrise and sunset on a revolving potter's wheel" (Mei) [C, S8; text 運鈞 S7].
  - Word test: *Mozi* 35 says *biao* 表 (a gauge or marker post), and *Mozi* 36 says *fa* 法 (三法) [C, S7]. Mei says "tests" [C, S8]; Fraser calls them "three fa (models)" [C, S9 Mohism §4].
  - The first Mohist book on method is titled 法儀, "On the Necessity of Standards" (Mei): "None has yet accomplished anything without standards" [C, S8].
- **What it is and its point: disputation (*bian*).** The "Lesser Selection" (小取): 夫辯者，將以明是非之分，審治亂之紀，明同異之處，察名實之理，處利害，決嫌疑 [C, S7]. Fraser: "by it we clarify divisions between this (*shì*) and not-this (*fēi*) to examine the guidelines of order and disorder; clarify points of sameness and difference to discern the patterns of names (*míng*) and stuff (*shí*); and settle benefit and harm to resolve uncertainty and doubt" [C, S9 Canons].
- **Disputation has a winner.**
  - Canon A74: 辯，爭彼也。辯勝，當也, "disputation is contending over a 'that'; winning disputation is fitting" (my rendering) [C, S7]. The explanation: one calls it "ox", the other "not-ox"; "是不俱當。不俱當，必或不當", they cannot both fit, so one must fail to fit [C, S7]. Fraser: "the side whose claim fits the thing wins" [C, S9].
  - Canon B35: 謂辯無勝，必不當, "to say that disputation has no winner is necessarily not fitting" (my rendering) [C, S7].
  - **Headline pairing with the *Zhuangzi*'s "who shall adjudicate?"** (already on the `zhuangzi` page; Giles's rendering of ch. 2: "If you beat me, and not I you, are you necessarily right and I wrong? … Who shall I employ as arbiter between us?" [C, S11]). Set the two side by side. That B35 is aimed at the *Zhuangzi* is a scholarly reading (Graham's, as I recall) [U]. The page can show the two positions without saying one answers the other.
  - Note: Giles follows the arbiter passage with an indented line, "Upon God, in whose infinity all contraries blend". That is his own commentary, not the text [C, S11].

## 5. The Daoists, and the schools seen from outside

**The *Daodejing*.**
- **What it is (or cannot be).** Ch. 1: 道可道，非常道. Legge: "The Tao that can be trodden is not the enduring and unchanging Tao. The name that can be named is not the enduring and unchanging name" [C, S10].
  - Word test: the middle 道 is a verb. Legge takes it as "tread"; most modern versions take it as "speak" [U for "most"]. The pun is the original's; the choice between the two senses is the translator's.
- **How it is done: by subtraction.** Ch. 48: 為學日益，為道日損. Legge: "He who devotes himself to learning (seeks) from day to day to increase (his knowledge); he who devotes himself to the Tao (seeks) from day to day to diminish (his doing)." The bracketed words are Legge's supplements; the Chinese is four characters a clause. "… till he arrives at doing nothing (on purpose)" [C, S10].
  - **The headline: *xue* and *dao* are set against each other here, the same two words that later join in *Daoxue*.**
  - Also usable: 絕學無憂, "When we renounce learning we have no troubles" (Legge places it at the head of ch. 20; other editions end ch. 19 with it [U]) [C, S10]. And 知者不言，言者不知, ch. 56 [C, S10].

**The *Zhuangzi*.** Already carried on the `zhuangzi` page (Qiwulun, fish trap). Link to it; don't restate.

**"Tianxia" (*Zhuangzi* 33): the art of the Way and its splitting.** This is the strongest native metaphilosophical text found in this round [C, S11 text]. The Chinese is quoted; the English is my rendering.
- 天下之治方術者多矣，皆以其有爲不可加矣。古之所謂道術者，果惡乎在？曰：无乎不在: "Many in the world practise a method, each holding that what he has cannot be bettered. Where is what the ancients called the art of the Way? Everywhere."
- 天下多得一察焉以自好，譬如耳目鼻口，皆有所明，不能相通: "most in the world get hold of one insight and are pleased with themselves; like ears, eyes, nose and mouth, each has its clarity but they do not connect." Each is 一曲之士, "a scholar of one corner".
- 是故內聖外王之道，闇而不明: "so the way of **sageliness within and kingliness without** is dark and not clear". **This is the locus of the phrase Feng later made the task of philosophy.** That Feng took it from here is [U]; that the phrase is here is [C].
- 百家往而不反，必不合矣 … 道術將爲天下裂: "the hundred schools go off and do not return, and will never meet … the art of the Way is about to be torn apart by the world."
- Giles is loose throughout ("one with God"; "the system of TAO is scattered in fragments over the face of the earth"). **A modern translation is needed for page quotes** (open check) [C, S11 Giles].
- The chapter then treats the schools one by one, Mozi first. Each "heard of its style and delighted in it" (聞其風而悅之), Zhuang Zhou included [C, S11].

**Sima Tan and Liu Xin: the Han surveys.**
- Sima Tan (in *Shiji* 130) quotes the *Yi* commentary, 天下一致而百慮，同歸而殊涂, "one aim and a hundred deliberations, one destination by different roads". He then says the six schools 此務為治者也，直所從言之異路，有省不省耳: "all of them work at bringing about order; they differ only in the road their words take, and in whether they see clearly or not" [C, S12; my rendering]. **Q2 headline: the shared point is order (*zhi* 治).** Feng gives the six as Yin-Yang, Literati (*ru*), Mohist, Names, Legalist, and the school of the Way and its Power (Sima Tan's 道德) [C, S18 ch. 3].
- Liu Xin (in *Hanshu* 30): each school 各引一端，崇其所善, "takes up one end and exalts what it is good at". "Though they have blind spots and shortcomings, taken together … they are branches and descendants of the Six Classics" (六經之支與流裔) [C, S12; my rendering]. He also derives each school from an office of state (儒家者流，蓋出於司徒之官 …) [C, S12]; that is detail, see the discard list.

## 6. Buddhism in China: Chan (headline only)

- **How it is done: not through words.** The four-phrase formula: "a special transmission outside the scriptures (*jiaowai biechuan*, 教外別傳); not established upon words and letters (*buli wenzi*, 不立文字); directly pointing to the human heartmind (*zhizhi renxin*, 直指人心); seeing nature and becoming a Buddha (*jianxing chengfo*, 見性成佛)" [C, S13 Hershock].
  - **Attribution check:** Hershock calls it "the four-fold phrasing that came to be used in **Song dynasty** China to characterize Chan distinctiveness" [C, S13]. So it is a later characterisation, not a saying of Bodhidharma.
  - Feng on the traditional lineage from Bodhidharma: it "is not supported by any documents dated earlier than the eleventh century … no scholar today takes the tradition very seriously" [C, S18 ch. 22].
- Hershock adds that the "anti-philosophical" reading is a reading. Chan literature is full of "references to canonical texts", and twentieth-century promoters stressed Chan's "apparent anti-rationality" [C, S13]. That is a headline caution for the page.
- A one-line illustration if wanted (Feng): asked "What is the First Principle?", Wenyi (d. 958) answered "If I were to tell you, it would become the second principle" [C, S18 ch. 22].

## 7. The Learning of the Way (Song–Ming "Neo-Confucianism")

- **What it is.** *Daoxue* 道學, "The Learning of the Way", whose aim at its outset was "to shift the emphasis of formal education away from literary attainments and refocus it on the moral Way" (Tiwald, citing Bol 2008) [C, S16]. It later splits into the Cheng-Zhu "Learning of Pattern" (*Lixue*) and the Lu-Wang "Learning of Heartmind" (*Xinxue*) [C, S16]. *Lixue* here is the same word the Jesuit-era scholars had matched with *philosophia*.
- **Its point: sagehood, which can be learned.** Zhou Dunyi, *Tongshu* ch. 20 "Learning to be a sage" (聖學): 「聖可學乎？」曰：「可。」, "'Can sagehood be learned?' 'It can.'" [C, S15; my rendering]. The *Great Learning*: "What the Great Learning teaches, is to illustrate illustrious virtue; to renovate the people; and to rest in the highest excellence … all must consider the cultivation of the person the root of everything besides" (Legge) [C, S14].
- **How it is done.**
  - ***Gewu zhizhi* 格物致知.** The *Great Learning*'s chain runs back from an ordered world to a cultivated person, then to a rectified heart, sincere thoughts and extended knowledge: "Such extension of knowledge lay in the investigation of things" (致知在格物) (Legge) [C, S14].
  - **Zhu Xi's reading.** Thompson: he "adapted and distilled the methodology of investigating things to extend knowledge"; it "incorporated reading and classical studies but ensured one's practical grasp of and personal resonance with … nature and phenomena as well as society". It went together with reverence (*jing*), "investigating things to discern their determinate or defining patterns (*li*)" [C, S16 Zhu Xi]. His reading of the canon became the basis of the civil service examination in 1313 [C, S16 Tiwald].
  - Zhu Xi's own words in the supplementary commentary (補傳: 即物而窮其理, "go to things and exhaust their pattern") [U, text not fetched].
  - **Wang Yangming against it: the bamboo.** He and a friend set out to investigate the bamboos in front of a pavilion to "investigate to the utmost the Pattern". The friend fell ill on the third day, and Wang himself after seven, by the usual telling [the seven days U] [C, S16 Van Norden, quoting Wang].
  - ***Zhixing heyi* 知行合一, the unity of knowing and acting** (*Chuanxilu*, juan 1):
    - 未有知而不行者；知而不行，只是未知, "There has never been one who knows and does not act; to know and not act is simply not yet to know" [C, S17; my rendering].
    - 知是行之始，行是知之成, "knowing is the beginning of acting, acting the completion of knowing" [C, S17; my rendering].
    - 某今說個知行合一，正是對病的藥. Van Norden's translation: "My current teaching regarding the unity of knowing and acting is a medicine directed precisely at this disease". The disease is those who "separate knowing and acting into two distinct tasks … and think that one must first know and only then can one act" [C, S17 text; S16 English].
  - **Method headline: the doctrine is a medicine, not a definition.** Van Norden: discussing knowing apart from acting is legitimate "but only in order to address the failings of specific kinds of individuals" [C, S16].
- **Optional stage, Qing: Han learning against Song learning.** Feng: the Qing controversy between *Hanxue* and *Songxue* "was really one as between the philosophical and scholarly interpretation of the ancient texts. The scholarly interpretation emphasized what it believed was their actual meaning; the philosophical interpretation emphasized what it believed they ought to have meant" [C, S18 ch. 27]. This is the counterpart to the India page's "Commentary" stage. A Q3 headline if a stage is wanted.

## 8. The modern question

Already checked and only to be reused: *zhexue*; Hu Shi 1919; Feng 1931/34; Feng 1948 (definition, *neisheng waiwang*, "elevation of the mind", suggestiveness).

- **What it is: Jin Yuelin's question** (section 1). Jin also names the difficulty: thought that "has the substance of philosophy without its form, or the form without the substance" (有哲學的實質而無哲學的形式，或有哲學的形式而無哲學的實質的思想) troubles the historian [C as quoted, S19].
- **New Confucians, the 1958 Manifesto** (为中國文化敬告世界人士宣言, signed by Mou Zongsan, Xu Fuguan, Zhang Junmai/Carsun Chang and Tang Junyi; dated New Year's Day 1958 and first printed in *Minzhu pinglun* and *Zaisheng*) [C, S20]. §4 is titled "The place of Chinese philosophical thought in Chinese culture, and how it differs from the West". All renderings below are mine.
  - **What it is.** Chinese philosophy, science, religion, politics, law and ethics have one cultural source. "In China's past there was no view that an individual's philosophical speculation could form a world of its own, still less that **a philosopher must build a system of thought by himself and set it out wholly in written works**" (更無哲學家必須一人自造一思想系統，以全表之於文字著作中之說) [C, S20 §4].
  - **How it is done.** So Chinese philosophical works take as their ideal 要言不繁, essential words and not many. They are "careless of fixing definitions and building arguments" (疏於界說之厘定，論證之建立), and that "is no cause for surprise". They cannot be understood apart from the philosopher's "whole personality and whole life" (全人格，全生活), his talks with teachers and friends, and his conduct in society [C, S20 §4].
  - **Its point.** The learning of heart-mind and nature (心性之學) "is precisely the core of Chinese scholarly thought" (正為中國學術思想之核心) [C, S20 §6].
  - Against the outsider's view: Chinese history and culture studied without sympathy and respect become "like dead fossils" (如同死的化石) [C, S20].
- **Mou Zongsan, one line?** *The Special Character of Chinese Philosophy* (中國哲學的特質, 1963): Chinese philosophy centres on "life" (生命) and values intuition over analysis [U, search summary only]. Optional. The Manifesto already carries the New Confucian headline under all four names.
- **The legitimacy debate, one intro line only:** from Hegel's "a moralist … not actually a philosopher" to Derrida 2001, Zheng Jiadong's "legitimacy" question (2000/01) and Defoort's four positions (2001). Details go to the "Is it philosophy?" piece.

## 9. Placement: page name and draft outline (options, not decisions)

**Page name.**
- (a) **"Philosophy in China"**, in parallel with "Philosophy in India" and "Philosophy in the Islamic world". Word test: "philosophy" is not the tradition's word, so the intro must say so, as the India page does. **Caution:** "philosophy in China" (在中國的哲學) is Jin Yuelin's own name for the approach he worried about, philosophy "discovered in China" by a Western measure. The name can be turned to advantage by opening the intro on Jin's question. Left unexplained, it would read as taking a side. Proposed.
- (b) **"China: the learning of the Way"**. This uses the tradition's own words, *xue* and *dao*. But *Daoxue* is the Song movement's name in particular, and the *Daodejing* sets *xue* against *dao*. So the title would privilege one stage.
- (c) **"Chinese philosophy"**. This is the very term Jin and Zheng put in question, and it breaks the series pattern. Not recommended.

**Intro (words and name).** No classical word matches "philosophy". *Zhexue* is Nishi's coinage via Japanese. The tradition's own words are *xue* (learning) and *dao* (the Way), with *jia*/*zhuzi* as library categories and *bian* for disputation. Feng: "Neo-Confucianism" is a Western coinage for *Daoxue*. Then Jin's question, with Hu Shi/Feng as the founding answers. Then one line on the legitimacy debate, pointing to its piece. "Who shall adjudicate?" stays on `zhuangzi`.

| Stage | What it is | Its point | How it is done |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confucius | learning (*xue*); "to allow that you do not know it" | learning "for oneself"; the *junzi* reachable, the sage beyond | learning with thinking; "a transmitter and not a maker"; one thread |
| The Mohists | disputation (*bian*): distinguishing this and not-this | settle benefit and harm, order and disorder | three standards (*biao*/*fa*); disputation has a winner: "fitting" |

Ordering: the stages above are chronological, and the masters argued with one another, as on the India page. ## 11. Discard list (do not place)

- SEP *Mohism*: "the world's earliest form of consequentialism", "initiated philosophical argumentation and debate in China", "like Socrates". These are priority and comparison claims; reception, not headline.
- Needham's "process philosophy … organismic" Zhu Xi, and comparisons with Plato, Aristotle and Aquinas (SEP Zhu Xi). Reception; the Needham one invites an "anticipated Whitehead" framing.
- Hu Shi's reading of *gewu zhizhi* as "hypothesis and verification by evidence" (SEP Zhu Xi). Reception; use only if the modern stage wants Hu Shi's method.
- Giles's interpolated commentary ("Upon God …", "one with God").
- Legge's "renovate the people": this follows Zhu Xi's emendation 親→新. A textual detail; don't stage it.
- Liu Xin's derivation of each school from an office of state. A detail.
- The Bodhidharma transmission story as history.
- Wang's bamboo story beyond one line, and its exact day counts.
- Mohist canons' logic beyond A74/B35 (excluded middle, kinds, etc.).
