# Metaphilosophy, the Western line against three questions: research note

Purpose: to source an overview (cultural and historical) of how the Western line answered the three general metaphilosophical questions the IEP names: (1) What is philosophy? (2) What is, or should be, its point? (3) How should one do it? The note covers the three twentieth-century streams as the IEP sorts them, and the earlier eras on questions 2 and 3, and it records what the site's person pages already carry. This is a working note, not a site page. Nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked against a source I opened, named by key (Sources at the end). **[C as IEP]** = the IEP's own wording, or a primary quoted by the IEP, checked in the IEP text but not in the primary. **[U]** = unverified (memory, a search summary, or a secondary I could not open). Quotations are given as found in the named edition or translation; renderings marked "my rendering" are mine.

Where a question gets no sourced answer for an era or current, it is marked **none**. No symmetry is forced.

---

## 0. The IEP's own frame

- The article is N. Joll, "Metaphilosophy", IEP, first published 17/11/2010, last revised 01/08/2017 [C, S1]. It lives at `https://iep.utm.edu/con-meta/`; the `/metaphil/` address returns "Not Found" [C].
- The three questions: "three very general metaphilosophical questions are (1) What is philosophy? (2) What is, or what should be, the point of philosophy? (3) How should one do philosophy?" They come after, and are drawn from, the article's sketch of pre-twentieth-century metaphilosophy ("As the foregoing sketch begins to suggest") [C, S1 §1.b].
- The sort: the article uses "(1) Analytic philosophy; (2) Continental philosophy; (3) pragmatism, neopragmatism, and post-Analytic philosophy", "solely for organizational purposes", and says the account "is organized by chronology as much as by theme". It flags the later Wittgenstein's placing as "particularly moot", notes that some deny analytic philosophy, and some continental philosophy, "any substantial existence", and leaves out "traditionalist philosophy", feminism and environmental philosophy [C, S1 §1.d].
- Its pre-1900 sketch is European only: "In the case of the sketch, 'Western' means European" [C, S1 §1].
- One slip: the sketch cites Kant's three questions as "A805 / B33". The questions stand at AA III 522 [C, S11], which is A805/B833 by the standard concordance [U]. "B33" is a typo for B833.

## 1. The three streams against the three questions

Headlines only. Each current gets one line per question, in the IEP's wording unless a primary is named.

### 1a. Analytic

**Pioneers (Russell, early Wittgenstein, Moore).**
- Q1. They shared the view that "all sound philosophy should begin with an analysis of propositions" (Russell 1900) [C as IEP]. Russell: logic "was the essence of philosophy" [C as IEP]. His 1914 Lowell lecture II is titled "Logic as the Essence of Philosophy" [C, S20]. Wittgenstein: "Alle Philosophie ist ‚Sprachkritik'" (4.0031) [C, S17]. 4.111: philosophy "is not one of the natural sciences", the word "must mean something which stands above or below, but not beside the natural sciences" (Ogden) [C, S17]. 4.112: "Die Philosophie ist keine Lehre, sondern eine Tätigkeit": "not a theory but an activity" (Ogden), "not a body of doctrine but an activity" (Pears–McGuinness) [C, S17]. Moore: philosophy should give "a general description of the whole Universe" [C as IEP].
- Q2. Tractatus 4.112: "Der Zweck der Philosophie ist die logische Klärung der Gedanken": "The object of philosophy is the logical clarification of thoughts" (Ogden), "Philosophy aims at…" (P–McG) [C, S17]. The IEP adds that for Wittgenstein "there are some things that philosophy is to leave well enough alone", and that Russell tended to regard ethical and political activity "as unphilosophical" [C as IEP].
- Q3. Analysis: Russell's two stages, "transformative" then "decompositional" [C as IEP]. Tractatus 6.53: "The right method of philosophy would be this: To say nothing except what can be said, i.e. the propositions of natural science … and then always, when someone else wished to say something metaphysical, to demonstrate to him that he had given no meaning to certain signs in his propositions" (Ogden) [C, S17]. Moore uses "normal language rather than logic" [C as IEP].

**Logical positivism.**
- Q1–Q3 in one passage, the Vienna Circle manifesto as the IEP quotes it: "The scientific world-conception serves life, and life receives it. The task of philosophical work lies in […] clarification of problems and assertions, not in the propounding of special 'philosophical' pronouncements. The method of this clarification is that of logical analysis" [C as IEP].
- Carnap, *The Logical Syntax of Language* (English 1937), Foreword: "Philosophy is to be replaced by the logic of science—that is to say, by the logical analysis of the concepts and sentences of the sciences, for the logic of science is nothing other than the logical syntax of the language of science" [C, S19]. §72 is titled "Philosophy Replaced by the Logic of Science" [C, S19]. One clause of nuance: §72 says the replacement thesis "cannot be established here and will not be assumed", and that "Neurath is definitely opposed to the continued use of the expressions 'philosophy', 'scientific philosophy'…" [C, S19].
- Q2, further: the verifiability principle's "destructive task" was "to eliminate metaphysics". "Unified science" also meant that "the sciences should collaborate in order to help solve social problems" (the Left Vienna Circle, Neurath) [C as IEP].

**Ordinary language philosophy and the later Wittgenstein.**
- Q1. The later Wittgenstein "retained his earlier view that philosophy was a critique of language". In the IEP abstract, philosophy is "a kind of therapy for what normally passes for philosophy" [C as IEP].
- Q2. PI §133: "the clarity that we are aiming at is indeed complete clarity. But this simply means that the philosophical problems should completely disappear. The real discovery is the one that makes me capable of stopping doing philosophy when I want to.—The one that gives philosophy peace" (Anscombe) [C, S18]. The IEP: "he wanted to end the worship of science" [C as IEP].
- Q3. PI §109: "We must do away with all explanation, and description alone must take its place … The problems are solved, not by giving new information, but by arranging what we have always known. Philosophy is a battle against the bewitchment of our intelligence by means of language" [C, S18]. §127: "The work of the philosopher consists in assembling reminders for a particular purpose" [C, S18]. §133: "There is not a philosophical method, though there are indeed methods, like different therapies" [C, S18]. Austin: ordinary language "is the first word" but not "the last word" (Austin 1979: 185) [C as IEP].

**The three revivals (1950s–1970s).**
- Normative philosophy. Q2: the practical ethicists "insisted that philosophy should treat 'real problems'" (Beauchamp) [C as IEP]. Q3: Rawls's reflective equilibrium, moving "back and forth between the initial judgments and the principles", and justice "political not metaphysical" [C as IEP].
- History of philosophy. Q3: Charles Taylor, "'Philosophy and the history of philosophy are one'; 'we cannot do the first without also doing the second'" [C as IEP]. The view is "associated with Hegel" [C as IEP].
- Metaphysics. Q1/Q3: Strawson's descriptive metaphysics "is content to describe the actual structure of our thought about the world", whereas revisionary metaphysics aims "to produce a better structure" [C as IEP]. Quine: "Ontological questions […] are on a par with questions of natural science"; "it is only within science itself, and not in some prior philosophy, that reality is to be identified and described". The philosopher's job is to translate the best science into "canonical notation" [C as IEP]. Kripke's "main appeal is to the intuitions of philosophers" [C as IEP].

**Naturalism and experimentalism.**
- Q1. Metaphilosophical naturalism "asserts a strong continuity between philosophy and science"; its problems are "tractable through the methods of the empirical sciences" [C as IEP].
- Q3. "Empirical philosophers enlist science…; experimental philosophers … themselves do science", and test the intuitions that armchair philosophy relies on [C as IEP].
- Q2. none stated as such.

### 1b. Pragmatist, neopragmatist, post-analytic

**Classical pragmatism (Peirce, James, Dewey).**
- Q1. "each of them held that philosophy is not fundamentally different to other inquiries"; "Experimental science is the exemplar of inquiry" [C as IEP]. James (1907), Lecture I: "The history of philosophy is to a great extent that of a certain clash of human temperaments" [C, S21].
- Q2. "pragmatism was a humanism. Its purpose was to serve humanity" [C as IEP]. James, Lecture II: "The whole function of philosophy ought to be to find out what definite difference it will make to you and me, at definite instants of our life, if this world-formula or that world-formula be the true one" [C, S21]. Dewey, *Reconstruction in Philosophy* (1920), ch. I: "the task of future philosophy is to clarify men's ideas as to the social and moral strifes of their own day. Its aim is to become so far as is humanly possible an organ for dealing with these conflicts" [C, S22]. Dewey, "The Need for a Recovery of Philosophy" (in *Creative Intelligence*, 1917): "Philosophy recovers itself when it ceases to be a device for dealing with the problems of philosophers and becomes a method, cultivated by philosophers, for dealing with the problems of men" [C, S23].
- Q3. The pragmatic maxim [C as IEP]. James, Lecture II: "The pragmatic method is primarily a method of settling metaphysical disputes that otherwise might be interminable", and "THE ATTITUDE OF LOOKING AWAY FROM FIRST THINGS, PRINCIPLES, 'CATEGORIES,' SUPPOSED NECESSITIES; AND OF LOOKING TOWARDS LAST THINGS, FRUITS, CONSEQUENCES, FACTS" (capitals his) [C, S21]. Dewey, quoting Peirce: "the 'laboratory habit of mind' extended into every area where inquiry may fruitfully be carried on" [C as IEP].

**Rorty.**
- Q1. Rorty rejects the "epistemological" metaphilosophy, which "presents philosophy as 'a tribunal of pure reason, upholding or denying the claims of the rest of culture'" (*Mirror*, 1980: 4). He takes up Sellars's definition, "an attempt to see how things, in the broadest possible sense of the term, hang together" [C as IEP].
- Q2. The philosopher is "not a 'cultural overseer'" but "an 'informed dilettante' and a 'Socratic intermediary'" (1980: 317). The aim is "edification", "finding new, better, more interesting, more fruitful ways of speaking". In politics he speaks of "putting politics first and tailoring a philosophy to suit" (1991b: 178) and of "cultural politics" [C as IEP].
- Q3. The hermeneutic back-and-forth: "we play back and forth between guesses about how to characterize particular statements … and guesses about the point of the whole situation, until gradually we feel at ease with what was hitherto strange" (1980: 319) [C as IEP].

**Post-analytic philosophy.**
- Q1. "a broad construal of the philosophical enterprise" [C as IEP abstract].
- Q2. It rejects "a certain kind of narrow professionalism", and holds "that Analytic philosophy needs to reconnect itself with the rest of culture" [C as IEP].
- Q3. It attempts "less to solve and more to dissolve or even discard philosophical problems" [C as IEP].

### 1c. Continental

**Husserl.**
- Q1. "Philosophy as Rigorous Science" (*Logos* I, 1910–11) opens: "From its earliest beginnings philosophy has claimed to be rigorous science" (Lauer, p. 71) [C, S24; the *Logos* reference as given in Lauer's introduction]. At the close: "Philosophy, however, is essentially a science of true beginnings, or origins" (p. 146) [C, S24]. In the IEP, phenomenology "describes phenomena", things "in the manner in which they appear" [C as IEP].
- Q2. The same opening: philosophy claims to be the science that "renders possible from an ethico-religious point of view a life regulated by pure rational norms" (p. 71) [C, S24]. In the *Crisis*, the denial of transcendental subjectivity "decapitates philosophy", and objectivism helps cause "a 'crisis of European humanity'" [C as IEP]. Phenomenology is "intrinsically ethical" [C as IEP, citing D. Smith].
- Q3. The epochē brackets "the natural attitude". The "principle of all principles" (*Ideas* §24) [C as IEP]. PRS, at the close: "The impulse to research must proceed not from philosophies but from things and from the problems connected with them" (p. 146) [C, S24].

**Existential phenomenology, hermeneutics, existentialism.**
- Q3. The successors' "considerable, and indeed meta­philosophical, differences" with Husserl concern method. Sartre holds that consciousness's inseparability from its objects "ruins Husserl's method of epoche". Merleau-Ponty: "The most important lesson of the reduction is the impossibility of a complete reduction". Heidegger calls for "a destruction … in which the traditional concepts … are deconstructed down to the … sources from which they were drawn". Gadamer: "The very idea of a definitive interpretation [of anything] seems to be intrinsically contradictory" [all C as IEP].
- Q2. "for the existentialist philosophy must be practical", though no one "can legislate values for another". The later Sartre: "Every philosophy is practical … [Every philosophy is] a social and political weapon" [C as IEP].
- Q1. none beyond "the philosophical primacy of description of experience" [C as IEP].

**Critical theory (Horkheimer, Adorno).**
- Q2. "According to Critical Theory, the point of philosophy is that it can contribute to a critical and emancipatory social theory" [C as IEP]. Horkheimer, "Traditionelle und kritische Theorie" (*Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung* 6, 1937): critical conduct, "auf diese Emanzipation gerichtet", has "die Veränderung des Ganzen zum Ziele" (p. 263; my rendering: "directed at this emancipation", it aims at "the transformation of the whole") [C, S25]. "An der Existenz des kritischen Verhaltens … hängt heute die Zukunft der Humanität" (p. 292; my rendering: "On the existence of the critical attitude … the future of humanity depends today") [C, S25].
- Q1. "Critical Theory conceives itself as philosophy's inheritor" [C as IEP].
- Q3. It is broad, interdisciplinary, and reads parts of philosophies as "unknowing reflections of social conditions" [C as IEP].

**Habermas.**
- Q1. "postmetaphysical thinking" rejects "a normative privilege for philosophy" and the idea that philosophy can "make claims about the world as a whole" [C as IEP].
- Q2. Morality and ethics are matters "for the discourse between citizens" (1992a: 158), not for the philosopher [C as IEP].
- Q3. The "communicative turn": human apprehension is "at root both linguistic and intersubjective" [C as IEP].

**The later Heidegger.**
- Q1. His metaphilosophy "holds that: philosophy is metaphysics; metaphysics involves a fundamental mistake; metaphysics is complicit in modernity's ills; metaphysics is entering into its end; and 'thinking' should replace metaphysics/philosophy" [C as IEP].
- Q3. "thinking" (*Denken*) is "a kind of thoughtful questioning", with "releasement" (Eckhart) and a poetic register: "All philosophical thinking" is "in itself poetic" [C as IEP].
- Q2. Thinking promises "a new ground and a new foundation upon which we can stand and endure in the world of technology without being imperiled by it" (*Discourse on Thinking*, 1966: 55) [C as IEP].

**Derrida.**
- Q3. Deconstruction is a "textual 'operation'". Derrida "holds that deconstruction is no method". It runs from a "faithful" and "interior" reading, to showing that the text undermines its own metaphysical oppositions, to a "productive moment" [C as IEP]. "Letter to a Japanese Friend" (1983): "Deconstruction is not a method and cannot be transformed into one" [C in a course excerpt, S26; the published translation not opened].
- Q2. To loosen the grip of "the metaphysics of presence" [C as IEP abstract]. From the 1980s: "deconstruction is justice" [C as IEP].
- Q1. "in philosophy the nature of philosophy is always and everywhere at issue" (IEP's gloss of Derrida 1995: 411) [C as IEP].

## 2. Earlier eras on questions 2 and 3

### Ancient

- **Socrates (in Plato).** Q2: "daily to discourse about virtue, and of those other things about which you hear me examining myself and others, is the greatest good of man, and … the unexamined life is not worth living" (*Apology* 38a, Jowett) [C, S2]. Greek: "ἐμαυτὸν καὶ ἄλλους ἐξετάζοντος, ὁ δὲ ἀνεξέταστος βίος οὐ βιωτὸς ἀνθρώπῳ" [C, S2]. 29d–30a: the citizens should care for "wisdom and truth and the greatest improvement of the soul" [C, S2]. Q3: "I proceed to interrogate and examine and cross-examine him" (29e) [C, S2]. The IEP calls it "his famous 'Socratic method'" [C as IEP].
- **Plato, dialectic.** Q3 (and Q1): "dialectic, and dialectic alone, goes directly to the first principle and is the only science which does away with hypotheses in order to make her ground secure", and it "uses as handmaids and helpers in the work of conversion" the mathematical sciences (*Republic* VII 533c–d, Jowett) [C, S3]. Dialectic is "the coping-stone of the sciences" (534e) [C, S3]. "the comprehensive mind is always the dialectical" (537c) [C, S3]. Q2: the IEP cites *Republic* 473c–d, that humanity prospers only "when philosophers are kings or kings' philosophers" [C as IEP].
- **Aristotle, starting from *endoxa*.** Q3: "We must, as in all other cases, set the observed facts before us and, after first discussing the difficulties, go on to prove, if possible, the truth of all the common opinions … or, failing this, of the greater number and the most authoritative" (*NE* VII.1, 1145b2–7, Ross) [C, S4]. Greek: "τιθέντας τὰ φαινόμενα καὶ πρῶτον διαπορήσαντας … τὰ ἔνδοξα" [C, S4]. *Topics* I.1: *endoxa* are opinions "accepted by every one or by the majority or by the philosophers"; I.2: for the philosophical sciences, "the ability to raise searching difficulties on both sides of a subject" helps, and dialectic "is a process of criticism wherein lies the path to the principles of all inquiries" (Pickard-Cambridge) [C, S5]. Q2: philosophy "is the least necessary but thereby the most divine of sciences", while ethics "had the aim of making men good" [C as IEP].
- **Hellenistic schools.** Q2: philosophy revealed "(1) what was valuable and what was not, and (2) how one could achieve the former and protect oneself against longing for the latter"; Cicero, "to study philosophy is to prepare oneself for death"; Plotinus, "Philosophy is the supremely precious" [all C as IEP].
- **Hadot.** Q2/Q3 are already carried by the site's meanings page: philosophy as "a method for training people to live and to look at the world in a new way". Philo's list of exercises (research, investigation, reading, listening, attention, self-mastery, indifference to indifferent things), as Hadot gives it, is [U]: the archive.org copies of *Philosophy as a Way of Life* are restricted.

### Medieval

- **Q2 (Aquinas, ST I q.1).** a.1: "It was necessary for man's salvation that there should be a knowledge revealed by God besides philosophical science built up by human reason" [C, S6]. a.8 ad 2: sacred doctrine "makes use even of human reason, not, indeed, to prove faith … but to make clear other things"; "since therefore grace does not destroy nature but perfects it, natural reason should minister to faith"; it uses the philosophers' authority "as extrinsic and probable arguments" [C, S6]. The handmaid passage (a.5) is already on the site.
- **Q3 (the school method).** "*lectio*: a teacher would read an authoritative text followed by a commentary"; then *meditatio*; then *quaestio*; "Eventually the discussion of *quaestiones* became a method of inquiry apart from the *lectio* … *Disputationes* were arranged to resolve controversial *quaestiones*"; the *quaestio* method was "initially used especially when two authoritative texts seemed to contradict one another" [C that Wikipedia says so, citing van Asselt 2011, S7]. The *Summa*'s own article form shows it: Objections, "On the contrary", "I answer that", Replies [C, S6]. Abelard's *Sic et Non* prologue ("dubitando ad inquisitionem venimus…") is [U]. Commentary on Aristotle as the professor's career is already on the site (Hadot).
- **Q1.** Already on the site (Clement, Peter Damian, Aquinas a.5).

### Early modern

- **Bacon, *Novum Organum*** (1620 [U]; Devey's edition). Q2: "the real and legitimate goal of the sciences is the endowment of human life with new inventions and riches" (I.81) [C, S8]. Q3: "There are and can exist but two ways of investigating and discovering truth": one "hurries on rapidly from the senses and particulars to the most general axioms", the other ascends "continually and gradually … which is the true but unattempted way" (I.19) [C, S8]. The empirics are like ants and the dogmatists like spiders; "The bee, a mean between both … The true labor of philosophy resembles hers" (I.95) [C, S8]. Word test: Bacon's "philosophy" here is natural philosophy and the sciences.
- **Descartes.** Q3: the four rules, *Discourse* Part II (Veitch). The first is "never to accept anything for true which I did not clearly know to be such"; the second, to divide each difficulty "into as many parts as possible"; the third, to proceed from "the simplest and easiest to know" by steps "to the knowledge of the more complex"; the last, "to make enumerations so complete, and reviews so general, that I might be assured that nothing was omitted" [C, S9]. Q2: the IEP's rendering of the *Principles* preface (CSM, Descartes 1988: 179) carries purposes the site's Veitch quote cuts off: wisdom as "a perfect knowledge of all things that mankind is capable of knowing, both for the conduct of life and for the preservation of health and the discovery of all manner of skills" [C as IEP].
- **Locke.** Q3: "if, in this historical, plain method, I can give any account of the ways whereby our understandings come to attain those notions of things we have" (*Essay*, Introduction, §2, "Design") [C, S10]. Q2: the under-labourer is already on the site. The IEP adds the love of truth (IV.xix), whose "unerring mark" is "The not entertaining any Proposition with greater assurance than the Proofs it is built upon will warrant" [C as IEP].
- **Hume.** Q2: "One considerable advantage that arises from Philosophy, consists in the sovereign antidote which it affords to superstition and false religion" ("Of Suicide") [C as IEP].
- **Kant.** For page wording, the *Critique* is quoted from the Akademie text (AA III = B edition); A/B numbers are by concordance [U]; the English is Meiklejohn [C, S11].
  - Q1/Q2, the *Weltbegriff*: "Bis dahin ist aber der Begriff von Philosophie nur ein Schulbegriff … Es giebt aber noch einen Weltbegriff (conceptus cosmicus) … In dieser Absicht ist Philosophie die Wissenschaft von der Beziehung aller Erkenntniß auf die wesentlichen Zwecke der menschlichen Vernunft (teleologia rationis humanae), und der Philosoph ist nicht ein Vernunftkünstler, sondern der Gesetzgeber der menschlichen Vernunft" (AA III 542; A838–39/B866–67 [U]) [C, S11]. Meiklejohn: "scholastic conception", "cosmical conception", "the science of the relation of all cognition to the ultimate and essential aims of human reason", the philosopher "not merely an artist … but a lawgiver, legislating for human reason". His footnote: a cosmical conception is "one in which all men necessarily take an interest" [C, S11]. Word test: *Weltbegriff* is Kant's term; "cosmical", and in other translations "worldly" or "cosmopolitan", is the translators'.
  - Q3: "Man kann nur philosophiren lernen": we can "only learn to philosophize" (AA III 542) [C, S11]. The critique is "ein Tractat von der Methode, nicht ein System der Wissenschaft selbst" (AA III 15, B preface; Bxxii [U]) [C, S11].
  - The questions: *Critique*: "Was kann ich wissen? Was soll ich thun? Was darf ich hoffen?" (AA III 522; A805/B833 [U]) [C, S11]. *Logik* (Jäsche, 1800 [U]): "Das Feld der Philosophie in dieser weltbürgerlichen Bedeutung" comes down to four questions, the fourth "Was ist der Mensch?"; metaphysics, morals, religion and anthropology answer them, "Im Grunde könnte man aber alles dieses zur Anthropologie rechnen" (AA IX 25) [C, S12]. On the *Weltbegriff*, philosophy is "eine Lehre der Weisheit", and "Philosophiren läßt sich aber nur durch Übung und selbsteigenen Gebrauch der Vernunft lernen" (AA IX 24–25) [C, S12]. Word test: the *Logik* was edited by Jäsche from Kant's lecture materials [U]; a page should say "the *Logic* edited by Jäsche (1800)".

### Nineteenth century

- **Hegel.** Q2: "To apprehend what is is the task of philosophy, because what is is reason … philosophy also is its time apprehended in thoughts"; "Philosophy cannot teach the state what it should be"; "To recognise reason as the rose in the cross of the present … implies reconciliation with reality. This reconciliation philosophy grants…" (*Philosophy of Right*, Preface, Dyde 1896) [C, S13]. The IEP's gloss: the point of philosophy "is to enable people to recognize the embodiment of their ideals in their social and political lives and thereby to be at home in the world" [C as IEP]. Q3: "Logical doctrine has three sides: [a] the Abstract side, or that of understanding; [b] the Dialectical, or that of negative reason; [c] the Speculative, or that of positive reason … stages or 'moments' in every logical entity" (*Encyclopaedia Logic* §79); in the dialectical stage "these finite characterisations or formulae supersede themselves, and pass into their opposites" (§81) [C, S14; translator Wallace U].
- **Marx.** Q2: the eleventh thesis is already on the site.
- **Comte** (Martineau's "freely translated and condensed" English [C, S15 title page]). Q1: knowledge passes through "three different theoretical conditions"; the mind "employs in its progress three methods of philosophizing … Hence arise three philosophies, or general systems of conceptions on the aggregate of phenomena" (vol. I, p. 2) [C, S15]. Q2/Q3: "Let us have a new class of students … whose business it shall be to take the respective sciences as they are, determine the spirit of each, ascertain their relations and mutual connection, and reduce their respective principles to the smallest number of general principles"; this means "constituting one more speciality from the study of scientific generalities" [C, S15]. The French wording is [U].
- **Neo-Kantians.** The Marburg "fact of science" is already on the site. Windelband, "Was ist Philosophie? (Über Begriff und Geschichte der Philosophie)", the opening essay of *Präludien* (1884 edition) [C, S16; first published 1882, U]. Q1 and Q3 in one sentence: philosophy in the systematic sense is "nichts Anderes … als die kritische Wissenschaft von den allgemeingiltigen Werthen. Die Wissenschaft von den allgemeingiltigen Werthen: das bezeichnet die Gegenstände; die kritische Wissenschaft: das bezeichnet die Methode der Philosophie" (pp. 28–29) [C, S16]. My rendering: "the critical science of universally valid values"; the values name its objects, "critical" its method. It is also a nineteenth-century instance of the question used as a title.
- **Dilthey.** Q1: the worldview typology. Q3: *Verstehen* against *Erklären* for the human sciences. Both are already on his person page. No primary on philosophy itself was checked here (*Das Wesen der Philosophie*, 1907 [U]).

## 3. Word tests

- *Lehre* (4.112): "theory" (Ogden) and "body of doctrine" (P–McG) are the translators' words; *Tätigkeit* is "activity" in both [C, S17].
- "Therapy": the IEP's "a kind of therapy" is its own characterisation. Wittgenstein's sentence is "methods, like different therapies" (PI §133) [C, S18].
- "Logic of science" is Carnap's (*Wissenschaftslogik*, German U). "Elimination" (1932) is the translator's, as the site already notes.
- *Endoxa*: "common opinions" (Ross), "opinions generally accepted" (Pickard-Cambridge). *Phainomena*: "observed facts" (Ross) [C, S4, S5].
- "Elenchus" is the scholars' noun (Vlastos's "The Socratic Elenchus", 1983 [U]). In *Apology* 38a Socrates' word is ἐξετάζειν, "examining" [C, S2].
- "Handmaids" at *Republic* 533d is Jowett's for συνερίθοις καὶ συμπεριαγωγοῖς, "fellow-workers and helpers in turning [the soul] round" (my rendering) [C Greek, S3]. It should not be read as an earlier occurrence of the *ancilla* formula.
- *Weltbegriff*: see Kant above.
- Horkheimer's term is *Theorie*. The essay opens "Das Problem, was Theorie sei" [C, S25]. Philosophy as such is addressed in Marcuse's "Philosophie und kritische Theorie" (ZfS 6.3, 1937) [U].
- Comte's English is Martineau's condensation; any wording a page states should be checked in the *Cours*.

## 5. Draft outline of the overview's Western sections

Each section line gives what it says on Q1 / Q2 / Q3.

1. **The word.** Q1: *philosophia*, the Heraclides story as reception, Hegel's "love of knowledge"; Japan's *tetsugaku*; the naming dispute (site page as is). Q2: none. Q3: none.
2. **A way of life.** Q1: Hadot, a choice of life before a discourse; the Stoic three parts. Q2: the examined life (*Apology* 38a); the Hellenistic schools (IEP); Aristotle's "most divine" science. Q3: Socratic examining; Platonic dialectic ("coping-stone"); Aristotle's *endoxa* and *aporiai*; Hadot's spiritual exercises.
3. **The servant of theology.** Q1: the handmaid (Clement, Peter Damian, Aquinas a.5). Q2: natural reason "should minister to faith", "to make clear other things" (a.8). Q3: *lectio*, *quaestio*, *disputatio*; commentary on Aristotle (Hadot); the article form.
4. **The whole system and the under-labourer.** Q1: Descartes's tree; Locke's under-labourer. Q2: Descartes's three uses (conduct of life, health, skills); Bacon's "endowment of human life"; Hume's "antidote". Q3: Bacon's ascent and the bee; Descartes's four rules; Locke's "historical, plain method".
   - Option A: **Kant** closes this section as a third self-image: the critique as "a treatise on method", the *Weltbegriff* (philosophy relating all knowledge to the essential ends of reason), the four questions. Option B: Kant opens the hinge. Option C: Kant gets his own short section between 4 and 5.
5. **The nineteenth-century hinge.** Q1: Hegel, philosophy as science and "its time apprehended in thoughts"; his rendering of the name as "love of knowledge", with a pointer to the end-of-philosophy piece (moved here from the Greek and Latin page's intro); Comte's three philosophies; Windelband's "critical science of universally valid values"; Dilthey's worldviews; "scientist" (1833–34) and the identity crisis (site page as is). Q2: Hegel's reconciliation; Marx's eleventh thesis; Comte's specialists in generalities; Ortega's "at the service of physics" (as is). Q3: Hegel's dialectic (*Enc.* §79–81); the neo-Kantians' method (Marburg's fact of science; Windelband's "critical"); Dilthey's *Verstehen*.
   - Option: Husserl's PRS (1911), "from its earliest beginnings philosophy has claimed to be rigorous science", as the bridge sentence into the streams.
6. **The three streams.**
   - 6a. **Analytic.** Q1: analysis of propositions; "critique of language"; "not a body of doctrine but an activity"; continuity with science (naturalism). Q2: "logical clarification of thoughts"; "serves life" (Vienna Circle); "complete clarity", "peace"; "real problems" (practical ethics). Q3: logical analysis, then the logic of science; ordinary language as "the first word"; "assembling reminders", "methods, like different therapies"; reflective equilibrium; descriptive metaphysics; canonical notation; experiment.
   - 6b. **Pragmatist and post-analytic.** Q1: not different in kind from other inquiry; a "clash of human temperaments" (James); not "a tribunal of pure reason" (Rorty). Q2: "what definite difference it will make to you and me"; "an organ for dealing with these conflicts"; "the problems of men"; edification; "putting politics first". Q3: the pragmatic maxim and method; the "laboratory habit of mind"; the hermeneutic back-and-forth; dissolving rather than solving.
   - 6c. **Continental.** (Carry over from the hub, where it stood until this page exists: Heidegger, "Was ist das – die Philosophie?", lecture at Cerisy-la-Salle, 1955, published 1956; it confined itself to the Greek origin of philosophy, starting from "The Greek word philosophia indicates the direction".) Q1: rigorous science of origins (Husserl); philosophy as metaphysics, to be replaced by "thinking" (later Heidegger); postmetaphysical thinking (Habermas); critical theory as philosophy's inheritor. Q2: a life under rational norms and the "crisis of European humanity"; authenticity and engagement; emancipation, "the future of humanity"; "deconstruction is justice". Q3: epochē and "to the things themselves"; destruction and the hermeneutic circle; interdisciplinary critique; "not a method", a "textual 'operation'".
7. **The IEP's sorting** (close). Q1–Q3 are not answered here. The section reports the IEP's frame: three streams "solely for organizational purposes", the moot placings, what it leaves out, and the alternative sorts (by aim, model, language, metaphysics) already on the hub.

Placement options, not decisions:
- (i) One overview page replacing `the-meanings.md`, sections 1–7 as above. It is long; the streams alone are about half of it.
- (ii) An overview hub (sections 1–5 and 7) plus one piece, "The three streams" (section 6), following the hub-plus-pieces split for dense subjects.
- (iii) As (ii), with the hub's existing "Ways of sorting" reduced to a pointer, so that the streams are not told twice. Some reduction is needed under (i) and (ii) as well.
