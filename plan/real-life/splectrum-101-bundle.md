## 5. Ideas and material not yet ready

### Candidate images for the principles [C]

One everyday image to carry each principle:

- **P0 — being implies language.** To be something is to stand apart from what's around you, and the edge where you meet the rest is where engagement starts. A house: the walls make it a house; the door and windows are how it deals with the street.
- **P1 — language is relational.** What you can see depends on what you're connected to. A fisherman and a tourist look at the same sea; one sees weather coming, the other a view.
- **P2 — the medium of experience.** Glasses you can't take off — not a filter over the "real" world, but the only way there is a world for you at all. The statue and the blind belong here.
- **P3 — sharing.** Two friends arguing whether a shirt is blue or green, until they agree on "teal". Agreement grows out of comparing; that's where shared reality and knowledge come from.
- **P4 — equal standing.** A grandmother's dialect, a chemist's formulas, a child's playground rules: each works in its own world, and none is "the real language" in principle, however different their reach.
- **P5 — the web.** Languages borrow, mix and breed new ones — kitchen words travelling between countries, slang becoming dictionary words. The richness lies in the connections.

### Material in the existing 101 page

The current single page (reworked 2026-09-07, about 2,200 words) holds the imagery to distribute over the nodes:

- **The one line** — meaning already attached through every sense; no objective reality at the point of experience; the statue and the blind (the rejected reconstruction picture); reality engaging with you in your language — it speaks what you understand; the game and its how-tos (taxi driver, policeman, husband, mother, friend) as the everyday face of language.
- **Everything has its language** — the blackbird working a lawn, the dog reading a street, roots in soil, the sunflower; the river and its bed, the magnet and iron filings (structured engagement without being alive — guard the word "life"); the question "what do all these languages have in common?"
- **Cognition, common face** — a process, not a thing; no outside view; acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience — nothing about brains, words or humans; speaking back: every player changes the game it plays (blackbird and lawn, river and bed, you and the room).
- **Never alone** — experience private, but you can tell; telling as speaking back at a larger scale; convergence into common understanding and knowledge; handing on — mother tongue, manners, faith — "that it came through others makes it no less yours"; the common grows at the table.
- **The own voice** — the reference point moves from the medium to the subject; the three values stated as what matters, with their tension.
- **The two realms** — realisation (learning, naming, expressing — the wine you can now tell apart, the doctor naming what you have, telling what happened until it makes sense); coexistence (values tied to the roles you take on; a workable balance settling through common understanding, never for good).
- **The say you have** — your reality is disclosed through the language you offer it, and that language can change: grow it, rework it, change what feeds it. Not positive-thinking magic; real say over what reaches you, what you make of it, what you give back. The three values in motion.

- **[A]** The original natural philosophy was the study of the content of the voices. SPLectrum's modern natural philosophy is not the study of all voices' content, but of what underpins it.
- **[A]** Philosophy as a specialised discipline should not reside in an ivory tower: a comprehensive study of all knowledge cannot afford to be blinkered. Ivory tower means detached, not academic (Rovelli: in academia, not in an ivory tower).
- **[C]** The line through history: natural philosophy studied the content; the specialised disciplines split off and took the content with them; the modern version does not reclaim it but takes up what the split left unstudied — what underpins every voice. The reference library listens to the content; the philosophy studies what underpins it.
- **[C]** What underpins every voice is the seed's ground (how a being and its language come about together, knowledge in relation, experience and sharing, how voices stand to one another), so the definition can anchor in the principles rather than add a layer above them.

- **[A]** Why language at the centre (2026-10-14): meaning, concepts and language — their dynamic and how they evolve — are understood in parts, not as a whole. This adds to the importance of putting language at the centre of reality. Our knowledge and understanding is held back by this. Our minds function on a complexity of language that is still mostly above our comprehension.
- **[A]** Embodiment is the place to talk about it. The way we think — the totality of language active within us — is shaped not only by conscious and unconscious (abstract) thought, but by the language spoken by our senses, internal and external. Research has findings on the aspects; what is missing is how these languages mix. A finding is not enough: that hungry judges give more severe sentences says nothing of how the languages combine to produce that outcome. The case has to be made from research showing the partial nature of the understanding.
- **[C]** The gap as a consequence of the split: the specialised disciplines each took a part (interoception, grounded concepts, the cognitive unconscious, the sensorimotor side of language, conceptual development), and nobody kept the whole. Integrating frameworks exist as proposals under test (predictive processing — Friston, Clark), not as settled understanding; meaning has no shared theory across disciplines; how concepts evolve is studied piecemeal. This is the concrete case for the modern natural philosophy stance: not the content of each field, but what underpins them together.
- **[C]** The hungry-judges study (Danziger, Levav and Avnaim-Pesso, PNAS 2011) is itself contested — critics point to case ordering and scheduling rather than hunger. On the page it either needs that caveat or can make the point twice over: without an account of how the languages mix, one cannot even tell what produced the outcome.
- **[C]** The warmth studies (Jules, 2026-10-14) — physical warmth and social warmth: Williams and Bargh (*Science*, 2008: holding a warm cup, a stranger judged "warmer"); IJzerman and Semin (*Psychological Science*, 2009: a warm room, feeling socially closer); Zhong and Leonardelli (2008: social exclusion, the room estimated colder). Embodied metaphor in action — the language of the skin mixing with the language of judgement. Also a casualty of the replication crisis (the Williams–Bargh effects failed several large replications). Same double edge as the hungry judges: the effects are unstable precisely because the mixing is not understood. Details from memory — to check in the research round.
- **[C]** Why the experiments are hard — two causes to keep apart so the page does not overclaim: (1) method (small samples, flexible analysis, publication bias — the effect may simply not exist; says nothing about understanding); (2) missing theory (hidden moderators: without an account of how the languages mix, nobody knows which conditions to hold steady; a mature science says when an effect should and should not occur). Psychology's own diagnosis of (2): the "theory crisis" (Muthukrishna and Henrich, *Nature Human Behaviour* 2019; Eronen and Bringmann 2021 — from memory, to check). A further, more careful point: lab experiments isolate one factor, and if thinking is the mixing of many languages, isolation removes what is being studied.
- **[A]** The hundred-year wait in basal cognition (Jules, 2026-10-14) as the case in point. Material already on the site (the basal cognition subject page): Jennings's *Stentor roeselii* repertoire (1906), dismissed for fifty years on a replication that used the wrong species and the wrong stimulus, vindicated in 2019 (carmine failed, microbeads worked) — 113 years on; Gelber's *Paramecium* conditioning explained away; results sitting orphaned in their own disciplines until Lyon's biogenic inversion gave them a frame and the field assembled (2018–2021), "the frame arriving last".
- **[C]** What it adds: the mirror image of the warmth studies. There, unstable effects may be false; here, a true effect was buried by a failed replication. Both for the same reason: no frame saying which conditions matter. And it shows the parts-not-whole state directly — sound results without a home, an anthropogenic paradigm that could only grade them as curiosities. Link the basal cognition subject page from the philosophy page, not the post (site → blog links are not allowed).
- **[A]** Fierce disagreement and ongoing controversy are often facilitated by poor comprehension (Jules, 2026-10-14). Example: the mountain of publications on free will and consciousness.
- **[C]** How to make it hold: (1) the comprehension meant is of the subject matter, not of the people disputing — the point is not that the disputants are slow; (2) other causes of volume to acknowledge: academic incentives, a live frontier (controversy can also mean progress under way); (3) SPLectrum's own angle — much of the dispute is languages talking past each other (the same word, "free", "conscious", held in different vocabularies), which is the adversarial habit the homepage answers with *where does it make sense?* Possible markers, from memory, to check: the PhilPapers survey split on free will (compatibilism the majority, libertarianism and no-free-will persisting); dozens of rival theories of consciousness, and adversarial collaborations (IIT against global workspace) that did not settle the question. ## 7. The single page as it stood (last reworked 2026-09-07)

The full text of the 101 before it was replaced by the bundle landing (2026-10-13) — the quarry for the nodes. Headings demoted to keep this document's outline.

*SPLectrum in plain words — what it is, as it stands today. A diagram will follow later; for now, the story.*

#### The one line

**SPLectrum in four words: reality disclosed in language.**

Start with something you can check right now. Look around, listen, touch something. Whatever reaches you — through your eyes, your ears, your skin, your tongue — arrives *already meaning something*. Things have names. Things stand in relationships — the cup is on the table, the voice is behind you, the milk has turned. You never receive a raw world and then work out what it is; by the time anything reaches you, the meaning is already attached.

We are not blind people touching a statue, trying to piece together what it really looks like. That picture — poor input, patient reconstruction, an objective object waiting at the end — is exactly what everyday experience is *not* like. There is no objective reality at the point of experience. Reality engages with you in your language: it speaks what you understand.

And the engagement has rules. Think of it as a game. To play, you need the pieces *and* the moves — the concepts and the rules together — and that package is what SPLectrum calls language. Much more than speech: your life is full of these games and their how-tos — how to be a taxi driver, a policeman, a husband, a mother, a friend. Each one a language: things it lets you see, moves it lets you make. That is the "language" in *reality disclosed in language*.

#### A world full of games

And none of this is a human specialty. Watch a blackbird working a lawn: it reads the ground, cocks its head at things that mean something to it and ignores everything else, acts, adjusts. A dog reads a street the way you read a room. Plants play too, on their own clock — roots negotiating soil, a sunflower tracking the day. Each of them is in structured engagement with what's around it: things that matter to it, moves it can make, rules the engagement follows. Each is playing its game of life.

Push further, and even things that are not alive engage by rules. A river negotiates its bed; a magnet and iron filings play out a strict little game on the tabletop. Nothing there is alive, and nothing here says otherwise — but the engagement is structured all the way down. Everything meets what surrounds it in *its* terms: its own concepts, its own rules. In SPLectrum's broad sense of the word — everything has its language.

A world full of games, then, from riverbed to lawn to taxi cab. And now the question SPLectrum actually starts from — not *which game is the right one*, or the richest, or the truest. The question is: **what do all these languages have in common?** That common ground — what holds for the experience itself, before anything is said about any particular reality — is what SPLectrum wants to talk about before anything else. First the common; only then the unpacking of specific realities, ours included.

#### The coin

That question splits what this site has to say into two, and it's worth being upfront about the split, because it is the most honest thing about SPLectrum. Some of what there is to say holds for *every* game on that list — blackbird, river, taxi driver alike. It follows from the sheer fact of existing and engaging, and it takes no sides: it can't tell you which game is truest, any more than the rules of grammar can tell you what to say. That is one face. And some of what there is to say is SPLectrum's *own* take — its account of what the experience of being in existence is like, and what matters in it. That enters the world as one voice among many, yours to disagree with. That is the other face. Two faces, one coin — and this page is built like the coin: the common first, the own voice after, and you'll always know which side you're reading.

#### The common: a process, lived from within

So, the common. The first thing to get right about it is that it is not a *thing* at all. Nothing described so far is a model you could lay out on a table and inspect from outside — a diagram of how experience works, drawn from nowhere in particular. There is no such outside; that was the point of the statue. What all the games have in common is a *process*, and you are not looking at it — you are in it, playing, right now. It was running before you could talk. It does not pause while you read about it. Whatever we say about the common, we say from inside the game, mid-play.

That running process has a name: cognition. SPLectrum defines it simply — *acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience* — and the definition is chosen with care, for what it leaves out as much as what it says. Nothing about brains. Nothing about thinking in words. Nothing that reserves it for humans. The blackbird acquiring the lawn, the root negotiating the soil, you making sense of this sentence: the same kind of process, run at very different scales, each complete in its own game. Making sense is not something done to experience afterwards — it *is* the experiencing, ongoing, everywhere there is a game being played.

And because it is a process, it runs in both directions. Reality speaks what you understand — but you speak back. Playing is never just listening: the blackbird doesn't only read the lawn, it changes it, worm by worm; the river carves the very bed that then steers it; you don't just read a room, you walk into it, and the room adjusts around you. Every player changes the game it plays. What you take in and what you give back are two sides of the same ongoing exchange — absorbing and emitting, one engagement. Keep that in your pocket: much later on this page it becomes something more personal. For now, the point is simply that nothing in any of these games is a spectator.

#### Never alone

Now bring in the others. What you experience is yours alone — nobody else sits in your seat, nobody hears this morning's conversation with your ears or carries your particular history into it. That privacy is not a wall, though, because you can *tell* someone. And telling is speaking back at a larger scale: you put your private experience into shared words, someone else does the same, and between you the words get compared, stretched, corrected — until something remarkable happens: the understanding stops being yours or theirs and becomes *common*. That is the quiet machinery under every conversation that ever ended with "yes — that's it, that's what I meant."

When many people converge like this — same words, same meanings, tested against each other's experience over and over — the shared understanding steadies into what we call knowledge. And it doesn't stay put: it gets handed on. Children are born straight into the middle of it. Nobody starts from scratch; you were given your mother tongue, your table manners, what counts as polite, what counts as obvious, which days matter and what happens on them. This handing-on goes all the way up to the things people hold deepest — religious faith among them. Whatever you believe, it reached you the human way: through parents, teachers, books, a community that held it before you and held it out to you. That it came through others makes it no less yours — that is how *anything* comes to be ours at all. There is no version of you that made sense of the world alone.

And the exchange never runs one way — that was the point of speaking back. You absorb what the common holds, you build your own from it, and what you make feeds back in: a phrase your family now uses because you coined it, a fix your colleagues copied, a way of seeing that rubbed off on a friend. Every generation starts a level up from the one before, because every generation adds. The common is not a monument you visit; it's a game kept alive by everyone playing it — and it grows at the table.

#### Changing sides

Here the page changes sides, and says so. Everything up to now has been the first face of the coin: what holds for every game there is, with no side taken. From here on, SPLectrum speaks with its own voice — its account of what the experience of being in existence is like, and what matters in it. One voice among many; everything from here is yours to disagree with.

What actually changes in the crossing is the reference point. The first face was about the *medium*: the games, the languages, the exchange running between every player and its world. The medium was central, and you appeared in the story as one player among all the others. On this face, the *subject* stands central: you, the player yourself — the one who experiences, makes sense, speaks back. Same world, same games; the point of reference has moved.

And with the subject central, SPLectrum's own voice starts from three core values of the subject — belonging, privacy, creativity — supported by the seed principles, and stated as what matters. *Belonging*: you are never a subject alone — family, communities, a time; their language is yours, and the shared is not a cage to escape but the ground under your feet, worth tending. *Privacy*: your sense-making is yours alone — the inner workshop where a person happens, deserving protection not because it hides something but because nothing else is more your own. *Creativity*: you add to the games something only you can add — the difference each subject feeds into the shared, where everything new comes from. The three go together, and they tension each other: the shared pulls one way, the private another, and creativity lives exactly in that pull.

#### The two realms

From the values, SPLectrum builds its picture of the experience itself, in two realms. The first realm is you and reality — SPLectrum calls it *realisation*. It has three sides, and you know all of them from inside. How you learn (epistemology): private experience worked into clearer language and steadied by sharing — the wine you couldn't tell apart last year and can now, the birds that were all just "birds" until you learned their names. How things get named (ontology): the settled units of meaning — the relief when a doctor finally names what you have, and the world reorganises around the word. How meaning gets expressed (aesthetics): not art-for-galleries but the everyday craft of putting meaning into language — telling what happened until it makes sense, finding the words that finally fit. Learning, naming, expressing: one activity, three sides, and the realm's name carries its double movement — coming to see, and making real.

The second realm is you and the others — *coexistence*. Other subjects are not scenery; they play their own games and make their own moves. What you owe and value toward them (ethics) lives inside the games you share: family, work, friendship, community — a spread of values tied to the roles you take on, not one rule for everything. And how everyone lives together (politics) is the ongoing play of all those games side by side, where a workable balance settles through common understanding — nobody decides it from above, and it never settles for good. Living together among free players.

#### The say you have

Now take out what you kept in your pocket. You speak back — that was true of every player in every game. Here is what it means for you in particular: your reality is disclosed through the language you offer it, and *that language can change*. You can grow it — learn, and things you literally could not see become visible. You can rework it — find better words for your own experience, and feel the experience itself change texture. You can change what feeds it — conversations sought out, communities joined and left. None of this is positive-thinking magic; the world doesn't change on command. But what reaches you, and what you make of it, and what you give back — there you have real say. That is the three values in motion: belonging chosen and rechosen, the workshop kept your own, and the difference only you can add.

#### In one breath

Reality disclosed in language — in every game, from riverbed to lawn to taxi cab, lived from within, never watched from outside. One face of the coin: what all the games have in common, where every voice stands equal. The other face: SPLectrum's own voice — three values, two realms — one account among many. And one thing held out to you personally: you have far more say over your reality than you may realise.
