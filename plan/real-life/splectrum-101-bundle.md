*Opened 2026-10-13. The plan for reworking SPLectrum 101 from one long page into a bundle: a coherent collection of compact pages, each giving the 101 on one aspect in everyday language. Tracks the structure, the state of every node, and the ideas not yet ready for publishing.*

## 1. The idea

- **[A]** SPLectrum 101 becomes a bundle — a coherent collection, not a single page. It gives the 101 on many aspects in a structured way, on pages that stay compact.
- **[A]** Each node is a paragraph or two, with links to more detail (the full pages in the strict sections) and/or to the next nodes in the structure.
- **[A]** The aim is explanations and examples in everyday language. Order of work: get the structure right first, then work the detail in plain language.
- **[A]** No aim for completeness. Only pages ready for publishing are added to the live structure; the rest is tracked here.
- **[C]** The bundle is a map, not a path: it mirrors how the project is built, so reading it also teaches the structure. Nodes may be reached from more than one place.

## 5. Ideas and material not yet ready

### Candidate images for the principles [C]

One everyday image to carry each principle:

- **P0 — being implies language.** To be something is to stand apart from what's around you, and the edge where you meet the rest is where engagement starts. A house: the walls make it a house; the door and windows are how it deals with the street.
- **P1 — language is relational.** What you can see depends on what you're connected to. A fisherman and a tourist look at the same sea; one sees weather coming, the other a view.
- **P2 — the medium of experience.** Glasses you can't take off — not a filter over the "real" world, but the only way there is a world for you at all. The statue and the blind belong here.
- **P3 — sharing.** Two friends arguing whether a shirt is blue or green, until they agree on "teal". Agreement grows out of comparing; that's where shared reality and knowledge come from.
- **P4 — equal standing.** A grandmother's dialect, a chemist's formulas, a child's playground rules: each works in its own world, and none is "the real language" in principle, however different their reach.
- **P5 — the web.** Languages borrow, mix and breed new ones — kitchen words travelling between countries, slang becoming dictionary words. The richness lies in the connections.

### Material in the existing 101 page

The current single page (reworked 2026-09-07, about 2,200 words) holds the imagery to distribute over the nodes:

- **The one line** — meaning already attached through every sense; no objective reality at the point of experience; the statue and the blind (the rejected reconstruction picture); reality engaging with you in your language — it speaks what you understand; the game and its how-tos (taxi driver, policeman, husband, mother, friend) as the everyday face of language.
- **Everything has its language** — the blackbird working a lawn, the dog reading a street, roots in soil, the sunflower; the river and its bed, the magnet and iron filings (structured engagement without being alive — guard the word "life"); the question "what do all these languages have in common?"
- **Cognition, common face** — a process, not a thing; no outside view; acquiring knowledge and understanding through experience — nothing about brains, words or humans; speaking back: every player changes the game it plays (blackbird and lawn, river and bed, you and the room).
- **Never alone** — experience private, but you can tell; telling as speaking back at a larger scale; convergence into common understanding and knowledge; handing on — mother tongue, manners, faith — "that it came through others makes it no less yours"; the common grows at the table.
- **The own voice** — the reference point moves from the medium to the subject; the three values stated as what matters, with their tension.
- **The two realms** — realisation (learning, naming, expressing — the wine you can now tell apart, the doctor naming what you have, telling what happened until it makes sense); coexistence (values tied to the roles you take on; a workable balance settling through common understanding, never for good).
- **The say you have** — your reality is disclosed through the language you offer it, and that language can change: grow it, rework it, change what feeds it. Not positive-thinking magic; real say over what reaches you, what you make of it, what you give back. The three values in motion.

### Open thread — engagement as offering

- **[A]** Engagement as the language (game) a subject *offers*, for reality to disclose into, with the rules of engagement included — the specifics of the disclosure shaped by what the medium has to offer. This is P0 territory.
- **[C]** Fit: the offering is the active side, disclosure the happening into the offered medium — the bidirectional built in at the root rather than added after. Disclosure had not been described as an engagement before on the site; the two-way, active character is new. Candidate homes: the say you have, or P0/P2. If it matures it also feeds the Seed vocabulary. Not yet settled for a beginner's page.

## 6. Carried from the September rework (2026-09-07)

Decisions made while reworking the single 101 page. They hold for the bundle unless revised.

### The opening picture [A]

- Lead with the essence: **reality disclosed in language.**
- Whenever we experience reality there is already meaning attached: things have names, there is a sense of relationships between things.
- It runs through the senses — sound, vision, touch, taste — and even there meaning is already attached: a language beyond speech.
- Our reality is always already coloured by the way we look at it. There is no objective reality at the point of experience.
- Counter-image: we are not blind people touching a statue, trying to imagine how it looks. Positive image: reality engages with us in our language — it speaks what we understand.
- The engagement has rules — a game, a language game. Language is the medium of exchange with everything needed to play: concepts and rules.
- Our lives are full of these how-tos — taxi driver, policeman, husband, mother — and that is the "language" in *reality disclosed in language*.

### The generalisation, early and simple [A]

- Take the games away from the human early, and simply: animals and plants are in structured engagement with their environment, all playing their game of life.
- Even dead objects play by rules. Guard: "game of life" must not imply they are alive — the structure of engagement generalises, aliveness does not.
- Then the pivot question: **what do all these languages have in common?** That is what SPLectrum talks about before anything else.
- Order principle: generalise first, then the common, then (only then) specifics.

### The two faces [A]

- The coin reformulated for the 101: (1) what we have in common by the fact that we exist; (2) what can be said about the experience of being in existence.
- The difference between the faces is the reference point: on the common face it is the language medium; on the own voice it is the subject. The own voice *starts* from the three core values of the subject, supported by the seed principles — not derived from common-face facts. (An earlier "each value is a fact from the first face, stood up for" approach was rejected.)
- The values belong to the own voice, as its core vocabulary.
- Privacy appears on both faces — as fact on the common face, as value on the own voice: the clearest case of the principles re-expressed in the own voice's vocabulary.
- The host stance — holding both faces, standing on neither — belongs at the hinge between them.
- The coin is introduced at the pivot, before the common is unpacked; the emphasis then goes to the dynamic — not a static model described from outside, but a process lived from within, flowing directly into cognition.

### The bidirectional [A]

- With the emphasis on process, we "speak back": we don't only absorb, we emit.
- Deliberately not called agency at the common level — the word is held for the own voice; at the common level it is the two-way character of playing: every player changes the game it plays.

### Imagery inventory

- **The statue and the blind** — the rejected picture: reconstruction from poor input toward an objective object. Useful as contrast.
- **Reality speaks what we understand** — engagement, not decoding.
- **The game and its how-to** — concepts and rules as one package; roles as the everyday face of language games.
- **The game of life, played by everything** — animals and plants in structured engagement; dead objects play by rules without being alive.
- **The pivot question** — what do all these languages have in common?
- **Lived from within, not described from outside** — the anti-static-model image leading into cognition.
- **Speaking back** — every player changes the game it plays: blackbird and lawn, river and bed, you and the room.
