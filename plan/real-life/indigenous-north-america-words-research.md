# Indigenous North America: words for knowledge, wisdom and thought — research note

Purpose: to find, with sources, a few words in North American Indigenous languages for knowledge, wisdom, thought or the one who knows, and a sourced sentence on the older record being oral and held nation by nation, for the opening paragraph of the metaphilosophy subject's Indigenous North America landing. Working note, not a site page; nothing here is a decision.

Marking: **[C]** = checked in a source I opened, named by its key in the Sources list. **[U]** = unverified (memory, a search-engine summary, or a source I could not open). Words are given in the source's own spelling; glosses are quoted as the source gives them.

## 1. Diné (Navajo)

- The Navajo Nation's Fundamental Law, *Diné Bi Beenahaz'áanii*, 1 N.N.C. §§ 201–206, enacted by Navajo Nation Council resolution CN-69-02 of 8 November 2002 [C, D1, D2]. § 201, *Diné Bi Beehaz'áanii Bitse Siléí*, "Declaration of the Foundation of Diné Law", is given in Diné bizaad with the Council's English [C, D1].
- The four terms, Diné text and the Council's English, as found [C, D1]:
  - "Nahasdzáán dóó yádiłhił nitsáhákees yił hadeidiilaa" — "Earth and universe embody thinking"
  - "Tó dóó dził diyinii nahat'á yił hadediilaa" — "Water and the sacred mountains embody planning"
  - "Niłch'i dóó nanse' ałtaas'éí iiná yił hadediilaa" — "Air and variegated vegetation embody life"
  - "Ko', adinídíín dóó ntł'iz náádahaniihjį' sihasin yił hadeidiilaa" — "Fire, light, and offering sites of variegated sacred stones embody wisdom"
  - "Nitsáhákees éí nahat'á bitsé silá. Iiná éí sihasin bitsé silá." — "Thinking is the foundation of planning. Life is the foundation of wisdom."
  - (The page encodes some characters imperfectly; the Diné lines above are as given on the second copy, D3, which matches the courts page where legible.)
- Word test: in the Council's English *sihasin* is "wisdom". Diné College's educational philosophy, built on the same four, reportedly renders *Siihasin* as "Assuring" and the four as Thinking, Planning, Living, Assuring [U — search summary; site unreachable]; a dissertation renders it "Achievement" [U]. The gloss of *sihasin* is a translator's choice and differs by source; if used, attribute the gloss to the Council's translation.
- Same law on knowledge, § 206 C: the values of Diné Common Law "must be used to harness and utilize the unlimited interwoven Diné knowledge, with our absorbed knowledge from other peoples" [C, D1]. § 204 F: children are to be "provided with education to absorb wisdom, self-knowledge, and knowledge" [C, D1]. A Diné statement, in law, of how its own knowledge relates to others'.
- The written law as a late step: the resolution's preamble says the Diné "have always been guided and protected by the immutable laws provided by the Diyin, the Diyin Diné é, Nahasdzáá and Yádilhil", that the Council "has not acknowledged and recognized such fundamental laws in the Navajo Nation Code", and that it "is greatly concerned that knowledge of these fundamental laws is fading, especially among the young people" (CN-69-02, whereas clauses 2–4) [C, D2, scanned resolution read as images].
- A commentary site calls § 201 "the only section that has its source in the Life Way verbally passed down" and the codification an attempt "to translate a fluid oral tradition into a rigid, Western-style statutory framework" [C, D3] — but D3 is a land-use commentary site, not the Nation's own; use only as a pointer.
- *Sa'ah Naagháí Bik'eh Hózhóón* (Diné College's name for its philosophy) and *hózhó*: not found in the § 201 text; the § 202 executive branch is *Hózhóójí Nahat'á* [C, D1]. The phrase itself and its gloss [U — Diné College unreachable].
- V. F. Cordova's dissertation, "The concept of monism in Navajo thought" (1992): not opened; no Diné words checked from it [U].

## 2. Lakota and Dakota

- *ksápa*, "wise, prudent", Lakota (citing Buechel's dictionary, EB:318a) and Dakota (citing Riggs, SRR:298a); Proto-Dakota \*ksápA [C, L1, Comparative Siouan Dictionary].
- Stephen Return Riggs, *A Dakota-English Dictionary*, ed. James Owen Dorsey (Contributions to North American Ethnology VII; Washington, 1890): "ksá-pa, adj. wise, prudent, having understanding"; "wó-ksa-pe, n. of ksapa; wisdom" [C, L2, OCR of the scan; hyphenation is the dictionary's syllable division]. Riggs was a missionary lexicographer, and the forms are Dakota (Santee); the Lakota form in a Lakota dictionary (Buechel 1970; Buechel and Manhart 2002; *New Lakota Dictionary* 2008) not opened [U — lending copies only].
- Oglala Lakota College (Pine Ridge; chartered by the Oglala Sioux Tribe 1971) names its library and archives *Woksape Tipi*; an IMLS grant record glosses its outreach project "Woksape Tipi Taniyohila Kici" as "House of Wisdom for Everyone" [C, L3, L4]. The college lists wisdom among "the humanistic values of respect, generosity, wisdom, fortitude, bravery and humility" [C, L3]; that its Lakota list gives *Wóksape* for wisdom [U — search summary of a third-party list].
- The college's central philosophy, *Wolakolkiciyapi*, "learning the Lakota ways of life in the community" [C, L3 "Who we are"; also NEH, L5].
- On orality, the college's statement "Lakota Perspective" (from its 2003 self-study): "The Lakota perspective is a world view which derives from the oral creation story of the Lakota people and is, therefore, a living, dynamic view handed down from generation to generation"; "No oral tradition can survive unless people know how to listen well and to share knowledge and wisdom" [C, L3].
- *wóableza* (understanding, reflection): not checked [U]. Iktomi as a figure of thought: no source opened frames it so [U]; not pursued.

## 3. Anishinaabe (Ojibwe)

All from the *Ojibwe People's Dictionary* (University of Minnesota; editor Nora Livesay, founder and linguistic editor John D. Nichols) [C, O1]:

- *gikendaasowin* (ni), "intelligence; knowledge"; derived noun of *gikendaaso* (vai), "s/he is knows, is smart, is intelligent, is educated" [sic, as on the page].
- *gikendan* (vti), "know it, know about it, find it out, found out about it, realize it"; word parts: /gik-/ "know"; /-end/ "act by thought on it; perceive it by thought; feel in the mind".
- *nibwaakaawin* (ni), "wisdom"; from *nibwaakaa* (vai), "s/he is wise, is intelligent".
- *mino-bimaadiziwin* (ni), "the good life"; example sentence: "Gidaa-wiisookawaag apane gichi-anishinaabeg. Gegoo imaa giga-onji-gikendaan ge-ani-izhi-mino-bimaadiziyan." — "You should often visit the elders. You will learn something there about leading a good life."
- Also: *debwewin* (ni), "truth"; *gikinoo'amaadiwin* (ni), "teaching, education".
- Word test: dictionary glosses, not a philosopher's terms; *gikendaasowin* is knowledge in general, not a word for philosophy. The dictionary is a collaboration with Ojibwe speakers (audio by named speakers); the editors are university linguists.

## 6. The record is oral, held nation by nation

- Checked statements that bear on it:
  - Lakota: the Oglala Lakota College statement above, the world view deriving "from the oral creation story of the Lakota people … handed down from generation to generation" [C, L3].
  - Diné: the Council in 2002 writing into the Code laws it says had guided the Diné "since time immemorial" and had not before been "acknowledged and recognized … in the Navajo Nation Code" [C, D2].
  - Ojibwe: the dictionary's example sends the learner to the elders for the good life [C, O1].
  - General: Shay Welch, *The Phenomenology of a Performative Knowledge System: Dancing with Native American Epistemology* (Palgrave Macmillan, 2019): story "operates through the oral tradition and relies on the sharing of pluralist individual experiences for knowledge construction" [C, as quoted by Lorraine Mayer in her review, APA Newsletter 19:2, 2020, A1].
- No source opened states in one sentence that the record is held "nation by nation". The phrase would be the page's own framing, backed by the pattern (each word above comes from one nation's institution or language). Either attribute through the examples, or find a direct statement (open check 3).

## Placement proposal (options, not decisions)

- **Opening paragraph.** The sibling landings open with the tradition's own words; this landing opens with the field's English names. Options: (a) put the paragraph below first and move the names paragraph second, matching Africa and Latin America; (b) keep the names first and insert the words as the second paragraph. Either way only [C] items, each attributed.
- **Choice of nations.** Diné, Lakota/Dakota and Ojibwe are the three with checked sources; three is the sibling pages' count. Haudenosaunee and Cherokee stay out until checked.
- **Backing.** Under the backing rule, a line page (e.g. a future "knowers" or "oral record" line) should carry these words fully before the landing states them; until then the landing sentence is self-sourced by attribution.
- **Person pages.** None proposed. Riggs, Nichols, Livesay, Welch, Mayer: external if named at all.

Proposed opening paragraph (all items [C]):

> The older record is oral, and each nation keeps its words in its own language. In the Navajo Nation's Fundamental Law, written into its code in 2002, the Holy People ordained *nitsáhákees*, thinking, *nahat'á*, planning, *iiná*, life, and *sihasin*, which the Council's English renders wisdom: "Thinking is the foundation of planning. Life is the foundation of wisdom" (*Nitsáhákees éí nahat'á bitsé silá. Iiná éí sihasin bitsé silá*). In Lakota and Dakota, wisdom is *wóksape*, from *ksápa*, "wise, prudent, having understanding" (Stephen Riggs's Dakota dictionary, 1890); Oglala Lakota College calls its library *Woksape Tipi*, the house of wisdom, and holds that "no oral tradition can survive unless people know how to listen well and to share knowledge and wisdom". In Ojibwe, knowledge is *gikendaasowin* and wisdom *nibwaakaawin*; the verb *gikendan*, "know it", carries a part the *Ojibwe People's Dictionary* glosses "act by thought on it; perceive it by thought; feel in the mind", and its example for *mino-bimaadiziwin*, "the good life", reads: "You should often visit the elders. You will learn something there about leading a good life."

Notes on the draft: the first sentence is the page's own framing (see §6); drop or attribute it if Jules prefers. "the house of wisdom" condenses the IMLS gloss "House of Wisdom for Everyone" of the longer project name. The Riggs gloss is the Dakota form; swap in a Lakota dictionary if open check 1 succeeds.
