## 1. Rovelli — meaningful information and agency

The full checked record of the meaning essay is in `plan/language/language-meta-subject.md` §2.1–2.5; the essentials are gathered here.

- **[C, checked at source]** "Meaning = Information + Evolution" (arXiv:1611.02420, 2016; contest version and book chapter "Meaning and Intentionality = Information + Evolution", FQXi 2017 / *Wandering Towards a Goal*, Springer, 2018). Relative information (correlation) is everywhere — "The world teams with correlated quantities" — so not sufficient for meaning; survival singles out the meaningful correlations: "surviving mechanisms survive by using correlations." "Intentionality is built into the definition"; the definition "does not directly refer to anything mental". Truth as "the effectiveness of the consequence of holding something for true". Recursive growth "up to contexts where there is a brain, language, society, norms". Physics as modal: "a science of how the world can be". The closing caveat: "we do not actually know the extent to which this structure is superimposed over the elementary texture of reality by ourselves … We are undoubtably limited parts of nature, and we are so even as understanders of this same nature." ("teams", "quite" for "quiet" and "undoubtably" are the paper's own spellings, confirmed in the contest version too.)
- **[A]** The closing paragraph is exactly SPLectrum's position on reality; P2 is the biggest link.
- **[C, checked via OpenAlex, 2026-10-11]** Uptake: about 30 citations across both versions (arXiv 17, book 14), modest and spread across the edges of several fields; nearly all onward influence runs through Kolchinsky and Wolpert (2018, 166 citations). No philosophy-of-mind or teleosemantics literature engages it directly. Notable citers: Jeffery, Pollack and Rovelli (2019); Barzegar, Margoni and Oriti on agency (2025); Milburn, Shrapnel and Evans on causal perspectivalism (2020); Murray, Marshall and Cronin's early assembly-theory paper (2018); a critique by Roman Krzanowski (arXiv, 2020; not yet read). Full grouping in the language document §2.2.
- **[C, abstract checked 2026-10-11; paper not yet read]** "Agency in Physics" (arXiv:2007.05300, 2020). Agency is "the breaking of an approximation under which dynamics appears closed"; structurally similar to memory, its time orientation traced to thermodynamic irreversibility; "Agency is therefore a physical mechanism that transforms low entropy into information. This may be the general mechanism at the source of the whole information on which biology builds." Companions: "Memory and Entropy" (2022); "How Oriented Causation Is Rooted into Thermodynamics" (*Philosophy of Physics*, 2023). Barzegar, Margoni and Oriti (2025) class Rovelli's as the *weak* characterisation of agency, Tononi's as strong, and place theirs between.
- **Site state.** The Rovelli person page has a draft paragraph on the meaning essay and a rewritten closer (uncommitted, reviewed). Open on it: the paper's spellings ([sic] or paraphrase); whether "Agency in Physics" joins the page once read. Its tight-affinity surface (P2) is not decided: extend `positioning/seed/semantic-information.md`, or a seed-ring piece on P2 with the essay as its spine. Huw Price (*Naturalism without Mirrors*, 2011; causal perspectivalism) — whether he earns a person page is a collaborative decision, still to raise.

## 3. Reality approached as meaningful information

- **[A]** It starts to become clear how we are going to approach "reality" in reality — and this article and the essays have helped. SPLectrum has language (in general) as the medium of experience of reality. Here they deal with an aspect of it: how mathematical language can express the named parts of reality. By being disciplined about "language as medium of description" — not saying which one — exploration of reality becomes exploration of how (complex) meaning works in language.
- **[A]** Language is not only linguistic. But it is all description in a sense — or probably better expressed as meaningful information.
- **[A]** The reason for using "description" earlier: when we talk about or discuss how other subjects interact amongst themselves, we create a description of what we think goes on (a model) — we have no direct experience. There is direct experience between the conversants, those holding the discussion.
- **[A]** And it would bring all languages on an equal footing. The language of the philosopher or scientist does not stand outside "the system". This is another version of "observer participation".
- **[A]** It's the observing in the way a philosopher (being part of a specific tribe) talks about a topic. A lot of philosophical discourse — and specially the argumentation techniques they employ — is done as if that discourse "is not a participant", stands outside.

- **[C]** The working formulation: exploring reality is exploring how meaningful information works — how complex meaning builds up, and how it is carried across forms. Words, mathematics, a receptor coupled to swimming are all forms of it; none is the base the others reduce to.
- **[C]** Two words, two positions. *Meaningful information* is what a subject holds — a relation, a correlation that matters to the subject holding it (P2; Rovelli's definition; relational quantum mechanics' relativity as structure). *Description* is the right word for what conversants build about other subjects' interactions: a model, held by the conversants, of what they think goes on there — with no direct experience of it. Direct experience runs between the conversants themselves. The model is itself meaningful information, but held by the describers, not by the described.
- **[C]** It sharpens the discourse point below: describing others is unavoidable and not the fault. The slip is forgetting that the description is a model held among the conversants — taking it for direct access to what goes on between the described, and the conversants for spectators rather than participants in their own exchange. It also matches the language document's formulation: we build the system description in our own language context to test it; we cannot take the bacterium's place.
- **[corrected]** Claude proposed "meaningful information" as the better word *instead of* "description", on the ground that description suggests a describer standing apart. The author: the describer does stand apart when discussing how other subjects interact — that is why "description" was used; it names a model without direct experience. Both words hold, for different positions: direct experience between the conversants, description of the others.
- **[C]** "Not saying which one" carries the weight: every form stands on equal terms, mathematics included. The physical is already inside, since what is explored is meaningful information all the way down — the bacterium's correlation as much as an equation.
- **[C]** "Complex" points to composition: Rovelli's recursive growth of meaningful information, "up to contexts where there is a brain, language, society, norms" (checked at source, 2026-10-07). Exploring reality is in part exploring how meaning composes and how it shifts between forms — the evolution core's concepts in motion.
- **[C]** On the equal footing: the account includes its own author. The philosopher's or scientist's language is one more form of meaningful information inside the system, held by a subject like any other — Rovelli's caveat says it of the physicist: "We are undoubtably limited parts of nature, and we are so even as understanders of this same nature" (checked at source). It is the positive face of the evolution core's critique of "the language assumed outside the system" (`plan/evolution-follow-on/evolution-follow-on.md`): there the move is diagnosed in others; here the approach is built so it cannot make it.
- **[C]** Read this way, the participant is the discourse: a tribe's way of talking about a topic — its vocabulary, its standards of argument, what it counts as a good move — is a form of meaningful information held in a community, inside the system it talks about. The argumentation techniques carry the stance of standing outside without stating it: arguments run as if their own terms were neutral ground rather than one tribe's language. Which techniques do this, and how, is a research question (candidates to examine, unchecked: the thought experiment judged by "our intuitions", conceptual analysis of what a word "really" means, the demand for necessary and sufficient conditions, the reductio aimed at a rival's terms).
- **[C]** The physics senses of participation stand beside it as analogues, not as the sense meant: in relational quantum mechanics the observer is one physical system among others; Wheeler's participatory universe has observation take part in bringing about what is observed (Séguin's contest essay works in that line — unchecked).
- **[corrected]** Claude took "observer participation" as a generalisation from observing to expressing — any holder of meaningful information as a participant — and reached for Wheeler as its nearest version. The author: the participation meant is the discourse of a specific tribe — the way philosophers talk about a topic, and the argumentation techniques that proceed as if the discourse stood outside.
- **[corrected]** Claude guarded the direction against a reading of "it's all just words" by appealing to SPLectrum's wide sense of language — treating language as linguistic first and stretching it down to the bacterium. The author: language is not only linguistic; there is nothing to stretch. What the medium carries is meaningful information, of which linguistic language is one form.

**The contest essays read this way — [C]**

The contest's question — "how can mindless mathematical laws give rise to aims and intention?" — takes the mathematical form as the base from which aims must be derived. Read through the direction above, it becomes a question of how aims are expressed across forms of meaningful information, and what each form can and cannot carry.

- **Rovelli** (checked at source) — meaning defined physically, physics as modal ("a science of how the world can be"), and the closing caveat that the modal structure may be superimposed by ourselves: the form noticing that it is a form.
- **Hoel** (unchecked, from recall of causal emergence) — a coarser description can carry more causal information than the microphysical one: the choice of level is not neutral; it changes what can be expressed.
- **Albantakis, Szangolies, Walker** (unchecked) — integrated information, self-reference, information flow: three formal forms asking the same question of one named part, goals.

All seventeen essays are held locally (see `sources/README.md`); reading them is the check for the unchecked items.

## 4. Purpose and the reversal

- **[C, checked at source in the contest version]** Rovelli: "The key of Darwin's discovery is that we can legitimately reverse the causal relation between the existence of the mechanism and its function. The fact that the mechanism exhibits a purpose —ultimately to maintain the organism alive and reproduce it— can be simply understood as an indirect consequence, not a cause, of its existence and its structure." Then: "What functions is there because it functions: hence it has survived. We do not need something external to the workings of nature to account for the appearance of function and purpose." He traces it to Empedocles and Aristotle's objection (*Physics* II.8, 198b35), and notes that the idea works once "shifted from the individual to the species".
- **[A]** Asked how much support there is for the statement.
- **[C, all unchecked]** Support within biology and the philosophy of biology: Darwin's own footnote in the Historical Sketch (from the third edition, 1861) quoting *Physics* II.8 — "We here see the principle of natural selection shadowed forth"; teleonomy (Pittendrigh, 1958; Mayr; Monod); the selected-effects theory of function (Wright, 1973; Millikan, 1984; Neander, 1991; Godfrey-Smith, 1994) and teleosemantics built on it; Dennett's free-floating rationales. Contested or qualified there: function without history (Cummins, 1975; organisational accounts — Mossio, Saborido and Moreno, 2009; the Swampman objection); exaptation and spandrels (Gould and Vrba, 1982; Gould and Lewontin, 1979); purpose as cause after all (Walsh, *Organisms, Agency, and Evolution*, 2015, and the agency strand of the extended-synthesis debate); Lennox, "Darwin was a teleologist" (1993); Kant's natural purposes; the classicists' doubts about reading Empedocles as a precursor.
- **[A]** You say strong support, but the loudest voice is about purpose — cause and effect, etc.
- **[corrected]** Claude reported "strong support" measured inside one tribe (biology and the philosophy of biology) as if it were the general picture — the very move of §3. Across discourse the picture reverses.
- **[C, unchecked]** The loudest voice: philosophy of action (Davidson, "Actions, Reasons, and Causes", 1963 — reasons are causes); history, economics, law, policy and management explaining change by agents' purposes; Kelemen's "promiscuous teleology" (children; and Kelemen, Rottman and Seston, 2013 — physical scientists under time pressure endorsing teleological explanations); biology's own working language ("in order to", "designed for"); design traditions. The reversal is established in one specialised discourse for one domain — organisms' traits; the boundary sits at the human.
- **[A]** In philosophy there is Hume.
- **[C, unchecked except where noted]** Hume three ways: (1) causation as constant conjunction and habit of expectation, the mind "spreading itself" on objects (on the Hume person page) — continued by Russell, "On the Notion of Cause" (1913), Huw Price's causal perspectivalism, and Rovelli on oriented causation; (2) the *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (1779): Philo's Epicurean hypothesis — matter in random motion, only stable arrangements persisting, order without design — the Empedocles idea before Darwin (absent from the Hume page); (3) reason as "the slave of the passions" — the Humean theory of motivation, the belief–desire model behind Davidson's reasons-as-causes. Hume on both sides: a possible "tension embodied in one thinker", like Quine.
- **[A]** Where the issue lies: a CAS has two components (broadly) — the landscape and the agents. Purpose and such lives at the agent level, but that is not where survival takes place (it is the species in Darwin). But the majority think in terms of the privileged individual / agent.
- **[C]** Reading: purpose at the agent level need not be denied; the slip is to put the selecting there too. Taking the agent as the privileged level makes its purposes look like the cause of what persists. Nuance held: in Darwin selection acts on individual variation while what persists and changes is the population or lineage; the level of selection is its own debate (gene, individual, group, species — Dawkins, Gould). The point holds whatever the answer: not at the agent's purpose — so "not at the agent level" rather than "at the species level".
- **[C, unchecked]** Field voices at the landscape level: Adam Ferguson (1767), "the result of human action, but not the execution of any human design"; Mandeville; Smith's invisible hand; Hayek's spontaneous order; Holland's complex adaptive systems (agents with internal models, patterns from interaction and environment). Evidence for the privileged-agent habit: the fundamental attribution error (Ross, 1977). Methodological individualism (Weber, Popper, Elster) and "great man" history as its defaults.
- Ties: the agency sketch (`plan/evolution-follow-on/agency-sketch.md`) — "direction in the landscape, not the agents", the headless chickens, the three-part central joint; and its 2026-10-05 spark (change routinely explained as caused by rational agents).

## 5. The privileged individual

- **[A]** We have it here for Darwinism. But there is free will, agency of a human, etc. A very large part of philosophy suffers from the privileged (human) individual syndrome.
- **[C, unchecked]** Where it shows: epistemology starting from the single mind (Descartes' cogito, other minds, first-person foundations); free will and moral responsibility (the individual as uncaused origin); social contract theory (individuals before society); philosophy of mind's first-person centre; rational choice; personal identity.
- **[C, unchecked]** The field's own counter-voices: Hume's bundle theory; Spinoza (free will as ignorance of causes); Hegel and Marx (the individual formed in social relations); Durkheim (social facts); Wittgenstein's private language argument; Berger and Luckmann (on the site); Buddhist *anattā*; the Confucian relational self; Ubuntu; feminist relational autonomy; the extended mind (Clark and Chalmers). Minority traditions, but present — so the alternative can be shown in the field's words rather than argued.
- **[C]** A distinction to hold: the position does not deny the individual — experience is individual, held by a subject, carried by common language (P2, P3). What goes is privilege: the individual as the level where explanation starts or where outcomes are settled.
- **[A]** It is a feature of the level of our cognition that we can shift the point of view away from us. A downside is the personification that then happens at that displaced point of view.
- **[C]** Reading: this may be the mechanism behind the privileged individual. Displacing the point of view is the capacity description depends on (§3) — modelling what goes on between others without being there. But the displaced point of view gets furnished as a person: an agent with purposes is set where the model needs a vantage. Hence "nature selects", "the gene wants", "the market decides", "evolution designs"; and at the limit the view from nowhere as an implicit observer — the God's-eye view personified. The Gaia reception (§6.7) is the same charge from the other side: "living" at the planetary level heard as a purposive agent.
- **[C, unchecked]** Field material: perspective-taking and theory of mind as a distinctive capacity (Tomasello on shared intentionality and the human ability to take another's perspective; on the site); Nagel, *The View from Nowhere* (1986) as the furthest displacement; personification and agency detection — Guthrie, *Faces in the Clouds* (1993), anthropomorphism as a default of perception; Barrett's hyperactive agency detection (2000s, cognitive science of religion); Dennett's intentional stance as the controlled use of it (a useful tool when known as a stance); Kelemen's promiscuous teleology (§4) as its developmental face.
- **[A]** You can only do it by layering those displaced-point-of-view languages on top of the "primary point of view" languages, and if the realisation of that layering disappears, then that is problematic.
- **[C]** Reading: the displaced point of view has no vocabulary of its own — it borrows from the primary one. The words available for what goes on "over there" are the words of the subject's own acting: want, try, choose, select, decide. That is why the displaced point of view comes out personified: personification is the layering showing through. Kept visible, the layering is a working tool; lost, the borrowed agent vocabulary reads as a finding about the world, and the displaced description passes for direct experience. The fault is the forgetting, not the layering — the same shape as the discourse that forgets it is a participant (§3).
- **[C, unchecked]** Field voices that put it in near the same terms: Nietzsche, "On Truth and Lies in a Nonmoral Sense" (1873) — truths as metaphors that have forgotten they are metaphors; Vaihinger, *The Philosophy of "As If"* (1911) — fictions useful while known as fictions, dogma once forgotten; Whitehead's fallacy of misplaced concreteness (*Science and the Modern World*, 1925) — the abstraction taken for the concrete; Lakoff and Johnson, *Metaphors We Live By* (1980) and *Philosophy in the Flesh* (1999) — abstract domains built on embodied, primary experience, with dead metaphors as the layering no longer noticed; Dennett's intentional stance — the layering kept visible by calling it a stance.
- **[C]** Kinship with §3: the privileged individual and the discourse standing outside may be one habit seen twice — the philosopher's position privileged as individual and as describer. A sibling of the evolution core's "the language assumed outside the system" (`plan/evolution-follow-on/evolution-follow-on.md`).
