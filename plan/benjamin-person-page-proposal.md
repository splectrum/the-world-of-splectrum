## Intro material

- Walter Bendix Schoenflies Benjamin, born 15 July 1892 in Berlin; died at Portbou, Catalonia, in late September 1940 (date contested — see *Uncertainties*).
- German-Jewish philosopher, literary and cultural critic, essayist and translator.
- Known for: the essay on the work of art in the age of its technological reproducibility (aura); the theses "On the Concept of History" (Angelus Novus, now-time, the critique of progress); the unfinished *Arcades Project*; "The Task of the Translator"; the theory of Ideas as constellations in the *Trauerspiel* book.
- Mostly received first as a literary critic; the philosophical basis of his work has been increasingly recognised over the past fifty years (SEP).

## Biography — facts and dates

**Formation**
- Eldest of three children in a prosperous, assimilated Jewish family in Berlin.
- At 13 he was sent to Haubinda, a progressive boarding school in Thuringia, where he came under the influence of the educational reformer Gustav Wyneken. He later wrote for Wyneken's journal *Der Anfang*.
- Studied at Freiburg and Berlin, attending lectures by Heinrich Rickert and Georg Simmel. He was active in the Youth Movement.
- In 1914 he broke publicly with Wyneken after Wyneken praised the war as an ethical experience for the young.
- From 1915, lifelong friendship with Gershom Scholem, which shaped his relation to Judaism and Kabbalah.
- 1919: doctorate *summa cum laude* at Bern, with the dissertation *The Concept of Art Criticism in German Romanticism*.

**The failed academic route**
- Wrote the *Habilitationsschrift* on the origin of the German mourning-play (*Ursprung des deutschen Trauerspiels*), largely on Capri in 1924.
- In 1925 he withdrew it from the University of Frankfurt, closing off an academic career; it had been rejected in both philosophy and literature.
- The book was published in 1928. In 1932–33 Adorno taught a Frankfurt seminar on it.

**Capri, Moscow, the Marxist turn**
- Met the Latvian Bolshevik theatre director Asja Lacis on Capri in 1924. They co-wrote "Naples" (1925), and *One-Way Street* (1928) is dedicated to her.
- Read Lukács's *History and Class Consciousness* on Capri. Visited Lacis in Moscow in winter 1926–27 (*Moscow Diary*).
- Lacis introduced him to Brecht in 1929, beginning a close friendship. Benjamin wrote on epic theatre, and in 1930–31 he was involved in the planned journal *Krise und Kritik* with Brecht, Bloch and Kracauer.
- Wrote and presented radio broadcasts, including for children, between the late 1920s and 1933.

**Exile (1933–1940)**
- Left Germany in 1933. Divided his exile between Paris, Ibiza, San Remo and Brecht's house near Svendborg.
- The Institute for Social Research (Horkheimer; Adorno as intermediary) provided a stipend and a place to publish in the *Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung*. This came with editorial intervention on key essays.
- His brother Georg was arrested in Germany and died in a concentration camp in 1942.
- Interned in a French camp for German nationals in autumn 1939, then released. Worked on the *Arcades Project* at the Bibliothèque Nationale.
- Before leaving Paris in 1940, he left the Arcades materials with Georges Bataille.

**Death**
- Without a French exit visa, he crossed the Pyrenees on foot with a small group guided by Lisa Fittko. Henny Gurland was also in the party.
- At Portbou the group was told they would be returned to France. That night he took a morphine overdose.
- The rest of the group was subsequently allowed to travel on.
- He was buried in Portbou; the grave's exact location is uncertain.

## Contributions

Listed roughly in order of writing. Items marked **[subject candidate]** have enough substance and reception of their own to warrant cross-linking to a subject page rather than being expanded on the person page.

1. **Early metacritique of Kant (1913–1918).** "Experience" (1913/14) and "On the Program of the Coming Philosophy" (1918) argue for widening Kant's concept of experience, which he saw as narrowed to the mathematical-mechanical model, by relating knowledge to language, after Hamann.

2. **Philosophy of language: "On Language as Such and on the Language of Man" (c. 1916, unpublished in his lifetime).**
   - Everything, animate or inanimate, communicates its mental being in language; human language is the language of naming.
   - The account is theological: the divine creative Word, Adamic naming, and a fall into the judging, abstract word and the multiplicity of languages.

3. **Romantic criticism and the Goethe essay.**
   - The dissertation reconstructs Early Romantic criticism (Schlegel, Novalis, grounded in Fichtean reflection) as the immanent completion of the work. It carries a critical afterword on its formalism.
   - "Goethe's *Elective Affinities*" (1924–25) distinguishes criticism, aimed at truth content, from commentary, aimed at material content. It introduces the "expressionless" as the caesura that breaks the semblance of a work.

4. **The *Trauerspiel* book (1928).**
   - The "Epistemo-Critical Prologue" sets out a theory of Ideas as constellations: arrangements of extremes, not concepts that subsume.
   - Origin (*Ursprung*) is distinguished from genesis (*Entstehung*): an eddy in the stream of becoming, including a work's fore- and after-history.
   - The book's substantive argument is a theory of baroque allegory against the Romantic valorisation of the symbol, with melancholy and "natural history". It engages Schmitt's theory of sovereignty.

5. **Translation: "The Task of the Translator" (written 1921, published 1923** as the preface to his translation of Baudelaire's *Tableaux parisiens*). **[subject candidate: translation]**
   - Translation does not exist to transmit information or serve the reader.
   - Translatability belongs to the work; translation belongs to the original's afterlife (*Fortleben*).
   - Languages differ in their way of meaning (*Art des Meinens*) while intending the same (*das Gemeinte*): *Brot* and *pain*.
   - Languages supplement one another towards "pure language" (*reine Sprache*).
   - Literal, interlinear translation is preferred over fluent rendering.
   - He was also a working translator: Baudelaire, and Proust with Franz Hessel.

6. **Political thought: "Critique of Violence" (1921)** and the fragment "Capitalism as Religion" (1921).
   - *Gewalt* covers force, power and authority as well as violence.
   - Law-making and law-preserving violence are grouped together as mythic violence, set against divine, law-destroying violence.
   - Sorel's proletarian general strike appears as a "pure means".
   - Capitalism is described as a cult without dogma that makes guilt/debt (*Schuld*) universal.
   - This material has since acquired canonical status in radical political theory; see *Reception*.

7. **Media, technology and the arts.** "Little History of Photography" (1931), "The Work of Art in the Age of Its Technological Reproducibility" (1935–39), "The Storyteller" (1936).
   - Aura is defined as the unique appearance of a distance, however close; its decline comes with technological reproducibility.
   - Cult value versus exhibition value; the optical unconscious; film as training perception for the apparatus.
   - The epilogue contrasts fascism's aestheticisation of politics with communism's politicisation of art.
   - Benjamin distinguishes a "first" technology (mastery of nature) from a "second" (interplay between nature and humanity).

8. **Mimesis.** "Doctrine of the Similar" and "On the Mimetic Faculty" (1933): language as the most complete archive of non-sensuous similarity.

9. **City writing and literary montage.** *One-Way Street* (1928); *Berlin Childhood around 1900* (1930s); city portraits (Naples, Moscow, Marseille); "Surrealism" (1929), with its idea of "profane illumination".

10. **The *Arcades Project* (Passagen-Werk, 1927–1940, unfinished).**
    - A philosophical "primal history" of nineteenth-century Paris, organised into 26 lettered convolutes, with exposés in 1935 and 1939.
    - Key figures and notions: the flâneur, collector, ragpicker; phantasmagoria, commodity and fashion; the dialectical image and "dialectics at a standstill".
    - Convolute N sets out the method: literary montage, and a historical materialism rid of the idea of progress.
    - The Baudelaire material spun off as "The Paris of the Second Empire in Baudelaire" (rejected by the Institute in 1938) and "Some Motifs in Baudelaire" (1940), on shock experience and the disintegration of aura. "Central Park" is a related set of fragments.

11. **Philosophy of history: "On the Concept of History" (1940).**
    - Against historicism ("the way it really was") and against the Social Democratic faith in progress.
    - Homogeneous, empty time is set against now-time (*Jetztzeit*); a "weak messianic power"; brushing history against the grain; the history of the victors.
    - The Angelus Novus reading of Klee's picture.
    - The final text is fragmentary and not intended for publication in this form.

12. **Literary studies.** Kafka (1934), Proust (1929), Karl Kraus (1931), Leskov ("The Storyteller"), Brecht (*Understanding Brecht*), "The Author as Producer" (1934).

## Key quotations

German originals are public domain and are given with the standard location. English equivalents in the Harvard *Selected Writings* (SW) should be cited by location only.

- On language: *"Es gibt kein Geschehen oder Ding weder in der belebten noch in der unbelebten Natur, das nicht in gewisser Weise an der Sprache teilhätte."* — "Über Sprache überhaupt und über die Sprache des Menschen" (GS II.1; SW 1, 62).
- On Ideas: *"Die Ideen verhalten sich zu den Dingen wie die Sternbilder zu den Sternen."* — *Ursprung des deutschen Trauerspiels*, Prologue (GS I.1).
- Opening of the translator essay: *"Keine Dichtung gilt dem Leser, kein Bild dem Beschauer, keine Symphonie der Hörerschaft."* — "Die Aufgabe des Übersetzers" (GS IV.1; SW 1, 253).
- On aura (definition): *"Ein sonderbares Gespinst aus Raum und Zeit: einmalige Erscheinung einer Ferne, so nah sie sein mag."* — "Kleine Geschichte der Photographie" (GS II.1; SW 2, 518). A near-identical formulation recurs in the Work of Art essay.
- On aura (decline): *"Was im Zeitalter der technischen Reproduzierbarkeit des Kunstwerks verkümmert, das ist seine Aura."* — "Das Kunstwerk im Zeitalter seiner technischen Reproduzierbarkeit" (GS I.2).
- On culture and barbarism: *"Es ist niemals ein Dokument der Kultur, ohne zugleich ein solches der Barbarei zu sein."* — "Über den Begriff der Geschichte", thesis VII (GS I.2; SW 4, 392).
- On method: *"Methode dieser Arbeit: literarische Montage. Ich habe nichts zu sagen. Nur zu zeigen."* — *Passagen-Werk*, Convolute N (N1a,8) (GS V.1; AP, 460).
- Adorno's objection to the Work of Art essay, letter of 18 March 1936 (*The Complete Correspondence*, 1999, 128). Paraphrase rather than quote: Adorno says the magical aura has been transferred too casually onto the autonomous work and assigned a counter-revolutionary function, and he suspects Brecht's influence.

## Reception — material to cover

Only the contested questions about Benjamin's own corpus belong here. Critical Theory as a tradition is **[subject candidate: Critical Theory / Frankfurt School]** and should be named, not developed.

- **The Scholem / Adorno / Brecht triangle.**
  - Scholem promoted a theological reading, Brecht's influence pulled towards a materialist one, and Adorno sought a mediation.
  - The long-running question of whether the late work is "theological" or "materialist" comes from these three friendships.
  - SEP (Osborne and Charles) argues that the question is badly posed if "theology" and "materialism" are taken in their received senses.
- **The estate disputes.**
  - Arendt, on one side, and Adorno and Scholem, on the other, disagreed over the literary estate and how to present him.
  - Adorno's two-volume *Schriften* (1955) emphasised the philosophical and city writings. Arendt's *Illuminations* (1968) presented him as a poetic thinker, a "pearl diver".
  - The disputes resurfaced in the late 1960s and again in the early 2020s.
- **The Institute and editorial intervention.**
  - Arendt claimed that the Brecht friendship endangered his funding and made him compliant with the Institute's edits.
  - Recent scholarship (Mulder 2015; Erazo-Heufelder 2017; Fleck 2023) partly corroborates this through the Institute's handling of funds.
- **Unity or fragments.** One view treats the corpus as brilliant, unsystematic fragments (Andrew Benjamin; Friedlander's description of that view). Another seeks an underlying coherence, either religious (Kohlenbach) or philosophical (Friedlander; Caygill's "philosophizing beyond philosophy").
- **Poststructuralist reception.**
  - Derrida: "Des Tours de Babel" on the translator essay; *Force of Law* (1990) on "Critique of Violence", together with his own "messianicity".
  - De Man: his 1983 lecture on "The Task of the Translator".
  - Scholars dispute how far Benjamin anticipates deconstruction (Weber versus Weigel).
- **The political-theology reception.**
  - Agamben (from 1970; *Homo Sacer*, *State of Exception*), following Taubes, sets up a Benjamin–Schmitt encounter.
  - Several scholars regard this as chronologically and textually weak (Kostko, Pomreneke, Weber, Hamacher).
- **Habermas** (1972) reads Benjamin's "pure means" of language as pointing towards a theory of communication. This reading has itself been criticised (Moran and Salzani).
- **Fields shaped by his work:** translation theory, film and media theory, cultural studies and the modern, historiography, the revival of Early German Romanticism, and more recently the philosophy of education (*Early Writings*, 2011; *Radio Benjamin*, 2014).

**Subject candidates, summarised**
- Translation (new).
- Critical Theory / Frankfurt School (new).
- Existing subjects the contributions touch: aesthetics in philosophy (aura, allegory, Romantic criticism); political philosophy's radical traditions (violence, capitalism as religion); hermeneutics (the critique of historicism); deconstruction (the Derrida and de Man readings).

## Uncertainties (as content)

- **Date of death.**
  - The official record at Portbou gives 26 September 1940.
  - Gurland's account, followed by SEP and others, gives 27 September.
  - Some popular sources give 25 September, which is the date of the crossing in some accounts.
- **Cause of death.**
  - Suicide by morphine overdose is the standard account.
  - Murder has been suggested, notably Stephen Schwartz (2001) alleging Stalinist agents, and the documentary *Who Killed Walter Benjamin* (2005). These remain unsubstantiated.
  - Questions have also been raised about which doctor attended him.
- **The briefcase.** Fittko reports Benjamin saying the manuscript he carried mattered more than he did. Its contents are unknown and it has never been found; speculation includes a finished Arcades text. This came to wider attention only around 1980.
- **The Work of Art essay exists in several versions.** There are three German versions (1935, 1936, 1939), and the French text published in 1936 (translated by Klossowski) was edited by the Institute. Which version counts as canonical varies by edition.
  - The English title also varies: "Mechanical" (Zohn, *Illuminations*) versus "Technological Reproducibility" (SW).
  - Aura's valence is contested: nostalgic loss, or welcome liberation.
- **"On the Concept of History."**
  - It was not written for publication and survives in several manuscript and typescript versions.
  - First printed in 1942 in an Institute hectograph memorial volume.
  - The title varies in English: "Theses on the Philosophy of History" (Zohn/Arendt) versus "On the Concept of History" (SW).
- **Scholarly accuracy.** Some scholars (Bullock; Menninghaus) question Benjamin's reading of Fichtean reflection in the dissertation.
- **Editions still in progress.** The *Kritische Gesamtausgabe* (Suhrkamp, 21 volumes from 2008) is due to be completed in 2027, so textual details may still shift.
