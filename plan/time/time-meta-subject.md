## 3. The angles — a first map

**[C, unchecked throughout — recalled in discussion, to be researched.]** Deliberately more than will survive.

**Physics.** Absolute time (Newton) against relational time (Leibniz); relativity — no universal now, the block universe; the thermodynamic arrow (Boltzmann, the past hypothesis); the problem of time in quantum gravity (time absent from the Wheeler–DeWitt equation; Barbour's timelessness; Rovelli and Connes's thermal time); time evolution as the dynamical rule.

**Philosophy of time.** McTaggart's A-series and B-series and his argument for the unreality of time; presentism, eternalism, the growing block; Augustine ("what then is time?", *Confessions* XI); Bergson's *durée* against clock time; Husserl's inner time-consciousness (retention, protention); Heidegger's temporality; Whitehead's process.

**Life and the earth.** Deep time (Hutton, Lyell); evolutionary time; developmental time; circadian and biological clocks; assembly theory's time as material — construction history as an object's size in time.

**Mind.** Time perception; memory and Tulving's "mental time travel"; the specious present (James).

**Society and culture.** Historical time and periodisation; social time (Durkheim, Elias); cyclical against linear time (the Indian kalpas, Greek cycles, the Christian line); tense and its absence in languages (the Hopi controversy — Whorf, Malotki).

**Engineering and economics.** Clocks and time standards; Lamport's logical time in distributed systems — no global clock, only an ordering of events; time preference and discounting; path dependence.
