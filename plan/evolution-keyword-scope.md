# Scoping: evolution as a keyword

Working document, opened 2026-09-13. Provisional throughout — deliberately not boxed in. The research comes first and may reshape everything below; this document exists to record where the thought stands and what is already on the ground, not to fix a plan.

## 1. The observation this starts from

**Not a claim, an observation about usage (Jules, 2026-09-13).** In ordinary language "evolution" already covers the living, the non-living and language alike: stellar evolution, the evolution of a species, of a language, of a city, of a design. Nobody argues for that range — it is how the word is used.

What is unusual is the **narrowing**. Biology took the word and gave it a technical definition — change in the heritable characteristics of populations across generations; in population-genetic terms, change in allele frequencies — and from inside that definition the other uses read as loose talk. Stellar evolution has no reproduction, no heredity, no differential survival; it is simply the word for a star's lifecycle.

So the position is not unifying scattered things under a new thesis. It is declining a specialisation and working at the generality the word already has, with the biological case as one instance rather than the definition.

The etymology cuts against the modern sense and is worth carrying: Latin *evolvere*, to unroll — an *ēvolūtiō* was the unrolling of a scroll, so the word originally implied that what unfolds was already written. Eighteenth-century biology used it exactly that way, for preformationism. Darwin largely avoided it, preferring "descent with modification"; it became the standard term afterwards and kept some of the teleological colouring it arrived with.

## 4. Homes the word has — first sketch, not a facet structure

Deliberately loose. The research decides what the facets are, and whether "facets by discipline" is even the right cut.

- **Biology** — the narrow technical sense; well backed.
- **Cosmology and stellar evolution** — change with no selection, reproduction or heredity at all. The clearest case of the word doing non-Darwinian work in a hard science. **Backing checked 2026-09-28: none** (no page mentions the phrase).
- **Chemical evolution and the origin of life** — where the biological machinery is being assembled and the definition does not yet apply to its own subject matter. **Backing checked 2026-09-28:** assembly theory, Walker, Cronin, Hazen carry the modern side; the phrase itself and its history (Calvin) appear nowhere.
- **Mineral evolution** — built as a subject; the word applied to a non-living, non-reproducing domain by geologists.
- **Assembly theory** — built; construction history and selection without the population-genetic apparatus.
- **Cultural evolution** — Boyd, Richerson, Henrich; a live dispute about how much of the biological apparatus transfers.
- **Language change / historical linguistics** — the comparative method, sound change, language families. One of the oldest uses of the word outside biology and possibly the most instructive, since descent-with-modification was borrowed *into* biology from philology as much as the other way round. **Backing checked 2026-09-28: none.** Saussure has a page (synchrony over diachrony, the turn *away* from historical linguistics); no Schleicher, Grimm, Bopp, Labov, Gray/Atkinson; no subject. The translation-theory bundle touches it only at the edges.
- **Technological, institutional and economic change** — evolutionary economics, the design literature. **Backing checked 2026-09-28: partial.** Veblen (the 1898 question), Schumpeter, Hayek and Hull (science as a selection process) have pages; the Darwinism bundle's *cultural-extensions* page has an evolutionary-economics section and a universal-Darwinism section. No Nelson & Winter, no Hodgson & Knudsen (generalised Darwinism), no Basalla (technology).
- **Evolutionary computation** — Holland built; genetic algorithms as the word applied to a deliberately engineered process.

## 6. Open, and staying open

- Is "evolution" one concept across these homes, or a family sharing a word? Biology's answer is that only its own case is evolution proper. The neutral material is what any other answer would have to meet — and the research may well find the question is not the interesting one.
- Does the doorway sort by discipline, or by something the material itself suggests? **Decided 2026-09-28 (Jules): by meaning, not domain.** The two senses of the word — unfolding and descent with modification — are the top cut; within descent, cases are graded by how much of what the word says they carry (Godfrey-Smith's space is the field's own instrument for that). The discipline a use lives in is where the meaning is found, not the sort key.
- Does the word's teleological inheritance deserve its own page, as the emergence bundle gave the definitional confusion its own pages?
- Where does the existing Darwinism bundle sit relative to a doorway — beneath it as the biology facet's backing, or does the doorway largely point at it?
- Naming: "evolution" is almost certainly right, being the keyword and the field's word both, but the territory-naming test should still be applied rather than assumed.

## 8. Research round 1 (2026-09-28) — the word, the criterion, the homes

Verified at source unless marked; sources at the end of the section. Nothing here is a plan.

**8.1 The word has two lineages, and the homes split along them.**

- *Unfolding.* Latin *evolvere*; eighteenth-century embryology used "evolution" for preformationist unfolding (Bonnet, Haller: the preformed germ unrolls). Darwin avoided the noun in the *Origin* of 1859 ("evolved" is its last word and only form); his first noun use is *Descent of Man* vol. 1 (1871), and the sixth *Origin* (1872) has about a dozen. **Corrected in round 2:** no statement by Darwin of his reasons was found; the preformation/Spencer explanation is the historians' inference, not his. Spencer used "evolution" from 1852 for a single law of change from homogeneity to heterogeneity across cosmos, life, mind and society — the fusion of both lineages into one cosmic principle, which is what the site's Spencer page already records.
- *Descent with modification.* Comparative philology had genealogical trees of languages before biology did: Schleicher's *Stammbaum* (1853/1860), and his 1863 open letter to Haeckel, *Die Darwinsche Theorie und die Sprachwissenschaft*, pointed out that linguistics already did what Darwin proposed, with real data where Darwin's diagram was hypothetical. Darwin returned the compliment in *Descent of Man* (1871): "Languages, like organic beings, can be classed in groups under groups… naturally according to descent." The borrowing ran both ways, as the scope guessed. Modern closure of the loop: Gray & Atkinson, *Nature* 2003, Bayesian phylogenetics applied to Indo-European.
- *Where each home sits.* **Stellar evolution** is the unfolding lineage surviving intact: Herschel compared stars to plants at growth stages; Lockyer (1880s–90s) built a stellar-evolution scheme on Secchi/Vogel spectral classes; Russell (1910s) gave stars a life cycle. No population, no heredity, no selection: a lifecycle, which is what the old word meant. **Chemical evolution** (Calvin, articles from the 1950s, book 1969) sits between: change toward life without yet having life's machinery. **Mineral evolution** (Hazen, built) is unfolding-with-history: diversification through coupled physical, chemical and biological stages, explicitly without reproduction. **Cultural, economic, technological, linguistic and scientific evolution** are all descent-lineage claims, and all argued in the field as *how far the Darwinian apparatus transfers*.

**8.2 The field has a criterion for the descent lineage, and it is graded, not binary.**

- Lewontin 1970, "The Units of Selection": evolution by natural selection occurs in any population with (1) phenotypic variation, (2) differential fitness, (3) heritability of fitness — and "any level of organization that can be grouped into a population of units has the potential to evolve." This is the generalisation the site's Lewontin page already carries. It is the ground for **universal Darwinism** (Dawkins 1983, the coinage, in Bendall ed. *Evolution from Molecules to Men*), Dennett's substrate-neutral algorithm and "universal acid" (1995), Campbell's blind-variation-and-selective-retention (1960) before both, and **generalised Darwinism** in economics (Hodgson & Knudsen, *Darwin's Conjecture*, 2010: variation, inheritance, selection as a framework for business practice, law, technology, language, custom, science).
- Godfrey-Smith, *Darwinian Populations and Natural Selection* (OUP 2009; Lakatos Award 2010) turns the recipe into a **space**: a Darwinian population is any collection with the capacity to evolve by natural selection; populations differ along quantitative parameters, chiefly heritability H, the smoothness of the fitness landscape C, and the dependence S of reproductive differences on intrinsic character; **paradigm cases** sit high on all, **marginal cases** low on some. Cultural evolution and memes are treated as marginal-to-questionable within the framework rather than in or out. **This is the best neutral instrument for the doorway's sorting question**: not "is X evolution?" but "where does X sit in Darwinian space?" — which is exactly the shape the site's own "graded, not gated" habit takes.
- The cultural case as the live dispute: SEP "Cultural Evolution" (Lewens, rev. May 2023) makes dual inheritance (Cavalli-Sforza & Feldman 1981; Boyd & Richerson 1985) the sustained programme, memetics "not well-established", Sperber's attractors the alternative, and states the disagreement as being about *how* to theorise cultural evolution and relate it to organic evolution, not whether it is important. Fracchia & Lewontin (1999): no analogues of reproduction and selection; sociocultural particularity is what the analogy lops off. The site's deep-time branch of philosophy of history already holds both sides of this, ending open.
- Economics: Veblen 1898 (page exists: the mechanistic-vs-cumulative-causation argument), Nelson & Winter 1982 (routines as the heritable unit; the founding text of the modern field), Basalla 1988 (technology, explicitly "evolution as metaphor"). Hull 1988 (page exists) applies the replicator-interactor scheme to science.

**8.3 What this suggests for the doorway, provisionally.**

- The sort is not by discipline. It is first by **lineage** (unfolding vs descent), then within descent by **position in Darwinian space**. Stellar and mineral evolution are not marginal Darwinian cases; they are the other lineage, and a doorway that files them as "loose talk" would be adopting biology's narrowing. Filing them as unfolding restores the word's older sense without a thesis.
- The teleological inheritance question (§6) resolves into the same cut: the unfolding lineage *is* the teleological one (what unfolds was already there), and Spencer is where it fused with descent and acquired the progress colouring. A page on the word's history would carry this; whether it is a doorway page or the reality side's is open.
- The Darwinism bundle is the descent lineage's biology backing and sits beneath the doorway. The doorway does not repeat it; it points at it as one home among the homes.
- Historical linguistics is the missing backing that matters most: the oldest descent-lineage home outside biology, the one that lent biology its tree, and nothing on the site. Candidate build: a subject (historical linguistics / the comparative method) with Schleicher as principal; Gray & Atkinson as the modern closure; Saussure's page already marks where the field turned away from it.

**8.4 Bearing on the agency/telescopic thread.** Godfrey-Smith's S parameter — how far reproductive differences depend on the entity's *intrinsic* character rather than its position — is a formal version of the inside/outside-description question the sketch carries (headlessness observer-relative; runners reshaping the landscape). Not pursued here; noted for when that thread opens.

## 9. Research round 2 (2026-09-28) — four homes at source

Full notes (five, with per-claim confirmed/unverified marks) are in `plan/evolution-research-round-2.md`. What they settle, and what they change:

**9.1 The word (history).** Haller 1744 brought "evolution" into biology for preformation; Lyell 1832 is the first species-sense use, a hostile paraphrase of Lamarck; Chambers 1844 used "development" throughout and never "evolution"; Spencer's 1852 essay already has "Theory of Evolution" and links embryo-from-cell to species-from-cell, which supports Richards (1992) that the embryological sense *carried* the species sense rather than being broken from it (against Bowler 1975). Darwin's first noun use is 1871, not 1872 (§8 corrected). On progress, Darwin's own text is two-sided: "no innate tendency to progressive development" (letter, 1872) and "does not necessarily include progressive development" (6th ed.) stand beside "will tend to progress towards perfection" (both editions); the "never say higher or lower" note is unconfirmed as to location. The allele-frequency definition's attribution to Dobzhansky 1937 is unverified and suspect. **Backing correction applied:** the Spencer page dated the "integration of matter…" formula to 1862; that wording is from 1867 onward, and the page now gives both.

**9.2 The unfolding homes.** Herschel 1789 read a stellar lifecycle off a garden of specimens; Lockyer 1890 and *Inorganic Evolution* 1900 borrowed the meaning explicitly from "organic evolution"; Russell 1913–14 read the H–R diagram as a single-star contraction track until Eddington and Bethe overturned it; Baade's Populations I and II (1944) are age classes, not lineages. Chaisson's cosmic evolution (2001) defines "developmental and generational changes" and widens selection to "preferential interaction of any object with its environment". Calvin's 1955 report already has four periods (nuclear, chemical, biological, psychosocial) and imports variation-and-selection via autocatalysis; Eigen 1971 derives Darwinian selection at the molecular level. Hazen 2008 disclaims Darwinian mechanism in so many words ("quartz does not out-compete olivine"), places mineral evolution under Chaisson's cosmic evolution, and was objected to on the word (Rosing 2008, Eldredge 2009); Wong–Hazen 2023 propose a law of increasing functional information with three kinds of "selection", of which Darwinian selection is one. **Structural point:** the hand-over from unfolding to descent sits at Calvin's autocatalysis → Eigen's molecular selection. Chaisson and Wong–Hazen do the opposite: they stretch "selection" until both lineages fall under one word. The doorway reports both moves; it adopts neither.

**9.3 Language change.** Schleicher's first tree is 1853, six years before the *Origin*, and his vocabulary was Hegelian *Entwicklung* and the organism's lifecycle; most nineteenth-century linguists kept the pre-Darwinian sense after 1859. So the oldest descent-lineage home outside biology *began on the unfolding side and crossed over* — which sharpens the meaning sort. Darwin's "struggle for life amongst words" is *Descent* vol. 1 ch. II (not ch. VI), quoting Müller; Whitney 1875 is the language-as-institution counter-voice. Neogrammarian sound laws (1878) are regularity of transmission, not selection; tree vs wave (Schmidt 1872) is the shape of descent independent of selection. Saussure first labelled his axes "static/evolutionary" before "synchronic/diachronic". The modern field answers "does language evolve Darwinianly?" on a five-rung ladder by apparatus carried: Croft 2000 and Pagel (full, utterance as replicator via Hull), phylogenetic linguistics (Gray & Atkinson 2003, Bouckaert 2012: method only), Keller 1994 (invisible hand), Lass 1997 (sceptic), and the pre-Darwinian sense beneath; the faculty-evolution debate (Hauser/Chomsky/Fitch vs Pinker/Jackendoff) is a separate question. Candidate principal: Schleicher (both sides of the sort), Croft as the modern one. **Backing: none on the site.**

**9.4 Economics, technology, science.** Read at primary texts where possible. Nelson & Winter 1982: routines "play the role that genes play", the theory is "unabashedly Lamarckian", and they disavow any higher-level abstract evolutionary theory. Schumpeter kept "evolution" for development from within and held that no appeal to biology would be of the slightest use. Marshall's "Mecca" line confirmed; Mokyr notes he never carried the programme out. Arthur 2009: the base mechanism "is not Darwinian". Mokyr: in nature there is no feedback from the manifest entity to its underlying structure, so the analogy limits. Hodgson & Knudsen 2010 (generalised Darwinism) vs Witt's continuity hypothesis, with Nelson himself among the sceptics. The most Darwinian apparatus in the cluster is in philosophy of science: Popper's natural selection of hypotheses, Campbell's blind variation; Kuhn's closing analogy in *Structure* was one he later retreated from. Godfrey-Smith's own chapter places culture as marginal and memes low on H and S. **The fields' self-placements spread across the gradient without anyone's help:** Schumpeter, Marshall, Veblen unfolding/cumulative; Alchian selection-only; Nelson & Winter Lamarckian; Hayek group-level and non-blind; Basalla and Ziman directed variation; Arthur non-Darwinian; Popper and Campbell blind-variation. **Backing:** Veblen, Schumpeter, Hayek, Hull, Arthur, Popper, Kuhn have pages; the Darwinism bundle's cultural-extensions page carries the Lamarckian caveats and names Nelson & Winter. Gaps noted, not built: the Popper page has no evolutionary-epistemology strand; the Arthur page lacks his own "not Darwinian" statement. Lacking entirely: Marshall, Alchian, Nelson & Winter, Hodgson, Witt, Mokyr, Campbell, Toulmin, Basalla, Godfrey-Smith — of whom Campbell, Toulmin, Mokyr and Godfrey-Smith have standing beyond the field.

**9.4b Civilisation and society (added the same day).** Almost everything called "evolution" at this scale is the unfolding sense, each author with a word of his own: Vico's corso and ricorso; Spengler's morphology of eight Cultures, explicitly anti-Darwinian, with civilisation as a Culture's old age; Toynbee's genesis, growth, breakdown and disintegration, rejecting Spengler's fixed lifespan; Sorokin's immanent fluctuation; Quigley's *Evolution of Civilizations* (1961), whose title word means the stage-sequence of one civilisation; Tainter's diminishing returns on complexity (1988); Diamond's collapse as choice (2005). The progress line is keyed to technology throughout: Morgan's ethnical periods by arts of subsistence (fire, bow, pottery, domestication, iron, alphabet), Engels built on him, Childe chose "revolution" deliberately, White's culture-equals-energy-times-technology law, Lenski's cumulation of technological information as prime mover; Steward and Sahlins's specific evolution sit at the border between the senses, adaptation language without heredity or sorting. Descent proper enters only with cultural group selection (Henrich; Turchin's *Ultrasociety*) and the assembly-theory lineage claim for the technosphere (Walker and Cronin: stacked lineages subject to natural selection), all weak on heritability, which is the weak point the site's cultural-extensions page already names. Turchin's cliodynamics and the Seshat databank (founded 2011; PNAS 2018, one principal component carrying 77% of the variance in social complexity) and Morris's four-trait social-development index are measurement programmes, and the historians' objection is that societies "cannot and should not be reduced to points in phase space". Technology at scale: Mumford's technics as shaped by choice and the megamachine of living parts; Ellul's autonomous technique; Gille's technical system; Kelly's technium as an extension of biological evolution; Haff's quasi-autonomous technosphere. Nisbet (1969) is the standing critique of the whole developmental metaphor as organic growth asserting inevitability. **Backing: the speculative and deep-time branches of philosophy of history carry the figures as history; no person pages** for Spengler, Toynbee, Vico, Comte, Tylor, Childe, Leslie White, Steward, Sahlins, Service, Lenski, Turchin, Morris, Diamond, Tainter, Mumford, Ellul, Kelly, Quigley, Sorokin, Nisbet, Boas or Ingold. Name collisions to watch: the site's Morgan is Thomas Hunt, its Kelly is George.

**9.5 What the round changes in the shape.** The meaning sort holds and gets a third feature: homes *migrate* between the two senses over time (linguistics crossed from unfolding to descent; chemistry hands over at autocatalysis; Chaisson and Hazen pull selection back over the unfolding side). A doorway that sorts by meaning can show the migrations; a doorway sorted by domain could not. The teleological question (§6) is now documented on both sides in Darwin's own text and is a page of its own by any measure. The person-page calls that arise — Schleicher, Godfrey-Smith, Campbell, Croft, possibly Chaisson and Calvin — are collaborative, none admitted by being listed.
